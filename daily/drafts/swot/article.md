**Quick answer:** A SWOT analysis is a four-box list of your **Strengths** and **Weaknesses** (things inside you or your business that you control) and your **Opportunities** and **Threats** (things outside you that you don't control). It is useful only if you take one more step: match the boxes against each other to decide what to *do*. That step is called the TOWS matrix.

Below are worked examples for a job switcher, a kirana store and a home bakery, the action step most guides skip, how to answer it in an interview, and what researchers actually say about where SWOT came from.

## The four boxes, and the one rule that matters

|  | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** (you control it) | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| **External** (you don't control it) | Opportunities | Threats |

The rule: **if you can change it by your own effort, it is internal.** Your skills, your shop's location and your savings are internal. A new metro line, a hiring freeze or a change in government rules is external. Most bad SWOTs break this rule. "I can learn Python" is not an opportunity; it is a plan. The opportunity is "Companies in my city are hiring data analysts who know Python." The weakness is "I don't know Python yet."

## Worked example 1: a personal SWOT for a fresher or job switcher

Take Priya (an illustrative example), a B.Com graduate from Indore, two years into a back-office accounting job, who wants to move into financial analysis.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Two years of real reconciliation and GST invoice work | No formal finance qualification beyond B.Com |
| Strong Excel (pivot tables, lookups) | Has never built a financial model |
| Good written English; trusted by her manager | Nervous in group discussions and interviews |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Finance and accounting back offices in Indian cities hire people who know both books and spreadsheets | Many applicants with CA Inter or CFA Level 1 compete for the same roles |
| Online courses in financial modelling are cheap or free | Routine accounting tasks are being automated |
| Her company has an internal job posting system | A long notice period can put off employers |

Every point is specific. "Hard-working" appears on almost everyone's SWOT and tells an employer nothing; "trusted by her manager to close the month-end books alone" says it with evidence.

## Worked example 2: a kirana store

Ramesh (also an illustrative example) runs a 20-year-old general store in a residential colony in Pune.

- **Strengths:** knows most customers by name; gives credit (khata) to regular families; open early and late; home delivery by his son within the colony.
- **Weaknesses:** no digital record of stock or credit; limited shelf space; little choice in packaged snacks and personal care brands.
- **Opportunities:** most customers already pay by UPI; distributor apps let small shops order stock from a phone; two new housing towers coming up nearby.
- **Threats:** quick-commerce apps delivering in minutes; a supermarket chain opening on the main road; rising rent.

## Worked example 3: a home bakery

Sana (an illustrative example too) bakes cakes and cookies from her flat in Hyderabad and takes orders on Instagram and WhatsApp.

- **Strengths:** eggless and custom-theme cakes that customers photograph and share; low overheads; repeat birthday customers.
- **Weaknesses:** capacity of one oven; no fixed price list, so she undercharges; depends entirely on her own time.
- **Opportunities:** festive gifting seasons (Diwali, Eid, Christmas); offices ordering for team celebrations; nearby cafés looking for local suppliers.
- **Threats:** many other home bakers on the same platforms; rising butter and chocolate prices; food-safety rules and costs as orders grow.

On that last point: even a home bakery needs basic FSSAI registration from day one. According to an FSSAI press release of March 2026, from 1 April 2026 food businesses with annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore need only FSSAI registration (the limit was ₹12 lakh before); above that they need a State licence (up to ₹50 crore) or a Central licence. Check the [FSSAI website](https://fssai.gov.in/) for the current rules.

## The step most guides skip: turning SWOT into actions (TOWS)

A SWOT is a description. To get a plan, pair the boxes, as Heinz Weihrich set out in "The TOWS matrix: a tool for situational analysis" (*Long Range Planning*, 1982):

| Pairing | Question to ask | Kirana store (Ramesh) | Home bakery (Sana) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S–O** (use strengths to grab opportunities) | How can what I'm good at win the new chance? | Offer khata and WhatsApp ordering to the new towers' residents before the apps reach them | Pitch eggless theme cakes to offices for team birthdays |
| **W–O** (fix weaknesses using opportunities) | Which opportunity helps me fix a gap? | Use a distributor app to widen the snack range without extra capital | Publish a price list for the Diwali gift boxes so she stops undercharging |
| **S–T** (use strengths to blunt threats) | How do my strengths protect me? | Compete on trust and credit, which apps don't give, not on price | Push the custom designs no mass seller copies |
| **W–T** (reduce weaknesses to survive threats) | What must I fix so a threat doesn't sink me? | Record credit digitally so bad debt doesn't pile up when margins tighten | Keep FSSAI registration current and work out true costs before taking bigger orders |

For Priya: **S–O** apply internally for an analyst role on her Excel record; **W–O** take a modelling course and build two models from listed Indian companies' annual reports; **W–T** do mock interviews so nerves don't cost her.

Then pick two or three actions, not twelve, and turn them into weekly habits (see [goals vs systems](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/goals-vs-systems)).

## How to answer "Tell me your SWOT" in an interview or GDPI

Campus placements, bank interviews and B-school personal interviews often ask for a SWOT. The panel is testing self-awareness, not looking for a perfect candidate.

1. **Strengths (two, with proof).** "I'm comfortable with numbers: I handled GST reconciliation for 40 clients on my own."
2. **Weakness (one, real, and what you're doing about it).** "Public speaking. I've joined my college debate society and now present in every team review." Avoid fake weaknesses like "I work too hard": panels hear it daily.
3. **Opportunity (external, linked to the role).** "Indian companies are investing heavily in analytics, and this role puts me at the centre of that."
4. **Threat (external, and how you'll handle it).** "Automation will take over routine reporting, so I'm learning modelling and SQL to stay on the analysis side."

Keep it to about a minute, and make sure it doesn't contradict your answer to [tell me about yourself](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/tell-me-about-yourself). More practice is in our [interview questions and answers](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/interview-questions-and-answers) guide.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing internal and external.** "Good market demand" is an opportunity, not a strength.
- **Writing a wish list.** "Will start a YouTube channel" is an action, not a SWOT point.
- **Vague words, too many points, no checking.** Five specific, ranked points per box, each tested against facts (sales, customer feedback, your appraisal), beat twenty vague ones.
- **Stopping at the grid.** Without the TOWS step, nothing changes.

## Limitations and criticism

SWOT has well-known critics. In "SWOT analysis: it's time for a product recall" (*Long Range Planning*, 1997), Terry Hill and Roy Westbrook reviewed 50 companies in a UK government manufacturing support scheme. According to the paper's abstract, more than 20 of them used a SWOT, and the analyses shared the same problems: long lists (over 40 factors on average), vague wording, no prioritising and no checking. Most damning, nobody used the SWOT output in the later stages of planning. You can read the abstract on [Oxford's research archive](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:e44a6a9e-9537-4fb6-848c-2d20998a7ce6).

SWOT is also a snapshot that dates quickly, and the same fact can sit in two boxes (a small team is both nimble and stretched). Treat it as a starting conversation, not an answer.

## Who invented SWOT? (Not quite who you've read)

Many websites credit **Albert Humphrey** of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI) in the 1960s. The documented story is more nuanced.

Researchers Richard Puyt, Finn Birger Lie and Celeste Wilderom traced the history through archives and interviews in "The origins of SWOT analysis" (*Long Range Planning*, 2023). They credit **Robert F. Stewart**, who led SRI's planning research group, as the originator. His group's 1965 **SOFT** approach asked managers to sort issues into safeguarding the Satisfactory, opening Opportunities, fixing Faults and thwarting Threats; the labels were later changed to Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats.

Humphrey was a real member of that SRI team, and he wrote about SWOT's origins in a 2005 SRI alumni newsletter, but the researchers could not confirm his account and place the invention with Stewart's group, not Humphrey alone. A follow-up published online in 2024 by Puyt, Lie and Dag Øivind Madsen (*Journal of Management History*) also rejects the claim that SWOT came from Harvard, and dates the switch from SOFT to SWOT to SRI's executive seminars in 1966.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between SWOT and TOWS?
SWOT lists your strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. TOWS pairs them (strength with opportunity, weakness with threat and so on) to produce specific strategies, as set out by Heinz Weihrich in 1982.

### Is a personal SWOT useful for students?
Yes, if it is specific and honest. It helps you choose courses or jobs, prepare for interviews and GDPI rounds, and decide which one or two skills to build next.

### How many points should each SWOT box have?
Three to five is enough. Rank them and focus on the top two in each box; long unranked lists were one of the main failings researchers found.

### Did Albert Humphrey invent SWOT analysis?
Not on his own, according to a 2023 archival study by Puyt, Lie and Wilderom, which credits the Stanford Research Institute group led by Robert F. Stewart; Humphrey was a member of that team.
