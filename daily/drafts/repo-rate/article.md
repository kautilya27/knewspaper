**Quick answer:** The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) lends money overnight to banks against government bonds. It is decided by RBI's six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) by majority vote, with one job written into law: keep retail inflation at 4%, within a band of 2% to 6%. On 7 October 2026 the MPC raised the repo rate by 0.25 percentage points to **5.50%**, according to RBI's policy statement.

That decision matters to you because most new floating-rate home loans from banks in India are tied directly to the repo rate. When it goes up, your EMI or your loan tenure goes up within about three months. The MPC meets next on **2 to 4 December 2026**, according to RBI's published schedule.

## The four rates in plain words

Banks borrow from and park money with RBI every day. The rates on those deals form a narrow "corridor" with the repo rate in the middle.

| Rate | What it is | Rate after 7 Oct 2026 (RBI) |
|---|---|---|
| Repo rate | RBI lends to banks overnight, against government bonds. This is the policy rate. | 5.50% |
| Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) | Banks park spare cash with RBI and earn interest, with no bonds needed from RBI. This is the floor of the corridor. | 5.25% |
| Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) | Emergency overnight borrowing for a bank that has run short. Costlier, so it is the ceiling. | 5.75% |
| Bank Rate | A longer-term reference rate, now kept equal to MSF and used mainly for penalties. | 5.75% |

So the corridor today runs from 5.25% to 5.75%. RBI uses its daily operations to keep the overnight interbank (call money) rate close to the repo rate inside that band.

### What happened to the reverse repo rate?

Older explainers still describe the reverse repo rate as the rate RBI pays banks to absorb their cash. Since April 2022, the SDF has taken over that role as the floor of the corridor. The reverse repo still exists as a tool RBI can use at its discretion, but it is no longer the rate that matters for the market. When you read about policy rates today, look for the SDF instead.

## Who sits on the Monetary Policy Committee

The MPC was set up under the RBI Act, 1934 (amended in 2016). It has six members:

- **Three from RBI:** the Governor (who chairs), the Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one RBI officer nominated by RBI's central board.
- **Three external members:** experts appointed by the Central Government for four years.

According to RBI's October 2026 statement, the current members are Sanjay Malhotra (Governor, chair), Poonam Gupta (Deputy Governor), Indranil Bhattacharyya (RBI), and external members Nagesh Kumar, Saugata Bhattacharya and Ram Singh.

### How the voting works

The rules are in Section 45ZI of the RBI Act:

1. At least four meetings a year (RBI holds six, every two months, each over three days).
2. Quorum is four members, one of whom must be the Governor (or the Deputy Governor in the Governor's absence).
3. Each member has one vote and the decision is by majority.
4. If the vote is tied 3-3, the Governor has a second, casting vote.
5. Each member's vote is recorded by name and each writes a statement of reasons. Under Section 45ZL, these are published in the minutes on the 14th day after the meeting.

The government can send its views in writing but does not vote. The committee votes on the repo rate and the "stance", a signal of the likely direction ahead. In October 2026 the rate hike was unanimous, but two members, Nagesh Kumar and Ram Singh, voted to keep the stance at "neutral" while the majority moved it to "calibrated tightening".

## The 4% target: who sets it and what "failure" means

The MPC does not choose its own target. The **Central Government**, in consultation with RBI, sets it every five years under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act. In March 2026 the government retained the target at **4% CPI inflation, with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%**, for **1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031**, as reported by Business Today and other outlets at the time.

Inflation here means year-on-year change in the all-India Consumer Price Index, published by the National Statistics Office (MoSPI). Our explainer on [how inflation is measured in India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-is-inflation-measured-in-india) covers how that index is built.

RBI is treated as having failed if average inflation stays above 6% or below 2% for **three consecutive quarters**. It must then send the government a report explaining why, what it will do, and how long it expects to take.

### Where inflation stands now

According to MoSPI's release of 12 November 2026, CPI inflation (base 2024=100) was **6.09% in October 2026**, up from 5.54% in September, with food inflation at 8.94%. That is one month above the upper limit, not yet a "failure" under the law. In October the MPC projected inflation at 6.0% for the October-December quarter and 5.2% for 2026-27 as a whole. RBI's statement said inflation and its outlook "are not benign as they were last year", with food and fuel prices the main drivers.

## How a repo change reaches your home-loan EMI

Since 1 October 2019, RBI has required banks to link all new floating-rate retail loans (home, auto and others) and loans to micro and small enterprises to an external benchmark. Most banks chose the repo rate, which gives the "repo-linked lending rate" or EBLR. The rules, set out in RBI's circular of 4 September 2019 (as amended in 2025):

- Your rate = benchmark (repo) + the bank's spread.
- The rate must be reset **at least once every three months**.
- The bank can change the credit-risk part of the spread only if your credit assessment changes substantially, and other parts of the spread only once every three years, though since 1 October 2025 banks may reduce them sooner.

### A worked example

Suppose you took a ₹50 lakh home loan for 20 years, and your bank charges repo plus 2.50%. (The spread is an illustration; check your own sanction letter.)

| | Before (repo 5.25%) | After (repo 5.50%) |
|---|---|---|
| Your loan rate | 7.75% | 8.00% |
| EMI on ₹50 lakh, 20 years | about ₹41,047 | about ₹41,822 |
| Extra per month | | about ₹775 |
| If EMI is kept the same instead | | tenure grows by about 12 months |

A 0.25 point hike looks small, but over a full 20-year loan the extra EMI adds up to roughly ₹1.86 lakh. Banks often stretch the tenure instead, which quietly costs more over time.

RBI's rules of 18 August 2023 on resetting floating rates give you choices here: when the rate goes up, your lender must offer a higher EMI, a longer tenure or a mix, may offer you a switch to a fixed rate (since 1 October 2025 this is at the lender's discretion, no longer compulsory), and must send you a quarterly statement showing EMIs left and the rate charged. If you are comparing lenders, our guide to [documents for a home loan application](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/essential-documents-for-your-home-loan-application) may help.

Older loans linked to a bank's MCLR or base rate move more slowly, because those rates follow the bank's own cost of funds and reset on fixed dates, often once a year.

## Common myths about the repo rate

**"RBI sets my loan rate."** It sets only the benchmark. The spread is your bank's, and two borrowers at the same bank can pay different rates.

**"One month above 6% means RBI has failed."** The legal test is the average over three straight quarters, not a single monthly print.

**"The government decides the repo rate."** The government sets the inflation target and appoints the external members. The rate itself is voted on by the MPC, and the government has no vote.

**"Raising rates lowers prices."** It slows the rise in prices by making borrowing dearer and saving more attractive, which cools demand over months. It does not make prices fall back, and it acts with a lag of several quarters.

## The main debate

The sharpest argument is about food. Much of today's inflation comes from food prices, which depend on monsoons and supply, not on interest rates. Critics say raising rates for a vegetable price spike hurts borrowers and growth without fixing the cause. The Economic Survey 2023-24 suggested studying whether India should target inflation excluding food.

The other side, which the government's March 2026 decision to keep the headline CPI target reflects, argues that food is a large share of what Indian households buy, and if high food prices persist they push up wages and other prices too. Acting early, on this view, stops a short spike becoming lasting inflation. Even inside the MPC, the October vote shows the split: all six agreed on the hike, but two members did not want to signal more hikes ahead.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the current repo rate in India?

The repo rate is 5.50%, after the MPC raised it by 0.25 percentage points on 7 October 2026. The SDF is 5.25% and the MSF and Bank Rate are 5.75%, according to RBI.

### When is the next RBI monetary policy meeting?

RBI's schedule for 2026-27 lists the next MPC meeting for 2 to 4 December 2026, with the decision announced on the last day. The one after that is 3 to 5 February 2027.

### How soon will my home-loan EMI change after a repo hike?

For a repo-linked loan, the new rate applies at your bank's next reset, which RBI requires at least once every three months. Many banks reset sooner, and often extend the tenure instead of raising the EMI unless you ask.

### Can the Governor overrule the other MPC members?

No. Each of the six members has one vote and the majority decides. The Governor gets an extra casting vote only when the vote is tied 3-3.
