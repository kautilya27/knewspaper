**Quick answer:** Rahul Gandhi (born 19 June 1970, New Delhi) is an Indian politician of the Indian National Congress and, since June 2024, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha. He has been an MP since 2004, apart from a disqualification of about four months in 2023, first from Amethi, then Wayanad, and now Rae Bareli in Uttar Pradesh. He studied at Rollins College in Florida and Trinity College, Cambridge, and led two long cross-country marches, the Bharat Jodo Yatra (2022–23) and the Bharat Jodo Nyay Yatra (2024).

This profile looks at his career and at what supporters and observers credit him with. Every fact comes from official records, his reported statements, fact-checkers or major news outlets, and disputed points carry the other view.

## Family background

Rahul Gandhi is the son of Rajiv Gandhi, India's sixth prime minister, and Sonia Gandhi, a former president of the Congress. Britannica notes that his grandmother Indira Gandhi and his great-grandfather Jawaharlal Nehru were both prime ministers too. His sister, Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, became the MP for Wayanad after he gave up that seat in 2024.

His school years were shaped by two killings in the family. According to a July 2026 Business Today profile, he went to St Columba's School in Delhi and joined The Doon School in Dehradun in 1981. After Indira Gandhi was assassinated in 1984, security worries meant he and his sister finished school at home with tutors. Rajiv Gandhi was assassinated in May 1991.

## Rahul Gandhi's education: degrees verified

| Stage | Institution | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| School | St Columba's, Delhi; The Doon School, Dehradun; then home tutors | Doon from 1981; home study after 1984 for security reasons (Business Today) |
| College, India | St Stephen's College, Delhi University | Joined to study History in 1989–90 and left after about a year (Business Today) |
| College, USA | Harvard University, then Rollins College, Florida | Moved from Harvard to Rollins after May 1991 for security reasons and studied under the surname "Vinci"; bachelor's degree from Rollins in 1994 (Congress party profile; Business Today for the "Vinci" surname; 1994 graduation also noted in Onmanorama's report on his 2019 nomination) |
| Postgraduate | Trinity College, University of Cambridge | M.Phil in Development Studies, 1995 |

The Cambridge degree has been questioned more than once. In 2009, the then Vice-Chancellor of Cambridge, Professor Alison Richard, confirmed in a letter that he was awarded an M.Phil in Development Studies in 1995 under the name "Raul Vinci", as reported by Deccan Herald and The Hindu. In April 2019, objections about his education and citizenship were raised against his Amethi nomination. The Returning Officer rejected all of them and declared the nomination valid, Onmanorama reported.

## Career before politics

After Cambridge, he worked for about three years at the Monitor Group, a management consulting firm in London. He returned to India in 2002 and co-founded Backops Services Pvt Ltd, a technology outsourcing and consultancy firm in Mumbai, where he was a director (Business Today; Congress party profile).

The 2023 episode is worth explaining. He was convicted in a criminal defamation case in Surat and given the maximum two-year sentence, which disqualified him from the Lok Sabha. On 4 August 2023, a three-judge bench of the Supreme Court stayed the conviction. Scroll reported that the bench noted the trial court had given no specific reason for the maximum sentence, and that a sentence even one day shorter would not have led to disqualification. The court also said his remarks were not in good taste and that public figures should be careful in speeches. He responded: "This had to happen as the truth emerges victorious one day or the other."

## The two yatras

### Bharat Jodo Yatra (2022–23)

The Bharat Jodo ("Unite India") Yatra began in Kanyakumari on 7 September 2022 and ended in Srinagar on 30 January 2023. The Week reported that it covered about 4,080 km through 12 states and two Union Territories, mostly on foot. Speaking at the end, he said, "The aim of the yatra was to unite India," and, "I met lakhs of people, talked to them."

### Bharat Jodo Nyay Yatra (2024)

The second march, focused on *nyay* (justice), started from Manipur on 14 January 2024 and ended in Mumbai in mid-March. It covered about 6,700 km across 15 states, partly by bus and partly on foot, according to news reports and the Congress party's own record.

## What supporters and observers credit him with

- **Meeting voters on the ground.** Supporters see the yatras, months of walking and meeting people directly, as his defining work.
- **A stronger opposition after 2024.** The Congress won 99 Lok Sabha seats in 2024, nearly twice its 2019 tally, and regained official Opposition status. The Leader of the Opposition post in the Lok Sabha had been vacant since 2014, and he filled it. In Rae Bareli he won by about 3.9 lakh votes, Scroll reported.
- **Pressing his issues in Parliament.** As Leader of the Opposition, he has demanded a caste census and a legal guarantee of minimum support price (MSP) for farmers, and questioned the Agniveer scheme and raised exam paper leaks, according to All India Radio's [report on his July 2024 budget speech](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/rahul-gandhi-demands-caste-census-legal-guarantee-of-msp-to-farmers/). When the Union Cabinet decided in April 2025 to count caste in the next census, he welcomed it: "We support this completely but we want a timeline" (Business Standard). In 2026 he raised the NEET-UG paper-leak allegations in Parliament (Business Today).
- **Taking responsibility.** His 2019 resignation letter said, "Accountability is critical for the future growth of our party."

Not every campaign has gone unchallenged. In August 2025 he alleged irregularities in voter rolls. The Election Commission rejected the claims and asked him either to submit them in a signed declaration or to apologise, All India Radio reported.

## The "Pappu" label and what fact-checkers found

"Pappu" is a derogatory nickname used against him online, largely in memes and short video clips. Independent fact-checkers have repeatedly found that some of the clips used to mock him were cut or misattributed. Here are some documented cases:

| Viral claim | What the full video showed | Fact-checker |
|---|---|---|
| He promised a machine that turns potatoes into gold (2017, Patan, Gujarat) | He was quoting a promise he said a political opponent had made to farmers, and said the words were not his | Alt News, April 2019 |
| He didn't notice his own slip of the tongue (Jaipur rally, January 2020) | He corrected himself and apologised straight away; the clip cut this out | Alt News, January 2020 |
| He called himself a tailor (June 2018 speech) | He was quoting a fashion designer to make a point about workers who go uncredited | BOOM, December 2023 (rated False) |
| He said Mahatma Gandhi created the Constitution (Munger, Bihar, August 2025) | He first credited Dr B.R. Ambedkar; the clip removed that line | [Alt News, August 2025](https://www.altnews.in/did-rahul-gandhi-credit-mahatma-gandhi-for-drafting-indias-constitution-no-viral-video-is-clipped/) (rated False) |

Together, these fact-checks show that trimmed clips have been a recurring part of the online mockery aimed at him. Not every viral clip of him is fake: he has made real slips, as any public speaker does. The lesson is to watch the full video before sharing.

### How commentary on his image changed after the yatras

After the first yatra, a Press Trust of India report quoted branding experts who said his "Pappufication" might be over. Advertising veteran Prahlad Kakkar said the beard he grew during the march "has given him a certain degree of seriousness." The Diplomat cited the report but left open whether he had really reshaped his public image. In December 2024, Outlook Business named him a "Changemaker", saying he had "transformed into a leader with an electorate of his own."

## Criticism, in brief

Critics point to the Congress's two heavy defeats while he was a top leader: 44 seats in 2014 and 52 in 2019, Al Jazeera reported. He resigned as party president after the second. Critics also see his rise as an example of dynasty politics. He addressed this at the University of California, Berkeley, in 2017, saying "most parties in India have that problem" and that the real question is whether a person is capable and sensitive (PTI via Business Standard).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Rahul Gandhi's educational qualification?

He holds a bachelor's degree from Rollins College, Florida (1994) and an M.Phil in Development Studies from Trinity College, Cambridge (1995). Cambridge's then Vice-Chancellor confirmed the M.Phil in a 2009 letter.

### Which constituency does Rahul Gandhi represent?

He is the Lok Sabha MP for Rae Bareli in Uttar Pradesh, elected in 2024. Earlier, he represented Amethi (2004–2019) and Wayanad (2019–2024).

### Is Rahul Gandhi the Leader of the Opposition?

Yes. He was recognised as Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha with effect from 9 June 2024, and the latest reports still describe him in that role. He marked two years in the post in June 2026.

### How long was the Bharat Jodo Yatra?

It covered about 4,080 km from Kanyakumari to Srinagar between 7 September 2022 and 30 January 2023, passing through 12 states and two Union Territories. The 2024 Nyay Yatra covered about 6,700 km from Manipur to Mumbai.
