**Quick answer:** No court in India will order you to keep working for an employer you want to leave. The Supreme Court said in *Sanjay Jain v. National Aviation Co. of India* (2018) that "to resign is a right of an employee who cannot be forced to serve". But a notice period in your contract is still a binding promise. If you leave early, your employer can usually recover notice pay, ask for damages it can justify, and, in some cases, hold back your relieving letter.

So the real question is what leaving early will cost you and what can lawfully be withheld. That depends on your contract, your role and, since 21 November 2025, the four labour codes.

## Where notice period rules actually come from

No single law sets one notice period for every private job. Several sources stack up:

| Source | Who it covers | What it says on notice |
|---|---|---|
| Your appointment letter and HR policy | Everyone who signed one | The main source. Typically 30, 60 or 90 days, shorter in probation |
| Indian Contract Act, 1872 | All contracts | Leaving early is a breach; Sections 73 and 74 allow compensation, capped at any amount the contract names |
| Specific Relief Act, 1963 | All contracts | Courts don't order specific performance of personal-service contracts, so the employer gets money, not your attendance |
| Standing orders under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020 | Establishments with 300 or more "workers" | Service conditions incl. exits; Model Standing Orders, 2026 issued 8 May 2026 |
| State Shops and Establishments Acts | Shops, offices, IT firms | Vary by state; mostly about notice the employer must give you |
| Code on Wages, 2019 | Practically all employees | Deadline for your final dues |

Under the labour codes, a "worker" is broadly non-managerial staff; supervisors earning over ₹18,000 a month are excluded (a ceiling the Labour Ministry notified on 30 January 2026). Your duties decide it, not your title. If you aren't a "worker", standing orders mostly don't apply and your contract matters even more.

## Can your employer force you to serve the full notice?

Not through a court order. Here's what courts have said:

- **Forced service:** *Sanjay Jain* treats resignation as a right unless service rules say otherwise. The remedy for a broken notice clause is money.
- **Notice clauses are not "restraint of trade":** Section 27 of the Contract Act voids agreements that stop you working elsewhere, but a notice or minimum-service clause governs your current job, not future ones. On 14 May 2025, in *Vijaya Bank v. Prashant B. Narnaware*, the Supreme Court upheld a ₹2 lakh payment for a manager who left before completing a three-year minimum term. It held the clause was "in furtherance of the employment contract and not to restrain future employment", according to [LiveLaw's report of the judgment](https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/employment-bond-valid-doesnt-violate-s27-contract-act-supreme-court-upholds-rs-2-lakh-penalty-premature-resignation-292226).
- **Damages must be reasonable:** Section 74 allows "reasonable compensation" up to the amount named in the contract, so a bond figure is a cap, not automatic. *Vijaya Bank* found ₹2 lakh reasonable for a senior officer given re-recruitment costs; a bigger sum for a junior employee could be judged differently.

## Relieving letters and experience certificates

This is where most disputes happen. No law specifically requires a private employer to issue a "relieving letter" on demand, but two 2026 developments matter.

**The Model Standing Orders, 2026** require establishments they cover to give a service certificate within 10 days of a worker's resignation, termination or retirement, according to law firm JSA's summary of the notified orders. It covers only those establishments and "workers".

**The Bombay High Court has backed an employer where a bond was broken.** In *Bharat Aviation v. Rahul Sudhindra Soni* (5 May 2026), the Bombay High Court set aside an Industrial Court order that had told an aviation firm to issue a relieving letter and service certificate. The engineer had left during a three-year post-training bond. The judge called these papers "just a consequential act post acceptance of the resignation", so an employer justified in refusing the resignation couldn't be forced to issue them. The findings were prima facie; the main case continues.

In practice, leaving in breach turns the letter into a negotiation, often settled by paying the buyout. If your employer refuses even after you've paid or served in full, a labour lawyer can tell you which forum suits your role and state.

## Final settlement: the 2-working-day rule

Under Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, 2019, wages due to an employee who resigns, is removed, dismissed or retrenched "shall be paid within two working days". The code took effect on 21 November 2025 with the other labour codes, according to the [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2192463&reg=48&lang=2).

- The clock most sensibly runs from your last working day, not the day you resign, as an analysis on LiveLaw points out.
- "Wages" here means basic pay, DA, retaining allowance and some allowances. Gratuity has its own 30-day timeline under the Code on Social Security, 2020. Whether leave encashment counts is still debated.
- The government can set a different deadline under Section 17(3). Policies promising "F&F in 45 days" sit uneasily with the code.

## How a notice buyout is calculated: a worked example

The formula most companies use is:

> Buyout = (monthly salary ÷ 30) × days of notice not served

The big variable is basic or gross salary; your appointment letter should say. Some firms divide by 26 instead of 30.

**Example:** Priya earns ₹1,00,000 a month gross, with basic pay of ₹50,000 (half her pay, in line with the labour codes' 50% wage rule). Her notice is 90 days, and her new employer wants her in 30 days. That leaves 60 days unserved.

| Basis in contract | Daily rate | Buyout for 60 days |
|---|---|---|
| Basic pay | ₹50,000 ÷ 30 = ₹1,667 | ₹1,00,000 |
| Gross pay | ₹1,00,000 ÷ 30 = ₹3,333 | ₹2,00,000 |

The basis alone doubles the bill. Her full and final settlement, if she leaves mid-month on a basic-pay contract:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Salary for 15 days of the final month | + ₹50,000 |
| 10 days' leave encashment on basic (if her policy pays it) | + ₹16,667 |
| Notice recovery, basic basis | − ₹1,00,000 |
| **Net** | **− ₹33,333 (Priya owes this)** |

A negative F&F is common, which is why people ask to adjust leftover leave against notice (many policies allow it) or ask the new employer for a joining bonus.

### Should GST be added to notice pay?

No, according to the tax department's own clarification. CBIC Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST (3 August 2022) says forfeiture of salary or a bond amount for quitting early is not consideration for a service, so it is not taxable. In late 2022 the Kerala High Court, in *Manappuram Finance v. Assistant Commissioner*, applied the circular to notice pay recoveries. It applied the circular to past periods and ordered a refund. An 18% GST charge added to a buyout would run against this circular.

### What it does to your tax and Form 130 (formerly Form 16)

- **Deducted from your F&F:** You should be taxed on what you actually received. Where your TDS certificate (Form 130 from tax year 2026-27; Form 16 for earlier years) showed more, the Ahmedabad bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal held in a 2017 case, under the old Income-tax Act, that the notice recovery is not taxable as salary. Ask HR to show the recovery clearly, and keep the F&F statement.
- **Paid from your own pocket:** No deduction is available.
- **Reimbursed by your new employer:** This is generally taxed as salary, like a joining bonus.

If the amount is large, check with a tax professional.

## Before you resign

- Read the exit clause: notice length, buyout basis, probation terms, any bond, and whether leave can offset notice.
- Resign in writing and keep the acknowledgement. Ask for your last working day in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 90-day notice period legal in India?

Generally yes. No central law caps the notice period a private contract can set, though any damages claimed for breaking it must be reasonable.

### Can my company refuse to accept my resignation?

It can't stop you leaving, but it can treat an early exit as a breach and recover notice pay or a bond amount. The Bombay High Court has said, at the interim stage, that a relieving letter follows acceptance of the resignation.

### Can my employer take the buyout from my salary without asking?

If your contract allows notice recovery, employers usually adjust it in the full and final settlement. If it exceeds what the contract allows, raise it in writing with HR, and consult a labour lawyer if needed.

### How long does a company have to pay my full and final settlement?

Under Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, in force since 21 November 2025, wages due on resignation must be paid within two working days. Gratuity has a separate 30-day timeline.
