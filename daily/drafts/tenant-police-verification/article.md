**Quick answer:** In most big Indian cities you can do tenant police verification online on the state or city police portal or app. Delhi has its citizen services portal, Mumbai, Navi Mumbai and Pune have tenant information forms on their police websites, Uttar Pradesh uses the UPCOP app, Haryana uses the Harsamay portal and Hyderabad uses the Hawk Eye app. The landlord fills in their own details, the property, the tenant's ID and photo, the tenant's workplace and one or two people who know the tenant, then keeps the acknowledgement.

No single national law makes it compulsory. It becomes compulsory because police commissioners and district magistrates issue orders under **Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)**, the provision that replaced Section 144 of the old CrPC in 2024. A landlord who ignores such an order can be booked under **Section 223 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**. Mumbai's service is free and UP's fee is ₹50.

## How the online process works

The forms look much the same in most cities. On Mumbai Police's Tenant Information form, the landlord enters:

1. **Owner details:** name, photo, mobile number, email and address, plus the police station covering the rented flat.
2. **Rented property:** full address and the agreement's start and end dates.
3. **Tenant details:** name, photo, permanent address and an uploaded ID (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, ration card or driving licence; JPEG or PNG up to 4 MB).
4. **Workplace details:** the tenant's occupation, office address and contact details.
5. **References:** at least one person who knows the tenant, with a phone number.

An OTP goes to the owner's phone to confirm the filing. Mumbai and Navi Mumbai Police also say the landlord's own address has to be different from the rented property's address.

UP's rules are similar but stricter about files. According to the Saharanpur district website, you need a recent passport-size photo of the tenant plus identity and address proof. Each file has to be 200 KB or smaller, and an application filed without the ₹50 fee counts as incomplete. UP's DGP said in January 2026 that a tenant verification now takes about eight days on UPCOP, down from 24 to 25 days (PTI).

### Documents to keep ready

- Tenant's photo and identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, passport or driving licence)
- Tenant's permanent address proof
- Tenant's employer or college details
- Your own ID, contact details and ownership details for the flat
- The rent or leave-and-licence agreement with its dates (see our guide to [getting a rental agreement online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-get-a-rental-agreement-online-in-india))
- For a foreign tenant: passport, visa and registration details. Mumbai Police orders ask for these as well.

## The legal basis, explained simply

**Section 163 BNSS** lets a district magistrate, or a police commissioner acting as executive magistrate, issue an urgent order to prevent danger or disturbance. LiveLaw notes that such an order generally lasts two months. That's why tenant orders keep getting reissued and why the dates in news reports change. A recent example is the Jammu district magistrate's order of October 2025. It gave owners until 6 November to report existing tenants and seven days for new ones, using a declaration delivered in person or by registered post, and it warned of action under Section 223 BNS (The Tribune).

**Section 223 BNS** punishes knowingly disobeying an order that a public servant is lawfully empowered to issue. According to the text of the Sanhita:

- if the disobedience causes or risks obstruction, annoyance or injury, the penalty is up to **six months' simple imprisonment, a fine of up to ₹2,500, or both**
- if it endangers human life, health or safety, or causes a riot, the penalty is up to **one year's imprisonment, a fine of up to ₹5,000, or both**

The Delhi cases reported in November 2025 were under the first clause. Some articles quote these penalties without the conditions attached; the higher one isn't automatic. Older articles cite IPC Section 188, which BNS 223 replaced from 1 July 2024.

In Maharashtra there is one more link. Pune Police say leave-and-licence agreements registered online through the IGR's i-SARITA e-registration system can be seen by police through CCTNS, the national crime records network. Even so, Pune Police still ask landlords to file the tenant details themselves.

## What tenants should check

Police verification protects tenants too. A filed record shows you were a lawful occupant if there's ever a dispute. Before you hand over documents:

- **Use only the official portal or police station.** Mumbai Police say the service is free and ask people to report anyone who demands money for it. A broker charging for a "police NOC" in Mumbai is charging for something you don't need.
- **Ask for the acknowledgement or request number** and keep a screenshot. If the landlord says they've filed but can't show anything, they probably haven't.
- **Check the details.** Your name, permanent address and workplace should be correct, because a mismatch can bring a police call or visit.
- **Know what false information costs.** Mumbai's form warns that giving the police false information is a punishable offence, and that applies to tenants and landlords alike.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is tenant police verification mandatory in India?
There is no single national law. It is compulsory wherever the police commissioner or district magistrate has issued an order under Section 163 BNSS, as in Delhi, Mumbai, Pune and Jammu. Breaking such an order can lead to a case under Section 223 BNS.

### What is the penalty for not doing tenant police verification?
Under Section 223 BNS, the penalty is up to six months' simple imprisonment, a fine of up to ₹2,500, or both. It rises to up to one year, ₹5,000, or both only if the disobedience endangers life, health or safety or causes a riot.

### How much does online tenant police verification cost?
Mumbai Police say their service is free, and UP charges ₹50 online. For Delhi and Haryana we couldn't find a current fee on an official page, so check the portal before you pay anyone.

### Who should do the verification, the landlord or the tenant?
The orders put the duty on the owner or the person renting out the property, including PG operators and property dealers. Tenants should still give correct details and ask the landlord for the acknowledgement.
