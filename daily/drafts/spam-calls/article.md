**Quick answer:** To stop spam calls and SMS in India, do three separate things. Set your Do Not Disturb (DND) preference by sending BLOCK PROMO to 1909 or using the TRAI DND app. Then complain about each spam call or SMS that still gets through, within 7 days, through the TRAI DND app or 1909. If the call was a scam (a fake bank, KYC, courier or police call), report it on Chakshu in the Sanchar Saathi portal or app instead.

Blocking a number on your phone does none of this. It only hides that number from you, and the sender carries on calling other people, often from a new SIM. In an [advisory of 24 November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2193548), TRAI said it had more than 21 lakh numbers and about one lakh spam-sending entities disconnected and blacklisted in a year, acting on citizen complaints. Pressing "block" doesn't count as a complaint.

## DND, a spam complaint and Chakshu are three different tools

Mixing these up is why people register DND, still get calls and decide "DND doesn't work".

| | DND preference | Spam (UCC) complaint | Chakshu report |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it does | Tells registered telemarketers not to send you promotions | Asks your operator to act against one specific caller or sender | Flags a suspected fraud to the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) |
| Use it for | Ads from banks, builders, coaching centres and shops | Promotional calls or SMS that reach you anyway | Fake KYC, "your SIM will be blocked", fake customer care, phishing links, job and investment offers |
| Where | SMS or call 1909, TRAI DND app, your operator's app or website | TRAI DND app, SMS or call 1909, operator app or portal | Sanchar Saathi portal or app |
| Time limit | None | Within 7 days of the call or SMS | None stated |
| What happens | The block applies to registered senders | A 15-day outgoing bar for the sender, then a one-year disconnection and blacklisting | Numbers checked and, where found to be misused, disconnected |

"UCC" (unsolicited commercial communication) is TRAI's term for spam. All of this comes under TRAI's Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018 (TCCCPR), which were tightened in February 2025 and again in September 2026.

## Step 1: Set DND with BLOCK PROMO

Under TRAI's Third Amendment to the regulations (notified in September 2026), the "block everything" option is now called **BLOCK PROMO**. It blocks all promotional calls and SMS. It does not block OTPs, bank alerts, delivery updates or government messages, because those count as transactional, service or government messages. TRAI's amendment moved everyone who had chosen the old "fully blocked" option to BLOCK PROMO automatically, so if you set full DND years ago you don't need to do it again.

Any network, the same way:

- **SMS:** send BLOCK PROMO to 1909
- **Call:** dial 1909 (toll-free) and follow the voice menu
- **App:** TRAI DND app (Android and iPhone), verify with OTP, choose full or partial blocking

If you still want offers in some areas, you can block categories one by one instead. The regulation has eight categories (codes 1 to 8): banking/insurance/financial products, real estate, education, health, consumer goods and automobiles, communication/broadcasting/entertainment/IT, tourism and leisure, and food and beverages. Send BLOCK followed by the number (for example BLOCK 2 for real estate) to 1909. UNBLOCK ALL to 1909 undoes everything.

### Operator-wise steps

| Operator | In the app or website | Also works |
|---|---|---|
| Jio | MyJio app: tap your profile icon, then Settings, Service settings, Do Not Disturb; choose categories and save (same path on jio.com) | 1909 by SMS or call |
| Airtel | Airtel's DND page on airtel.in: enter your number, verify the OTP, choose the full block option | 1909 by SMS or call |
| Vi | Vi app: menu, My Account, DND, then choose your preferences | 1909 by SMS or call; DND page on myvi.in |
| BSNL | No app route needed | SMS BLOCK PROMO to 1909, or call 1909 from your BSNL number |

App menus change often; 1909 works on every network. According to Jio, a preference takes effect within 24 hours and you get an SMS with a registration number.

## Step 2: Complain about every spam call that still gets through

DND only works on registered telemarketers, who use 140-series numbers and approved SMS headers. In a Rajya Sabha reply in February 2026, the Ministry of Communications said most spam complaints are about unregistered telemarketers using regular 10-digit numbers. DND can't stop these. A complaint can.

### How to file a complaint

- **TRAI DND app:** open the report option, pick the call or SMS from your log and submit. The easiest route, since the number and date come from the log.
- **SMS to 1909:** write a short line with the spam number or SMS header and the date, for example: UCC, 98XXXXXXXX, 20/11/26, loan offer call.
- **Call 1909** and register it with the agent or the voice menu.
- Your operator's app or website also takes complaints.

According to TRAI's February 2025 press release, a valid complaint needs only four things: your number, the sender's number or header, the date you received it, and a short description. Complaints without the sender's number or the date may be rejected.

### The rules after the 2025 and 2026 amendments

Many older guides, and some FAQ pages still online, give out-of-date figures. The current rules:

- **7 days to complain.** The window was extended from 3 days to 7 days in February 2025. A complaint made after 7 days is only recorded as a report.
- **You don't need DND to complain about unregistered callers.** Since 2025 you can complain about spam from 10-digit numbers even if you never registered a preference.
- **Action threshold:** an unregistered sender faces action once there are 5 complaints from different people within 10 days (the earlier rule was 10 complaints in 7 days). Under the 2026 amendment, 3 complaints in 10 days are enough when the operator's AI system has also flagged the number. This part comes into force 90 days after notification, in December 2026.
- **Penalty for the sender:** on the first violation, outgoing services on all of the sender's numbers are barred for 15 days. On a repeat violation, every connection is cut across all operators for one year and the sender is blacklisted.
- **Penalty for the operator:** an operator that misreports spam figures pays ₹2 lakh for the first instance, ₹5 lakh for the second and ₹10 lakh for each one after that.
- **Appeal (from December 2026):** once this part of the 2026 amendment comes into force, 90 days after notification, you can appeal within 15 days if you're unhappy with how a complaint was settled, through the DND app, your operator's app or 1909.

One complaint won't cut a number off, but yours may be one of the 3 or 5 that do.

## Step 3: Use Chakshu when it's a scam

Chakshu is for fraud, not marketing: someone posing as your bank, TRAI, a courier company, the electricity board or the police, a phishing link, or a job, lottery or "guaranteed" investment offer. How to report, according to DoT's guidance:

1. Open sancharsaathi.gov.in (or the Sanchar Saathi app) and choose Chakshu under Citizen Centric Services.
2. Choose the medium (call, SMS or WhatsApp) and the type of fraud, and enter the date and time.
3. Add the number or message, enter your own details, verify with an OTP and submit.

In February 2026 the government told the Rajya Sabha that citizens' Chakshu reports had led to about 39 lakh mobile connections being disconnected and 2.27 lakh handsets being blacklisted.

**If you have already lost money, Chakshu is the wrong place.** Call the National Cybercrime Helpline on **1930** or file at cybercrime.gov.in straight away, because a quick report gives the bank a better chance of freezing the money. While you're on Sanchar Saathi, it's also worth [checking how many SIMs are registered on your Aadhaar](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/sims-on-my-aadhaar), since fraudsters often use SIMs taken out in someone else's name.

## How to tell a genuine call or SMS from spam

TRAI's number series now help you judge a call before you answer it:

- **140xxxxxxx:** promotional (marketing) calls from registered telemarketers. Safe to ignore. If you're on DND and still get one, complain.
- **1600xxxxxx:** service and transactional calls from banks, mutual funds, pension funds and government bodies. TRAI gave banks and NBFCs deadlines between January and March 2026 to move these calls to the 1600 series.
- **A 10-digit mobile number** claiming to be your bank or a government office is a red flag.

SMS headers carry a suffix too: **-P** for promotional, **-S** for service, **-T** for transactional and **-G** for government messages. A "bank" SMS that comes from a plain mobile number, or has no suffix, isn't from a registered sender.

Under the 2026 amendment, caller ID apps may not tag numbers from the 140, 1600 and 1601 series as spam, and spam reports they collect must go to the operators' system. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I still get spam calls after activating DND?

DND only binds registered telemarketers. Most spam now comes from unregistered 10-digit numbers, which you have to complain about within 7 days.

### Will BLOCK PROMO stop my OTPs and bank alerts?

No. According to TRAI's 2026 amendment, BLOCK PROMO blocks only promotional communication. Transactional, service and government messages, including OTPs, still reach you.

### Is blocking a number on my phone enough?

No. Blocking only hides that number on your own phone. The operator never hears about it, so the sender isn't penalised and keeps calling others.

### Should I report a fake KYC call to 1909 or Chakshu?

Use Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi for fraud attempts like fake KYC calls. 1909 is for promotional spam. If money has already left your account, call 1930 immediately.
