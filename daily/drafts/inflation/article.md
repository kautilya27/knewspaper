**Quick answer:** India measures inflation mainly with two indices. The **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**, published every month by the National Statistics Office (NSO) of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), tracks the retail prices households pay. The **Wholesale Price Index (WPI)**, published by the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), tracks prices at the first point of bulk sale. Both were rebased in 2026: CPI now uses 2024 = 100 and WPI uses 2022-23 = 100.

The figure you hear on the news as "retail inflation" is the year-on-year change in the all-India CPI (Combined). It is also the number the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is legally mandated to target: 4%, within a band of 2% to 6%. According to MoSPI, it was **6.09% in October 2026** (provisional), up from 5.54% in September.

## What CPI and WPI each measure

Take a kilo of onions: WPI catches its price when a trader buys in bulk, CPI the price you pay at the sabzi shop.

| | CPI (retail) | WPI (wholesale) |
|---|---|---|
| Published by | NSO, MoSPI | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT |
| Current base | 2024 = 100 (launched 12 February 2026) | 2022-23 = 100 (launched 15 June 2026) |
| Previous base | 2012 | 2011-12 |
| What it covers | Goods and services households buy | Goods only, at the first bulk sale |
| Basket | 358 items (308 goods, 50 services) | 957 items (up from 697) |
| Prices | Retail prices paid by consumers | Basic prices, excluding net taxes and trade and transport margins |
| Usual release | Around the 12th of each month | Around the 14th of each month |
| Main use | RBI's inflation target, real incomes | Costs for producers, GDP deflators, contract escalation |

The biggest difference: **WPI has no services**, so rent, school fees, doctor visits and mobile plans are in CPI only. WPI also weighs fuel, metals and chemicals heavily, so it reacts faster to crude oil prices.

## Inside the new CPI basket

MoSPI sets the basket and weights from how households actually spend. The new series uses the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) 2023-24 instead of 2011-12 spending patterns. Prices are collected monthly from 1,465 villages and 1,395 urban markets, recorded on tablets, and 12 online markets in cities with more than 25 lakh people are tracked weekly.

Separate indices are published for rural, urban and combined (all-India). Here is how the main weights changed in the combined index, according to MoSPI:

| Category (combined CPI) | New weight (2024 series) |
|---|---|
| Food and beverages | 36.75% |
| Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels | 17.67% |
| Transport | 8.80% |
| Clothing and footwear | 6.38% |
| Health | 6.10% |
| Personal care, social protection and miscellaneous | 5.04% |
| Furnishings and household equipment | 4.47% |
| Information and communication | 3.61% |
| Restaurants and accommodation | 3.35% |
| Education services | 3.33% |
| Paan, tobacco and intoxicants | 2.99% |
| Recreation, sport and culture | 1.52% |

The categories now follow the international COICOP 2018 classification, with 12 divisions instead of the old 6 groups. That is why the old and new food shares are not a like-for-like comparison. MoSPI says that, measured the old way, the food share fell from **45.86% to 40.10%**. Food is still much heavier in the rural index (about 42% on the new basis) than in the urban index (about 30%), while housing weighs far more in cities.

Rural house rent is now counted, and items such as OTT subscriptions have come in. Free items, such as free ration grains, are left out: MoSPI says CPI by definition covers only what households pay for, as the IMF's CPI manual advises.

### The debate over food weights

The lower food weight is the most discussed change. Some economists and commentators argue that it may widen the gap between the official figure and the inflation felt by poorer, food-heavy households. MoSPI's answer is that the weights simply follow what households reported spending in the 2023-24 survey. MoSPI also says it aims to revise the base about every five years, as each new consumption survey comes in, so the basket doesn't go stale again.

## The new WPI and the producer price index

The WPI's new series (2022-23 = 100) gives 22.76% weight to primary articles (crops, minerals, crude oil), 14.11% to fuel and power, and 63.13% to manufactured products, according to the Office of the Economic Adviser. Alongside it, the government launched **producer price indices (PPI)**, including a first set of seven service PPIs covering banking, railways, air travel and telecom. The plan, according to PIB, is to publish WPI alongside the PPIs for five years and then discontinue WPI, so users have time to switch.

For October 2026, WPI inflation was **12.29%**, up from 11.39% in September, the highest in the new series. Fuel and power drove it, at 32.84%. Much less of that showed up in retail prices: in the same month, CPI's housing, water, electricity and fuel division rose 2.90% and transport 5.84%.

## Headline vs core inflation

**Headline inflation** is the full CPI, food and fuel included. **Core inflation** strips out the most volatile parts, usually food and fuel, to show the underlying trend. MoSPI publishes only the headline index and its parts, not an official core figure. Core numbers you see in reports are calculated by the RBI or by economists, and MoSPI has said it is for the RBI to decide which items go into core.

Should the RBI target core instead? The Economic Survey 2023-24 suggested examining a target that leaves out food, since food prices are driven by weather and supply, not interest rates. The RBI's August 2025 discussion paper argued for staying with headline CPI: food and fuel make up more than half of what Indian households spend, and lasting food inflation spills over into wages and other prices. In March 2026 the government kept the headline CPI target.

## Why the RBI targets CPI at 4%

Under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, the central government sets the inflation target in consultation with the RBI, every five years. Flexible inflation targeting started in 2016. On 25 March 2026 the government kept the target at **4% CPI inflation, with a lower limit of 2% and an upper limit of 6%, for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031**.

The RBI's six-member Monetary Policy Committee sets the repo rate with that target in mind. "Flexible" means short-term swings within the band are tolerated while growth is kept in view. The target counts as missed only if average inflation stays outside 2% to 6% for **three consecutive quarters**; the RBI must then report to the government on the reasons, its remedy and a timeline. October's 6.09% is one month above the band, not a failure. ## How to read a monthly CPI release: October 2026

MoSPI's October 2026 press release came out on 12 November 2026. Here is what to look for.

**1. The headline number.** The all-India combined index was 110.05 in October 2026, against 103.74 in October 2025. Inflation is the percentage change over the same month a year earlier:

(110.05 − 103.74) ÷ 103.74 × 100 = about 6.08%

MoSPI reports **6.09%** because it calculates from indices before they are rounded to two decimals.

**2. Rural vs urban.** Rural inflation was 6.63% and urban 5.41%. The gap is mostly food: food inflation (the Consumer Food Price Index) was 9.09% in villages and 8.68% in cities, and food weighs more in the rural basket.

**3. What drove it.** Food inflation for the country was 8.94%. Onion prices were up 116.22% on a year earlier, garlic up 60.74% and silver jewellery up 52.39%. Potato, at minus 14.24%, and cauliflower, at minus 10.57%, got cheaper.

**4. Month-on-month change.** From September (109.49) to October (110.05), prices rose about 0.51%. The yearly figure gives the level; the monthly change shows the current direction.

**5. "Provisional" means it will be revised.** Each month's figure is finalised a month later; September's final figure is 5.54%. November's data is due on 14 December 2026.

The full release is on [MoSPI's website](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestreleasesfiles/1794480048335-Press%20Release%20of%20CPI%20for%20October%202026%20.pdf). The WPI details are in [PIB's note on the new WPI series](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2272872&reg=3&lang=1).

## Why your own inflation can differ

The CPI is an average for the whole country. Your household's basket is not the national basket. As a rough check, multiply October's division-wise inflation by your own spending shares:

| | Family A: food-heavy | Family B: city renter |
|---|---|---|
| Food and beverages (8.51%) | 50% of spending | 25% |
| Housing and fuel (2.90%) | 10% | 30% |
| Transport (5.84%) | 10% | 15% |
| Education (4.02%) | 10% | 10% |
| Health (2.05%) | 10% | 10% |
| Clothing (4.96%) / eating out (10.06%) | 10% clothing | 10% eating out |
| **Approximate personal inflation** | **about 6.2%** | **about 5.5%** |

This simple weighted average is only a rough guide, but it shows the point. Other reasons your own number can differ:

- **One big bill dominates.** If your rent went up 10% at renewal or school fees rose 12%, that matters more to you than any national average.
- **Where you live.** According to MoSPI, October inflation among large states and UTs was highest in Tamil Nadu (7.29%) and Telangana (7.26%); Delhi was at 3.80%.
- **What you notice.** The onion price that doubled sticks in memory; the potato price that fell 14% doesn't.

Imported costs feed in too. A weaker rupee makes crude oil and other imports dearer, as explained in [why the rupee is falling against the dollar](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/why-is-the-rupee-falling-against-the-dollar).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which index is used to measure inflation in India?

Retail inflation is measured by the all-India CPI (Combined) published by MoSPI, and that is the index the RBI targets. Wholesale inflation is measured by the WPI from the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT.

### What is the new base year of CPI and WPI?

CPI moved to base 2024 = 100 with the January 2026 data, released on 12 February 2026, replacing the 2012 base. WPI moved to base 2022-23 = 100 from 15 June 2026, replacing 2011-12.

### Why is WPI inflation so much higher than CPI inflation right now?

WPI has no services and gives much more weight to fuel, power and metals. In October 2026, fuel and power prices rose 32.84% at the wholesale level, pushing WPI to 12.29%, while CPI was 6.09%.

### Does one month of inflation above 6% mean the RBI has failed its target?

No. Under the framework, failure means average CPI inflation stays above 6% or below 2% for three consecutive quarters. Only then must the RBI explain the reasons and its plan to the government.
