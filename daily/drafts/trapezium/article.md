**Quick answer:** The area of a trapezium is half the sum of its two parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular distance between them. In symbols: **Area = ½ × (a + b) × h**, where a and b are the parallel sides and h is the height. If the parallel sides are 12 cm and 8 cm and the height is 5 cm, the area is ½ × 20 × 5 = 50 cm².

Put simply: average the two parallel sides, then multiply by the height. Finding a missing side, working without the height and measuring a plot in gaj all come from this one formula.

## What is a trapezium?

A trapezium is a quadrilateral with **exactly one pair of parallel sides**. The parallel sides are often called the bases. The other two sides are the legs, or non-parallel sides. The **height** is the perpendicular distance between the bases. It is not the length of a slanting side.

Special cases:

- **Isosceles trapezium:** the two non-parallel sides are equal, so the shape is symmetric.
- **Right trapezium:** one leg is perpendicular to the bases, so that leg is the height.

### Trapezium or trapezoid?

The same shape goes by different names in different countries. Indian textbooks (NCERT and state boards) and British books call it a **trapezium**. In the USA and Canada it is a **trapezoid**. (Confusingly, American dictionaries such as Merriam-Webster still use "trapezium" for a quadrilateral with *no* parallel sides.) An American "area of a trapezoid" page uses the same formula. For CBSE and state board exams, write "trapezium".

## Why the formula works: two triangles

Take trapezium ABCD with AB ∥ DC, AB = a, DC = b and height h. Draw the diagonal AC. It cuts the trapezium into two triangles:

1. **Triangle ABC** has base AB = a. Its height is the distance from C to line AB, which is h. Area = ½ × a × h.
2. **Triangle ACD** has base DC = b. Its height is the distance from A to line DC, which is also h. Area = ½ × b × h.

Add the two areas:

Area = ½ × a × h + ½ × b × h = ½ × (a + b) × h

Both triangles share the height h because the bases are parallel. You can [find a triangle's area in other ways too](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-triangle-trigonometry), for example from two sides and the angle between them, and these are useful when a trapezium problem gives angles instead of the height.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: All three values given

Parallel sides 12 cm and 8 cm, height 5 cm.

Area = ½ × (12 + 8) × 5 = ½ × 20 × 5 = **50 cm²**

### Example 2: Finding a missing parallel side

A trapezium has area 180 m², height 12 m and one parallel side 18 m. Find the other parallel side.

Rearrange: a + b = 2 × Area ÷ h = 2 × 180 ÷ 12 = 30. So b = 30 − 18 = **12 m**.

### Example 3: Finding the height

Area 91 cm², parallel sides 9 cm and 17 cm.

h = 2 × Area ÷ (a + b) = 182 ÷ 26 = **7 cm**

### Example 4: Isosceles trapezium with slanting sides given

Parallel sides 16 cm and 10 cm, each non-parallel side 5 cm. The height is not given.

The longer base sticks out by (16 − 10) ÷ 2 = 3 cm on each side. Each slanting side is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with base 3 cm, so h = √(5² − 3²) = √16 = 4 cm.

Area = ½ × (16 + 10) × 4 = **52 cm²**

## When you only know the four sides

Class 9 field problems often give all four sides but no height. Split the shape instead.

**Example 5:** A field is a trapezium with parallel sides 25 m and 10 m and non-parallel sides 14 m and 13 m. Find its area.

1. From one end of the shorter side (10 m), draw a line parallel to one leg, down to the longer side. This cuts off a **parallelogram** with sides 10 m and 13 m and leaves a **triangle**.
2. The triangle's sides are 14 m, 13 m and 25 − 10 = 15 m.
3. Heron's formula: s = (15 + 14 + 13) ÷ 2 = 21. Area = √(21 × 6 × 7 × 8) = √7056 = 84 m².
4. The triangle's height on the 15 m side is the trapezium's height: h = 2 × 84 ÷ 15 = 11.2 m.
5. Trapezium area = ½ × (25 + 10) × 11.2 = **196 m²**

Check: parallelogram (10 × 11.2 = 112 m²) + triangle (84 m²) = 196 m², which matches.

## Units, and measuring a plot in India

Area is always in **square units**. If the lengths are in cm, the area is in cm². If they are in m, it is in m². Use the same unit for all the lengths before you multiply.

A plot on a bending road or a field along a canal is often roughly a trapezium. In much of north India, residential plots are sold in **gaj**. One gaj is one square yard, which is 9 square feet or about 0.836 m².

**Example 6:** A plot has a 40 ft front, a 30 ft back (the two parallel sides) and a depth of 60 ft measured at right angles between them.

- Area = ½ × (40 + 30) × 60 = **2,100 sq ft**
- In gaj: 2,100 ÷ 9 ≈ **233.3 gaj**
- In m²: 2,100 × 0.0929 ≈ **195.1 m²**

Measure the depth straight across, at right angles to the front and back. Do not measure along a side boundary that slants. The formula needs the perpendicular distance, so a slanting measurement gives too large an area. Official land records use their own surveys, so treat this as a rough check.

## Formulas at a glance

| You want | Formula |
|---|---|
| Area | ½ × (a + b) × h |
| Height | h = 2 × Area ÷ (a + b) |
| Missing parallel side | b = (2 × Area ÷ h) − a |
| Height of an isosceles trapezium (leg c) | h = √(c² − ((a − b) ÷ 2)²) |

## Common mistakes

- **Using a slanting side as the height.** The height is always perpendicular to the parallel sides.
- **Adding the wrong pair.** Add only the two parallel sides, never a parallel side and a leg.
- **Forgetting the ½.** (a + b) × h is the area of the doubled parallelogram, not of the trapezium.
- **Mixing units.** With sides 1.2 m and 80 cm and height 50 cm, convert to 120, 80 and 50 cm first: ½ × 200 × 50 = 5,000 cm², or 0.5 m².
- **Wrong units in the answer.** Area is in cm² or m², not cm or m.


## Frequently asked questions

### What is the formula for the area of a trapezium?

Area = ½ × (sum of the parallel sides) × (perpendicular height), or ½ × (a + b) × h. The answer is in square units such as cm² or m².

### How do you find the area of a trapezium without the height?

If you know all four sides, split the trapezium into a parallelogram and a triangle. Find the triangle's area with Heron's formula, use it to get the height, and then apply ½ × (a + b) × h. For an isosceles trapezium you can find the height with Pythagoras' theorem.

### Is a trapezium the same as a trapezoid?

Yes, in the usual school sense. India and the UK say trapezium for a quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides, and the USA calls the same shape a trapezoid. The area formula is identical.

### Which NCERT chapter teaches the area of a trapezium?

In the new NCERT books it is in Class 8 Ganita Prakash Part II, Chapter 7 (Area). Heron's formula, which you need when only the sides are known, is in Class 9 Ganita Manjari Part I, Chapter 6 (Measuring Space: Perimeter and Area). Older editions put it in "Mensuration" and "Heron's Formula".
