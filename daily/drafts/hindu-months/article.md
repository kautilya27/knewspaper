**Quick answer:** The 12 Hindu lunar months are Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashvin, Kartika, Margashirsha, Pausha, Magha and Phalguna. Chaitra roughly matches March–April and Phalguna roughly matches February–March, but the fit is loose: each month runs from one moon phase to the next, so its Gregorian dates change every year.

There is also a second complication. North India mostly ends each month on the full moon (the *Purnimanta* system), while Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and much of the south end it on the new moon (*Amanta*). So for about two weeks every month, the same day carries different month names. Today, 20 November 2026, both systems agree: it is Kartika Shukla Ekadashi, and Devuthani Ekadashi is observed today.

## The 12 masas, their English equivalents and a festival in each

The table uses the amanta month for the "roughly" column. In the purnimanta system the same-named month begins about 15 days earlier, on the day after the previous full moon. Both columns can slide by up to three weeks from year to year.

| # | Masa (other spellings) | Roughly (amanta) | Purnimanta month begins after | A major festival |

The festivals in the last column fall on the same day in both systems because they come in the bright half (*shukla paksha*) of the month or on the full moon. Only dark-half festivals change their month name, as the next section shows.

## Amanta vs Purnimanta: same day, different month name

Every lunar month has two halves: the waxing *shukla paksha*, which ends on the full moon (Purnima), and the waning *krishna paksha*, which ends on the new moon (Amavasya).

- **Amanta** months start the day after Amavasya, so they run shukla paksha first, then krishna paksha.
- **Purnimanta** months start the day after Purnima, so they run krishna paksha first, then shukla paksha.

The shukla paksha always has the same month name in both systems. The krishna paksha gets the name of the *following* month in purnimanta reckoning. That's why three famous festivals have two "official" months:

| Festival | Tithi | Purnimanta (north) | Amanta (south/west) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diwali | Krishna Amavasya | Kartika | Ashvin |
| Maha Shivaratri | Krishna Chaturdashi | Phalguna | Magha |
| Janmashtami | Krishna Ashtami | Bhadrapada | Shravana |

Drik Panchang lists both labels for Diwali and for [Maha Shivaratri](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/maha-shivaratri). The tithi, and so the date, is identical; only the label differs.

**Who uses which?** According to Drik Panchang, amanta is mainly followed in Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and Tripura. Purnimanta is mainly followed in Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh. Telangana follows the same Telugu panchang as Andhra Pradesh. In Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Bengal and Odisha, everyday month names come from the solar calendar instead (see below).

### A worked example from 2026

Shravan 2026 shows the gap clearly. According to the *Free Press Journal*, Shravan ran from 30 July to 28 August in north India but from 13 August to 11 September in Maharashtra. Raksha Bandhan, on Shravana Purnima, was 28 August in both.

## Why Hindu festival dates move every year

The traditional calendar is *lunisolar*: months follow the moon, but the year must keep pace with the seasons. According to Britannica, 12 lunar months make a year of about 354 days, roughly 11 days shorter than the solar year. A festival tied to a tithi therefore comes about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year. To stop the months drifting through the seasons, an extra month (*Adhik Maas*, also called Purushottam or Mal Maas) is inserted. Britannica gives the frequency as about once every 30 months; Dekho Panchang gives about 32.5 months. The rule: a lunar month in which the sun does not enter a new zodiac sign is the extra month, and it takes the name of the month that follows it.

Diwali's recent dates show the pattern. Drik Panchang lists them as:

- 2025: 20 October
- 2026: 8 November (about 19 days later, because 2026 had an extra month)
- 2027: 29 October (about 10 days earlier)
- 2028: 17 October (earlier again)

**Adhik Maas 2026 and the next one.** 2026 had an Adhik Jyeshtha from 17 May to 15 June, followed by the regular (*nija*) Jyeshtha. That's why 2026 had 13 lunar months. According to Dekho Panchang and *Patrika*, the next Adhik Maas is Adhik Chaitra in 2029 (about 16 March to 13 April 2029). Bhadrapada is the next one after that, in 2031.

**Why some dates barely move.** Festivals tied to the sun's entry into a zodiac sign (*sankranti*) stay near the same Gregorian date. [Makar Sankranti](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/makar-sankranti) is always around 14–15 January, and in 2027 it falls on 15 January because the sun enters Makara after sunset on the 14th. Tamil Puthandu, Vishu, Pohela Boishakh and Odisha's Pana Sankranti stay around 13–15 April for the same reason.

## The Indian National Calendar (Saka) is different

The Government of India's [Know India portal](https://knowindia.india.gov.in/national-identity-elements/national-calendar.php) says the national calendar, based on the Saka era, was adopted on 22 March 1957. It is used alongside the Gregorian calendar for the Gazette of India, All India Radio news broadcasts, and calendars and communications issued by the government. Chaitra is its first month. 1 Chaitra normally falls on 22 March, and on 21 March in a leap year.

Unlike the traditional calendar, it is a fixed *solar* calendar with a 365-day year and no lunar months. The month names are the same, but the months last 30 or 31 days and start on fixed dates:

| Saka month | Days | Starts |
|---|---|---|
| Chaitra | 30 (31 in leap year) | 22 March (21 in leap year) |
| Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadra | 31 each | 21 Apr, 22 May, 22 Jun, 23 Jul, 23 Aug |
| Ashvin, Kartika, Agrahayana, Pausha | 30 each | 23 Sep, 23 Oct, 22 Nov, 22 Dec |
| Magha, Phalguna | 30 each | 21 Jan, 20 Feb |

The Saka year is the Gregorian year minus 78 (from 22 March), so 2026–27 is Saka 1948. Its Kartika (from 23 October) only roughly overlaps the lunar Kartika (from 27 October in the north).

For the traditional side, the government publishes the [Rashtriya Panchang](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/rashtriy_panchang.php) through IMD's Positional Astronomy Centre in Kolkata. According to IMD, it has appeared since 1957–58 to unify India's calendar practices, now in 14 languages, with tithis, nakshatras and principal festivals.

## Solar month names: Tamil, Malayalam, Bengali, Odia

In Tamil Nadu, Kerala, West Bengal and Odisha, the everyday calendar is solar: each month starts at a sankranti, when the sun enters a new sign. So these months begin mid-way through a Gregorian month.

| Sun enters | Tamil | Malayalam | Bengali | Odia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mesha (mid-April) | Chithirai | Medam | Boishakh | Baisakha |
| Vrishchika (mid-November) | Karthigai | Vrischikam | Agrahayan | Margasira |
| Makara (mid-January) | Thai | Makaram | Magh | Magha |

- **Tamil:** Chithirai, Vaikasi, Aani, Aadi, Avani, Purattasi, Aippasi, Karthigai, Margazhi, Thai, Maasi, Panguni. The year starts with Chithirai in mid-April.
- **Malayalam:** Chingam, Kanni, Thulam, Vrischikam, Dhanu, Makaram, Kumbham, Meenam, Medam, Edavam, Midhunam, Karkidakam. According to Kerala Tourism, the year starts with Chingam in mid-August, though Vishu in Medam is also celebrated as a new year.
- **Bengali and Odia:** these use names close to the Sanskrit ones (Boishakh/Baisakha first), starting in mid-April with Pohela Boishakh and Pana Sankranti. According to Dekho Panchang, the Odia months run Baisakha, Jyestha, Asadha, Srabana, Bhadra, Aswina, Kartika, Margasira, Pausa, Magha, Phalguna and Chaitra.

The 2026 sun-entry into Vrishchika was on 16 November at about 7:48 pm, so Tamil Karthigai began on 17 November.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the first month of the Hindu calendar?
In most lunar calendars, Chaitra is the first month, and the new year (Gudi Padwa, Ugadi) falls on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada. Gujarat's new year (Bestu Varas) starts instead on the first day of amanta Kartika, which All India Radio reported was 10 November in 2026. The solar calendars of Tamil Nadu, Bengal and Odisha begin in mid-April.

### Which Hindu month is it in November 2026?
It is Kartika in both lunar systems. In the north, Margashirsha begins on 25 November, after Kartik Purnima on 24 November. In amanta regions, Kartika continues until the Amavasya of 8 December.

### When is the next Adhik Maas?
The last was Adhik Jyeshtha, from 17 May to 15 June 2026. The next is Adhik Chaitra in 2029, from about mid-March to mid-April.

### Is the Saka calendar the same as the Hindu panchang?
No. The Saka national calendar, adopted in 1957, is a fixed solar calendar whose months start on set Gregorian dates. The traditional panchang uses lunar months that shift every year and decides festival dates.
