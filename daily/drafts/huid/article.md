**Quick answer:** To check a HUID number, find the 6-character code stamped next to the BIS logo and purity mark on your jewellery (you may need a magnifier). Open the free **BIS Care** app from the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), tap **Verify HUID**, type the code, and match what the app shows against the piece and your bill.

The app shows the purity, the type of article, the article's status (and, in some cases, the jeweller) and the Assaying and Hallmarking Centre (AHC) that tested it. If those details match, the code is genuine and belongs to that kind of item. A match does not mean anyone has tested the metal in your hand today. For that you need a lab test, and BIS rules let you get one cheaply. Both are explained below.

## Reading the hallmark: the three marks on today's jewellery

Since 1 July 2021, gold jewellery hallmarked in India carries three marks, according to a [PIB release from the Ministry of Consumer Affairs](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1904262). Older pieces carried four, and the jeweller's logo and the hallmarking centre's logo have now been dropped.

### 1. The BIS Standard Mark

This is the triangle-shaped BIS logo. It shows the piece went through India's official hallmarking scheme.

### 2. The purity mark (caratage and fineness)

This is printed together, for example **22K916**. "22K" is the carat. "916" is the fineness, meaning parts of pure gold per thousand, so 916 means at least 91.6% gold.

### 3. The HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification)

This is a 6-character alphanumeric code (letters and numbers) given to each hallmarked article. It is the only mark you can look up. From 1 April 2023, jewellers have not been allowed to sell gold jewellery or artefacts without a HUID.

## Karat to fineness: what the purity number means

BIS's brief on the hallmarking scheme lists these grades under IS 1417 (the Indian standard for gold jewellery). According to PIB (March 2026), 14K, 18K, 20K, 22K, 23K and 24KS are the grades covered by mandatory hallmarking. 9K can also be hallmarked; PIB (March 2026) describes it as a voluntary grade.

| Carat on the mark | Fineness on the mark | Minimum gold content | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24KS | 995 | 99.5% | Mandatory grade |
| 23K | 958 | 95.8% | Mandatory grade |
| 22K | 916 | 91.6% | Mandatory grade |
| 20K | 833 | 83.3% | Mandatory grade |
| 18K | 750 | 75.0% | Mandatory grade |
| 14K | 585 | 58.5% | Mandatory grade |
| 9K | 375 | 37.5% | Voluntary |

The carat and fineness must sit in the same row ("22K750" would be a red flag), and the bill must show the same carat as the piece.

## How to check a HUID number in the BIS Care app

1. Install **BIS Care** from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store. The publisher is Bureau of Indian Standards, and it is free.
2. Register once with your name and mobile number. You get an OTP to confirm it.
3. Tap **Verify HUID** and enter the 6-character code exactly as stamped. Watch for look-alikes such as 0/O and 1/I.
4. Compare the result with the piece and the bill:
   - **Purity:** it should match the stamped mark, for example 22K916.
   - **Article type:** it should match what you're holding. A ring's HUID showing "chain" is a warning sign.
   - **Jeweller and status:** the app also shows the article's status. While a piece is still listed as 'Available at Jeweller', it may hide the jeweller's name and address. Where a name is shown, it should match the shop, or the shop should be able to explain why it doesn't.
5. If the app shows no record, or the details don't match, don't pay yet. Ask the jeweller, and if it stays unresolved, complain (see below).

According to its App Store listing, 2026 versions of the app add HUID and article photos, article status and a recorded gross weight to HUID results, and silver HUID checks were added in November 2025. If a photo appears, compare it with your piece too.

## What a HUID check proves, and what it doesn't

The app reads a record from the BIS database. It does not test anything.

| A matching HUID result tells you | It does not tell you |
|---|---|
| The code was issued by the BIS system and is not made up | That the metal in your hand is the metal that was tested |
| The purity the AHC certified for that article | That the piece hasn't been altered since it was marked |
| The type of article it was issued for | The net gold weight. Where a weight is shown, it is the gross weight recorded at hallmarking, stones included |
| Which registered jeweller and which AHC handled it | Whether the making charges or price are fair |

A genuine HUID could in theory be copied onto another piece, which is why the article-type match matters. For a big wedding purchase or an inherited set, the only real proof of purity is an assay at a BIS-recognised AHC.

Also check the **net gold weight** on the bill against the shop's scale. BIS guidelines for jewellers (July 2026) ask registered shops to keep a weighing balance and a magnifier of at least 10x for customers to use.

## Your rights as a buyer under BIS rules

### What the bill must show

Under BIS guidelines, a bill for hallmarked jewellery must list each article separately with:

- Net weight of precious metal
- Purity in carat and fineness
- The hallmarking charge
- A statement that you can get the purity checked at any BIS-recognised AHC

The hallmarking charge, which the jeweller pays the hallmarking centre, has been ₹75 per gold article plus taxes since September 2026, up from ₹45, under the [BIS (Hallmarking) Amendment Regulations, 2026](https://www.bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Bureau-of-Indian-Standards-Hallmarking-Amendment-Regulations-2026-1.pdf).

### Get it tested at a hallmarking centre

Any consumer can take jewellery, hallmarked or not, to a BIS-recognised AHC for testing for a fee. When BIS opened AHCs to consumers' un-hallmarked jewellery in 2022, PIB put the charge at ₹200 for up to four articles and ₹45 per article for five or more; ask the AHC for its current rate. The AHC issues a test report; the list of centres is on bis.gov.in.

### Compensation if purity is short

If a hallmarked article is found to be less pure than marked, BIS says the buyer is entitled to **twice the difference in value** caused by the purity shortfall, for the weight sold, **plus the testing charges**. The jeweller pays this under Rule 49 of the BIS Rules, 2018, [according to PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1904262). The [BIS consumer protection page](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/consumer-protection/?lang=en) sets out the compensation rule.

Here is a simplified illustration with an assumed price. Real disputes may value the gold differently.

- A 10 g chain is marked 22K916, but a test finds 900. The shortfall is 16 parts per thousand, or 0.16 g of pure gold.
- At an assumed ₹10,000 per gram of pure gold, the shortfall is worth ₹1,600.
- Compensation would be twice that, ₹3,200, plus what you paid for the test.

Gold prices move every day, and in India they also move with the rupee-dollar rate, because gold is priced internationally in dollars. That's one reason [a weaker rupee can push up jewellery prices](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/why-is-the-rupee-falling-against-the-dollar). The value of any shortfall depends on the rate used.

### How to complain

BIS records hallmarking complaints centrally. You can file one through the BIS Care app or the complaint portal on bis.gov.in, by email to complaints@bis.gov.in, or in writing to the Public Grievance Officer at the nearest BIS regional or branch office. Keep the bill, test report and photos of the hallmark.

## Where hallmarking is compulsory, and what's left out

- **Coverage:** Mandatory hallmarking began on 23 June 2021 in 256 districts. Seven more phases followed. BIS's district list shows **392 districts** covered after the eighth phase (3 August 2026).
- **Exemptions:** BIS's scheme brief lists gold articles **below 2 grams**, **kundan, polki and jadau** jewellery, gold bullion and gold thread, and certain export and exhibition cases. When the scheme started, PIB also listed watches and fountain pens, and exempted jewellers with an annual turnover up to ₹40 lakh.
- **Old jewellery:** Pieces with the older four-mark hallmark remain valid. BIS says a hallmark stays valid for the life of the article. You can still sell old jewellery to jewellers, hallmarked or not.
- **Silver:** Silver hallmarking is voluntary. Since 1 September 2025, any silver piece that is hallmarked must carry a HUID, according to PIB.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I check a HUID number online without the app?

BIS points consumers to the **Verify HUID** feature in its BIS Care app on Android and iOS. Treat third-party sites claiming to verify HUIDs with caution.

### My old jewellery has no HUID. Is it fake?

Not necessarily. Pieces hallmarked before 1 July 2021 carry the older four-mark hallmark, which is still valid, and any piece can be tested at a BIS-recognised centre for a small fee.

### Is a 24K gold coin covered by the same HUID rules?

Mandatory hallmarking applies to gold jewellery and artefacts. BIS lists bullion among the exemptions, so coins and bars follow separate arrangements. Check the bill and the seller's certification before buying.

### What if the HUID shows a different jeweller's name?

The shop may have sourced it from another registered jeweller, so ask. If the purity or article type also doesn't match, don't buy, and consider a complaint through the BIS Care app.
