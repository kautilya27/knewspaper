**Quick answer:** Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours of the interview, ideally the same day. Address it to the person who interviewed you (or to each panel member separately), mention one specific thing you discussed, say you are still interested in the role, and keep it under about 150 words. In India, check the hiring route first: on campus, your placement cell's rules may not allow you to email the company at all.

The note is not a second interview or a place to renegotiate salary. Below are templates for the situations Indian candidates actually face, plus the email phrases worth retiring.

## Who should get the email in Indian hiring

Indian hiring often runs through HR, consultancies or placement cells, not just one hiring manager.

| How you were hired | Who to thank | How |
|---|---|---|
| Direct application, you met the hiring manager | The hiring manager, and the HR person who set it up | Separate short emails, or one to the manager with HR in Cc |
| Panel interview | Each panel member, if you have their emails | A separate note to each, mentioning something that person asked |
| Only the HR or recruiter's email is known | The HR/recruiter | Thank them and ask them to pass on thanks to the panel |
| Placement consultancy or staffing firm | Your consultant | Thank the consultant; don't go around them to the client company |
| On-campus placement drive | Usually nobody directly | Follow your placement cell's rules; ask the placement coordinator if a note is allowed |

The campus row matters. Some Indian colleges restrict direct contact during a drive. NIT Sikkim's placement rules, for example, say students "shall not directly contact any representative, recruiter, HR personnel, or employee of a recruiting organization" during an ongoing recruitment process, and that violations can lead to disciplinary action. IIT Madras's [recruiters' guide for 2025-26](https://placement.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Recruiters_Guide_&_Policies_Document_Placements_2025-26.pdf) bars companies from contacting students directly without the Placement Office's written consent, and routes all offers through that office. So a well-meant email can break the rules. If your college doesn't allow it, thank the panel in person at the end of the interview and leave it there. Off-campus, thank whoever interviewed you.

**Can't find the interviewer's email?** Don't guess addresses. Reply on the thread where HR sent the interview invite. A LinkedIn message is fine as a backup if you had a real conversation with that person.

## Timing and subject line

According to SHRM, send it within 24 hours, ideally the same day, from the same email address you applied with. Robert Half also recommends the 24-hour window. If you had two rounds in one day, one email after the last round is enough.

Subject lines should tell the reader what this is before they open it:

- Thank you – [Your Name], [Role] interview
- Thank you for today's conversation – [Role]
- [Your Name] – thank you, HR round on [date]

If you're replying on the existing interview thread, keep the original subject so it stays grouped.

## Templates you can adapt

Replace everything in square brackets. Delete any line that isn't true for you. A note that sounds like a template defeats the point.

### 1. Fresher, after an off-campus interview

> Subject: Thank you – [Your Name], [Role] interview
> Dear [Mr/Ms Surname],
> Thank you for taking the time to interview me today for the [Role] position. I enjoyed our discussion about [specific topic, e.g. how the team tests releases before deployment], and it made me more interested in joining.
> My final-year project on [topic] gave me hands-on practice with [skill], and I would be glad to bring that to your team.
> Please let me know if you need any documents or further information from my side.
> Regards,
> [Full name]
> [Phone number]

### 2. Experienced professional, after a manager round

> Subject: Thank you – [Your Name], [Role] interview
> Dear [First name or Mr/Ms Surname],
> Thank you for the conversation today. Your point about [challenge they mentioned, e.g. reducing customer onboarding time] stayed with me. At [current company], I [one concrete result, e.g. cut onboarding from 10 days to 4 by redesigning the KYC flow], and I think a similar approach could work here.
> I'm keen on the role and look forward to hearing about next steps.
> Best regards,
> [Full name]

Use their first name only if they introduced themselves that way in the interview.

### 3. After a virtual interview

> Subject: Thank you for today's video interview – [Role]
> Dear [Name],
> Thank you for speaking with me over [Teams/Zoom/Google Meet] today. [If there was a glitch: Thank you also for your patience when my connection dropped briefly.] I appreciated hearing about [specific point].
> I remain very interested in the [Role] position and am happy to share anything else you need.
> Regards,
> [Full name]

### 4. After the HR round with a salary discussion

This is where people overstep. Thank them, confirm what was agreed if it helps, and don't reopen the negotiation in the thank-you note.

> Subject: Thank you – [Your Name], HR discussion on [date]
> Dear [Name],
> Thank you for today's discussion. To confirm what I shared: my current CTC is ₹[amount] lakh per annum, my expected CTC is ₹[amount] lakh, and my notice period is [number] days.
> Please let me know if you need payslips or any other documents. I look forward to hearing from you.
> Regards,
> [Full name]

Repeat only figures you have already shared in the interview. If current CTC never came up, leave that part out rather than putting a new number in writing.

If notice period came up and you're unsure where you stand, our explainer on [notice period rules in India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/notice-period-rules) covers buyouts and what your appointment letter decides.

### 5. After a rejection

It costs nothing, and it leaves the door open for the next opening at the same company.

> Subject: Thank you – [Role]
> Dear [Name],
> Thank you for letting me know, and for the time you and the team spent with me. I'm disappointed, but I enjoyed learning about [team/product].
> If a suitable role opens up in the future, I would be glad to be considered.
> Regards,
> [Full name]

Don't ask them to reconsider. If you want feedback, one polite line ("If you have any feedback on how I could improve, I would value it") is enough, and accept silence as an answer. On campus, NIT Sikkim's rules say any request for reconsideration goes only through the placement cell.

## Indian-English email phrases to retire

These are common in Indian offices, but they can sound stiff or unclear, especially to hiring managers outside India. In a short note, every phrase stands out.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Kindly do the needful | Could you please [the actual thing]? |
| Please revert back / revert at the earliest | Please let me know / I look forward to hearing from you |
| PFA / PFB | I've attached my updated CV / Please see below |
| I am having 3 years of experience | I have 3 years of experience |
| Myself Rahul | I am Rahul / My name is Rahul |
| Respected Sir/Madam | Dear Ms Rao / Dear Hiring Team |
| Hoping for a positive response | I look forward to hearing from you |
| Please find the attached herewith | I've attached… |

A note on "revert": the Oxford Learner's Dictionary lists "revert" in the sense of "reply" as *Indian English, rather formal*. Since revert already means to come back, "revert back" says "back" twice. Outside India, many readers know revert only as "go back to an earlier state".

## If you hear nothing: the follow-up email

Wait for the timeline the interviewer or HR gave you. If none was given, about a week is a reasonable gap, which is also what Naukri's campus guide suggests. Send one follow-up on the same thread:

> Subject: Re: Thank you – [Your Name], [Role] interview
> Dear [Name],
> I hope you're well. I wanted to check whether there is any update on the [Role] position I interviewed for on [date]. I remain very interested and am happy to provide anything else you need.
> Regards,
> [Full name]

If there's still no reply after another week, send one last short note, then stop and keep applying elsewhere.

Before you hit send, check the name's spelling and keep attachments out unless asked. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does a thank-you email really make a difference in India?

It rarely decides the outcome alone, but it is a cheap way to show professionalism and interest, and it gives you a chance to add a point you forgot. When two candidates are close, a thoughtful note can only help.

### Should I send the thank-you email to HR or the interviewer?

Send it to the person who interviewed you. If you only have the HR or recruiter's email, thank them and ask them to pass on your thanks to the panel; if you came through a consultancy, thank your consultant.

### Can I send a thank-you message on WhatsApp or LinkedIn?

Email is the safer default. Use WhatsApp only if the recruiter has been messaging you there, and LinkedIn only as a backup when you have no email address.

### Should I send a thank-you note after a campus placement interview?

Only if your placement cell allows it. Some Indian colleges forbid students from contacting recruiters directly during a drive, so check the rules or ask your placement coordinator first.
