**Quick answer:** Open the government's Sanchar Saathi portal (sancharsaathi.gov.in) or app, choose **Know Mobile Connections in Your Name**, enter any one of your mobile numbers and the OTP. You will see every mobile number issued against your identity across all operators. Tick any number that isn't yours as **Not My Number**, or one you no longer use as **Not Required**, and submit.

The check is free and takes about two minutes. One person can hold at most **9 mobile connections** across all operators, or **6** in the Jammu & Kashmir, Assam and North-East service areas, according to a Department of Telecommunications (DoT) circular dated 17 August 2026. A number you report is sent to the operator for re-verification, and it is disconnected if nobody can prove it's theirs.

## Why bother checking

A SIM on your ID in a fraudster's phone can run scam calls or receive OTPs, and the trail leads back to you. In a statement on 24 November 2025, DoT warned that when a SIM taken in someone's name is misused, "the original user may also be held liable as an offender", especially if they handed the SIM to someone else.

This is not a rare problem. In a Lok Sabha reply on 7 August 2024, the government said about 40.6 lakh connections had been disconnected after citizens marked them "Not My Number", and another 14.4 lakh after they were marked "Not Required". By October 2026, according to the Ministry of Communications, Sanchar Saathi had resolved more than 4 crore citizen requests about mobile connections in people's names.

## How to check the SIMs on your Aadhaar, step by step

The feature used to be called TAFCOP. It now sits under the citizen services on [Sanchar Saathi](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/), and the Sanchar Saathi mobile app has the same option.

1. Go to sancharsaathi.gov.in and open **Citizen Centric Services**, then **Know Mobile Connections in Your Name**. On the app, pick the same option from the home screen.
2. Enter a 10-digit mobile number that is currently with you, and type the captcha.
3. Enter the OTP sent to that number.
4. The portal lists every mobile number registered with the same identity, across Jio, Airtel, Vi and BSNL.
5. Mark each number you don't recognise as **Not My Number**. Mark each one that is yours but which you've stopped using as **Not Required**. Leave the rest alone, or mark them **Required**.
6. Click **Report** and note any reference or request number the portal shows, so you can follow up.

A few things people get wrong:

- **You don't type your Aadhaar number anywhere.** You log in with a mobile number, and DoT's system groups connections issued to the same person. Most SIMs are issued after Aadhaar e-KYC, hence "SIMs on my Aadhaar".
- **Use a number in your own name.** If you log in with a SIM that's registered to a parent or your employer, you will see their connections, not yours.
- **Grouping is "best-effort", in DoT's own words**, so treat the list as a strong signal rather than a legal record. If you know about a SIM that isn't shown, ask the operator directly.

## What "Not My Number", "Not Required" and "Required" actually do

| What you choose | When to use it | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| Not My Number | You never took this SIM and don't recognise it | Sent to the operator for re-verification; disconnected if it fails |
| Not Required | It's yours, but you've stopped using it (an old work SIM, a parent's spare) | Treated as a request to disconnect; the operator closes it after verification |
| Required | It's yours and in use | Nothing changes |

According to the government's 2024 Lok Sabha answer, flagged numbers are re-verified by the operator and disconnected if they fail. Whoever is using a "Not My Number" connection must prove their identity, or lose it.

Two practical points. First, **don't mark a number Not Required just to tidy up** if your bank, UPI, DigiLocker or Income Tax login still sends OTPs to it. Move those to a number you'll keep first. Second, a disconnected number isn't handed to a new customer straight away. DoT's KYC reforms of August 2023 set a 90-day gap before a disconnected number can be reallocated.

## How many SIMs can one person have in India?

| Where your connections are | Maximum mobile connections in one person's name |
|---|---|
| Most of India | 9, across all operators combined |
| Jammu & Kashmir, Assam and North-East service areas | 6 |
| Companies, trusts, societies (business connections) | No fixed cap, but KYC of every end-user is needed |

The 9/6 limit is not new. The nine-connection cap goes back to DoT instructions of 9 August 2012. A DoT letter of 7 December 2021 restated it, set the lower limit of 6 for J&K, Assam and the North-East, and ordered excess connections to be re-verified. The 17 August 2026 circular repeats it. The 2026 circular also makes the limit much harder to get around:

- DoT's Digital Intelligence Platform groups each person's connections across operators.
- From 23 August 2026, operators get a reference photo of anyone already at the limit on DoT's Digital Intelligence Platform. From 24 August they must screen new connections against it, at first the day after activation.
- Any connection activated over the limit has to be suspended immediately. Operators must build this check into real-time activation by **30 November 2026**.

**If you're already over the limit:** under the 2021 instructions, all your connections are flagged for re-verification. You choose which ones to keep and surrender the rest. According to reports of that order, numbers that aren't re-verified lose incoming calls within 45 days and are disconnected within 60 days. Numbers flagged by police, banks or as nuisance callers move faster: outgoing calls are cut in 5 days, incoming in 10, and the number is disconnected in 15. If you don't respond, connections that aren't re-verified are disconnected.

## Penalties under the Telecommunications Act, 2023

The Act separates "more SIMs than allowed" from fraud.

| What happened | Provision | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Using SIMs beyond the notified number | Third Schedule, item 1(b) | Civil penalty up to ₹50,000 the first time, up to ₹2 lakh for each later instance |
| Getting a SIM "through fraud, cheating or personation" | Section 42(3)(e) | Up to 3 years in jail, a fine up to ₹50 lakh, or both |
| Tampering with IMEI or other telecom identifiers | Section 42(3)(c) | Same as above |
| Helping or abetting any of these | Section 42(6) | Same as the offence itself |

Under Section 42(7), the offences in Section 42 are cognisable and non-bailable. You can read the full text on [India Code](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20101/1/a2023-44.pdf).

In plain terms: a tenth SIM means a civil fine and disconnection, not jail. The criminal provisions are for fake-ID SIMs, SIM racketeers and anyone who knowingly passes their SIM to fraudsters. In one recent case reported in September 2026, DoT blacklisted a Chhattisgarh point-of-sale agent who had activated 25 SIMs on customers' documents without their knowledge.

## If you find a SIM you never took

1. Report it as **Not My Number** on Sanchar Saathi straight away. This is the official route.
2. If you've already lost money, or think the number was used in a fraud, call the cybercrime helpline **1930** or file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in, and keep your Sanchar Saathi reference.
3. Check that your Aadhaar biometrics are locked in the mAadhaar app or on the UIDAI site, so your fingerprint can't be used for another e-KYC without you.
4. Recheck Sanchar Saathi after a few weeks to confirm the number has gone.

If your phone itself is lost or stolen, that's a different process: see our guide to [blocking a lost phone through CEIR](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/block-lost-phone-ceir), which works on the handset's IMEI rather than the SIM. For wider habits that keep scammers away from your OTPs, read [how to protect your data from cyberattacks](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-protect-your-data-from-cyberattacks).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I check how many SIMs are on my Aadhaar without my registered mobile number?

No. Sanchar Saathi needs a working mobile number, ideally one in your own name, to receive the OTP. Without one, visit your operator's store with your ID.

### Is it illegal to have more than 9 SIM cards in India?

You can't hold more than 9 personal mobile connections (6 in J&K, Assam and the North-East). Extra connections are re-verified and disconnected. The Telecommunications Act, 2023 provides a civil penalty of up to ₹50,000 the first time and up to ₹2 lakh for each later instance, which is far below the criminal penalties for SIM fraud.

### How long does it take for a reported SIM to be deactivated?

DoT does not publish a fixed deadline for reports made on Sanchar Saathi. The operator re-verifies the number first and disconnects it if verification fails. Check the portal again after a few weeks, and follow up with the operator if the number still appears.

### Does marking "Not Required" close my number immediately?

No. It's a request to disconnect a connection that is yours, and the operator processes it after verification. Before you mark a number Not Required, move any bank, UPI or government logins off it.
