**Quick answer:** Since April 2026, a confirmed train ticket cancelled more than 72 hours before departure loses only a flat fee per passenger (₹240 in AC First, ₹200 in AC 2-tier, ₹180 in AC 3-tier and AC Chair Car, ₹120 in Sleeper, ₹60 in Second Class, plus GST on AC classes). Between 72 and 24 hours, 25% of the fare is deducted; between 24 and 8 hours, 50%; and inside 8 hours there is no refund at all.

RAC and waitlisted tickets are cheaper to drop: a clerkage of ₹60 per passenger (plus GST in AC) if you cancel up to 30 minutes before departure. Confirmed Tatkal tickets get no refund. The worked examples below show what you actually get back.

## What changed in 2026

The Railway Passengers (Cancellation of Ticket and Refund of Fare) Rules, 2015, still govern refunds, but the time slabs moved. Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced the change on 24 March 2026, and according to the government's [New India Samachar](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/flipbook/2026/Apr/2nd/English/files/basic-html/page7.html) (PIB, April 2026), the old 48, 12 and 4-hour windows became 72, 24 and 8 hours. Most reports put the start date at 1 April 2026; the ministry itself said the rules would roll out between 1 and 15 April.

The reason given is the earlier chart. Under a Railway Board circular of 16 December 2025, applied by zonal railways from late December 2025, the first reservation chart is prepared:

- **for trains leaving between 05:01 and 14:00:** preferably by 8 pm the previous day
- **for trains leaving between 14:01 and 05:00:** at least 10 hours before departure

With seats now allotted 9 to 18 hours ahead, deadlines moved back so freed berths can still reach waitlisted passengers.

One caution: as of today, IRCTC's own refund rules document and the indianrail.gov.in refund page still show the 2015 slabs (48/12/4 hours). The new slabs come from the ministry's announcement and PIB, and booking apps now apply them.

## Cancellation charges for confirmed tickets

| When you cancel (before scheduled departure) | Deduction per passenger |
|---|---|
| More than 72 hours | Flat fee only (see class table) |
| 72 to 24 hours | 25% of fare, but not less than the flat fee |
| 24 to 8 hours | 50% of fare, but not less than the flat fee |
| Less than 8 hours | No refund |

The flat fee doubles as the minimum charge in the 25% and 50% slabs. The amounts below come from the 2015 rules, and the March 2026 announcement did not change them.

| Class | Flat fee / minimum charge |
|---|---|
| AC First Class / Executive Class | ₹240 + GST |
| AC 2-tier / First Class | ₹200 + GST |
| AC 3-tier / AC Chair Car / AC 3 Economy | ₹180 + GST |
| Sleeper | ₹120 |
| Second Class (reserved) | ₹60 |

The IRCTC convenience fee and payment gateway charges on e-tickets are not refunded in any slab.

## Worked examples under the current rules

Per passenger, on the ticket fare, leaving out GST on AC classes.

**1. AC 3-tier confirmed, fare ₹1,800, cancelled five days ahead.** You're in the 72-hour-plus slab, so only ₹180 is deducted. Refund: ₹1,620 (less GST on the ₹180).

**2. Same ticket, cancelled 40 hours before departure.** 25% of ₹1,800 is ₹450, which is more than the ₹180 minimum. Refund: ₹1,350.

**3. Same ticket, cancelled 12 hours before departure.** 50% is deducted. Refund: ₹900.

**4. Sleeper confirmed, fare ₹400, cancelled 30 hours ahead.** 25% would be ₹100, but the Sleeper minimum is ₹120, so ₹120 is deducted. Refund: ₹280.

**5. Train leaves at 7:30 am; you cancel at 1 am the same night.** That is 6.5 hours before departure, inside the 8-hour cut-off, so you get nothing back. Count the hours carefully on overnight journeys (see our [12-hour and 24-hour clock](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/12-hour-and-24-hour-clock) guide).

**6. Sleeper RAC, fare ₹400, cancelled two hours before departure.** RAC and waitlisted tickets pay only clerkage, ₹60. Refund: ₹340. After 30 minutes before departure, there's no refund.

**7. AC 3-tier Tatkal, confirmed, fare ₹2,100.** Cancel it at any time and you get ₹0. A confirmed Tatkal ticket is never refundable. Had it still been waitlisted, you'd lose only the clerkage.

## RAC and waitlisted tickets

According to IRCTC's refund rules:

- **Clerkage:** ₹60 per passenger, plus GST for AC classes, if you cancel up to 30 minutes before departure. After that, there's no refund.
- **Confirmed at the chart:** a waitlisted or RAC ticket that gets a berth by the final chart counts as a confirmed ticket, so the confirmed-ticket slabs apply. With the chart now ready the evening before, a waitlisted ticket for a 9 am train that gets confirmed at 8 pm and is cancelled at 11 pm (10 hours before departure) falls in the 50% slab, not the ₹60 one.
- **Waitlisted e-tickets:** if every passenger on the PNR is still waitlisted after the chart is prepared, the names are dropped and the fare is refunded automatically to your payment account, minus clerkage. You don't need to cancel. Counter (i-ticket and PRS) waitlisted tickets are not dropped and must be cancelled up to 30 minutes before departure.
- **Family or group tickets with mixed status:** if some passengers are confirmed and others waitlisted, and nobody travels, cancel or file a TDR up to 30 minutes before departure to get the confirmed passengers' fare back, minus clerkage.

## Tatkal refund rules

- A confirmed Tatkal ticket gets no refund.
- RAC or waitlisted Tatkal tickets follow the normal clerkage rule.
- For a group Tatkal ticket that is only partly confirmed, the confirmed passengers get a refund (minus clerkage) only if the whole ticket is surrendered up to 30 minutes before departure.
- If the railway puts you in a lower class, you get back the fare difference and the difference in Tatkal charges.

## When to file a TDR, and the time limits

A TDR (Ticket Deposit Receipt) is how you claim a refund when you can't simply cancel, such as a very late train, a broken AC or a diversion. In March 2026, the ministry said e-ticket refunds would increasingly be processed automatically without a TDR. A cancelled train is already refunded automatically. For the other cases, IRCTC still lists the TDR route and its time limits:

| Reason | File TDR by |
|---|---|
| Train cancelled | Not needed, refund is automatic |
| Train more than 3 hours late and you didn't travel | Before the train actually departs |
| AC not working | Within 20 hours of actual arrival at destination |
| Train diverted and you didn't travel | Within 72 hours of scheduled departure |
| Train terminated short of your destination | Within 72 hours of scheduled arrival |
| Given a lower class than booked | Within 2 days of the TTE's certificate |
| Missed a connecting train (linked PNRs) because the first was late | Within 3 hours of the first train's actual arrival |

**How to file a TDR:** log in to the IRCTC website or Rail Connect app, open My Transactions, choose File TDR, select the PNR and passengers, and pick the reason. Some reasons need a certificate from the TTE. Track it under TDR history; the refund goes to the original payment method. The full list of reasons is in [IRCTC's refund rules document](https://contents.irctc.co.in/en/RefundCancellationRules.pdf).

## Vande Bharat Sleeper and Amrit Bharat II are stricter

These newer trains have their own rule, notified on 16 January 2026 (G.S.R. 41(E)). IRCTC lists 25% deducted if you cancel more than 72 hours ahead, 50% between 72 and 8 hours, and no refund within 8 hours. Even an early cancellation costs a quarter of the fare.

Two other 2026 changes help: counter tickets can now be cancelled at any railway station, and the boarding point can be changed up to 30 minutes before departure, according to the ministry.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is deducted if I cancel a confirmed ticket 48 hours before the train?

48 hours falls in the 72-to-24-hour slab, so 25% of the fare is deducted, subject to the class minimum (₹180 in AC 3-tier, ₹120 in Sleeper). Under the pre-April 2026 rules, a cancellation a little over 48 hours ahead cost only the flat fee.

### Is there any refund if I cancel within 8 hours of departure?

No. A confirmed ticket cancelled less than 8 hours before scheduled departure gets no refund, unless the case qualifies for a TDR, such as a train running more than 3 hours late.

### Do I need to cancel a waitlisted e-ticket myself?

No. If all passengers on the e-ticket are still waitlisted after the chart, the ticket is cancelled automatically and the fare, minus ₹60 clerkage per passenger (plus GST in AC), is refunded. Counter tickets must be cancelled by you.

### Can I get a refund on a confirmed Tatkal ticket?

No, not if you cancel it yourself: confirmed Tatkal tickets are non-refundable. If the railway cancels the train, the e-ticket fare is refunded automatically.
