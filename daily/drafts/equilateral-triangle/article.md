**Quick answer:** The area of an equilateral triangle with side a is (√3/4) × a². For a side of 10 cm, that is (√3/4) × 100 = 25√3 ≈ 43.30 cm².

The same triangle has height (√3/2) × a and perimeter 3a. If you know the area and need the side, reverse the formula: a = √(4 × Area ÷ √3). Below you will find why the formula works, a ready table for common sides, solved textbook-style problems (including ₹ cost questions) and the mistakes that cost marks.

## Where this comes up in the NCERT syllabus

Students in India usually meet this formula in Class 9. In the new NCERT Class 9 textbook [*Ganita Manjari*](https://ciet.ncert.gov.in/audio-book/186) (Part I, used from the 2026-27 session), it falls under Chapter 6, "Measuring Space: Perimeter and Area", which covers the area of triangles and Heron's formula. In the older rationalised textbook it sits in Chapter 10, "Heron's Formula", whose very first exercise question is a traffic signal board shaped like an equilateral triangle with a perimeter of 180 cm. We solve that one below.

The formula comes back in Class 10 trigonometry (through sin 60° = √3/2), and it is handy in the mensuration questions of competitive exams, where knowing it by heart saves you working out the height each time.

## The formula, height and perimeter at a glance

For an equilateral triangle with each side a:

| What you want | Formula | Side = 6 cm |
|---|---|---|
| Area | (√3/4) × a² | 9√3 ≈ 15.59 cm² |
| Height (altitude) | (√3/2) × a | 3√3 ≈ 5.20 cm |
| Perimeter | 3a | 18 cm |
| Side from area | √(4 × Area ÷ √3) | 6 cm |
| Area from height | h² ÷ √3 | 9√3 ≈ 15.59 cm² |

All three angles are 60°, and the height from any corner cuts the opposite side into two equal halves of a/2.

## Why the area is (√3/4) × a²: three short proofs

### 1. Using Pythagoras (the height method)

Drop a perpendicular from the top corner to the base. It splits the triangle into two right-angled triangles, each with hypotenuse a and base a/2.

By Pythagoras, h² = a² − (a/2)² = a² − a²/4 = 3a²/4, so h = (√3/2) × a.

Now use the usual area of a triangle, ½ × base × height:

Area = ½ × a × (√3/2) × a = (√3/4) × a².

### 2. Using Heron's formula

This is how the NCERT Heron's formula chapter treats it. For sides a, a, a, the semi-perimeter is s = 3a/2. Then s − a = a/2 for each side, and

Area = √[s(s − a)(s − a)(s − a)] = √[(3a/2) × (a/2) × (a/2) × (a/2)] = √(3a⁴/16) = (√3/4) × a².

Check with a = 10 cm: s = 15, so Area = √(15 × 5 × 5 × 5) = √1875 ≈ 43.30 cm², the same as 25√3.

### 3. Using ½ab sin C

If you know two sides and the angle between them, area = ½ × a × b × sin C. Here both sides are a and the angle is 60°, so Area = ½ × a × a × (√3/2) = (√3/4) × a². We have explained the general version in our guide to the [area of a triangle using trigonometry](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-triangle-trigonometry).

## Solved examples

### Example 1: Traffic signal board (NCERT-style)

A signal board is an equilateral triangle with perimeter 180 cm. Find its area.

- Side a = 180 ÷ 3 = 60 cm.
- Area = (√3/4) × 60² = (√3/4) × 3600 = 900√3 cm².
- Using √3 ≈ 1.732: 900 × 1.732 = 1558.8 cm².

In square metres, divide by 10,000: about 0.156 m².

### Example 2: Finding the side from the area

The area of an equilateral triangle is 16√3 cm². Find its side and perimeter.

- (√3/4) × a² = 16√3, so a²/4 = 16, giving a² = 64 and a = 8 cm.
- Perimeter = 3 × 8 = 24 cm.

When the area is given as a decimal, say 173.2 m², use √3 ≈ 1.732: a² = 4 × 173.2 ÷ 1.732 = 400, so a = 20 m.

### Example 3: Finding the area from the height

The height of an equilateral triangle is 6√3 cm. Find its area.

- From h = (√3/2) × a: a = 2h ÷ √3 = 2 × 6√3 ÷ √3 = 12 cm.
- Area = (√3/4) × 144 = 36√3 ≈ 62.35 cm².

Shortcut: Area = h² ÷ √3 = 108 ÷ √3 = 36√3. Same answer.

### Example 4: Cost of levelling and fencing a park (₹ problem)

A park is an equilateral triangle with side 30 m. Find the cost of levelling it at ₹12 per m² and fencing it at ₹45 per metre.

- Area = (√3/4) × 30² = 225√3 m² ≈ 225 × 1.732 = 389.7 m².
- Levelling cost = 389.7 × 12 = ₹4,676.40.
- Perimeter = 3 × 30 = 90 m, so fencing cost = 90 × 45 = ₹4,050.
- Total = ₹8,726.40.

Notice that the rounding of √3 changes the rupee answer. With √3 ≈ 1.73 the levelling cost comes to ₹4,671, and with 1.7 it drops to ₹4,590. That is why most textbooks say "take √3 = 1.732" — follow the value the question gives.

### Example 5: Area left after cutting a triangle from a rectangle

An equilateral triangle of side 4 cm is cut from a rectangular card 10 cm × 6 cm. Find the area left (√3 = 1.732).

- Rectangle = 60 cm².
- Triangle = 4√3 ≈ 6.928 cm².
- Area left ≈ 60 − 6.928 = 53.072 ≈ 53.07 cm².

Composite shapes like this also appear with trapeziums; our [area of a trapezium](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-a-trapezium) guide covers that shape.

## Common mistakes that cost marks

- **Writing √3/2 instead of √3/4.** √3/2 belongs to the height, not the area. For side 10, √3/2 × 100 gives 86.60, exactly double the right answer.
- **Squaring the wrong thing.** Only the side is squared: (√3/4) × a², not (√3 × a)²/4. For side 10 the wrong version gives 75 instead of 43.30.
- **Rounding √3 too early or too roughly.** Keep the answer as k√3 until the last step, then use the value the question gives (usually 1.732). Using 1.7 for a park of side 30 m changed the cost by about ₹86 in Example 4.
- **Using the side as the height.** In ½ × base × height, the height is (√3/2) × a ≈ 0.866a, always shorter than the side.
- **Mixing units.** A side in cm gives cm². To convert cm² to m², divide by 10,000, not 100.
- **Forgetting the perimeter step.** Many questions give the perimeter, not the side. Divide by 3 first.

## Two more facts worth knowing

All the centres of an equilateral triangle (centroid, incentre, circumcentre, orthocentre) are the same point, which sits one-third of the way up the height. That gives the inradius a/(2√3) and the circumradius a/√3. For side 10 cm these are about 2.89 cm and 5.77 cm. Combine them with the [area of a circle](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-a-circle) formula and you can solve the "circle inscribed in a triangle" questions that appear in Class 10 practice sets.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the formula for the area of an equilateral triangle?

Area = (√3/4) × a², where a is the length of one side. Using √3 ≈ 1.732, this is roughly 0.433 × a².

### How do you find the side of an equilateral triangle if the area is given?

Multiply the area by 4, divide by √3 and take the square root: a = √(4 × Area ÷ √3). For an area of 16√3 cm², the side is 8 cm.

### What is the height of an equilateral triangle with side 10 cm?

The height is (√3/2) × 10 = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 cm. It also bisects the base into two 5 cm halves.

### Is the area of an equilateral triangle in NCERT Class 9?

Yes. It is worked out using Heron's formula in the Class 9 area chapter: Chapter 6 of the new *Ganita Manjari* Part I, and Chapter 10 ("Heron's Formula") in the older rationalised textbook.
