**Quick answer:** Your in-hand salary is your CTC minus the money that never reaches your bank account each month. Take out the employer's PF contribution and any gratuity provision to get your gross salary. Then subtract your own PF, professional tax and income tax (TDS). In the illustrations below, that leaves roughly 80% to 90% of CTC as take-home pay at 6 to 15 LPA.

The new Labour Codes add one twist. Since 21 November 2025, at least half of your pay has to count as "wages" for PF and gratuity. If an employer raises basic pay to meet this rule without raising the CTC, more of the package goes to retirement savings and the monthly credit can drop a little. The worked examples below show how big that shift is.

## What CTC includes that your salary account never sees

CTC (cost to company) is everything an employer spends on you in a year. It usually has three buckets:

- **Fixed monthly pay:** basic salary, HRA, special allowance and other allowances.
- **Retirement benefits:** the employer's PF contribution and, at many companies, a gratuity provision.
- **Other items:** variable or performance bonus, insurance premiums and perks.

Only the first bucket reaches you monthly, and it shrinks after deductions.

## The 50% wage rule under the Labour Codes

The four Labour Codes [took effect on 21 November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2192463&reg=48&lang=2). The Code on Wages defines "wages" as basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. Items such as HRA, conveyance allowance, statutory bonus, overtime, commission and the employer's PF contribution are left out.

If those excluded items add up to more than half of your total remuneration, the excess is counted back into wages. Gratuity and retrenchment compensation are not counted in this test. In practice, many employers are moving basic pay (plus DA) up to about 50% of pay.

This matters for two reasons:

1. **PF** is a percentage of wages, so a higher basic can mean a higher PF deduction.
2. **Gratuity** is based on your last drawn wages, so a higher basic means a bigger gratuity, and a bigger provision if gratuity sits inside your CTC.

## Two more changes to know

### The EPF wage ceiling is now ₹25,000

The EPF wage ceiling went up from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month, [effective 17 September 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2311548). The employee contributes 12% of wages. The employer also pays 12%, split as 8.33% to the pension scheme (EPS) and 3.67% to your PF account. When PF is calculated on the ceiling, your share is capped at ₹3,000 a month, up from ₹1,800. Many employers instead calculate PF on the full basic, so check which method your offer letter uses.

### Gratuity after one year for fixed-term staff

Gratuity is 15 days' wages for every completed year of service, based on the last wages drawn, up to ₹20 lakh. For monthly-paid staff it is usually worked out as **last monthly wages × 15/26 × years of service**. Permanent employees still need five years of service. Fixed-term employees now qualify after one year of continuous service.

## How to calculate in-hand salary from CTC

1. **Gross salary** = CTC − employer PF − gratuity provision − variable pay.
2. **Taxable income (new regime)** = gross salary − ₹75,000 standard deduction.
3. **Income tax** = tax under the slabs + 4% health and education cess.
4. **Annual in-hand** = gross salary − employee PF − professional tax − income tax. Divide by 12 for the monthly figure.

### New tax regime slabs for FY 2026-27

Budget 2026 left the slabs unchanged. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026.

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4 lakh | Nil |
| ₹4–8 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8–12 lakh | 10% |
| ₹12–16 lakh | 15% |
| ₹16–20 lakh | 20% |
| ₹20–24 lakh | 25% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% |

A rebate makes the tax nil when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. With the standard deduction, that covers salary income up to ₹12.75 lakh. Our guide to the [old vs new tax regime](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/old-vs-new-tax-regime) compares the two.

## Worked examples at 6, 10 and 15 LPA

**Assumptions (illustration only; real salary structures vary):**

- No variable pay. CTC = basic + allowances + employer PF + gratuity provision.
- Employee PF equals employer PF.
- Gratuity provision = 4.81% of basic (15/26 of a month's basic, spread over 12 months).
- Professional tax = ₹2,500 a year (₹200 a month, ₹300 in February), as in Maharashtra and Karnataka at these salaries. Some states, such as Delhi, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh, don't levy it.
- New tax regime, no other income.

Each table compares three structures:

- **Old style:** basic is 40% of CTC.
- **50%, full PF:** basic is 50% of CTC, and PF is 12% of the full basic.
- **50%, capped PF:** basic is 50% of CTC, and PF is on the ₹25,000 ceiling.

### 6 LPA (₹6,00,000 CTC)

| ₹ a year | Old style | 50%, full PF | 50%, capped PF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer PF | 28,800 | 36,000 | 36,000 |
| Gratuity provision | 11,538 | 14,423 | 14,423 |
| Gross salary | 5,59,662 | 5,49,577 | 5,49,577 |
| Income tax | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Monthly in-hand** | **44,030** | **42,590** | **42,590** |

A 50% basic here is exactly ₹25,000 a month, the new PF ceiling, so the last two columns match. Take-home falls by about ₹1,440 a month. Retirement savings (both PF shares plus gratuity) rise from ₹69,138 to ₹86,423 a year.

### 10 LPA (₹10,00,000 CTC)

| ₹ a year | Old style | 50%, full PF | 50%, capped PF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer PF | 48,000 | 60,000 | 36,000 |
| Gratuity provision | 19,231 | 24,038 | 24,038 |
| Gross salary | 9,32,769 | 9,15,962 | 9,39,962 |
| Income tax | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Monthly in-hand** | **73,522** | **71,122** | **75,122** |

The PF method now decides the result. With PF on the full basic, take-home falls by about ₹2,400 a month. If your employer moves from PF on the full basic to PF on the ₹25,000 ceiling, take-home rises, because less of the CTC goes to PF. If PF is on the ₹25,000 ceiling in both cases, the higher basic trims take-home by only about ₹400 a month, through the bigger gratuity provision. Separately, the ceiling's rise from ₹15,000 lifts a capped employee's own PF from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 a month, so take-home falls by up to ₹1,200 a month whatever the basic. Tax stays nil.

### 15 LPA (₹15,00,000 CTC)

| ₹ a year | Old style | 50%, full PF | 50%, capped PF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer PF | 72,000 | 90,000 | 36,000 |
| Gratuity provision | 28,846 | 36,058 | 36,058 |
| Gross salary | 13,99,154 | 13,73,942 | 14,27,942 |
| Income tax (with cess) | 81,768 | 77,835 | 86,259 |
| **Monthly in-hand** | **1,03,574** | **1,00,301** | **1,08,599** |

Taxable income crosses ₹12 lakh, so there is no rebate. A bigger basic with full PF lowers income tax, because more of the CTC goes into employer PF and the gratuity provision, which aren't part of your taxable salary here. It still cuts take-home by about ₹3,270 a month. Take-home is higher with capped PF only when compared with an old structure that took PF on the full basic. If PF is on the ₹25,000 ceiling in both cases, the 50% rule cuts take-home by about ₹500 a month. Capped PF also means more tax and less retirement saving.

## How to sanity-check your offer letter

- **Find the basic + DA share.** If it is well below half of fixed pay, ask how the company applies the 50% rule.
- **Check whether PF is on the full basic or capped at ₹25,000.** From 10 LPA up, this makes the biggest difference to take-home pay.
- **See whether gratuity is inside CTC.** That amount is paid only when you leave after becoming eligible.
- **Separate variable pay.** A 15 LPA offer with 10% variable guarantees only ₹13.5 lakh.
- **Match TDS to your chosen regime**, then reconcile it with Form 16 when you [file your ITR online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-file-itr-online).

Comparing two offers? Look at in-hand pay and retirement benefits, not CTC alone. Our piece on [when to switch companies in India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/when-is-the-perfect-time-to-switch-companies-in-india) covers the timing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will the new Labour Codes reduce my take-home salary?

It can, if your employer raises basic pay to meet the 50% rule without raising CTC. The drop is largest when PF is calculated on the full basic. If PF is on the ₹25,000 ceiling, the drop is small, mainly from a bigger gratuity provision. Take-home rises only if the employer also switches from full-basic PF to capped PF.

### What is the formula to convert CTC to in-hand salary?

In-hand salary = CTC − employer PF − gratuity provision − variable pay − employee PF − professional tax − income tax. Divide by 12 for the monthly figure.

### Is there income tax on a 10 LPA salary in the new regime?

In our illustration, no. Taxable income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction stays under ₹12 lakh, so the rebate brings tax to nil. Income from other sources could change that.

### Is gratuity paid every month with salary?

No. Gratuity in your CTC is a provision, not a monthly payment. It is paid when you leave after five years of service, or after one year if you are a fixed-term employee, and earlier on death or disablement.
