**Quick answer:** To sit NEET UG 2027 you must be at least 17 years old by 31 December 2027 (born on or before 31 December 2010), and you must have passed, or be appearing in, Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology, and English. There is no upper age limit and no limit on the number of attempts. Open school (NIOS and recognised state open school) candidates can apply, and so can NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals.

The NTA has not yet released the NEET UG 2027 information bulletin (as of 19 November 2026). Everything below is based on the NEET UG 2026 bulletin and the National Medical Commission's (NMC) current regulations. A parliamentary panel has recommended exploring age and attempt limits, so read the 2027 bulletin line by line when it comes out on [neet.nta.nic.in](https://neet.nta.nic.in).

## Is there a limit on NEET attempts?

No. This is the most repeated myth about NEET, and it usually comes from mixing up three different rules.

1. **The 2017 caps.** In January 2017 the rules for NEET (then run by CBSE) capped both age (25 for unreserved and 30 for reserved candidates) and attempts (three, counted from 2017), as Business Standard reported. The attempt cap was dropped in 2017 after the Supreme Court-appointed oversight committee opposed limits on attempts and age, according to Medical Dialogues, and this is where most "three attempts" claims online come from. The age cap lasted longer: according to ThePrint, the NMC decided at its meeting on 21 October 2021 that "there should not be any fixed upper age limit", and told the NTA on 9 March 2022.
2. **The four-attempt rule in GMER 2023.** The NMC's Graduate Medical Education Regulations 2023, notified on 2 June 2023, say an MBBS student gets no more than four attempts to clear the First Professional (first-year) exam, and must finish the course within nine years of admission, as reported by Medical Dialogues. This applies after you get an MBBS seat. It has nothing to do with how many times you can take NEET.
3. **The 2026 recommendation.** On 9 October 2026, the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, in its 382nd report, recommended that the Education Ministry explore, with the Health Ministry, "possibilities of reasonable age limits and reasonable attempt limits to all major examinations including the NEET-UG", according to ThePrint. This is only a recommendation. No rule has been notified as of today.

So a repeater taking NEET for the third or fifth time in 2027 is eligible under current rules. Any cap would have to appear in writing in the 2027 bulletin.

## Subjects: English, Biotechnology and "additional subject" cases

The NEET UG 2026 bulletin lists seven qualifying routes. Most students use code 01 (appearing in Class 12 this year) or code 02 (already passed Class 12); the rest cover intermediate, pre-medical, first-year degree and B.Sc. routes.

Some points people often get wrong:

- **English is compulsory.** You need English as a subject in the qualifying exam, along with Physics, Chemistry and Biology/Biotechnology, including the practicals.
- **Biotechnology counts in place of Biology.** The bulletin writes it as "Biology / Biotechnology" everywhere. The NEET paper itself still tests Botany and Zoology, so a Biotechnology student has to prepare those separately.
- **Biology as an additional subject is allowed.** In November 2023, after GMER 2023 came in, the NMC allowed candidates who studied the required subjects "even as additional subjects after passing Class 12th from duly recognized boards". This covers, for example, a PCM student who later passes Biology from NIOS. The 2026 bulletin repeats this wording.

### What about 50% in PCB in Class 12?

Older NEET bulletins, such as NEET 2021's, said general candidates needed at least 50% in Physics, Chemistry and Biology taken together, and SC, ST and OBC-NCL candidates 40%. Many websites still quote these figures. The eligibility chapter of the NEET UG 2026 bulletin lists the subjects and qualifying exams but does not set a Class 12 percentage.

If your PCB aggregate is below 50% (or 40% in a reserved category), check the counselling brochure of the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC) or your state's counselling authority before you count on a seat. Counselling bodies and colleges apply their own admission rules.

### Why open school students used to be confused

A 2017 amendment to the old 1997 medical education rules barred open school and private candidates from NEET. The Delhi High Court struck that down in 2018 and the Medical Council of India appealed. According to Medical Dialogues, the Supreme Court disposed of that appeal in an order dated 27 February 2024, recording an NMC letter of November 2023 that "open schools recognized by CBSE and State Education Boards shall be recognized" for NEET.

In a public notice dated 22 February 2025, the NMC's Undergraduate Medical Education Board said that under GMER 2023 "studying through NIOS is allowed for appearing for NEET UG Examinations", as reported by Medical Dialogues.

## Planning to study MBBS abroad? NEET first, then FMGL 2021

The NEET UG 2026 bulletin says Indian citizens and OCI cardholders who want to do MBBS at a foreign university must also qualify NEET. Without it, a foreign degree won't lead to a licence to practise in India.

The NMC's Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate (FMGL) Regulations, in force from 18 November 2021, also require:

- a course of at least 54 months, taught in English
- a 12-month internship at the same foreign university
- completion of the whole course within 10 years of joining
- registration as a doctor in that country, on the same terms as its own citizens
- a further 12-month supervised internship in India, and passing the screening or exit test the NMC prescribes

The NMC's [FAQ on the FMGL Regulations](https://nmc.org.in/storage/cms/rules-regulation-nmc/yFQCJ2Bf9yZU5zBfLCNUFIFG2E3optNdIAQfas9Z.pdf) explains each condition. Check any foreign university against them before paying fees.

## What could change for 2027

The Education Minister announced on 15 May 2026, after the NEET UG 2026 paper was cancelled over a leak, that NEET would move to computer-based testing from 2027. That changes how the exam is held, not who can sit it. The only eligibility change being discussed is the panel's suggested age and attempt limits, and none has been notified.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the age limit for NEET UG 2027?

You must be 17 or older on or before 31 December 2027, so you must be born on or before 31 December 2010. There is no upper age limit under current NMC rules.

### How many times can I attempt NEET?

As many times as you like. Neither the NMC nor the NTA caps NEET attempts. The four-attempt rule in GMER 2023 is about clearing first-year MBBS exams, not NEET.

### Can NIOS students take NEET and get an MBBS seat?

Yes. The Supreme Court accepted in 2024 that open schools recognised by CBSE and state boards are valid for NEET, and the NMC confirmed in a February 2025 public notice that studying through NIOS is allowed.

### Is Biotechnology accepted instead of Biology for NEET?

Yes. NMC rules and the NTA bulletin accept Biology or Biotechnology, along with Physics, Chemistry and English. The NEET paper still has Botany and Zoology questions, so Biotechnology students need to cover those topics too.
