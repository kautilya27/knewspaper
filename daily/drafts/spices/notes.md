# Notes: spices-names-in-english-and-hindi

## Top results reviewed (query: "spices names in english and hindi")
- vegrecipesofindia.com glossary (comparable page): ~100 spices, English + Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Konkani, Gujarati; many blank cells; no botanical names; no Bengali/Malayalam column. Look-alike questions (kalonji vs kala jeera, shah jeera vs caraway, kali jeeri) only argued in comments, partly contradictory (author sides with shah jeera = caraway).
- spiceupthecurry.com list: Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam; no Bengali; no botanical names; calls kalonji "onion seed"; lists "kala jeera" as alt Hindi name for shahjeera = caraway; no radhuni; no look-alike explanations.
- Wikipedia "List of Indian spices" (read via mirror wiki.krisyotam.com; en.wikipedia.org fetch blocked): Hindi only, few botanical names; no Bengali/Gujarati/Marathi; dill and black cumin missing.
- geeksforgeeks, myweekendkitchen, pw.live, leverageedu, merirasoi appear in SERP; the GfG link resolved to its homepage only. These are simple English–Hindi lists.

## Gap we fill

- Dedicated section untangling kalonji / kala jeera / shahi jeera (incl. Bengal vs North India meaning), javitri vs jaiphal, ajwain vs radhuni vs ajmod (and Malayalam ayamodakam overlap), saunf vs sowa vs aniseed, kabab chini (cubeb) vs allspice, dalchini vs cassia, tej patta vs Laurus nobilis.

## Sources and what each confirms
- https://indianspices.com/node/12.html (Spices Board, Bishop's weed): Trachyspermum ammi; Bengali Jowan/Joan, Gujarati Yavan, Marathi Onva, Malayalam Omum.
- https://indianspices.com/node/28.html (Spices Board, Dill): Anethum graveolens; Anethum sowa = Indian dill, native N India, bolder flavour; Hindi/Bengali Sowa, Gujarati Surva, Marathi Surva/Shepu, Malayalam Sathakuppa.
- https://www.indianspices.com/spice-catalog/fennel.html (Spices Board, Fennel): Foeniculum vulgare; Bengali Mauri/Muhiri, Gujarati Variari, Marathi Badishep, Malayalam Perum jeerakam.
- https://indianspices.com/spices-development/spice-catalogue2679.html?page=1 (Spices Board): Cinnamomum cassia, Cinnamomum verum (true cinnamon).
- https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/ftp07/chapter/76/ (INFLIBNET e-PG Pathshala, nomenclature of spices): botanical names for cardamom small/large, pepper, chilli, ginger, turmeric, coriander, cumin, fennel, fenugreek, clove, caraway (Carum carvi), mustard (Brassica juncea), saffron, mace & nutmeg (Myristica fragrans; mace = aril, nutmeg = seed), dill, cassia, aniseed (Pimpinella anisum), allspice (Pimenta dioica), bay leaf (Laurus nobilis) vs tejpat (Cinnamomum tamala), asafoetida, celery (Apium graveolens).
- https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:77128917-1 (Kew POWO): Elwendia persica accepted; Bunium persicum homotypic synonym; native Iran to Central Asia and W Himalaya.
- https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:711687-1 (Kew POWO): Nigella sativa accepted.
- https://www.downtoearth.org.in/food/the-fifth-element-why-radhuni-needs-to-be-mainstreamed-88661 (Down To Earth): radhuni = Trachyspermum roxburghianum; Hindi ajmod; Malayalam ayamodakam; Bengali cuisine; replaces mustard in West Bengal panch phoron.

- Malayalam "ayamodakam" given for ajwain (Katzer) and radhuni (Down To Earth); left Malayalam radhuni cell blank.
- Left blank ("–"): Gujarati and Marathi nigella; Marathi and Malayalam shahi jeera; Gujarati/Marathi/Malayalam radhuni; Bengali star anise; Bengali dry ginger; Gujarati and Malayalam cubeb (Katzer gives Gujarati "tadamari" for cubeb, not used, low confidence).
- Malayalam dry ginger "chukku": Katzer entry was truncated in fetch; name is standard but not seen in full.
- Bengali dill: Spices Board gives "Sowa"; "shulpa" is also common in Bengal but not verified, not used.
