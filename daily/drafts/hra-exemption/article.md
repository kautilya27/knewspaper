**Quick answer:** Your tax-free HRA is the **lowest** of three amounts: the HRA you actually received, the rent you paid minus 10% of your salary, and 50% of salary (if you live in one of eight listed cities) or 40% (anywhere else). Here "salary" means basic pay plus dearness allowance where your terms of employment count it, not your full CTC. Whatever HRA is left over is taxed as salary.

One big thing changed from 1 April 2026, when the Income-tax Act, 2025 took over from the 1961 Act: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad joined Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai in the 50% group. One thing didn't: the exemption still only works under the old tax regime. Under the new regime, which is the default, all of your HRA is taxable.

## Where the rule sits now

According to the Income Tax Department's published text, the HRA exemption is now in **Schedule III (serial number 11)**, read with **section 11** of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The three-way limit is in **Rule 279** of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. These replace section 10(13A) of the 1961 Act and Rule 2A of the 1962 Rules. If you want the full old-to-new mapping, see our [guide to the new Income-tax Act's section numbers](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/new-income-tax-act-section-mapping).

Which law applies depends on the year:

| Your income for | Law that applies | Cities at 50% |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2025-26 (April 2025 to March 2026, return filed in 2026) | Section 10(13A), 1961 Act, with Rule 2A | Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai |
| Tax year 2026-27 onwards (from 1 April 2026) | Schedule III No. 11, 2025 Act, with Rule 279 | Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad |

So someone in Pune filing their return for FY 2025-26 still uses the 40% limit. The 50% limit applies to their salary from April 2026.

Rule 279 lists cities by name, not metro regions. It says "Delhi", not Delhi NCR, so a flat in Gurugram, Noida or Ghaziabad is generally read as falling in the 40% group. The same goes for Thane or Navi Mumbai, which aren't Mumbai.

## The three limits, in plain words

Rule 279 says the exempt amount is the least of:

1. **The HRA your employer actually paid you** for the period.
2. **Rent paid minus 10% of salary** for the same period.
3. **50% of salary** for a home in the eight listed cities, or **40% of salary** anywhere else.

Some details trip people up:

- **Salary** is basic pay plus dearness allowance (DA), counting DA only if your terms of employment include it. Special allowance, LTA, bonus and other allowances are not counted. Our [CTC to in-hand salary explainer](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/ctc-to-in-hand-salary) shows how these components fit together.
- **The period** is the time during the year that you actually lived in the rented home. If you started renting in September, you work out all three limits for September to March only, not for the full year.
- **You must actually pay rent and must not own the home you live in.** Schedule III makes both of these conditions. Owning a flat in another city doesn't stop you claiming HRA where you rent.
- If your rent or city changes during the year, calculate each stretch separately and add the results.

## Worked example 1: Bengaluru, and why the new city list matters

Priya works in Bengaluru. Her basic pay is ₹50,000 a month and she gets no DA. Her HRA is ₹25,000 a month, and she pays ₹35,000 a month in rent. She is under the old regime and rented for the whole year.

- Salary for the year: ₹50,000 × 12 = ₹6,00,000
- HRA received: ₹25,000 × 12 = ₹3,00,000
- Rent paid: ₹35,000 × 12 = ₹4,20,000

| Limit | Tax year 2026-27 (Bengaluru at 50%) | FY 2025-26 (Bengaluru at 40%) |
|---|---|---|
| Actual HRA received | ₹3,00,000 | ₹3,00,000 |
| Rent minus 10% of salary (₹4,20,000 − ₹60,000) | ₹3,60,000 | ₹3,60,000 |
| 50% or 40% of salary | ₹3,00,000 | ₹2,40,000 |
| **Exempt HRA (lowest)** | **₹3,00,000** | **₹2,40,000** |
| **Taxable HRA** | **Nil** | **₹60,000** |

With the same pay and rent, ₹60,000 more of her HRA is tax-free from April 2026, only because Bengaluru moved into the 50% group.

## Worked example 2: a non-metro city (Jaipur)

Rahul lives in Jaipur. His basic pay is ₹40,000 a month and he gets ₹4,000 DA, which his terms of employment provide for as part of salary. His HRA is ₹15,000 a month and his rent is ₹14,000 a month.

- Salary: (₹40,000 + ₹4,000) × 12 = ₹5,28,000
- HRA received: ₹15,000 × 12 = ₹1,80,000
- Rent paid: ₹14,000 × 12 = ₹1,68,000

| Limit | Amount |
|---|---|
| Actual HRA received | ₹1,80,000 |
| Rent minus 10% of salary (₹1,68,000 − ₹52,800) | ₹1,15,200 |
| 40% of salary | ₹2,11,200 |
| **Exempt HRA (lowest)** | **₹1,15,200** |
| **Taxable HRA** | **₹64,800** |

Many people assume the exemption equals the rent they pay. It doesn't: the 10% cut usually decides the answer when rent is close to HRA. Rahul's rent is also above ₹1 lakh a year, so he has to give his employer his landlord's PAN (see below).

## Worked example 3: paying rent to your parents (Lucknow)

Ananya lives in a house in Lucknow that belongs only to her mother. She pays her mother ₹20,000 a month by bank transfer under a written rent agreement. Ananya's basic pay is ₹70,000 a month with no DA, and her HRA is ₹28,000 a month.

- Salary: ₹70,000 × 12 = ₹8,40,000
- HRA received: ₹28,000 × 12 = ₹3,36,000
- Rent paid: ₹20,000 × 12 = ₹2,40,000

| Limit | Amount |
|---|---|
| Actual HRA received | ₹3,36,000 |
| Rent minus 10% of salary (₹2,40,000 − ₹84,000) | ₹1,56,000 |
| 40% of salary | ₹3,36,000 |
| **Exempt HRA (lowest)** | **₹1,56,000** |
| **Taxable HRA** | **₹1,80,000** |

The law doesn't stop you paying rent to a parent. But the claim has to be real, and the new form makes the relationship visible:

- The house must belong to the parent. If you own it, or own part of it, the Schedule III condition that you don't own the home is in doubt.
- Pay through a bank account, so the payments can be traced. Cash payments with receipts alone are harder to defend.
- Your parent must declare the rent as income in their own return. The tax saved is only real if your parent's tax on that rent is lower than yours.
- Form 124 asks for your relationship with the landlord, so this arrangement is now disclosed to your employer every year.

## Rent receipts, landlord PAN and Form 124

Under the 2025 Act, you declare your HRA claim to your employer in **Form 124**, which replaces Form 12BB. The Income Tax Department lists it under section 392 of the new Act and Rule 205. According to Rule 205 as published by the Income Tax Department, it asks for:

- the rent paid and the landlord's name and address
- the **landlord's PAN, if total rent for the year is more than ₹1,00,000**, the same threshold as under the old Form 12BB
- your **relationship with the landlord**, if any.

Keep the rent agreement and monthly receipts or bank statements. Employers usually ask for them before giving you the exemption in your TDS. If you skip your employer's deadline, you can still claim the exemption in your return under the old regime, as long as you have the documents.

There's also a separate rule if your rent is more than ₹50,000 a month. In that case you, the tenant, must deduct 2% TDS from the rent and deposit it (section 393 of the 2025 Act, earlier section 194-IB).

## The new-regime trap

Section 202 of the 2025 Act, which replaced section 115BAC, sets the new regime's tax rates. It computes income **without certain Schedule III exemptions**, including serial number 11, the HRA exemption. The new regime is also the default. So if you don't tell your employer you want the old regime, your full HRA is taxed in your monthly TDS, even if you hand in rent receipts.

According to section 202, a salaried person who wants the old regime exercises the option with the return filed under section 263(1), the return due by the deadline. You can then claim the HRA exemption in that return and get back any extra TDS. Whether the old regime is worth it depends on all your deductions, not just HRA. Our [old vs new tax regime comparison](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/old-vs-new-tax-regime) walks through that.

The official text of Rule 279 is on the [Income Tax Department's website](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/rule-279-1).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I claim HRA if I pay rent but my employer doesn't give me HRA?

No. The first limit is the HRA you actually received, so with no HRA the exemption is nil. The old regime has a separate deduction for rent paid by people who get no HRA (section 80GG under the 1961 Act, now section 134 of the 2025 Act), with its own limits.

### Is Gurugram or Noida treated as Delhi for the 50% limit?

Rule 279 names "Delhi" only, not the NCR, so rented homes in Gurugram, Noida, Faridabad or Ghaziabad generally get the 40% limit.

### What if I rented only for part of the year?

Rule 279 works on the "relevant period", the months you actually lived in the rented home. Work out all three limits using only the salary, HRA and rent for those months.

### Do I need rent receipts if my rent is below ₹1 lakh a year?

You don't need to give the landlord's PAN below that limit, but your employer can still ask for rent receipts or the agreement. Keep them in case the tax department asks.
