**Quick answer:** A relieving letter says your old employer has accepted your resignation and released you on a stated last working day. An experience letter (often called a service certificate) records what you did there: your designation, your dates of service and, sometimes, your role. New employers usually ask for the relieving letter to confirm you have exited properly, and the experience letter to prove your work history.

If HR delays or refuses either one, background-check teams often accept other proof: the resignation acceptance email, your EPFO service history showing the exit date, payslips and your full-and-final statement. The new labour codes also require a service certificate within 10 days of leaving, but only for some employees, as explained below.

## Relieving letter vs experience letter at a glance

| | Relieving letter | Experience letter / service certificate |
|---|---|---|
| What it proves | You resigned, it was accepted, and you were released on a set date | How long you worked there and in what role |
| Usual contents | Name, employee ID, resignation date, last working day, a line saying you are relieved of duties | Name, designation(s), joining and leaving dates, nature of work; sometimes a remark on conduct |
| When it is issued | On or soon after the last working day, once handover and clearance are done | Often with the relieving letter; can also be asked for later |
| Who asks for it | Your next employer, at joining or during background verification | Next employers, universities, visa and immigration offices, sometimes lenders |
| Tied to your notice period? | Yes. Leaving without serving or buying out notice is the most common reason it is held back | Less directly. It records facts that already happened |
| Any legal requirement? | No law uses the term "relieving letter" | A service certificate is required under model standing orders for covered workers (see below) |

A combined "relieving-cum-experience letter" is fine if it shows your last working day, designation and tenure.

## Does any law make the employer give these letters?

### The labour codes and the 2026 model standing orders

The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 came into force on 21 November 2025 and replaced the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946. On 8 May 2026 the Union Labour Ministry notified Model Standing Orders, 2026, with separate versions for manufacturing, mining and services, [according to Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/centre-notifies-final-ir-wage-code-rules-issues-model-standing-orders-126050801633_1.html).

According to an analysis by the law firm JSA, the services-sector model says a service certificate must be issued "within 10 (ten) days of discharge, termination, retirement or resignation". India Briefing describes the certificate as covering the nature of work, designation and length of employment. That is essentially an experience letter.

The limits matter, though:

- **Size of the establishment.** Standing orders under the Code apply to industrial establishments with 300 or more workers on any day in the previous 12 months, according to JSA. If your employer has not had its own standing orders certified, the model ones are treated as adopted.
- **Who counts as a "worker".** The labour codes generally exclude people employed mainly in managerial or administrative roles. Under the Industrial Relations Code itself, supervisory staff earning more than ₹18,000 a month are also excluded (the Labour Ministry set the same limit under the Code on Wages in January 2026). So a team lead or manager at an IT firm may not be able to rely on this rule.
- **Centre vs state.** The Centre's model covers establishments where it is the "appropriate government". States frame their own rules for the rest, so the details can differ.

The old 1946 central rules had a similar line. As amended, they said every workman is entitled to a service certificate on discharge, termination, retirement or resignation, showing the nature of work and period of employment, according to the [Labour Ministry's copy of the 1946 Central Rules](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/industrialemploymentstandingorders1centralrules1946.pdf) (Schedule I-B).

### Shops and Establishments Acts

Most offices and IT and services companies are registered under their state's Shops and Establishments Act, which mainly covers working hours, leave and termination notice. These Acts do not necessarily require an exit certificate: Telangana's 1988 Act, for example, requires a written appointment order and written termination, but no service certificate. Check your state's Act and your appointment letter, since many HR policies promise these letters anyway.

### What courts have said

There is no blanket right to a relieving letter. In *Bharat Aviation Pvt Ltd v Rahul Sudhindra Soni* (5 May 2026), the Bombay High Court dealt with an aircraft engineer who had signed a three-year training bond and resigned early. The High Court set aside an Industrial Court order directing the company to issue his relieving letter and service certificate. It held that a relieving letter follows from an accepted resignation, and since the employer was justified in not accepting a resignation that broke a valid bond, it could not be forced to issue either document at that stage, as reported by LiveLaw and SCC Online. The findings were prima facie; the main complaint is still to be decided.

The lesson: settle any bond, unserved notice or company property first. Your claim is much stronger with no open obligations.

Courts have also protected employees on the other side of the move. In 2024, the Delhi High Court set aside a fertiliser company's cancellation of an offer it had made only because the candidate's relieving letter from his previous employer was late, as reported by HR Katha. The letter had since arrived.

## What new employers usually accept instead

Verification teams want to confirm that you worked there and that you have left. If the letter is stuck, offer a bundle like this, and ask the recruiter in writing what they will accept.

1. **Resignation acceptance email** from HR or your manager, ideally with the last working day in it.
2. **EPFO service history.** Log in to the EPFO member portal with your UAN and open "Service History". It lists each employer with your date of joining and date of exit, according to Business Today. An exit date marked by your old employer is good independent proof. You can mark the exit date yourself, but only two months after leaving, and it cannot be changed afterwards, according to EPFO's FAQs as cited by Business Today.
3. **Last three to six payslips** and bank statements showing salary credits stopping.
4. **Full-and-final statement** or the bank credit of your settlement. Our guide to [full and final settlement](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/full-and-final-settlement) explains what it should include.
5. **Your TDS certificate (Form 16)** for the year, which shows the employer and the salary paid.
6. **Offer and appointment letters, and any promotion letters**, to prove your designation.

## How to get the letter if HR refuses

Work through these steps in order and keep everything in writing.

1. **Close the gaps.** Confirm your notice was served or bought out (see our explainer on [notice period rules](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/notice-period-rules)), your laptop and ID card were returned, and your clearance form is signed.
2. **Write a formal request** to HR with your employee ID, last working day and the documents you need, and ask for a date.
3. **Escalate.** After about a week, write again, copying the HR head and your former manager, and ask for any reason in writing.
4. **Raise a grievance.** Larger establishments must have a grievance redressal committee under the Industrial Relations Code.
5. **Approach the labour department.** File a complaint with your district or state labour commissioner's office. This helps most if you are a "worker" under the codes or dues are also pending.
6. **Send a legal notice through a lawyer.** If the delay is costing you a job or there is a bond or damages dispute, see a lawyer who handles employment cases. This article is not legal advice.

## Sample formats (plain text)

**Request for relieving and experience letter**

Subject: Request for relieving letter and experience certificate – [Name], Employee ID [ID]

Dear [HR name],
I resigned on [date], and my last working day was [date]. My handover and asset return were completed on [date]. Please issue my relieving letter and experience certificate showing my designation and dates of service by [date]. If anything is pending from my side, please tell me in writing.
Regards, [Name, phone]

**Simple experience letter wording**

To whom it may concern. This is to certify that [Name] worked with [Company] from [joining date] to [leaving date]. Their last designation was [designation], and their work involved [one line on the role]. Signed, [Name, designation, company seal, date].

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an experience letter the same as a relieving letter?

No. A relieving letter confirms your resignation was accepted and gives your release date. An experience letter records your role and tenure. Many companies combine them in one letter.

### Can a company refuse a relieving letter if I did not serve my notice period?

It often does. In a 2026 interim ruling on a training bond, the Bombay High Court refused to force an employer to issue one, saying a relieving letter follows an accepted resignation; its findings were prima facie. Settling the notice period or buyout usually unlocks it.

### Is the EPFO service history accepted as proof of employment?

Many verification teams accept it because it comes from EPFO and shows both joining and exit dates, but acceptance is each employer's choice. Ask your new employer's HR in writing.

### Within how many days must a service certificate be issued?

The services-sector Model Standing Orders, 2026 say within 10 days of leaving. That applies to covered workers in establishments with 300 or more workers; others should check their contract and company policy.
