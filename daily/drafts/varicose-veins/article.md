**Quick answer:** Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins, usually on the legs, that look blue or dark purple and may bulge above the skin. They form when the small valves inside a vein stop working properly, so blood pools instead of flowing back up to the heart. Common symptoms are aching, heaviness, swollen ankles and itchy or discoloured skin, often worse after a long day on your feet.

Most varicose veins are not dangerous, but a vein that bleeds, a leg sore that won't heal, or a painful swollen calf needs a doctor. Chest pain or sudden breathlessness with a swollen leg is an emergency.

## What varicose veins look and feel like

Leg veins have to push blood upwards against gravity. One-way valves inside them stop blood from slipping back down. When those valves weaken, blood collects in the vein, which stretches, swells and twists. That is a varicose vein.

According to the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/varicose-veins/), the usual symptoms are:

- pain, aching or a heavy feeling in the legs
- swollen ankles or feet
- itching, dryness or scaly skin over the veins
- skin colour changes around the affected area
- symptoms that get worse after standing for a long time and ease when you rest with your legs raised

A 2016 Indian expert consensus on chronic venous disease (Dekiwadia and colleagues, *Phlebological Review*) adds tingling, burning, cramps, restless legs and tired legs.

## What causes varicose veins

The direct cause is faulty vein valves. The NHS says you are more likely to get varicose veins if you:

- are a woman
- are older
- are overweight
- are pregnant
- spend long periods standing or sitting
- have a family history of varicose veins
- have had a deep vein thrombosis (DVT) before

## Why standing jobs matter in India

Long hours on your feet keep pressure on the leg veins all day. Indian studies show how this plays out in real jobs:

| Study | Who was studied | What it found |
|---|---|---|
| *Clinical Epidemiology and Global Health*, 2021 | 150 police personnel in Belagavi, Karnataka, who stood for about 6.7 hours a day on average | 14.7% had varicose veins and another 20% were at risk; risk was linked to how long they stood |
| *Journal of Ecophysiology and Occupational Health*, 2024 (Chaya, Sharma and Vinayaka) | 100 security guards | About 15% had varicose veins |
| Prajapati and colleagues, 2015, cited in the 2016 Indian consensus | Traffic police in Ahmedabad | 2.91% had varicose veins |

These are small studies of specific groups, not a national figure, but they show varicose veins turn up regularly in jobs where people stand all shift, with rates from about 3% to 15% across these groups.

The Indian consensus also names nurses, teachers, traffic police, office workers, homemakers, and shop, hotel and factory workers as higher-risk groups, and notes that many can't simply change jobs. If that is you, these NHS self-care habits can ease symptoms:

- move around rather than standing still for long stretches
- when sitting, raise your feet where you can
- lie down with your legs up on cushions after work
- walk or exercise regularly to keep blood moving in the calves
- keep a healthy weight and don't smoke, as smoking damages veins
- moisturise dry, itchy skin and avoid knocks that could make a vein bleed

## Warning signs: when to see a doctor

Varicose veins don't always need treatment, but some problems do, and a few are emergencies.

| What you notice | What it may mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Aching, swelling or itching that bothers you, or veins getting worse | Symptomatic varicose veins | See a doctor (a general physician or surgeon) at a routine visit |
| A sore on the leg or ankle that hasn't healed in 2 weeks, or darkened, hard or scaly skin near the ankle | Venous skin damage or a venous leg ulcer | See a doctor soon |
| A varicose vein that is bleeding | Bleeding from a thin-walled vein | Lie down, raise the leg, press firmly on the spot, and get urgent medical help; call 112 if bleeding is heavy or won't stop with pressure |
| Throbbing pain and swelling in one calf or thigh, with red or darkened, warm skin | Possible deep vein thrombosis (DVT), a clot in a deep vein | Get urgent medical help the same day |
| A swollen, painful leg plus breathlessness or chest pain | Possible pulmonary embolism, a clot that has moved to the lungs | **Emergency:** call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department; don't drive yourself |

The NHS says a bleeding varicose vein needs urgent medical help. The clot advice follows the [NHS guidance on DVT](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/), which warns that chest pain or breathlessness with DVT symptoms can be life-threatening, and that redness is harder to see on brown skin.

## How doctors check and treat varicose veins

A doctor examines your legs while you stand. If treatment is considered, specialists usually confirm it with a **duplex ultrasound scan**, which shows which veins are leaking.

### What NICE recommends, and why stockings are no longer first choice

The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidance on varicose veins (CG168) recommends referral to a vascular service for people with symptomatic varicose veins (first-time or recurrent), skin changes thought to be caused by poor vein circulation, superficial vein thrombosis with suspected venous incompetence, or an active or healed venous leg ulcer.

For confirmed varicose veins where the main vein is leaking, NICE sets out an order of treatment:

1. **Endothermal ablation**, which uses laser or radiofrequency energy through a thin tube to heat and seal the faulty vein from inside. It is usually done under local anaesthetic.
2. **Ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy**, if ablation isn't suitable. A foam is injected to block the vein.
3. **Surgery** (tying off and removing the vein), if foam isn't suitable either.

NICE says compression hosiery should **not** be offered to treat varicose veins unless these procedures are unsuitable. Stockings can ease symptoms but don't fix the faulty valve. The NHS adds that compression stockings should not be used by people with poor arterial blood flow in the legs, so get a doctor's advice before buying them. During pregnancy, procedures are usually put off and stockings may be suggested instead.

Which option suits you depends on your scan, your other health conditions and the hospital. That decision belongs with a vascular surgeon or general surgeon who has examined you.

### Is varicose vein treatment covered in India?

Under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, the National Health Authority's Health Benefit Package 2.0 list includes a package called "Management of Varicose Veins" under general surgery. Package lists have been revised since, and what is offered varies by state and hospital. If you have a PM-JAY card, ask the Ayushman Mitra at an empanelled hospital whether your procedure is covered.

Treatment purely to improve how veins look is cosmetic. It is usually not covered by government schemes and may not be covered by health insurance either, so check your policy.

## Can you prevent varicose veins?

Not always: age, sex and genes can't be changed. The self-care habits above lower the strain on your veins, and they still matter after treatment, because varicose veins can come back. ## Frequently asked questions

### Are varicose veins dangerous?

Usually not. Most cause only discomfort or concern about how they look. They can lead to skin damage, leg ulcers, bleeding or, less often, blood clots, so new pain, sores or bleeding should be checked by a doctor.

### Do varicose veins go away on their own?

No. A damaged vein valve doesn't repair itself, although symptoms can ease with leg-raising, exercise and weight loss. Veins that appear during pregnancy often improve after delivery, according to the NHS.

### Which doctor should I see for varicose veins?

Start with a general physician or family doctor. If treatment is needed, you will usually be referred to a vascular surgeon or a general surgeon, who will arrange a duplex ultrasound scan.

### Are spider veins the same as varicose veins?

No. Spider (thread) veins are tiny damaged blood vessels near the skin's surface that form a web-like pattern and rarely bulge. Varicose veins are larger, swollen, twisted veins that often bulge and are more likely to cause aching or swelling.
