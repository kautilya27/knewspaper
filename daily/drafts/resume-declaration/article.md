**Quick answer:** A declaration in a resume is a one- or two-line statement at the end, such as "I hereby declare that the information given above is true to the best of my knowledge and belief", followed by place, date and your name or signature. It is expected on a biodata, on campus placement forms and in many government applications in India. On a private-sector resume sent to a company or uploaded to a job portal, it is optional and most recruiters don't look for it.

What gives the line weight is not the resume. It is the official forms you sign later. Government recruiters such as UPSC and the Railway Recruitment Boards print their own declarations with clear penalties for false information.

## When you need a declaration and when you don't

| Where you are sending it | Add a declaration? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Private company, job portal (Naukri, LinkedIn), startup | Optional, usually skip | Recruiters scan for skills and results. The line takes space and adds no information. |
| MNC or overseas job, ATS-based applications | Skip | Outside South Asia, a declaration looks unusual. It adds nothing for applicant tracking systems. |
| Campus placement form or college template | Add it if the template has it | Placement cells often ask for one, and you should follow the format you are given. |
| Biodata for a government office, school, hospital or PSU walk-in | Add it, with place, date and signature | Many offline applications are still printed and signed. |
| Online government exam form (UPSC, SSC, RRB, state PSC) | Not on your resume. The form has its own | You accept the declaration printed on the portal. A resume usually isn't asked for at all. |

If you are unsure, read the job advertisement. When an advertisement asks for a "bio-data in the prescribed format", use that format exactly, including its declaration. Our [biodata format guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/biodata-format) explains how a biodata differs from a resume.

## How to write it: placement and format

Put the declaration at the very end, after education, skills and any other sections. Keep it to one or two sentences.

The usual Indian layout looks like this:

> **Declaration**
> I hereby declare that the information furnished above is true, complete and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.

> Place: Pune
> Date: 23/11/2026
> (Ananya Kulkarni)

Some practical points:

- **Place and date** go on the left and your name goes on the right, in brackets, under the space for a signature. On a printed copy, sign above your name in blue ink.
- **Date format:** use DD/MM/YYYY or "23 November 2026". Update the date each time you send the resume. A declaration dated eight months ago tells the reader you haven't touched the file since.
- **Place** is the city you are applying from, not your native place.
- **Digital copies:** a scanned signature image is fine for email applications. Don't paste one into a resume you upload to a portal, where anyone who downloads the file can copy it.

## Sample declarations you can adapt

### For freshers and students

- "I hereby declare that all the details mentioned above are true to the best of my knowledge and belief."
- "I confirm that the information provided in this resume, including my academic record, is correct, and I will produce original certificates when asked."

The second version suits campus placements, where marks and CGPA are checked against your marksheets. For the rest of a first resume, see our [student resume guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/student-resume).

### For experienced professionals

- "I declare that the information above is accurate and complete, and that I can provide documents in support of my employment history."
- "I hereby declare that the particulars given above are true. I am currently serving a notice period of 60 days." (Mention the notice period only if it is accurate. The interviewer will ask about it anyway.)

### For government jobs and biodata

- "I hereby declare that all statements made in this application are true, complete and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief. I understand that if any information is found false or incorrect at any stage, my candidature or appointment is liable to be cancelled."
- For candidates already in government service: "I declare that I have informed my Head of Office/Department in writing about this application."

The second line is not a formality. UPSC advertisements specifically ask serving government employees to inform their Head of Office or Department in writing about applying.

## What the declaration means, legally and practically

A declaration on a private resume is not an affidavit. You don't swear it before a notary, so it adds no special legal force of its own. If you lie on a resume, a private employer can withdraw an offer or end your employment after background verification, whether or not you wrote the line. Leaving it out does not protect you, and adding it does not make an honest mistake a crime.

Government recruitment is different. There, the declarations you sign are written by the recruiter and spell out the consequences:

- **At the application stage:** UPSC's recruitment advertisements warn candidates that they "should not furnish any particulars that are false or suppress any material information", and that candidature is liable to be rejected if a claim is found false. A candidate found guilty of this kind of misconduct can be disqualified, debarred by the Commission permanently or for a set period, barred by the Central Government from employment under it, and made liable to criminal prosecution.
- **At the verification stage:** the Attestation Form used for central government jobs (for example, the one published by RRB Chennai) warns that furnishing false information or suppressing factual information would be a disqualification and is likely to make the candidate unfit for government employment. The candidate signs a certificate, with place and date, accepting that the authorities can terminate the appointment and that they are liable to criminal, civil or legal action.
- **A real example:** in July 2024, UPSC cancelled the provisional candidature of Puja Khedkar for the Civil Services Examination 2022 and permanently debarred her from its exams. According to the [UPSC statement published by PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2039580), she had taken more attempts than allowed by faking her identity. She has challenged the decision in the Delhi High Court.

Courts have also set limits on how strictly employers can apply these rules. In *Avtar Singh vs Union of India* (2016), a three-judge Supreme Court bench held that candidates must truthfully disclose convictions, arrests and pending criminal cases. The bench also ruled that employers must weigh the facts. A trivial matter that was declared truthfully need not cost someone the job, while deliberate concealment weighs heavily. A candidate can be held to have suppressed information only if the form specifically asked for it, though the employer may still weigh other relevant facts when judging fitness. A truthful declaration also does not oblige the employer to appoint. Confirmed employees are entitled to a departmental inquiry before removal.

The practical lesson: answer every question on a government form fully. If you have a past case, a gap or a pending result, disclose it in the space provided rather than leaving it out.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying a declaration that promises something false.** "I have never been involved in any legal proceedings" is risky if a matter is pending against you, even a minor one.
- **A stale date or the wrong city.** Recruiters notice "Place: Kanpur" on an application for a Bengaluru job you say you are already working in.
- **Using it to fill space.** On a one-page private-sector resume, a stronger achievement line is worth more. Our guide on [how to make a resume](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-make-a-resume) covers what to put there instead.
- **Two declarations.** If a college or government template already includes a declaration, don't add your own as well.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a declaration compulsory in a resume in India?

No. No law or rule requires it on a resume. It is expected on biodata and many campus or offline government applications, but private employers and job portals don't need it.

### Should freshers write a declaration in their resume?

Add one if your college placement template includes it or if you are applying offline with a printed biodata. For applications on portals and to private companies, it is optional. Use the space for projects or internships if the page is full.

### Does a resume declaration need a signature?

A printed or physically submitted resume or biodata is usually signed above your name, with place and date. For email or portal uploads, typing your name is enough.

### What happens if I give false information after signing a declaration?

For government jobs, the recruiter's own rules apply. These include rejection of candidature, debarment, termination of appointment and possible criminal prosecution, as stated in UPSC advertisements and the central government Attestation Form. Private employers can withdraw an offer or end employment if background checks show the information was false.
