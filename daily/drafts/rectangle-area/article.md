**Quick answer:** The area of a rectangle is **length × breadth** (A = l × b), written in square units such as cm², m² or sq ft. A room 12 ft long and 10 ft wide has an area of 12 × 10 = **120 sq ft**, which is about **11.15 sq m**.

That one formula covers most school sums. In real life, the hard part is usually something else: converting between square feet and square metres, working out how many tiles to buy, or finding a missing side when you only know the area. This guide covers all of that with fully worked examples.

## The formula and why it works

A rectangle has four right angles, and its opposite sides are equal. If you cover a 4 cm × 3 cm rectangle with 1 cm × 1 cm squares, you get 4 squares in each row and 3 rows, so 12 squares in all. Multiplying length by breadth is just a quick way of counting those unit squares.

| What you want | Formula |
|---|---|
| Area | A = l × b |
| Length, if area and breadth are known | l = A ÷ b |
| Breadth, if area and length are known | b = A ÷ l |
| Diagonal | d = √(l² + b²) |
| Perimeter (boundary, not area) | P = 2(l + b) |
| Area of a square (l = b) | A = side² |

Two rules make most mistakes go away:

- **Both sides must be in the same unit** before you multiply.
- **The answer is in square units.** Metres times metres gives square metres, not metres.

## Square feet vs square metres: the conversion Indians need most

Flats, shops and plots in India are usually advertised in square feet, while textbooks and many official documents use square metres. The conversion comes from the definition of the international foot, which is exactly 0.3048 m, according to the US [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/geodesy/international-foot.html).

Since area is length × length, you square the factor:

- 1 sq ft = 0.3048 × 0.3048 = **0.09290304 sq m** (exact)
- 1 sq m = 1 ÷ 0.09290304 ≈ **10.7639 sq ft**
- 1 sq yard (often called a gaj) = 3 ft × 3 ft = **9 sq ft**

| Area | Square feet | Square metres |
|---|---|---|
| Bedroom 12 ft × 10 ft | 120 | 11.15 |
| Living room 14 ft × 11 ft | 154 | 14.31 |
| Room 3.5 m × 3 m | 113.02 | 10.5 |
| 1 sq m | 10.7639 | 1 |

A common slip is to multiply by 3.28 (feet in a metre) instead of 10.76 when converting area. That would make a 10.5 sq m room look like 34 sq ft instead of 113 sq ft.

When a builder quotes a flat's size, also check which area they mean. Carpet area and built-up area are different numbers for the same flat; our explainer on [carpet area vs built-up area](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/carpet-area-vs-built-up-area) covers that.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Room area in sq ft and sq m

A bedroom is 12 ft long and 10 ft wide.

- Area = 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft
- In square metres: 120 × 0.09290304 = 11.148 sq m, about **11.15 sq m**

### Example 2: How many tiles for a floor?

The same 12 ft × 10 ft floor is to be covered with 2 ft × 2 ft tiles.

- Area of one tile = 2 × 2 = 4 sq ft
- Tiles needed = 120 ÷ 4 = **30 tiles**

This works neatly because 12 and 10 are both multiples of 2: you get 6 tiles along the length and 5 along the breadth, and 6 × 5 = 30.

Now try a room of 11 ft × 9 ft with the same tiles. Dividing areas gives 99 ÷ 4 = 24.75, which suggests 25 tiles. But tiles are laid in rows. Along 11 ft you need 6 tiles (5.5 rounded up), and along 9 ft you need 5 (4.5 rounded up), so a full grid uses **30 tile positions**, with the last row and column cut. That is a safe maximum: if the cut-off pieces are reused (a 2 ft tile cut in half gives two 1 × 2 ft strips), you need closer to 25. Counting along each side tells you the most you could need; dividing areas tells you the least. In practice, people also buy a few extra for breakage and cutting; how many depends on the tile and the layout, so ask the person laying them.

### Example 3: Finding the length from the area

A rectangular plot has an area of 360 sq m and a breadth of 15 m. Find its length.

- l = A ÷ b = 360 ÷ 15 = **24 m**

### Example 4: Finding both sides from area and perimeter

A rectangle has an area of 120 sq m and a perimeter of 46 m.

- Perimeter 46 means l + b = 23
- Area 120 means l × b = 120
- Which two numbers add to 23 and multiply to 120? **15 m and 8 m** (15 + 8 = 23, 15 × 8 = 120)

### Example 5: Area when the diagonal is given

A rectangle has a diagonal of 13 cm and one side of 5 cm.

- The diagonal and two sides form a right triangle, so other side = √(13² − 5²) = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12 cm
- Area = 12 × 5 = **60 cm²**

The diagonal on its own is not enough to find the area: many different rectangles share a 13 cm diagonal. You need one side or the ratio of the sides. A TV screen is a good example. A 55-inch TV is measured along the diagonal, and its 16:9 shape fixes the sides: width ≈ 47.94 in, height ≈ 26.96 in, so screen area ≈ **1,292.6 sq in**. (The shortcut: area = d² × 16 × 9 ÷ (16² + 9²) = 55² × 144 ÷ 337.)

### Example 6: Area of a path around a park (NCERT-type)

A park is 50 m long and 30 m wide. A path 2.5 m wide runs all around it, outside. Find the area of the path.

- The path adds 2.5 m on each side, so the outer rectangle is 50 + 5 = 55 m by 30 + 5 = 35 m
- Outer area = 55 × 35 = 1,925 sq m
- Park area = 50 × 30 = 1,500 sq m
- Path area = 1,925 − 1,500 = **425 sq m**

If paving costs ₹120 per sq m (an illustrative rate), the path would cost 425 × 120 = **₹51,000**.

If the same 2.5 m path ran *inside* the park, the inner rectangle would be 50 − 5 = 45 m by 30 − 5 = 25 m, or 1,125 sq m, and the path would be 1,500 − 1,125 = **375 sq m**. Students often forget to add or subtract the path width on *both* sides.

## Area vs perimeter: not the same thing

Area is the space inside; perimeter is the length of the boundary. Rectangles with equal areas can have very different perimeters, and the other way round:

| Rectangle | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| 6 m × 4 m | 24 sq m | 20 m |
| 12 m × 2 m | 24 sq m | 28 m |
| 5 m × 5 m | 25 sq m | 20 m |
| 9 m × 1 m | 9 sq m | 20 m |

For a fixed perimeter, the square encloses the most area. That is also why fencing is priced per running metre (perimeter) while flooring is priced per square metre (area).

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Mixing units.** A table top 2 m × 50 cm is not 100 of anything. Convert first: 2 m × 0.5 m = 1 sq m, or 200 cm × 50 cm = 10,000 sq cm.
2. **Converting area with a length factor.** 1 m = 100 cm, but 1 sq m = 100 × 100 = 10,000 sq cm.
3. **Writing the wrong unit.** An area answer in "m" instead of "sq m" usually loses marks.
4. **Confusing area with perimeter.** Read the question: tiles, carpet and paint need area; fencing, borders and skirting need perimeter.
5. **Assuming doubling the sides doubles the area.** Doubling both sides makes the area four times as big: a 4 × 3 rectangle (12) becomes 8 × 6 (48).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the formula for the area of a rectangle?

Area = length × breadth. Both sides must be in the same unit, and the answer is in square units such as sq cm, sq m or sq ft.

### How do I convert square feet to square metres?

Multiply square feet by 0.0929 (exactly 0.09290304) to get square metres; to go from square metres to square feet, multiply by 10.7639. For example, 120 sq ft is about 11.15 sq m.

### How do I find the length of a rectangle if I know its area?

Divide the area by the breadth. A plot of 360 sq m with a breadth of 15 m has a length of 360 ÷ 15 = 24 m.

### Can I find the area of a rectangle from its diagonal alone?

No. You also need one side or the ratio of the sides, because many rectangles share the same diagonal. With one side known, find the other using the Pythagoras theorem, then multiply.
