**Quick answer:** Makar Sankranti 2027 is on **Friday, 15 January 2027**. The Sun enters Makara (Capricorn) at **9:14 pm IST on Thursday, 14 January**, according to Drik Panchang for New Delhi. Because that moment comes after sunset, the holy bath, puja and daan move to the next morning.

For New Delhi, Drik Panchang's figures put the **punya kaal at 7:15 am to 5:46 pm** on 15 January (sunrise to sunset), with the **maha punya kaal from 7:15 am to about 9:00 am**. Lohri falls the evening before, on Thursday, 14 January 2027. The central government's 2027 list gives Makar Sankranti only as a **restricted holiday, dated 14 January**. More on that mismatch below.

**Where sources disagree:**

- **The exact minute.** Drik Panchang gives 9:14 pm. Wikipedia's table of sankranti times gives 9:09 pm IST. Panchangs use slightly different astronomical models, so a few minutes of difference is normal. Both times are after sunset, so the festival day is 15 January either way.
- **Punya kaal.** Astroyogi gives a longer window (7:10 am to 6:02 pm) without naming a city. Use the timings for your own city from your family panchang or Drik Panchang.
- **Lohri.** Drik Panchang puts Lohri on 14 January. Bhakti Bharat lists 13 January. Most families in Punjab light the Lohri fire on the eve of Sankranti, so 14 January fits the 15 January Sankranti.

## Why is Makar Sankranti sometimes on 14 January and sometimes on 15?

Most Hindu festivals follow the Moon, so their dates move by weeks every year. Makar Sankranti is one of the few that follows the Sun, which is why it nearly always lands on 14 or 15 January. Three things decide which of the two it is.

### 1. It marks the Sun entering sidereal Makara

Indian calendars measure the zodiac against the fixed stars (sidereal). Makar Sankranti is the moment the Sun's longitude reaches the start of Makara on that sidereal scale. This is not the winter solstice. The solstice, which astronomers call the start of the Sun's northward journey, falls around 21 or 22 December. The two dates drifted apart over the centuries, and according to Wikipedia's Makar Sankranti article, they last fell together around 291 CE.

### 2. The sankranti moment moves about 6 hours later each year

According to Wikipedia, the time difference between two consecutive Makar Sankrantis is about 6 hours 10 minutes beyond a calendar year. So the moment slides later each year, and the leap day pulls it back by a day every fourth year. You can see this in the recent times listed on Wikipedia:

| Year | Sun enters Makara (IST) | Festival day |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 14 Jan, about 8:54 am | 14 Jan |
| 2026 | 14 Jan, about 3:05 pm | 14 Jan |
| 2027 | 14 Jan, about 9:09 to 9:14 pm | 15 Jan |
| 2028 | 15 Jan, about 3:22 am | 15 Jan |

### 3. The sunrise rule

The rituals of Sankranti (snan, Surya puja, daan) are done in daylight. Drik Panchang explains the rule: if the sankranti moment falls after sunset, the punya kaal moves to the next day's sunrise. In 2027 the Sun enters Makara at night on 14 January, so 15 January becomes the festival day. In 2026 the moment fell in the afternoon, so 14 January was the day.

### The slow drift

The leap-day correction doesn't fully cancel the extra minutes each year, so the festival creeps later over the long run. That is why it now falls on 14 or 15 January and will keep moving later. Wikipedia notes that Makar Sankranti will fall on 16 January for the first time in 2102, because 2100 is not a leap year.

## Is Makar Sankranti a holiday in 2027?

**Central government offices:** not a gazetted holiday. The Department of Personnel and Training's holiday memo for 2027 (F. No. 12/2/2023-JCA, dated 16 July 2026) puts these in the **restricted holiday** list for central government offices in Delhi and New Delhi:

| Restricted holiday (DoPT, 2027) | Date | Day |
|---|---|---|
| Makar Sankranti / Magha Bihu | 14 January 2027 | Thursday |
| Pongal, Guru Gobind Singh's Birthday | 15 January 2027 | Friday |

A central government employee can take any two restricted holidays in a year. Makar Sankranti and Pongal are also among the 12 optional occasions. Coordination committees in state capitals outside Delhi pick three holidays from these 12 for central offices in their state. So in some cities, Sankranti or Pongal can be a full holiday for central offices. You can read the full memo on the [Department of Posts' copy of the DoPT order](https://utilities.cept.gov.in/dop/pdfbind.ashx?id=15435). For the gazetted holidays, see our [central government holiday list](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/central-government-holiday-list) explainer.

**The 14 vs 15 January catch:** the DoPT date (14 January) is one day earlier than the panchang festival day (15 January). Temples, home rituals and most melas will follow the panchang. If you are planning leave, apply for the date your office list shows.

**State holidays:** state governments set their own lists. Pongal, Uttarayan, Magh Bihu and Sankranti are usually public holidays in the states that celebrate them. Check your state government's 2027 notification for the exact date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Makar Sankranti 2027 on 14 or 15 January?

It is on Friday, 15 January 2027. The Sun enters Makara at 9:14 pm on 14 January, according to Drik Panchang, and the rituals move to the next sunrise because that moment is after sunset.

### What is the punya kaal on Makar Sankranti 2027?

For New Delhi, Drik Panchang gives the punya kaal as 7:15 am to 5:46 pm on 15 January, and the maha punya kaal as 7:15 am to about 9:00 am. The holy dip and daan are best done in the maha punya kaal.

### When is Lohri in 2027?

Drik Panchang lists Lohri on Thursday, 14 January 2027, the evening before Makar Sankranti. Some calendars show 13 January, so check with your local gurdwara or community.

### Is Makar Sankranti a government holiday in 2027?

For central government offices in Delhi, it is a restricted holiday on 14 January 2027, with Pongal on 15 January. State governments set their own holidays, and Pongal, Uttarayan and Magh Bihu are usually holidays in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Assam.
