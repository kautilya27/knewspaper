**Quick answer:** From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. For most salaried people the deductions are the same; only the section numbers moved. Section 80C is now section 123, 80D is section 126, home loan interest under 24(b) is section 22(1)(b), the standard deduction is in section 19, and the HRA exemption sits in Schedule III (serial number 11), read with section 11.

The real changes are fewer than the headlines suggest. "Previous year" and "assessment year" are replaced by a single "tax year", several sections were merged, Form 16 becomes Form 130, and the new Income-tax Rules, 2026 changed a few allowances. The full lookup table is below.

## What actually changed, and what only got a new number

According to the [CBDT's FAQs on the transition](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/81799/11848482/FAQs-on-Interplay-and-Transition.pdf/05f80c1a-073c-a5d7-fb6f-55509242be53?t=1774082865717) (released in March 2026), "The Income Tax Act, 2025 does not impose any new tax." The same FAQs say the new Act has 536 sections against 819 in the old one. Most of that shrinkage comes from merging and reorganising, not from removing benefits.

**Real changes you will notice:**

1. **Tax year replaces previous year and assessment year.** The CBDT FAQs define a tax year as "a period of twelve months contained in a financial year", and drop the assessment year concept. Income earned from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 is "tax year 2026-27", and the return is also for tax year 2026-27, not "AY 2027-28".
2. **Exemptions moved out of section 10.** The long list in old section 10 now sits in Schedules II to VII, read with section 11. Salary exemptions such as gratuity, leave encashment and commuted pension moved into a table in section 19.
3. **Mergers.** Sections 80C, 80CCC and 80CCE are now one section (123). Savings interest deductions 80TTA and 80TTB are both in section 153. Most TDS sections are consolidated into section 392 (salary) and section 393 (other payments).
4. **New form numbers.** The salary TDS certificate is Form 130 for tax year 2026-27 onwards (CBDT FAQs). The investment proof declaration you give your employer, earlier Form 12BB, is now Form 124 (ClearTax, Zoho Payroll).
5. **Some allowance rules changed** through the Income-tax Rules, 2026, which are separate from the Act. More on these below.

**What did not change:** the new regime slabs, the ₹60,000 rebate up to ₹12 lakh income, the ₹1.5 lakh limit for 80C-type investments, the 80D limits, the ₹2 lakh cap on interest for a self-occupied home, and the ₹75,000 standard deduction in the new regime. Each of these appears in the new Act with the same amounts, according to the section texts on the Income Tax Department's website.

## The "tax year" change, explained with dates

The confusing part for most people this year is which Act applies to which return. The CBDT FAQs answer this directly:

- **Income earned in FY 2025-26** (April 2025 to March 2026) is governed by the 1961 Act. You file it as AY 2026-27 under the old section numbers, even though you are filing in 2026. Your employer gives you Form 16 for this year.
- **Income earned from 1 April 2026** falls under the 2025 Act as tax year 2026-27. Your salary TDS for this year is under section 392, and the TDS certificate will be Form 130, due by 15 June 2027 according to the FAQs.

So if you are filing a revised or belated return for last year, or replying to a notice about an older year, the old section numbers still apply. The saving clause in section 536 keeps pending cases, PANs, past filings and losses carried forward valid under the new law. If you're unsure which return form applies, see our guide on [how to file ITR online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-file-itr-online).

## Changes that came from the new rules, not the Act

Some real changes for salaried employees came through the Income-tax Rules, 2026, which took effect along with the Act on 1 April 2026:

- **HRA:** the higher 50% of salary limit now applies in eight cities: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai as before, plus Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad (according to BDO India and Moneylife). Other places stay at 40%. Upstox reports that this sits in Rule 279.
- **Children's education allowance:** up from ₹100 to ₹3,000 a month per child, for up to two children. Hostel allowance rose from ₹300 to ₹9,000 a month per child (BDO India). Upstox notes these apply only under the old regime.

HRA and these allowances still matter only if you stay in the old regime, because section 202 keeps most exemptions out of the new regime. Our explainer on the [old vs new tax regime](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/old-vs-new-tax-regime) shows how that choice works.

## How to use this when you read your payslip or Form 130

Your HR or payroll software will now quote new numbers. A proof request "under section 123" is the same as your old 80C proofs. "Deduction under section 19" is your standard deduction. An HRA line referring to "Schedule III" is the old 10(13A) exemption. The amounts don't change because the label did.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is section 80C still available under the new Income-tax Act?

Yes. It is now section 123, read with Schedule XV, with the same ₹1.5 lakh limit, according to the section text on the Income Tax Department's website. Sections 80CCC and 80CCE were merged into it.

### Which Act applies to my ITR for FY 2025-26?

The 1961 Act. According to the CBDT's transition FAQs, income earned in FY 2025-26 is assessed as AY 2026-27 under the old law, so the old section numbers apply to that return.

### Where is HRA exemption in the Income-tax Act, 2025?

It is in Schedule III (serial number 11), read with section 11, which covers allowances given to meet rent you actually pay for a home you don't own. The calculation limits are in the Income-tax Rules, 2026.

### Did tax rates or deduction limits change because of the new Act?

No. The CBDT says the new Act imposes no new tax, and the slabs, rebate and main deduction limits are carried over unchanged. Changes to rates or limits still come through the yearly Finance Act.
