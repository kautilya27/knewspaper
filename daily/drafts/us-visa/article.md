**Quick answer:** To apply for a US visa from India, choose your visa type (B1/B2 for tourism or business), fill the DS-160 form online, create a profile on the official appointment system linked from the US Embassy India website, pay the $185 fee, book a biometrics slot at a Visa Application Centre and an interview slot, attend both, and track your passport until it returns with the visa.

Several rules changed in 2025 and 2026, and many guides online still describe the old ones. Almost everyone now needs an in-person interview, you should apply in your country of nationality or residence, and you get only one free reschedule. The fees and rules below were checked against US State Department pages on 26 November 2026.

## Step 1: Choose the right visa

Most Indians going on a holiday, visiting family or attending business meetings need the **B1/B2 visitor visa**. The State Department's reciprocity table for India says a B1/B2 visa is usually issued for up to 120 months (10 years), for multiple entries, with no extra issuance fee. An officer can still give a shorter visa.

| Visa | Who it is for | Application fee | Extra steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1/B2 | Tourism, family visits, business meetings | $185 | None |
| F-1 | Full-time students | $185 | I-20 from the university, $350 SEVIS fee |
| H-1B | Specialty-occupation workers | $205 | Approved I-797 petition from the employer |

The State Department set these fees in June 2023. The fee is non-refundable, even if your visa is refused. Students pay the SEVIS fee separately on fmjfee.com, according to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

## Step 2: Fill the DS-160

The DS-160 is filled on the State Department's Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC). According to its DS-160 FAQ:

- Answer **in English only**. The one exception is your name in your native script.
- Note your **Application ID**. Your answers are saved each time you click "Next", and you have 30 days to finish a half-filled form.
- Upload a photo that meets the photo rules.
- Someone can help you fill the form, but **you must click "Sign Application" yourself**. Parents can sign for children under 16.
- Print the **confirmation page with the barcode**. You will need it at every stage.

Your answers must match your passport and documents. The State Department warns that false statements can lead to a permanent refusal.

## Step 3: Create your profile and pay the fee

Start from the visa section of the US Embassy and Consulates in India (in.usembassy.gov) and follow its link to the appointment system. The State Department's India pages currently point to **USVisaScheduling.com** for consulate appointments and to **ustraveldocs.com/in** for Visa Application Centre details. Always open these links from the embassy page, because lookalike sites exist.

In your profile, enter your passport details and DS-160 number, then pay the fee shown in the system. Never pay through an agent's personal account.

**What about the $250 "visa integrity fee"?** A US law signed on 4 July 2025 created it, and many websites already add it to their cost totals. But as of 26 November 2026, the State Department's official fee page does not list it. Some reports say a few posts collect it at visa issuance, after approval; if you are asked for it, follow the instructions from the embassy or consulate.

## Step 4: Book the two appointments

First-time applicants usually book two slots:

1. **Biometrics at a Visa Application Centre (VAC)**, still widely called the OFC. Your photo and fingerprints are taken here. This slot comes before the interview date.
2. **The interview** at the US Embassy in New Delhi or at a consulate in Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad or Kolkata.

**Rescheduling:** Since 1 January 2025, the US Embassy in India has allowed **one free reschedule**, according to the embassy's announcement of December 2024. If you reschedule a second time or miss the appointment, you must pay the fee again and book afresh.

### How long is the wait?

Wait times change every week, so check the State Department's [Visa Appointment Wait Times tool](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/wait-times.html) for your city and visa type before you book flights. The State Department says the figure is only an estimate based on workload and staffing. It does not guarantee a slot, and it leaves out the processing after the interview and the time to return your passport. New slots open regularly, so check back for an earlier date.

You can request an emergency appointment for a genuine urgent need after booking the earliest regular slot. Weddings, graduations, conferences and last-minute holidays do not qualify.

## Step 5: Attend the biometrics and the interview

### Documents checklist

- **Passport**, generally valid for at least six months beyond your stay, plus any old passports
- **DS-160 confirmation page** and **appointment confirmation**
- **A printed photo**, if the DS-160 photo upload failed
- **Ties to India:** employment letter with leave approval, salary slips, income tax returns (ITRs), business or property papers
- **Funds:** recent bank statements
- **Trip details:** an invitation from your host, a conference registration or an itinerary
- **Spouses:** a marriage certificate
- **F-1:** I-20, SEVIS fee receipt, admission letter, mark sheets and proof of funding
- **H-1B:** I-797 approval notice, an employer letter and recent pay slips

For spouses, the State Department's India page lists certificates from the Registrar of Marriages or a religious authority. It says a "marriage agreement" or "deed of marriage" does not prove a marriage. Our guide to [marriage registration](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/marriage-registration) explains how to get a registered certificate.

Under section 214(b) of US immigration law, the officer starts by assuming that a visitor intends to immigrate. Your answers should therefore show what brings you back to India: your job, family, studies or business. Keep them short and truthful, and make sure they match your DS-160.

**Students and H-1B applicants:** the State Department asks F, M and J applicants (since June 2025) and H-1B and H-4 applicants (since 15 December 2025) to set all their social media profiles to "public".

## Step 6: Track your passport

If your visa is approved, the officer usually keeps your passport. Follow its status in your appointment account. You will be told by SMS or email when it is ready for doorstep delivery or for pick-up at the point you chose.

A **221(g)** slip means the officer needs more documents or administrative processing. Submit anything asked for through the official channel. The State Department advises waiting 180 days before you ask about the status, unless it is an emergency.

When the visa arrives, check that your name and date of birth are correct. Before you travel, compare the foreign-currency markups in our [best credit cards in India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/best-credit-cards-in-india) guide.

## Interview waiver (dropbox): who still qualifies

The State Department's [interview waiver update](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/interview-waiver-update-sept-18-2025.html), effective 1 October 2025, says nearly all applicants need an in-person interview, **including children under 14 and adults over 79**. An Indian visitor can use the dropbox only if all of these apply:

- You are renewing a **B1, B2 or B1/B2** visa **within 12 months** of its expiry
- The earlier visa was issued for full validity, and you were at least 18 when you got it
- You are applying in your country of nationality or residence
- You have never been refused a visa, unless the refusal was overcome or waived
- There is no apparent ineligibility

The officer can still call you for an interview. F-1 and H-1B renewals are no longer on the waiver list.

Since 6 September 2025, the State Department has asked nonimmigrant visa applicants to book their interview in their country of nationality or residence. Applying while you are visiting a third country, such as the UAE, can make it harder to qualify, and fees paid there are not refunded or transferred.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the US visa fee for Indians?

According to the State Department, the fee is $185 for a B1/B2 or F-1 visa and $205 for an H-1B. Students also pay a $350 SEVIS fee. Indians pay no issuance fee on a B1/B2 visa.

### Can I get a US tourist visa without an interview?

Only if you are renewing a B1/B2 visa within 12 months of its expiry and you meet the other conditions: full validity, age 18 or older when it was issued, and no refusals. Even then, the officer may still call you for an interview.

### Is the US visa fee refunded if my visa is rejected?

No. The fee is non-refundable. You also pay it again if you miss your appointment or reschedule more than once.

### Do I need a visa agent?

No. Every step is online, on the State Department's CEAC and the official system linked from in.usembassy.gov. No agent can guarantee approval, because only the consular officer decides.
