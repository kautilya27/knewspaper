# Notes: high-fibre-foods

## Sources for key facts (all checked 19 Nov 2026)
- Fibre per 100 g (total / insoluble / soluble), all foods in the table: NIN, Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, Table 1 (Proximate Principles and Dietary Fiber), https://nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf. I re-checked the raw text rows for atta (A019), ragi (A010), bajra (A003), jowar (A005), Bengal gram whole (B002), rajmah red (B020), green gram whole (B011), red gram dal (B021), guava (E028), gooseberry/amla (E021), methi (C020), bhindi (D056), linseeds (H014), almond (H001), ground nut (H012). Rice, brown rice, chana dal, moong dal, masoor dal, soybean brown, apple, banana, green peas, carrot and spinach came from one extraction pass of the same table. Values are mean, rounded to 1 decimal.
- ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, https://www.nin.res.in/dietaryguidelines/pdfjs/locale/DGI24thJune2024fin.pdf: at least 50% of cereals whole grains (Guideline 1); fibre slows carbohydrate/fat absorption, raises satiety, reduces glucose/lipids (p. 8); Table 1.3 fibre by food group (cereals 6, millets 7, pulses 12, nuts 9, vegetables/fruits 2, animal foods/oils 0 g per 100 g); My Plate 2000 kcal (250 g cereals, 85 g pulses, 400 g veg, 100 g fruit, 35 g nuts/seeds); fibre reduces constipation and piles risk and protects against CHD, diabetes, obesity (Guideline 6); about 8 glasses / 2 litres of water a day (Guideline 14); juice removes fibre-rich pulp (Guideline 14); processing depletes fibre (Guideline 15); fibre not among mandatory label nutrients (Guideline 17, Table 17.1). The 2024 guidelines give no general g/day fibre target (only ~25 g/1000 kcal in pregnancy, not used in the article).
- "About 38 g" from My Plate: my own calculation (250x6 + 85x12 + 400x2 + 100x2 + 35x9, /100 = 38.2 g), labelled in the text as our calculation.
- NIN What India Eats (2023): urban adults ~94 g vegetables/day vs 400 g advised. https://www.nin.res.in/downloads/What_India_Eats Report_13th_April_23.pdf
- WHO adults at least 25 g naturally occurring fibre/day: https://www.who.int/news/item//17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates
- NHS 30 g/day and lower risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, bowel cancer: https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/digestive-health/how-to-get-more-fibre-into-your-diet/
- Increase gradually, one change at a time; sudden increase causes wind, bloating: Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust leaflet, https://www.worcsacute.nhs.uk/leaflets/high-fibre-diet/
- Soluble vs insoluble functions: Cleveland Clinic, https://health.clevelandclinic.org/whats-the-difference-between-soluble-and-insoluble-fiber
- IBS fibre advice (cut wholegrains/nuts/seeds for diarrhoea; more soluble fibre for constipation; see GP): https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/irritable-bowel-syndrome-ibs/diet-lifestyle-and-medicines/
- Strictures: Hull University Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, https://www.hey.nhs.uk/patient-leaflet/dietary-advice-for-strictures-and-adhesions/
- CKD: Guy's and St Thomas' NHS FT, https://guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/diet-and-chronic-kidney-disease
- Red-flag bowel symptoms and urgency: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bowel-cancer/symptoms/

## Sample day arithmetic (IFCT raw values)
40 g whole moong x 17.04 = 6.82; 60 g atta x 11.36 = 6.82; 30 g toor dal x 9.06 = 2.72; 100 g bhindi x 4.08 = 4.08; 100 g guava x 8.59 = 8.59; 60 g white rice x 2.81 = 1.69; 30 g red rajma x 16.57 = 4.97. Total 35.7 g, "about 36".

## Information gain vs top results
Top results (Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health, Healthline, HelpGuide, MSKCC) are US-centric: US cups/ounces, US daily targets, foods like split peas, raspberries, bran flakes. None gives Indian foods with measured Indian values. This draft adds: NIN IFCT 2017 values (total, soluble, insoluble) for 26 Indian foods; whole vs split dal comparison; ICMR-NIN 2024 guidance and My Plate; an Indian sample day worked to ~36 g; WHO/NHS targets side by side; who should check with a doctor (IBS, strictures, CKD) and red-flag bowel symptoms.
