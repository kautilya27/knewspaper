**Quick answer:** HIV is a virus that slowly weakens the immune system. Some people get a short flu-like illness 2 to 6 weeks after infection, then have no symptoms for years. Without treatment, HIV can lead to AIDS, its most advanced stage. The only way to know is a blood test, which is free at government testing centres (ICTCs) across India.

HIV spreads through blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk, mainly through sex without a condom, shared needles and from mother to baby. It does not spread by touch, hugs, shared food or toilets. Treatment, called ART, is free at government ART centres and lets people living with HIV lead long, healthy lives.

## HIV in India: the latest numbers

According to the government's India HIV Estimation 2025 report, released on World AIDS Day (1 December) 2025, about **25.61 lakh people** were living with HIV in India in 2024. National prevalence is low, though: **0.20%** in 2024, against a global average of 0.7%, according to a Press Information Bureau (PIB) backgrounder for World AIDS Day 2025. It gives these trends:

| India | 2010 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| New HIV infections in a year | 1.25 lakh | 64,500 (about 49% fewer) |
| AIDS-related deaths in a year | 1.73 lakh | 32,200 (about 81% fewer) |
| HIV prevalence | 0.33% | 0.20% |

PIB says more than 18 lakh people living with HIV were receiving free ART by 2025.

## Symptoms of HIV, stage by stage

No symptom is unique to HIV. The signs below are common to many illnesses, so they can't tell you whether you have it. Only a test can.

### Stage 1: Acute HIV (first few weeks)

According to the NHS, some people get a short flu-like illness about **2 to 6 weeks** after infection, with:

- fever and sore throat
- swollen glands in the neck, armpits or groin
- muscle aches and tiredness
- a skin rash

WHO notes that some people have no symptoms at this stage. The virus multiplies fast, so it can pass on easily even when the person feels fine.

### Stage 2: Chronic HIV (years without symptoms)

HIV then works quietly. The US National Institutes of Health (NIH) says this stage usually lasts **10 years or more** without treatment, though it can be faster. The person may feel completely healthy but can still pass HIV on. As the immune system weakens, WHO lists signs such as swollen lymph nodes, weight loss, fever, diarrhoea and cough.

### Stage 3: AIDS (advanced HIV)

WHO defines advanced HIV disease in adults as a CD4 count (a type of immune cell) below 200 cells/mm³, or the presence of certain serious illnesses. The body struggles to fight infections it would normally control, such as tuberculosis (TB), some pneumonias and certain cancers. Without treatment, NIH says people with AIDS typically survive about 3 years. Treatment still helps at this stage.

## HIV vs AIDS: what's the difference?

| | HIV | AIDS |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The virus (human immunodeficiency virus) | The late stage of untreated HIV infection |
| Does everyone with HIV get it? | – | No. With treatment, most people never develop AIDS (NHS) |
| How it's found | HIV blood test | Doctor's assessment, CD4 count and illnesses present |

## How HIV spreads, and how it doesn't

**HIV can pass on through:**

- vaginal or anal sex without a condom (oral sex carries a much lower risk)
- sharing needles, syringes or other injecting equipment
- mother to baby during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding, if the mother isn't on treatment
- unscreened blood

**HIV does not spread through** hugging, shaking hands, kissing, sharing food, water, cups or clothes, toilets, swimming pools, mosquito bites, coughs or sneezes. WHO and the NHS say everyday contact such as hugging, kissing and sharing cups doesn't spread HIV.

## U=U: undetectable means untransmittable

When a person living with HIV takes ART regularly, the virus in the blood (viral load) can fall so low that tests can't detect it. The [WHO](https://who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hiv-aids) says people on ART with an undetectable viral load **will not transmit HIV to their sexual partners**. The NHS says it usually takes about 6 months of treatment to get there.

That's why testing early and staying on treatment protects both you and your partner. India's national programme offers free viral load testing to people on ART, according to a 2023 Lok Sabha reply from the health ministry.

## Getting an HIV test in India

### Where to go

Go to an **Integrated Counselling and Testing Centre (ICTC)**. NACO says these are mostly in government hospitals, usually in the general OPD or the obstetrics and gynaecology department. The health ministry says the programme's prevention, detection and treatment services are **free**. You get counselling before and after the test.

Under the HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017, no one can test you without your **informed consent** (Section 5), with narrow exceptions, and your result cannot be shared without your written consent, except in limited cases such as a court order (Section 8).

Testing in pregnancy matters too: treatment greatly lowers the chance of passing HIV to the baby, and PIB says a dual HIV and syphilis test kit is being rolled out for antenatal screening. If you've noticed the [early signs of pregnancy](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/early-symptoms-of-pregnancy), ask about it at your first check-up.

To find the nearest ICTC or ART centre, call the **National AIDS Helpline 1097**: toll-free, 24x7, from any mobile or landline, according to [NACO](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/living-with-hiv-aids/hotlines-helplines/).

### The window period

A test done too soon can miss the infection. WHO says most people develop detectable antibodies **within 28 days**. During this window period, many rapid tests show negative even though the person has HIV and can pass it on. If you test negative soon after a possible exposure, WHO advises testing again after 28 days; the ICTC counsellor will tell you when.

### When to get tested

Get tested if you had sex without a condom with a partner whose status you don't know, have a partner living with HIV, have shared needles, have been diagnosed with TB or a sexually transmitted infection, are pregnant, or had a needle-stick injury.

Don't read too much into a single symptom. A burning feeling while passing urine, for example, is far more often a [urinary tract infection](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/uti-symptoms-causes). If you're worried, a test settles it.

## PEP: emergency protection within 72 hours

If you think you were exposed to HIV in the last few days (a condom burst, sexual assault, a needle-stick injury), go to a government hospital or a doctor **immediately**. Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is a short course of HIV medicines that can stop the virus taking hold. According to the NHS, it must be started **within 72 hours** of exposure, so don't wait for symptoms. A doctor decides whether you need it.

## Treatment: ART is free and lifelong

There is no cure yet, but antiretroviral therapy (ART) keeps the virus under control. The health ministry told the Lok Sabha in 2023 that people living with HIV get free, lifelong treatment at more than 700 ART centres, and under the "test and treat" policy everyone diagnosed is offered ART, according to PIB.

With treatment, the NHS says most people with HIV can live a long and healthy life. Good food, sleep and exercise, and other [science-backed ways to support immunity](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/science-backed-ways-boost-immunity), help general health but never replace ART.

## The law protects people living with HIV

The HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017, in force since 10 September 2018, bans discrimination because of HIV status (Section 3) in:

- jobs (hiring, firing, unfair treatment)
- schools and colleges
- health care
- renting or buying a home
- public places, public or private office, and insurance (unless based on actuarial data)

Nobody can make an HIV test a condition for a job, admission or health care. Spreading hatred against people living with HIV can bring up to 2 years in prison and a fine of up to ₹1 lakh (Section 37). Complaints go to the Complaints Officer at the institution or the State Ombudsman; PIB says Ombudsmen have been appointed in 34 states and UTs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you tell if someone has HIV by looking at them?

No. Most people in the chronic stage look and feel healthy. The only way to know your status is an HIV test.

### Is HIV testing free in India?

Yes. Counselling and testing are free at government Integrated Counselling and Testing Centres (ICTCs), and the result is confidential. Call 1097 to find the nearest centre.

### Can a person living with HIV marry and have children?

Yes. On regular ART with an undetectable viral load, they will not pass HIV to a sexual partner, according to WHO, and treatment in pregnancy greatly lowers the risk to the baby. A doctor at the ART centre can guide couples planning a family.

### How long after exposure should I get tested?

Most people develop detectable antibodies within 28 days, according to WHO, so test again after that if an early test is negative. If the exposure was within the last 72 hours, ask a doctor about PEP straight away.
