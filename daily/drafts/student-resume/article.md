**Quick answer:** A student resume replaces work history with evidence: academic and personal projects, internships (even short or virtual ones), positions in clubs, fests, NSS or NCC, hackathons, and certifications such as NPTEL courses. Put education and projects at the top, write each item as what you did and what came of it, and keep it to one page. For campus placements, use your college's template and add only what you can prove with a certificate.

The basic layout, file format, personal details and how to show CGPA are the same for everyone, and our guide on [how to make a resume](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-make-a-resume) covers them. This article is only about the student's problem: an empty "Experience" section, and how to fill a page honestly.

## What counts as experience when you've never had a job

Recruiters hiring freshers want proof that you can finish something, work in a team and use what you studied. Most students have that proof; it's just scattered across college life.

| What you did in college | Where it goes on the resume | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Final-year or mini project | Projects | Problem, your part in the team, tools, and the result |
| Internship (in-person, virtual or a short summer one) | Internships | Organisation, dates, 2–3 points on what you produced |
| NSS or NCC | Positions of responsibility or Achievements | Certificate level, role, one concrete activity |
| College fest, club, student council | Positions of responsibility | Your role, team size, what you organised or raised |
| Hackathon or case competition | Achievements (or Projects, if you built something) | Name of the event, your result, what you built |
| NPTEL / SWAYAM course | Certifications | Course name, offering institute, score or category |
| Sports or cultural events at university level or above | Achievements | Level reached (zonal, state, national) |

An activity with a role and an outcome beats a bare participation entry, and every line is a possible interview question, so list only what you can talk about for two minutes.

## How to write each item so it doesn't look thin

### Projects

For B.Tech, BCA and B.Sc students, projects are the heart of the resume. Give each a title and two bullets (what it does, what you did), name the tools, and link a GitHub repository or demo if you have one. Commerce and arts students have projects too: a dissertation, a field survey, a ratio analysis of a listed company or a social media campaign for the fest.

### Internships

Short or unpaid internships count. Write what you produced, not what the company does. "Prepared 40 GST invoices a week in TallyPrime" (an illustrative example) says more than "Learnt about accounting operations." If you don't have one yet, our guide on [how to get an internship](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-get-an-internship) explains where students in India find them.

### NSS and NCC

Write the certificate or level you reached and one thing you did, such as "Organised a blood donation camp with 20 volunteers." NCC's B and C certificates carry weight in some government and defence recruitment. Jamia Millia Islamia's NCC incentives page, for example, notes that Central and State Governments give preference to cadets with B or C certificates. In defence entry, the UPSC CDS (II) 2026 notification reserves 13 of the 100 Indian Military Academy seats for NCC 'C' certificate (Army Wing) holders. On a private-sector resume, one line is enough.

### Fests, clubs and student bodies

"Member, Cultural Committee" says little. "Sponsorship lead, annual fest: approached 30 local businesses and raised ₹1.2 lakh" (illustrative figures; use your real ones) shows sales skill and handling money. Our list of [resume action verbs](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/resume-action-verbs) helps with the opening words.

### Hackathons

Name the event and your honest result: winner, finalist, or "built a working prototype in 36 hours". The Smart India Hackathon is a national event run by the Ministry of Education's Innovation Cell with AICTE. All India Radio reported that the grand finale of its 2025 edition began on 8 December 2025 at centres across the country. Reaching the grand finale is worth a line on its own.

### NPTEL and SWAYAM courses

NPTEL courses, run by the IITs and IISc and offered on SWAYAM, are widely recognised, because the final exam is proctored. Under the scheme NPTEL describes in its course announcements, the final score is 25% assignments and 75% proctored exam, and the certificate category depends on that score:

| Final score | What the certificate shows |
|---|---|
| Below 40% | No certificate |
| 40–59% | Successfully completed |
| 60–74% | Elite |
| 75–89% | Elite + Silver |
| 90% and above | Elite + Gold |

NPTEL also requires a minimum in both parts (10/25 in assignments and 30/75 in the exam, per its announcements), and each certificate can be verified online by its ID. Check your course page, as rules can change.

So write it as: "Programming in Java (NPTEL, IIT Kharagpur), Elite + Silver, 12 weeks". The category says more than the course name.

## Campus placement resumes: how they differ

If you're sitting for placements or internships through your college's training and placement cell, the cell's rules decide the format, not any website.

- **Fixed format.** Many colleges make every student use the same template. IIT (BHU)'s placement policy for students states that all resumes follow a fixed format and are filled in through the placement portal.
- **Proof for every line.** IIT Madras's students' guide for placements (2023–24) says any point without a proof or certificate will not be permitted, and students can't apply to companies until their resume is approved. IIT (BHU)'s policy says unsupported entries are deleted and that wrong or misleading information can get a student debarred for the whole session.
- **More than one version.** IIT Madras lets students upload several resumes for different roles plus a master resume with every point. A master copy is a good habit anywhere: you trim it for each company instead of rewriting.
- **Marks and backlogs are checked separately.** Company drive notices often set cut-offs on Class 10, Class 12 and degree marks, and on backlogs. These are filled in on the portal or the application form from your official marksheets, so the resume can't hide them. Don't drop Class 12 marks from a resume just because they're weak.

Start collecting NSS and NCC certificates, internship letters, fest appointment letters and NPTEL e-certificates now, before verification deadlines arrive.

## Worked example: a B.Com student, before and after

Priya (a made-up example) is in the final year of B.Com at a Pune college and is applying for an accounts trainee role.

**Before**

- Career objective: "To work in a reputed organisation where I can use my skills and grow."
- Education: B.Com, HSC, SSC
- Skills: MS Office, hardworking, good communication, team player
- Extra-curricular: Participated in NSS, member of commerce association, attended GST seminar
- Hobbies: reading, listening to music, travelling
- Declaration and signature

**After**

1. **Summary:** Final-year B.Com student (Accounting and Finance) with a 2-month internship at a CA firm, skilled in TallyPrime, GST invoicing and Excel; looking for an accounts trainee role in Pune.
2. **Education:** B.Com with CGPA and scale, then HSC and SSC percentages with board and year.
3. **Internship:** Accounts intern at a CA firm, 2 months: prepared GST invoices and reconciled purchase registers for 6 small clients; helped file monthly returns under supervision.
4. **Projects:** Ratio analysis of two listed FMCG companies over five years (college project, Excel); the findings were presented in class.
5. **Certifications:** One NPTEL or SWAYAM course in accounting or finance, with its category.
6. **Positions of responsibility:** NSS volunteer, 2 years: coordinated a financial literacy session for women's self-help groups in a nearby village.
7. **Skills:** TallyPrime, GST returns, Excel (VLOOKUP, pivot tables), English, Hindi, Marathi.

What changed: the empty phrases became checkable facts, the seminar she only attended disappeared, and NSS moved from one word to one result. Hobbies went because there was no room and they didn't relate to the job (if you do include them, keep to ones you can talk about in an interview).

**A B.Tech student** would use the same order with a bigger Projects section (two or three, with GitHub links), a Technical skills block, and hackathon results under Achievements.

## What to leave out of a student resume

- **School prizes and activities** once you're in the third or final year of college, unless they're national-level.
- **Participation certificates** for webinars, workshops and seminars you only attended.
- **Course lists** copied from the syllabus. Recruiters know what a B.Com or B.Tech covers.
- **Skill ratings** like stars or "Java: 80%". They mean nothing and invite awkward questions.
- **Soft-skill labels** ("team player", "hardworking") with no example behind them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make a resume as a first-year student?

Yes. It will be short: education, one or two projects or activities, and skills. Start one in first year and add to it each semester, so you aren't trying to remember details in final year.

### Should I mention backlogs on my resume?

A resume usually shows your CGPA, not a backlog count. If a company or your placement cell asks about backlogs on the application form, answer truthfully, because they're checked against your marksheets.

### Are free online certificates worth adding?

Add only those related to the job and, ideally, backed by an exam, like NPTEL courses. Three relevant certificates look better than fifteen unrelated ones.

### Should I use the college template or my own design?

For on-campus placements, use the college template if one is given. For off-campus and internship applications, a plain one-column, one-page resume works best.
