**Quick answer:** If you resign, are dismissed or are retrenched, your unpaid wages must reach you within two working days. That is Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, 2019, in force since 21 November 2025. Gratuity has a separate 30-day deadline, and your PF goes through EPFO, which has to settle a complete claim within 20 days.

So "full and final settlement" is really several payments on different clocks. A company policy that says "F&F in 45 days" does not override the law's two working days for wages.

## Which part of your F&F is due when

| Item | Law | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Salary for days worked, and allowances that count as wages | Code on Wages, Section 17(2) | Within 2 working days |
| Leave encashment | OSH Code, Section 32(1)(vi), for "workers"; otherwise your leave policy | 2 working days for workers; otherwise debated |
| Deductions (PF, professional tax, TDS, advances) | Code on Wages, Section 18 | Taken from the same payment |
| Gratuity | Code on Social Security, Section 56(3) | Within 30 days of becoming payable |
| Provident fund | EPF Scheme, 2026, paragraph 54(7) | 20 days after EPFO receives a complete claim |
| Reimbursements, non-statutory bonus, incentives | Your contract and policy | As the policy says |
| TDS certificate (Form 130, earlier Form 16) | Income-tax Act, 2025 | By 15 June after the tax year |

The Code's text, on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15793/1/aA2019-29.pdf), says wages "shall be paid within two working days of his removal, dismissal, retrenchment or, as the case may be, his resignation". The [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2192463&reg=48&lang=2) confirmed that all four labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025. The appropriate government can set a different limit under Section 17(3).

### When the two days start

The Code ties the deadline to the resignation, but a resignation takes effect on your last working day, not the day you send the email. An analysis on LiveLaw reads it that way. If your last day is Friday 13 November 2026 and your office works Monday to Friday, wages are due by Tuesday 17 November.

### Is leave encashment inside the two days?

For "workers" (broadly, non-managerial staff), yes. The OSH Code says a worker who is discharged, dismissed or quits must be paid for accrued leave "before the expiry of the second working day". For managers and others outside that definition, it depends on whether leave encashment counts as "wages" under the Code on Wages, which LiveLaw's analysis expects to be contested. The Labour Ministry's FAQs say it is not part of the allowances in that definition.

## A worked F&F settlement, line by line

**Example (illustration only):** Ananya joined on 1 June 2020 and resigned. Her last working day is 13 November 2026. Her monthly pay is ₹1,00,000: basic pay ₹50,000 (this is her "wages" under the 50% rule), HRA ₹20,000 and special allowance ₹30,000. Her notice was 60 days; she served 50. She has 18 days of earned leave and a ₹4,500 travel claim.

**Due by Tuesday 17 November (two working days, assuming she counts as a "worker" for the leave rule):**

| Line | Working | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Salary, 1 to 13 November | ₹1,00,000 × 13/30 | + ₹43,333 |
| Leave encashment, 18 days on basic | ₹50,000 ÷ 30 × 18 | + ₹30,000 |
| Her PF on November basic | 12% of ₹21,667 | − ₹2,600 |
| Professional tax (Maharashtra, Karnataka) | | − ₹200 |
| Notice shortfall, 10 days on basic (if her contract allows it) | ₹50,000 ÷ 30 × 10 | − ₹16,667 |
| TDS | See below | ₹0 |
| **Net payable** | | **₹53,866** |

**On separate clocks:**

| Line | Working | Amount and deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Gratuity | ₹50,000 × 15/26 × 6 years | ₹1,73,077, by 13 December |
| Travel reimbursement | Company policy | ₹4,500 |
| PF balance (both shares plus interest) | EPFO | Transfer or withdraw |

Some points the numbers hide:

- **Gratuity counts only 6 years, not 6 years 5 months.** The Code on Social Security counts each completed year "or part thereof in excess of six months". Had Ananya stayed into December, her part year would have crossed six months and counted as a seventh year, worth about ₹28,800 more.
- **The five-year rule still applies to permanent staff.** Gratuity needs five years of continuous service, except on death or disablement. Fixed-term employees now get it pro rata after one year, according to the Press Information Bureau. The current ceiling is ₹20 lakh, according to the Labour Ministry's FAQs. The 15/26 formula is how most employers turn "15 days' wages" into a monthly figure.
- **Notice recovery isn't on the Code's list of deductions.** Section 18 allows only listed deductions (fines, absence, loss caused by neglect, advances and loans, tax, PF and a few others) and caps the total in a wage period at 50% of wages. Notice-pay recovery isn't on that list, so employers rely on the contract when they adjust it. Our [notice period guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/notice-period-rules) covers buyouts, negative F&Fs and GST.
- **Daily rates vary.** Some firms divide by 26, and some pay leave on gross pay; your policy decides.
- **TDS here is nil.** Assuming no new job before March, Ananya's taxable salary for the year stays under ₹12 lakh even with the leave encashment added, and the new regime's rebate makes the tax nil. Gratuity and leave encashment on leaving are partly or fully exempt, now under Section 19 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly Sections 10(10) and 10(10AA)). For private employees the lifetime caps have been ₹20 lakh and ₹25 lakh respectively, the latter notified by CBDT in May 2023. Our [section mapping guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/new-income-tax-act-section-mapping) shows the old and new numbers.

If your basic is set differently, see how the 50% wage rule changes PF and gratuity in our [CTC to in-hand salary guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/ctc-to-in-hand-salary).

## Your PF after you leave

The EPF Scheme, 2026 replaced the 1952 scheme from July 2026. Three rules matter at exit:

- **Transfer:** Under paragraph 43 you apply on EPFO's portal; the scheme allows automated transfers but doesn't guarantee them. Check that your old employer has marked your date of exit.
- **Full withdrawal:** Under paragraph 49(2), you can withdraw the full balance only after 12 months without covered employment. A proviso waives the wait for women members who resign in specified cases. Retirement at 55 and retrenchment are separate grounds.
- **Settlement time:** Paragraph 54(7) gives EPFO 20 days to pay a complete claim. It must point out defects within 20 days and can owe 12% a year as penal interest for unexplained delays.

## Papers to collect before your last day

1. **A written F&F statement** showing each line above. Don't sign a "no further claims" letter until the numbers match.
2. **Your final wage slip.** Under the Central Rules it must be issued on or before payment.
3. **Relieving and experience letters.** No law requires these from every private employer, though the Model Standing Orders, 2026 require a service certificate within 10 days where they apply. The notice period guide above covers disputes over them.
4. **Your TDS certificate.** For salary from April 2026, it is Form 130, due by 15 June 2027. Give your new employer your previous salary and TDS figures so your tax isn't under-deducted.
5. **Your PF exit date and UAN**, checked on EPFO's member portal.

## What to do if your F&F is delayed

1. **Write to HR and payroll** with your last working day, the amounts you expect and the legal deadline. It becomes your evidence.
2. **Approach the labour department.** For most private offices the state government is the "appropriate government" under Section 2(d) of the Code on Wages. The Centre covers railways, mines, banks, insurers, central PSUs and a few other sectors. The Inspector-cum-Facilitator can call for the employer's registers and records under Section 51.
3. **File a wage claim under Section 45.** The employee, a registered union or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator can apply to the authority appointed under the Code within three years. The authority can order compensation of up to ten times the amount due, and it aims to decide within three months. Under the Central Rules, claims are filed in Form II, on paper or online; state rules may use their own forms.
4. **For gratuity**, apply to the competent authority under Section 56 of the Code on Social Security. A late employer owes simple interest unless the delay was your fault and the authority permitted it in writing.
5. **For PF**, raise a grievance on EPFiGMS, EPFO's grievance portal.

The Labour Ministry's **SAMADHAN** portal also takes wage, gratuity and dispute claims, but where your state is the appropriate government, the state labour office is the more direct route. Under Section 54, paying less than what the Code requires can cost an employer a fine of up to ₹50,000, rising for a repeat offence.

This explains the law; it isn't legal or tax advice. For large or disputed amounts, a labour lawyer can tell you which authority suits your state and role.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the time limit for full and final settlement in India?

Two working days for wages under Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, 30 days for gratuity, and 20 days for EPFO to settle a complete PF claim.

### Can my company hold my F&F until I return the laptop?

Section 17(2) makes no exception for pending clearances, so wages can't simply wait for them. Section 18 does allow a deduction for loss or damage caused by your neglect, within the 50% cap.

### Am I eligible for gratuity after 4 years and 7 months?

As a permanent employee, five years of continuous service is the condition, so generally not. Fixed-term employees get pro-rata gratuity after one year. Once you cross five years, any part year over six months counts as a full year.

### Where do I complain if my final settlement is not paid?

Wage claims go to the authority under Section 45 of the Code on Wages (usually through your state labour department) within three years. Gratuity goes to the competent authority, and PF complaints to EPFiGMS.
