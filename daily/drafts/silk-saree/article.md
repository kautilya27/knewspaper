**Quick answer:** The surest way to know a saree is pure silk is the Silk Mark label from the Silk Mark Organisation of India (SMOI). It carries a hologram and a unique serial number that you can check on SMOI's website. Buy from a Silk Mark authorised outlet and keep the original bill. If you are still unsure, take the saree to one of SMOI's 10 chapters, which double as silk testing centres.

Most home tests are weaker than they sound. The burn test does work for fibre type, but you have to sacrifice a few threads. The ring, touch and water-drop tests prove very little. Zari needs its own check, because Silk Mark does not cover zari at all.

## What the Silk Mark label is, and what it looks like

SMOI, set up in 2004 by the Central Silk Board (CSB) under the Ministry of Textiles, registers weavers, manufacturers and retailers as "authorised users". They sign an agreement to put the label only on 100% pure natural silk with SMOI.

According to SMOI, the label comes in two forms: a **paper hang tag** and a **sew-in label**. Both carry the Silk Mark logo and a **hologram with a unique serial number** that traces the product to the authorised user; the SMOI FAQ also lists a **QR code** among its security features.

### How to verify a Silk Mark label

1. **Check the serial number** on SMOI's Label Authentication page: pick the series letter (A to Z) and enter the number.
2. **Check the seller.** Use the "Locate Authorised Outlets" page on the Silk Mark Organisation of India's website (silkmarkindia.com) to see whether the shop is a registered authorised user.
3. **Look at how the label is attached.** Hang tags have been reused on other products (one reason a fabric-fused label was later introduced). A sew-in label is harder to move, and the bill should name the item and say it carries the Silk Mark.
4. **Keep the original bill.** If a labelled saree turns out not to be pure silk, SMOI says it will help you get a replacement or refund, and with a consumer court case if needed. Repeat offenders lose their membership.

The SMOI helpline is +91 82828 24345 (Monday to Friday, 10 am to 5 pm).

### What Silk Mark does not certify

- **Zari.** SMOI's FAQ says plainly that Silk Mark does not assure the purity or quality of zari.
- **Origin or weave.** The label does not tell you whether a saree is a GI-registered Kanchipuram or Banarasi, or whether it is handloom or powerloom.
- **Blends.** Silk-cotton and silk-polyester sarees cannot carry the label at all, because it goes only on 100% pure silk.

Each label costs an authorised user just ₹5.90 (SMOI), so it signals honest dealing, not an expensive saree.

## The four Indian silks, in brief

According to the CSB, India is the world's second-largest producer of silk and its largest consumer. It produces all the commercial types of silk.

| Silk | Where it comes from (SMOI/CSB) | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| Mulberry | Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, J&K, West Bengal | Kanchipuram sarees are woven in pure mulberry silk, per their GI application |
| Tasar (tussar) | Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Bihar; oak tasar in the sub-Himalayan belt | India is the only producer of tropical tasar |
| Eri | Mainly Assam and other north-eastern states | Its cocoons are open-ended, so the silk is spun, not reeled. About 80% of India's vanya (non-mulberry) silk (2023-24, Ministry of Textiles) |
| Muga | Assam (over 95%), plus border areas and Cooch Behar | Natural golden shimmer. The CSB calls it the most expensive silk |

All four are genuine silk and can carry the Silk Mark.

## Home tests: which ones actually work

| Test | What it really tells you | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Burn test | Whether the thread is a protein fibre (silk), cellulose (cotton, viscose "art silk") or synthetic (polyester, nylon) | Works, but destroys the threads you burn |
| Ring test | Only how thin and soft the fabric is | Unreliable |
| Rub/warmth/feel | Very little | Unreliable. SMOI says silk is "very difficult" to identify by look and feel |
| Water drop | Mostly the fabric's finish | Unreliable, and water can leave marks on silk |
| Price | A rough warning sign only | A very cheap "pure Kanjivaram" is a red flag, but a high price proves nothing |

### The burn test, done properly

SMOI's "How to Test Silk" page describes it: silk burns slowly, smells like burnt hair and leaves a black bead that crumbles between your fingers. Cotton and rayon keep burning, smell like burning paper, and rayon leaves a white ash. Nylon and polyester melt like plastic and leave hard beads you cannot crush.

The limits:
- **It is destructive.** Pull a few threads only from an unseen edge, such as the unfinished end of the pallu or a blouse piece, and do it over a sink, away from anything that can catch fire.
- **It tests only the threads you burn.** If the lengthwise threads (warp) are silk and the crosswise threads (weft) are not, a single thread can fool you. Test one of each.
- **It says nothing about zari or quality.** Wool would also smell like burnt hair. A burn test cannot tell good silk from poor silk, or Kanchipuram from a copy.

### Why the ring test is a myth

Pulling a saree through a finger ring tests thinness, not fibre. Fine polyester georgette slides through; a heavy, genuine Kanjivaram with a wide zari border may not.

## Checking the zari: what "pure", "tested" and "imitation" mean

Real zari is not solid gold. According to the Tamil Nadu government's GI application for Kancheepuram silk, the zari used by its silk weavers' co-operatives was about **57% silver, 0.59% gold, 24% silk and 18.41% copper**. In other words, a silk core is wrapped in silver (alloyed with copper) and given a thin gold coating. A 2012 FashionUnited report said Tamil Nadu then relaxed the norm to **40% silver and 0.5% gold**.

The Ministry of Textiles' handicrafts portal describes **imitation zari** as copper wire electroplated with silver. "Tested zari" (sometimes called half-fine) is a trade term for this kind of copper-based zari with a silver and gold finish. **Metallic zari** uses metallised plastic film and contains no precious metal.

Unlike gold jewellery, which carries a [BIS hallmark and HUID number](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-check-huid-number) you can look up, zari has no consumer hallmark yet. The Bureau of Indian Standards' silk committee (TXD 28) took up a proposed computerised zari testing and grading method in December 2024. The proposal, from the Central Silk Technological Research Institute, suggested gold grades from A (above 0.55%) to E (below 0.20%) and silver grades from A (above 50%) to E (below 20%). It is a proposal, not an adopted standard.

What you can do:
- **Ask the seller to write the zari type on the bill** ("pure zari", "tested zari"), along with the saree's weight.
- **Look at the back of the zari at a hidden spot.** If a gentle scratch shows reddish copper under the gold, it is copper-based zari. A whitish silver layer points to silver-based zari. This is only a rough guide.
- **Use a zari testing facility.** The Kancheepuram district administration says a zari testing unit set up with the Tamil Nadu government and Tamilnadu Zari Ltd checks gold and silver content for a nominal fee.

## Buying a GI saree: check the authorised users

"Kancheepuram Silk" is a registered geographical indication (GI application number 15). The applicant was Tamil Nadu's Department of Handlooms and Textiles, the certificate is dated 2 June 2005, and the registration is valid until 6 October 2034. "Banaras Brocades and Sarees" got its GI certificate on 4 September 2009, according to the Varanasi district administration.

The [GI Registry's public search](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/) lists each GI's registered authorised users. For Kancheepuram Silk, for example, it lists Co-optex. A shop missing from the list may still be honest, but a GI authorised user plus a Silk Mark label is the strongest proof of origin and purity you can get.

## Getting a saree tested in a lab

SMOI says all 10 of its chapters also work as silk testing centres: Srinagar, New Delhi, Guwahati, Varanasi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Chennai and Palakkad. For a saree that carries the Silk Mark label, SMOI says the confirmatory test is free.

In 2019, SMOI also opened nine retail testing counters in Bengaluru. They charged ₹20 per test, used flame and microscope tests, and did not damage the garment, according to Fibre2Fashion. Call the helpline before you travel to check current fees and timings.

A real silk saree, cared for well, can last for generations. That makes it the opposite of [fast fashion](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/fast-fashion-bad-for-you-and-the-environment), and worth these few checks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Silk Mark label guarantee the zari is real?
No. SMOI's FAQ says Silk Mark assures only that the silk is 100% pure. For zari, ask the seller to write the zari type on the bill, or get it tested at a zari testing unit.

### Can I check a Silk Mark number online?
Yes. Go to the Label Authentication page on silkmarkindia.com, pick the series letter, enter the serial number and search. You can also confirm the shop on the "Locate Authorised Outlets" page.

### Is the burn test safe for my saree?
It destroys the threads you burn, so only pull a couple of threads from a hidden, unfinished edge. Test one lengthwise and one crosswise thread. For a costly saree, a test at an SMOI chapter is the better option.

### Is tussar silk "less pure" than mulberry silk?
No. Tasar, eri and muga are all natural silks recognised by the CSB, and they can carry the Silk Mark. They simply look and feel different from mulberry.
