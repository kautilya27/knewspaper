**Quick answer:** A hexagon is a closed flat shape with 6 straight sides, 6 corners (vertices) and 6 angles. Its interior angles always add up to 720°. In a regular hexagon, all sides are equal and each angle is 120°, so its area is (3√3/2) × a², where a is the side length.

That one formula, plus the angle sum, covers most school questions. Below, each rule is worked out step by step, so you can rebuild it in an exam even if you forget it, with solved examples in cm and mm.

## What makes a shape a hexagon?

The name comes from Greek: *hex* means six and *gonia* means angle. Any polygon with exactly six sides counts, whether it looks neat or lopsided. Every hexagon has 6 sides, 6 vertices, 9 diagonals and interior angles totalling 720°.

## Types of hexagon: regular, irregular, convex, concave

These are two separate questions. "Regular or irregular?" asks whether the sides and angles are equal. "Convex or concave?" asks whether any corner points inwards.

| Type | What it means | Angles | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular | All 6 sides equal and all 6 angles equal | Each 120° | Cross-section of a pencil, a honeycomb cell |
| Irregular | Sides or angles not all equal | Any values that add up to 720° | A plot of land with six boundary walls |
| Convex | Every interior angle is less than 180°; no corner points inwards | All under 180° | Every regular hexagon |
| Concave | At least one interior angle is more than 180° (a "dent") | One or more above 180° | A six-sided outline with one corner pushed inwards |

A regular hexagon is always convex, because each of its angles is 120°. So there is no such thing as a "regular concave hexagon". An irregular hexagon can be convex or concave.

## Why the angles add up to 720°

Pick one corner of any convex hexagon and draw lines from it to every corner it is not already joined to. You get 3 diagonals, and they cut the hexagon into 4 triangles. Each triangle's angles add up to 180°, so:

Sum of interior angles = 4 × 180° = 720°

The same idea works for any polygon. A shape with n sides splits into (n − 2) triangles, which gives the general rule:

Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°

For n = 6, that is 4 × 180° = 720°. In a regular hexagon all six angles are equal, so each one is 720° ÷ 6 = 120°. Each exterior angle is 180° − 120° = 60°, and six of them make 360°.

**Example 1 (missing angle):** Five angles of a hexagon are 100°, 135°, 110°, 140° and 125°. Find the sixth.
These five add up to 610°. So the sixth angle = 720° − 610° = 110°.

## Area of a regular hexagon, derived from six triangles

Join the centre of a regular hexagon to all six corners. You get six triangles. Each one has two sides equal to the distance from the centre to a corner, and the angle at the centre is 360° ÷ 6 = 60°. A triangle with a 60° angle between two equal sides is equilateral, so all six triangles are equilateral with side a.

You may already know that the [area of an equilateral triangle](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-equilateral-triangle) is (√3/4) × a². Six of them give:

Area of regular hexagon = 6 × (√3/4) × a² = (3√3/2) × a²

Taking √3 ≈ 1.732, this is roughly 2.598 × a².

You can also check it another way. Cut the hexagon along its longest diagonal and you get two equal trapeziums. Each has parallel sides 2a and a, and height (√3/2) × a. Using the [area of a trapezium](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/area-of-a-trapezium), one half is ½ × (2a + a) × (√3/2) × a = (3√3/4) × a². Double it and you get (3√3/2) × a² again.

Some books write it as Area = ½ × perimeter × apothem, where the apothem (centre to the middle of a side) is (√3/2) × a. It simplifies to the same formula.

**Example 2:** Find the area and perimeter of a regular hexagon of side 6 cm.
Area = (3√3/2) × 6² = (3√3/2) × 36 = 54√3 ≈ 93.53 cm².
Perimeter = 6 × 6 = 36 cm.

**Example 3 (working backwards):** A regular hexagon has a perimeter of 48 cm. Find its area.
Side a = 48 ÷ 6 = 8 cm. Area = (3√3/2) × 64 = 96√3 ≈ 166.28 cm².

## Perimeter of a hexagon

Perimeter is just the total length of the boundary.

- Regular hexagon: Perimeter = 6 × a
- Irregular hexagon: add all six sides, P = a + b + c + d + e + f

## Diagonals: how many, and how long?

A polygon with n sides has n(n − 3)/2 diagonals. For a hexagon, 6 × 3 ÷ 2 = 9 diagonals. The reasoning: from each of the 6 corners you can draw a diagonal to 3 non-neighbouring corners, which gives 18, but every diagonal gets counted twice (once from each end), so 18 ÷ 2 = 9.

In a regular hexagon of side a, these 9 diagonals come in only two lengths:

| Diagonal | How many | Length | Side 10 cm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short diagonal (skips one corner) | 6 | √3 × a | ≈ 17.32 cm |
| Long diagonal (to the opposite corner, through the centre) | 3 | 2 × a | 20 cm |

The long diagonal is twice the side because it is made of two sides of the equilateral triangles from the area proof. The short diagonal, √3 × a, is also the distance between two opposite parallel sides (called the distance "across flats").

## Real-life problems: floor tiles and nut-bolts

**Example 4 (tiles):** A room floor measures 3 m × 4 m. It is to be covered with regular hexagonal tiles of side 10 cm. About how many tiles are needed?
Floor area = 3 m × 4 m = 12 m² = 1,20,000 cm².
Area of one tile = (3√3/2) × 10² = 150√3 ≈ 259.81 cm².
Tiles needed ≈ 1,20,000 ÷ 259.81 ≈ 461.9, so at least 462 tiles. In practice you buy extra, because tiles along the walls have to be cut.

Hexagons are popular for tiles for the same reason as honeycombs: regular hexagons fit together with no gaps. Only three regular polygons can tile a floor on their own: equilateral triangles, squares and regular hexagons. That is because their angles (60°, 90° and 120°) divide 360° exactly.

**Example 5 (nut and spanner):** The head of a hexagonal nut is a regular hexagon. A 17 mm spanner fits it, meaning the distance across flats is 17 mm. Find the side of the hexagon.
Across flats = √3 × a, so a = 17 ÷ √3 ≈ 9.81 mm.
The distance across corners is 2 × a ≈ 19.63 mm. This is why a spanner grips the flat sides: the nut is wider corner to corner.

Nuts are hexagonal because six flat sides give a firm grip, and you only need to turn the spanner by 60° before it fits the nut again, which helps in tight spaces.

## Why do bees build hexagonal cells?

Bees make wax with their own bodies, so the less wax a comb needs, the better. The question is which shape divides a flat surface into equal-sized cells using the least total wall length. The Greek mathematician Pappus discussed this, and people long believed the answer was the regular hexagon. This idea is called the honeycomb conjecture.

It was proved only in 1999 (the proof was published in 2001). According to Thomas C. Hales's paper [The Honeycomb Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906042), any division of the plane into regions of equal area has a total perimeter at least as large as the regular hexagonal grid. Triangles and squares also tile without gaps, but for the same area per cell they need more wall. So the hexagon really is the most wax-saving pattern.

## Where hexagons show up in your textbook and beyond

In the current NCERT book *Ganita Prakash* for Class 6, the chapter "Symmetry" asks how many lines of symmetry a hexagon with equal sides and equal angles has. The answer is 6. A regular hexagon also looks the same after a turn of 60°, so it has rotational symmetry of order 6.

A hexagon also turns up in 3D shapes. A hexagonal prism, like an unsharpened pencil, has 8 faces, 18 edges and 12 vertices. You can check these with Euler's formula in our guide to [vertices, faces and edges](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/vertices-faces-edges): 8 + 12 − 18 = 2.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many sides and angles does a hexagon have?

A hexagon has 6 sides, 6 vertices and 6 interior angles. The interior angles always add up to 720°, and in a regular hexagon each angle is 120°.

### What is the formula for the area of a regular hexagon?

Area = (3√3/2) × a², where a is the side length. This works because a regular hexagon is made of six equilateral triangles, each with area (√3/4) × a².

### How many diagonals does a hexagon have?

A hexagon has 9 diagonals, from the formula n(n − 3)/2 = 6 × 3 ÷ 2. In a regular hexagon, 6 are short diagonals of length √3 × a and 3 are long diagonals of length 2 × a.

### Can a regular hexagon be concave?

No. A concave hexagon needs at least one angle greater than 180°, but every angle of a regular hexagon is 120°. Only an irregular hexagon can be concave.
