**Quick answer:** To report cyber fraud in India, call the national helpline **1930** at once (it works 24 hours a day), file a complaint on **cybercrime.gov.in** under "Financial Fraud", and report the transaction to your bank through its official app or helpline. Try to do all three within the first hour.

The first two can get the money frozen before it moves on. The bank report starts RBI's customer-protection clock, which today gives you zero liability only if you report within 3 working days. For transactions from **1 January 2027**, new RBI rules give a 5-day window and, for losses up to ₹50,000, a one-time compensation.

## The first hour, minute by minute

Police cyber units, such as Telangana's, call the first hour after the money leaves your account the **golden hour**. Fraudsters quickly split stolen money across "mule" accounts and wallets. A complaint made in those minutes can reach the receiving bank while the money is still there, so it can be put on hold (a lien).

| When | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Call **1930** with the transaction details | The complaint goes to the banks and apps that received the money |
| 10–20 min | Block card, UPI and net banking through your bank's app or 24x7 helpline; report the debit as fraud | Stops more debits; losses after you report fall on the bank |
| Within the hour | Complete the complaint on cybercrime.gov.in; save the acknowledgement number | A record the police and bank both use |
| Same day | Write to the bank (email or in-app) quoting the complaint number | Proof of the date you reported |
| Next days | Follow up with police and bank; ask if money was frozen | A freeze is not a refund |

Got a fraud call, SMS or WhatsApp but **lost no money**? Report it on **Chakshu** in the government's Sanchar Saathi portal. Sanchar Saathi says reports should be made within 30 days for action, and that anyone who has already lost money should go to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in instead. For ordinary spam, see our guide to [stopping spam calls](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/stop-spam-calls).

## What to keep ready before you call

- your bank name, account number or card's last four digits
- transaction ID or UTR number, date, time and amount of each debit
- the account number, UPI ID or wallet the money went to (from the bank SMS or UPI history)
- the fraudster's phone number, website, app or social media profile
- screenshots of chats, payment pages and SMS alerts (delete nothing)

## Filing on cybercrime.gov.in

The National Cybercrime Reporting Portal is run by the Ministry of Home Affairs' Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C). Choose "Register a Complaint", then **Financial Fraud**; verify your mobile with OTP; enter the incident, suspect and your own details; upload screenshots; and submit. Save the complaint ID sent by SMS and email and download the PDF. If you called 1930 first, complete the portal complaint using that acknowledgement number.

Speed is what makes this work. In a written reply to the Lok Sabha in August 2026, the Home Ministry said over ₹11,158 crore had been saved across more than 32.8 lakh complaints up to 30 June 2026.

### Complaint vs FIR

A portal complaint is not automatically an FIR; state police verify it and decide. For big losses, I4C's **e-Zero FIR** system (a Delhi pilot from May 2025 for losses above ₹10 lakh, which the government told the Supreme Court in August 2026 now works in 19 states, each setting its own loss threshold) turns a 1930 or portal complaint into a Zero FIR automatically. You then have **three days** to visit the cybercrime police station and convert it into a regular FIR.

## How much of the loss the bank must bear

RBI's answer depends on whose fault it was and how fast you told the bank.

### Transactions until 31 December 2026 (RBI circular of 6 July 2017)

- **Zero liability** if the bank was negligent, whether or not you report. Also zero for a third-party breach (neither you nor the bank at fault) reported within **3 working days** of the bank's alert.
- **Limited liability** for a third-party breach reported in **4 to 7 working days**: the lower of the transaction amount or ₹5,000 (basic savings accounts), ₹10,000 (other savings accounts, smaller current accounts, credit cards up to ₹5 lakh limit) or ₹25,000 (other current accounts, credit cards above ₹5 lakh).
- **After 7 working days**, the bank's board-approved policy decides.
- **If you were negligent**, for example by sharing an OTP, you bear the loss until you report; the bank bears anything after.
- The bank must give a provisional (shadow) credit within 10 working days of your report and settle within 90 days. The bank has to prove customer liability.

### Transactions from 1 January 2027 (RBI directions of 24 June 2026)

- The rules cover payments you were **coerced** into making (such as "digital arrest" scams) and debits using credentials stolen by fraud, not just unauthorised debits.
- **Zero liability** for a third-party breach reported within **5 calendar days**.
- Banks must resolve domestic fraud complaints within **45 calendar days** (60 for cross-border) and give a credit card shadow reversal within 5 calendar days.
- RBI lists as customer negligence: sharing OTPs or PINs, ignoring a clear bank scam warning, installing malicious apps, and not updating your mobile number with the bank.

### The new compensation of up to ₹25,000

For frauds in the year from 1 January 2027 (RBI will then review it), an individual with a gross loss of up to **₹50,000** can get **85% of the net loss or ₹25,000, whichever is lower**, including losses caused by the customer's own negligence. Conditions: the loss is genuine; you report to **both** the bank and 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in within **5 calendar days**; and it is available **once in a lifetime**. The bank pays within 5 calendar days of your application. RBI funds most of it.

| Gross loss (nothing recovered) | 85% | Compensation |
|---|---|---|
| ₹12,000 | ₹10,200 | ₹10,200 |
| ₹45,000 | ₹38,250 | ₹25,000 (cap) |
| ₹60,000 | not eligible | none under this scheme |

The bank decides eligibility under RBI's rules, so treat this as a framework, not a promise of payment.

## What happens to frozen money

A lien keeps money in the fraudster's account from moving; it does not send it back. Under the Home Ministry's SOP of January 2026, frauds **below ₹50,000** can be refunded without a court order, and banks must lift a freeze within 90 days if no court or restoration order arrives. A Money Restoration module on the portal began working in April 2026.

Larger amounts usually need a magistrate's order, applied for with the investigating officer's help, or a **Lok Adalat** settlement. Telangana, for instance, has returned held money to victims in bulk through Lok Adalats. Your investigating officer or District Legal Services Authority can tell you which route applies.

## If the bank doesn't respond: RBI Ombudsman

If the bank doesn't reply within **30 days**, or you're unhappy with its reply, complain under the **Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026**, in force from 1 July 2026. According to RBI's FAQs, file within 90 days after the bank's deadline or its last reply, whichever is later, at [cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in), by email to crpc@rbi.org.in, or by post to RBI's Chandigarh processing centre. It is free. The toll-free number **14448** helps with process and status but can't take complaints. Awards can include up to ₹3 lakh for harassment and expenses.

## Once the money is dealt with

- If a stolen phone was involved, [block it through CEIR](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/block-lost-phone-ceir).
- Change passwords and turn on two-step verification; our [guide to protecting your data from cyberattacks](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-protect-your-data-from-cyberattacks) covers the basics.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 1930 the same as 112?
No. 1930 is the National Cyber Crime Reporting Helpline for financial cyber fraud and works 24 hours a day. Use 112 when someone is in physical danger.

### Can I report cyber fraud after a few days?
Yes, but the chance of freezing the money drops fast, and bank liability depends on timing: 3 working days under today's rules, 5 calendar days for transactions from 1 January 2027.

### Will I definitely get my money back?
No one can promise that. It depends on whether the money was frozen, how RBI's liability rules apply to your case, and the bank's or court's decision.

### Do I need to visit a police station?
Not always. With an e-Zero FIR (for large losses in the states and UTs that use it (19 states, the government told the Supreme Court in August 2026); each sets its own threshold, ₹10 lakh in Delhi's pilot) you must visit within three days; otherwise follow what the police tell you after verifying your complaint.
