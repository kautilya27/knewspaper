## What happens at the festival

Hornbill is often called the "festival of festivals" because it brings the festivals of Nagaland's tribes to one place. Each tribe has a traditional *morung* (a community house) at the heritage village, where you can see its architecture, crafts and food.

A typical day at Kisama looks like this:

- **Morning (from about 9 am):** the main cultural programme in the arena: tribal dances, folk songs, and traditional games such as archery, Naga wrestling and bamboo-pole climbing.
- **Afternoon:** quieter. This is the best time to walk through the morungs, the craft and food stalls and the photo exhibitions.
- **Evening:** music. A rock contest and concerts run alongside the festival (in past editions the music events were ticketed separately), and Kohima and Dimapur hold night markets and carnivals.

In 2025 the state also held festival extensions in other districts, including Mon, Dimapur, Wokha and Zünheboto. Check the Nagaland Tourism announcements for the 2026 line-up before you fix your dates.

## Permits: what Indians and foreigners need

### Inner Line Permit for Indian tourists

Nagaland is an Inner Line state under the Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation, 1873. Any Indian citizen from outside the state needs an ILP to enter. Dimapur, Chümoukedima and Niuland districts are also covered, so you need it even if you only fly or take the train into Dimapur.

According to the official portal ([ilp.nagaland.gov.in](https://ilp.nagaland.gov.in/)):

| ILP detail | For domestic tourists |
|---|---|
| Fee | ₹200 (fresh permit; payment-gateway charges extra) |
| Validity | 30 days |
| Renewal | Not available for this category; apply again if needed |
| Documents | A recent passport-size photo and one ID: Aadhaar, PAN, Voter ID, bank passbook or ration card |
| Guarantor | Not needed for domestic tourists |

Some older travel blogs still quote ₹50 for 15 days. That is out of date; go by the fee page on the portal.

**How to apply, step by step:**

1. Open the ILP portal and choose to apply for a new permit (domestic tourist).
2. Enter your details and the phone number you will carry; the portal sends an OTP.
3. Upload your photo and one ID from the list above.
4. Pay the fee online and download the permit.
5. Carry a printout and the original ID. Each traveller applies separately.

Applicants from Karbi Anglong, Dima Hasao, Karimganj and Hailakandi districts of Assam must also upload an NRC document, as the portal's checklist says.

### Protected Area Permit for foreign tourists

The Union Home Ministry reimposed the Protected Area Regime in Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram in December 2024, ending a relaxation that had been in place, with extensions, since 2011. As of 12 November 2026 it was still in force: the Nagaland government has asked the Centre to withdraw it before the festival, but no change had been announced.

In 2025 the Centre lifted the PAP for the ten festival days only (1–10 December), announced on 29 November, with nationals of Afghanistan, China and Pakistan excluded. A similar step may come for 2026, but plan as if the PAP applies and check the news closer to the date.

What this means for foreign visitors, according to advisories from the Dimapur police (May 2025, repeated in February 2026):

- A **PAP** is needed to visit Nagaland, including Dimapur, Chümoukedima and Niuland.
- Every foreigner must **register with the Foreigners Registration Officer (FRO)** of the district they visit within **24 hours of arrival**, whatever their age and including OCI cardholders.
- Nationals of **Afghanistan, China and Pakistan** (and foreigners of those origins), and citizens of **Myanmar**, need **prior approval from the Ministry of Home Affairs**. Myanmar is listed in the February 2026 advisory but not the May 2025 one.

Applications go through the Ministry of Home Affairs' e-FRRO portal. Permits can also be issued by Indian missions abroad, FRROs, the Home Commissioner in Kohima, the Resident Commissioner of Nagaland in New Delhi and the Secretary (Tourism), Nagaland. If you are bringing foreign friends, apply well before December and keep copies of the passport, visa and permit with you.

## How to reach Kisama

There are no regular flights or trains into Kohima itself. Almost everyone comes through **Dimapur**, which has the state's airport and main railway station (with trains from Guwahati, Dibrugarh and other cities).

| Leg | Option | Typical time | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimapur → Kohima (about 74 km, NH-29) | Shared taxi from near the railway station | 3–4 hours | Notified cap about ₹220 a seat; travellers report ₹400–500 in festival season |
| Dimapur → Kohima | Private taxi | 2.5–3.5 hours | About ₹2,800–4,000 per car |
| Dimapur → Kohima | State bus | Slower; one or two a day | About ₹200–250 |
| Kohima → Kisama (about 12 km) | Shared festival taxi from PR Hill, or reserved taxi | Short drive, but slow in festival traffic | ₹120 a head shared; about ₹317–700 reserved (2024 official festival fares) |

Nagaland's transport authority notified statewide taxi fares in November 2025 (₹3 per km per passenger on trips under 100 km), and in 2024 the All Nagaland Taxi Association published fixed Kisama fares for the festival period. The private-taxi, bus and time figures come from one travel operator's 2025 guide, so treat them as indicative and agree the fare before you get in. Shared taxis mostly leave between early morning and mid-afternoon, so if your train or flight reaches Dimapur late, plan a night there and drive up the next morning. The road to Kohima climbs through hills and can slow sharply in festival traffic, so don't book anything in Kohima for the same evening you land.

## Where to stay

Rooms in Kohima fill up weeks ahead for the first weekend and the opening days. Your options:

- **Kohima town:** hotels and guesthouses, with the best access to taxis and restaurants. You travel 12 km to Kisama each day.
- **Homestays in villages near Kisama:** closer to the venue and a good way to see village life; book directly and ahead.
- **Camps and tents near Kisama:** set up for the festival season; they must be booked in advance and can be cold at night, so ask whether bedding and heaters are included.
- **Dimapur:** cheaper and easier to find, but the daily drive (up to 3–4 hours each way) makes it a poor base for more than one day.

Pack warm layers: Kohima's December nights are in single digits, and later in the month they can drop further (the Eastern Mirror reported 3.4°C in Kohima on 28 December one recent year).

## A simple 3-day plan

**Day 1: Arrive in Dimapur, travel to Kohima.** Keep your ILP printout ready for checks. Walk around Kohima in the evening.

**Day 2: Kisama.** Pick a mid-festival weekday if you can, and reach by 9 am for the morning programme, spend the afternoon at the morungs and food stalls, then head back for an evening concert or night market.

**Day 3: Kohima and a second Kisama visit.** See the Kohima War Cemetery, which marks the 1944 Battle of Kohima, in the morning, then return to Kisama for the afternoon or a different day's programme.

If you have more time, add a day trip to a nearby village or a trek; arrange it through a registered local guide.

## Tips to get more out of the festival

- **Avoid the opening day and weekends** if crowds bother you: weekdays in the middle of the festival are calmer.
- **Carry cash.** Food stalls and taxis may not take cards, and mobile signal can be patchy in crowds.
- **Ask before photographing people,** especially elders in traditional dress.
- **Respect the morungs:** they are cultural spaces, not just photo backdrops.
- **Keep the venue clean:** carry your waste back, as the festival promotes sustainable tourism (part of the 2025 entry fee went to a sustainable tourism fund).

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Hornbill Festival in 2026?

It runs from 1 to 10 December 2026, the 27th edition, at the Naga Heritage Village in Kisama near Kohima. It has run on 1–10 December each year since the mid-2000s (the 2020 edition was held online).

### Do Indians need a permit to attend the Hornbill Festival?

Yes, Indian citizens from outside Nagaland need an Inner Line Permit. The online domestic permit costs ₹200 and is valid for 30 days.

### Can foreigners attend the Hornbill Festival?

Yes, but they currently need a Protected Area Permit (applied for through the e-FRRO portal) and must register with the district Foreigners Registration Officer within 24 hours of arrival. In 2025 the PAP was lifted for the festival days only; nothing had been announced for 2026 by 12 November.

### How much is the Hornbill Festival entry ticket?

In 2025 the entry ticket at Kisama was ₹150 per head for all visitors, up from ₹50 the year before. The 2026 price had not been announced at the time of writing, and in past editions music events were ticketed separately.
