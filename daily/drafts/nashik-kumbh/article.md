**Quick answer:** The Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Simhastha Kumbh Mela began with flag hoisting on 31 October 2026, but the three big bathing days, the Amrit Snans, fall in August and September 2027: **2 August, 31 August, and 11 September (Nashik) / 12 September (Trimbakeshwar)**. These are the dates the Maharashtra government announced in June 2025, as reported by PTI.

The Mela formally runs until the flag is lowered on 24 July 2028, but the crowds will peak around those Amrit Snan days. If you want a quieter visit, plan for a day between them, and book your stay and transport early either way.

Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis announced the schedule after a meeting with officials and akhara heads in June 2025. According to [the PTI report carried by ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/simhastha-kumbh-mela-to-begin-in-nashik-trimbakeshwar-on-oct-31-next-year/2643959) and Deccan Herald, these are the key dates:

"Amrit Snan" is the government's new name for what used to be called "Shahi Snan".

### Why do some websites show more snan dates?

Several blogs add days such as Shravan Purnima or Bhadrapada Purnima. Devotees do bathe on many auspicious days: PTI reports 44 bathing occasions in Nashik and 53 in Trimbakeshwar. But only the three Amrit Snans, when the akharas bathe in procession, were announced as the main dates, and they will have the strictest traffic and crowd rules. Check any other "main snan" date against official sources before you book.

The tithi names follow the Marathi calendar, in which a month ends on Amavasya. That is why 2 August counts as an Ashadha Amavasya. Because it falls on a Monday, it is also a Somvati Amavasya. ## Why it is called Simhastha: the 12-year cycle

According to tradition, the Kumbh is held at the four places where drops of amrit fell during the Samudra Manthan: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik–Trimbakeshwar. Each site's Mela is tied to a position of Jupiter (Guru, or Brihaspati). Jupiter takes roughly 12 years to go round the zodiac.

At Nashik, the Mela is held when Jupiter is in Leo (Simha rashi), which is why it is called the **Simhastha** Kumbh. All India Radio reported that the 2026 Mela began at the moment of Jupiter's entry into Leo. The last Simhastha was in 2015–16. UNESCO added the Kumbh Mela to its list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.

The main baths fall in the months of Shravan and Bhadrapada, which this time are August and September 2027, right in the monsoon. Expect rain, a full Godavari and slippery ghats.

## Nashik vs Trimbakeshwar: why there are two venues

This Kumbh is unusual because it is held in two places about 30 km apart:

- **Ramkund, Panchavati (Nashik city):** a bathing tank on the Godavari. The **Vaishnava** akharas (the bairagi orders, devotees of Vishnu and Rama) take their Amrit Snan here.
- **Kushavarta Tirtha, Trimbakeshwar:** a sacred tank near the Trimbakeshwar Shiva temple, one of the 12 Jyotirlingas, close to the source of the Godavari. The **Shaiva** akharas (the sanyasi orders, devotees of Shiva) and the Udasin akharas bathe here.

According to historical accounts, the Mela was once held only at Trimbak. In 1789, Shaiva sanyasis and Vaishnava bairagis clashed over the order of bathing. After that, the Peshwa administration moved the Vaishnava baths to Ramkund in Nashik. That split continues today, which is why Trimbakeshwar's final snan (12 September) falls a day after Nashik's (11 September).

**Which one should you visit?** It depends on what matters to you:

| | Nashik (Ramkund) | Trimbakeshwar (Kushavarta) |
|---|---|---|
| Akharas | Vaishnava | Shaiva and Udasin |
| Setting | Riverside ghats in a large city | Small temple town at the foot of the Brahmagiri hills |
| Also visit | Kalaram temple, Panchavati | Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga |
| Space | More ghats, more hotels | Smaller tank and town, so it fills up faster |
| Getting there | Nearest to Nashik Road railway station | Shuttle buses from Nashik parking areas |

Shiva devotees (the Jyotirlinga draws big crowds on [Maha Shivaratri](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/maha-shivaratri) too) often pick Trimbakeshwar, and Rama devotees Ramkund. Many pilgrims visit both on different days.

## How to reach: rail, road and the shuttle plan

**By train.** Central Railway is preparing five main stations for Kumbh traffic: **Nashik Road, Deolali, Odha, Kherwadi and Kasbe Sukene**, according to the Ministry of Railways (All India Radio, July 2025). In April 2026, Central Railway told the press it had sanctioned 88 works worth ₹1,370 crore. These include holding areas at the stations, CCTV and announcement systems. Igatpuri and Sainagar Shirdi are also included. The aim is to finish the works by 31 March 2027. Special train arrangements have been talked about, but no timetable has been published yet. Watch Central Railway announcements in mid-2027.

**By road, on Amrit Snan days.** The Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Simhastha Kumbh Mela Authority (NTKMA) and MSRTC have published a three-tier plan, reported by Free Press Journal and Punekar News in June 2026:

1. Private vehicles may go only as far as designated **outer parking areas**. There are 46 of them in the plan.
2. **MSRTC shuttle buses**, about 4,500 in all, take pilgrims from the parking areas and the five railway stations to transfer points near the ghats. 1,485 of these buses are set aside for the Nashik–Trimbakeshwar route alone.
3. The areas around Ramkund and the Trimbakeshwar temple become **pedestrian-only zones**.

The authority says the network can carry more than 33 lakh passengers a day. Even so, expect long waits and a lot of walking at the final stretch.

## Crowd safety: what to expect and what to do

The 2015 Simhastha drew about 2.5 crore people, according to PTI. The Chief Minister's Office has said it expects four to five times that in 2027. Crowd control matters at Nashik in particular. In 2003, 39 pilgrims died in a crush on a narrow lane leading to Ramkund on a snan day, according to news reports at the time, including The Hindu's.

According to the administration's plan (Free Press Journal, October 2026):

- Entry and exit routes will be separated, with one-way walking corridors and barricades.
- Each ghat and temple will have a maximum safe capacity, and entry will be held back once it is reached.
- If crowds build up, pilgrims will be sent along other routes to **holding areas**.
- CCTV, drones and a command-and-control centre will watch the crowds. Big screens and public-address systems will carry instructions.

What this means for you:

- **Go on a non-Amrit Snan day if you can.** The ghats are open throughout, and the akhara processions and peak crowds are concentrated on the four dates.
- **Follow the official channels.** The authority's website, [simhasthamahakumbh.maharashtra.gov.in](https://simhasthamahakumbh.maharashtra.gov.in), went live in November 2026. It will carry bathing timings, transport and parking details, and emergency contacts.
- **Plan for a lot of walking.** Keep children and elderly relatives close and agree on a meeting point.
- **Don't push forward when a gate is held.** The pause in the holding area is how the system prevents a crush.
- **Book early and flexibly.** Rooms for August–September 2027 will go fast; take refundable bookings until timings are published.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the first Amrit Snan of Nashik Kumbh 2027?

The first Amrit Snan is on Monday, 2 August 2027 (Ashadha Somvati Amavasya). It takes place at both Ramkund in Nashik and Kushavarta in Trimbakeshwar, according to the schedule the Maharashtra government announced.

### How long does the Nashik Kumbh Mela last?

The Simhastha period began with flag hoisting on 31 October 2026 and ends when the flag is lowered on 24 July 2028. The biggest crowds are expected around the Amrit Snans in August and September 2027.

### Is Trimbakeshwar part of the Nashik Kumbh Mela?

Yes. Trimbakeshwar is the second venue of the same Kumbh. Shaiva and Udasin akharas bathe at Kushavarta in Trimbakeshwar, while Vaishnava akharas bathe at Ramkund in Nashik.

### Can I drive to Ramkund on an Amrit Snan day?

No. Under the authority's transport plan, private vehicles must stop at outer parking areas on Amrit Snan days. From there, MSRTC shuttle buses and pedestrian routes take you to the ghats.
