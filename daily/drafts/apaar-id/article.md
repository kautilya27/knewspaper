**Quick answer:** An APAAR ID (Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry) is a 12-digit lifelong student ID under the Centre's "One Nation, One Student ID" plan. It holds your marksheets, certificates and college credits in DigiLocker. School students get it through their school, using UDISE+ records and a parent's consent. College students can create it themselves in DigiLocker or on the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) portal. For the 2027 board exams, CBSE says filling in the APAAR ID is optional.

Is it mandatory? No. The official portal says parents give consent voluntarily, and on 20 July 2026 the Supreme Court directed that every APAAR consent form must let parents refuse. CBSE had planned to make APAAR compulsory for registration, but its September 2026 circular for the 2027 boards made it optional. Colleges, however, use it to store your credits, as explained below.

## What an APAAR ID actually does

Your school or college stores report cards, board results, degrees and credits against this number. According to the official portal, [apaar.education.gov.in](https://apaar.education.gov.in/), the ID is meant to stay with a student from school to higher education and skilling.

A [PIB factsheet](https://pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=150693) says 26.35 crore verified APAAR IDs had been generated by 2 July 2026, of which 16.62 crore belonged to school students.

### How APAAR links to your ABC ID

The Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) is the University Grants Commission's digital credit bank for higher education. Colleges first gave students a 12-digit "ABC ID". According to PIB, the ABC ID has since been renamed the APAAR ID, so a college student does not need two numbers. If you already have an ABC ID, that is your APAAR ID.

Why this matters for college:

- Under NEP 2020's multiple entry and exit system, your credits sit in ABC against your APAAR ID. That lets you leave after a certificate or diploma year and come back later.
- Universities upload your credits to the NAD-ABC portal. PIB notes that UGC set 30 June 2026 as the deadline for colleges to upload data from the 2025 exams.

### What the Supreme Court said

A group of parents challenged the scheme in *Abhishek Baxi v. Union of India*. They argued that the consent was not real, because CBSE had made APAAR a condition for board exam registration. On 20 July 2026, a bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant disposed of the petition with three directions, without ruling on the scheme's validity. The government side had not yet been heard and was given liberty to seek clarification. The directions were:

1. The model consent form must clearly let a parent or guardian withhold or refuse consent. The court applied paragraph 19 of the Orissa High Court's 12 December 2025 ruling in *Rohit Anand Das v. State of Odisha* across India.
2. All collection, storage and sharing of APAAR data falls under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
3. Student data cannot be shared with any private company or third party, except as the law allows and only for the scheme's own purposes.

The order does not deal with board registration directly, but CBSE's rules now allow for refusal. For the 2026 exams, its September 2025 relaxation told schools to enter "REFUSED" and keep a copy of the parent's written refusal, or "NOGEN" when an ID could not be generated. Governing body minutes reported in February 2026 still called APAAR a "mandatory field" for 2026-27. The September 2026 LOC circular for the 2027 boards, however, says filling of APAAR is optional.

## How to create an APAAR ID through school (UDISE+)

Students up to Class 12 cannot make an APAAR ID on their own. The school creates it in UDISE+, using the student's PEN (Permanent Education Number). The official portal lists these steps:

1. **Check details.** The school confirms the student's name, date of birth, gender, parents' names and mobile number.
2. **Parental consent.** For a minor, a parent signs the consent form. After the Supreme Court order, this form should offer a clear option to refuse.
3. **Authentication.** The school checks the student's identity against Aadhaar. The name in UDISE+ must match the name on the Aadhaar card.
4. **Generation.** UDISE+ generates the 12-digit ID against the PEN and sends it to the student's DigiLocker.
5. **Download.** Log in to DigiLocker and open "Issued Documents" to find the APAAR card.

To check the status, ask your class teacher or school office. Schools can see it in the APAAR module of UDISE+. The official helpline is 1800-889-3511.

## How to create an APAAR ID via DigiLocker (college students)

If you are in college and your institution is on ABC, you can do it yourself:

1. Sign up on DigiLocker with your Aadhaar-linked mobile number, verify the OTP and set a 6-digit PIN.
2. Use "Search Documents" and look for "Academic Bank of Credits".
3. Choose the **APAAR/ABC ID Card** option.
4. Enter your details: identity type, admission year, institution name and course.
5. Tick the consent box to share your Aadhaar details, then click "Get Document".
6. Your card appears in "Issued Documents". Download it as a PDF and share the number with your college.

You can also start from abc.gov.in (My Account → Student). PIB says students in remote areas can get help at their nearest Common Service Centre.

## Fixing a name or date of birth mismatch

Most failed APAAR IDs come from data that does not match. For example, "Priya Sharma" on Aadhaar may be "Priya Kumari Sharma" in school records. The ID will not generate until both records agree. A July 2025 Lok Sabha reply shows how common such mismatches are: in Maharashtra alone, over 2 lakh students failed Aadhaar validation in UDISE+ (the school database APAAR is built on), and the state named mismatches in name, spelling and date of birth as common reasons.

First decide which record is wrong. The birth certificate is usually the best tie-breaker.

| What is wrong | Where to fix it | What to bring |
|---|---|---|
| Aadhaar has the wrong spelling or date of birth | Aadhaar enrolment/update centre (UIDAI) | Birth certificate or other proof that UIDAI accepts |
| School records are wrong | Written application to the principal | Birth certificate, old school records, and an affidavit or gazette notice if needed |
| Both are right but formatted differently (initials, surname order) | Ask the school to update UDISE+ to match Aadhaar exactly | Copy of Aadhaar |

Once the record is corrected, the school updates UDISE+ and submits the APAAR request again. For CBSE students, this matters even though APAAR is optional for the 2027 boards: under the September 2026 circular, if a school does enter an APAAR ID in the LOC, the student's name must match the ID. Fix any mismatch first, or the school may have to leave the ID out.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is APAAR ID and ABC ID the same?

Yes. According to PIB, the ABC ID used in higher education has been renamed the APAAR ID. The same 12-digit number holds your college credits in the Academic Bank of Credits.

### Can parents refuse consent for APAAR ID?

Yes. The Supreme Court's order of 20 July 2026 requires the consent form to clearly allow parents to withhold or refuse consent. Under CBSE's September 2026 circular, schools can file a 2027 board LOC without an APAAR ID.

### Can I make an APAAR ID without the school?

School students cannot. Their ID is generated by the school through UDISE+ using the PEN. College students can create it themselves through DigiLocker or abc.gov.in.

### Why is my APAAR ID not generating?

The usual cause is a mismatch in name, date of birth or gender between Aadhaar and school (UDISE+) records. Correct the wrong record first, then ask the school to resubmit the request.
