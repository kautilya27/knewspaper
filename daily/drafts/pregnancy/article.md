**Quick answer:** The most common early signs of pregnancy are a missed period, nausea (with or without vomiting), unusual tiredness, sore or swollen breasts and needing to pass urine more often. The NHS says these usually start when you are around 4 to 6 weeks pregnant, which is roughly the time a period would be due or a little after.

None of these signs proves a pregnancy on its own, and some women notice very few of them. A home pregnancy test, taken from the first day of a missed period, is the simple next step, followed by a visit to a doctor or your local ANM (auxiliary nurse midwife) to confirm the result and start antenatal care.

## Common early signs of pregnancy

Not every woman gets every symptom, and how strong they feel varies. These are the signs the NHS and the US National Institutes of Health (NICHD) list most often:

- **Missed period:** usually the first clue, though less useful if your cycle is irregular.
- **Light spotting:** a small amount of bleeding around the time your period was due, sometimes called implantation bleeding. It is much lighter than a normal period.
- **Nausea or vomiting:** often called "morning sickness", though it can happen at any time of day.
- **Tiredness:** feeling worn out after your normal daily routine.
- **Breast changes:** tender, heavy or swollen breasts, similar to the soreness some women feel before a period.
- **Passing urine more often:** without pain or burning.
- **Other changes:** constipation, more vaginal discharge (without itching or soreness), a metallic taste in the mouth, a stronger sense of smell, new food cravings or a sudden dislike of foods you usually enjoy, headaches and mood swings.

## When do pregnancy symptoms start? A rough timeline

Doctors count pregnancy weeks from the **first day of your last period**, not from conception. So "4 weeks pregnant" is about two weeks after conception, around when your next period would be due. The table combines timings from the NHS and NICHD; treat it as a guide, not a rule.

| Sign | When it may appear |
|---|---|
| Tiredness | From about 1 week after conception |
| Breast tenderness | About 1 to 2 weeks after conception |
| Light spotting | About 6 to 12 days after conception, close to the expected period |
| Missed period | Around 4 weeks of pregnancy (counted from your last period) |
| Nausea or vomiting | Anywhere from about 2 to 8 weeks after conception |
| Mood swings, frequent urination | In the first few weeks |
| Most early symptoms together | Around 4 to 6 weeks of pregnancy |

## Pregnancy or something else? Common look-alikes

Tender breasts, bloating, mood changes and tiredness are also common in the days before a period, and a late period has many possible causes. The NHS lists, among others:

- Stress
- Sudden weight loss or being overweight
- Very heavy exercise
- Hormonal contraception, such as the pill or injection
- Breastfeeding
- PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome, which the NHS now also calls PMOS)
- An overactive or underactive thyroid, diabetes and some other medical conditions
- Perimenopause, usually between the ages of 45 and 55

Burning or pain while passing urine is not a typical pregnancy sign and can point to an infection. Our guide to [UTI symptoms and causes](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/uti-symptoms-causes) explains what to look out for. Because symptoms overlap so much, a test is the only way to know.

## When to take a home pregnancy test

### How a pregnancy test works

Home pregnancy tests check your urine for a hormone called **hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin)**. According to the NHS, the body starts making it about six days after fertilisation, and levels rise over the following days. Kits are sold at chemists across India and are simple to use at home.

### The best time to test

- The NHS says most tests can be used **from the first day of a missed period**.
- If you don't know when your period is due, test **at least 21 days after you last had unprotected sex**.
- Some very sensitive kits work a little earlier, but testing too early raises the chance of a false negative.

Follow the instructions on the pack. A positive result is usually reliable; a negative one is less so, especially if you tested early. If it is negative and your period still hasn't come, test again after a few days, and see a doctor if a second test is also negative.

## Warning signs that need urgent medical care

Some symptoms in early pregnancy can point to a serious problem. Go to the nearest hospital or emergency department, or call an ambulance, if you have:

- **Sharp, sudden or severe tummy pain, especially low down on one side**, with or without vaginal bleeding or brown watery discharge. Pain in the tip of the shoulder, feeling very dizzy or faint, or looking very pale make this more urgent. Discomfort when peeing or pooing can also occur. The NHS lists these as possible signs of an **ectopic pregnancy**, where the pregnancy grows outside the womb, usually between weeks 4 and 12. It needs emergency care.
- **Heavy vaginal bleeding**, or any bleeding with severe pain, dizziness or fainting.
- **Vomiting so often that you cannot keep food or drink down**, or signs of dehydration (strong thirst, dizziness, passing only a little dark urine) or weight loss. This can be **hyperemesis gravidarum**, a severe form of pregnancy sickness that needs prompt treatment.
- **High fever**, a **severe headache with blurred vision**, fits, or swelling all over the body. These are among the danger signs on India's Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card.

Even light bleeding or spotting during pregnancy should be checked by a doctor the same day. It is not always serious, but only an examination can tell.

## What to do next in India after a positive test

### 1. Confirm the pregnancy and register early

Visit a gynaecologist, your nearest government health centre (sub-centre, PHC or CHC) or your area's ANM or ASHA. The MCP card used across India advises women to **register with the health centre in the first trimester**. The WHO also recommends that the first antenatal contact take place within the first 12 weeks of pregnancy.

At registration you will get an **MCP card**, which records your check-ups, tests and vaccinations. Take it to every visit.

### 2. Use the free government services

- **PMSMA (Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan):** a free antenatal check-up, including tests and medicines, on the **9th of every month** at designated government health facilities. It is meant for women in the second and third trimesters, so it adds to your regular check-ups rather than replacing them. Details are on the [official PMSMA website](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/).
- **JSSK (Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram):** entitles every pregnant woman to free delivery in public health institutions, including caesarean section, along with free medicines, tests, diet, blood and transport.
- **JSY (Janani Suraksha Yojana):** a cash-assistance scheme that encourages delivery in a health facility. Ask your ASHA or ANM about eligibility in your state.

### 3. Look after your health from the start

- **Talk to your doctor about every medicine you take**, including ones bought over the counter, ayurvedic or home remedies, before continuing them.
- **Folic acid:** doctors usually advise taking folic acid while trying to conceive and in the early weeks of pregnancy, as it lowers the risk of certain birth defects of the brain and spine. Your doctor will decide the right dose for you, since some women need a higher one.
- **Eat well:** good nutrition matters from the first weeks. Our article on [the importance of a well-balanced diet](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/the-importance-of-a-well-balanced-diet) covers the basics.
- **Get your haemoglobin checked:** anaemia is common among women in India and matters more during pregnancy. Read about [anaemia symptoms and causes](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/anemia-symptoms-causes).
- **Avoid tobacco and alcohol**, and rest when you feel tired.

If the pregnancy was not planned or you feel unsure, you can talk privately to a doctor about your options and the support available.

## Frequently asked questions

### How soon after sex can I know if I'm pregnant?

Most home tests give a reliable result from the first day of a missed period. If your periods are irregular, test at least 21 days after unprotected sex. Testing earlier can give a false negative.

### Can I have pregnancy symptoms but a negative test?

Yes. You may have tested too early, or the symptoms may be due to something else, such as an approaching period or stress. Test again after a few days, and see a doctor if your period still hasn't come.

### Is light bleeding in early pregnancy normal?

Light spotting around the time your period is due can happen in early pregnancy. However, any bleeding during pregnancy should be checked by a doctor. Heavy bleeding or bleeding with severe pain needs emergency care.

### Is it possible to be pregnant without any symptoms?

Yes. Some women notice few or no symptoms in early pregnancy, which is why a missed period followed by a test is the most dependable early check.
