**Quick answer:** Carpet area is the floor you can actually use inside the flat, measured as RERA defines it. Built-up area adds the external walls and usually the balcony. Super built-up area (or "super area") adds your share of lobbies, lifts, staircases, the clubhouse and other common spaces. Only carpet area has a legal definition. It is in section 2(k) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, and it is the figure your agreement for sale should price.

The gap can be large. A property consultancy, Anarock, reported that across India's top seven cities the average "loading" (the share of super area that is not carpet area) rose from 31% in 2019 to 40% in early 2025. On that average, a buyer of a 1,000 sq ft super area flat gets about 600 sq ft of usable floor. The worked example below shows how to turn a builder's super-area quote into a price per usable square foot.

## The three areas at a glance

| Measure | What it includes | Defined in law? |
|---|---|---|
| Carpet area | Usable floor inside the flat, including internal partition walls | Yes, RERA section 2(k) |
| Built-up area | Carpet area plus external walls, and usually the exclusive balcony or terrace | No, market term |
| Super built-up area | Built-up area plus a proportionate share of common areas | No, market term |

Because only carpet area is defined, it is the one figure that means the same thing in every RERA-registered project.

## What RERA says carpet area is

Section 2(k) of the RERA Act defines carpet area as the net usable floor area of an apartment. It **excludes**:

- the area covered by the external walls,
- areas under service shafts,
- the exclusive balcony or verandah area, and
- the exclusive open terrace area.

It **includes** the area covered by the internal partition walls. So the wall between your bedroom and living room counts, but the outer walls of the flat do not. Kitchens and toilets count too.

A balcony or terrace is "exclusive" when it is attached to the flat and meant only for that buyer's use. It is not part of carpet area, but it is not hidden either: section 4(2)(h) requires the builder, when registering a project, to disclose the number, type and carpet area of the apartments along with their exclusive balcony, verandah and open terrace areas. You can read the full text in the [RERA Act on India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19012/1/reraact.pdf).

## Is a flat sold on carpet area by law?

The Act requires carpet area in the disclosures; the pricing rule comes from state RERA rules. Their model agreements for sale state the total price "based on the carpet area", with the proportionate cost of common areas included in that price. You still pay for the lobby and clubhouse, but the price is expressed against the floor you get.

Some regulators have gone further. The Haryana Real Estate Regulatory Authority, Gurugram, notified regulations in May 2021 that treat selling on super area, covered area or "sale area" as an unfair practice. Under them, conveyance deeds in its jurisdiction are registered on carpet area only.

Sales staff may still quote a rate per super sq ft; the RERA carpet area of that exact unit is the figure to compare.

## Worked example: converting a super-area quote

Two illustrative projects in the same area:

- **Project A:** 1,250 sq ft super area at ₹8,000 per sq ft. Price: ₹1 crore. RERA carpet area: 850 sq ft.
- **Project B:** 1,200 sq ft super area at ₹8,500 per sq ft. Price: ₹1.02 crore. RERA carpet area: 900 sq ft.

On the brochure rate, A looks cheaper. Now divide the price by the carpet area.

| | Project A | Project B |
|---|---|---|
| Total price | ₹1,00,00,000 | ₹1,02,00,000 |
| Super area | 1,250 sq ft | 1,200 sq ft |
| Carpet area | 850 sq ft | 900 sq ft |
| Loading (% of super area) | 32% | 25% |
| Loading (% of carpet area) | about 47% | about 33% |
| Price per super sq ft | ₹8,000 | ₹8,500 |
| **Price per carpet sq ft** | **about ₹11,765** | **about ₹11,333** |

Project B costs ₹2 lakh more, but each usable square foot is about ₹430 cheaper, and you get 50 sq ft more floor.

### How the loading figures are worked out

The loading is the difference between super and carpet area: 400 sq ft for A and 300 sq ft for B. Divided by super area, that is 32% and 25%; divided by carpet area, about 47% and 33%.

Both conventions are in use, so "30% loading" can mean different things. Anarock's 40% figure is measured against super area. Always ask "per cent of what?"

### What if you only have the super area?

At Anarock's reported 40% average, a 1,250 sq ft super area flat would have about 750 sq ft of carpet area. Anarock put the early-2025 averages at 43% in the Mumbai region and 36% in Chennai, the highest and lowest of the seven cities. These are market averages, not legal limits; the real number is the one in the RERA registration and your agreement.

### Square feet or square metres?

Some state portals, including MahaRERA, show carpet area in square metres. One square metre is 10.764 sq ft, so Project A's 850 sq ft is about 79 sq m and Project B's 900 sq ft is about 83.6 sq m.

## The 3% variation clause, and why it is not uniform

Final carpet area is confirmed after construction, when the occupancy certificate is issued. If it is smaller than promised, the builder refunds the excess with interest; if larger, it can be billed at the next payment milestone, both at the agreed rate per square foot.

The tolerance depends on your state. In Maharashtra, MahaRERA's Order No. 38 of 2022 (13 December 2022) made the area clause non-negotiable. It caps the variation at 3%, and a shortfall within the cap must be refunded with interest within 45 days. Other states' forms differ. Bihar's agreement format, for example, has no percentage cap. One agreement filed on the Haryana RERA portal allows an increase of up to 5% and gives 90 days for refunds. Read the area clause in your own state's form rather than assuming 3%.

**Example:** Project A is priced at about ₹11,765 per carpet sq ft. If the final carpet area comes out at 830 sq ft instead of 850 (a 2.35% shortfall), the 20 sq ft difference is worth about ₹2.35 lakh. Under a clause like Maharashtra's, that money comes back with interest.

## How to check carpet area on a state RERA portal

1. Find the project's RERA registration number. Section 11(2) of the Act requires it on every advertisement and brochure, along with the authority's website address.
2. Open your state RERA website (for example MahaRERA, UP RERA or K-RERA) and search for the project by name or registration number.
3. In the project details, find the apartment summary, which usually lists each unit type with its carpet area and any exclusive balcony or terrace area.
4. Open the uploaded sanctioned plans and the builder's draft agreement for sale. Check that the carpet area for your unit type matches the brochure and the quote.
5. Note the timing: under section 13(1), a builder cannot take more than 10% of the flat's cost before a written agreement for sale is registered, so any mismatch is best raised before that stage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a balcony included in carpet area under RERA?

No. Section 2(k) excludes the exclusive balcony or verandah and the exclusive open terrace from carpet area. The builder must disclose those areas separately under section 4(2)(h).

### What is a normal loading factor in India?

There is no legal norm. Anarock reported an average of 40% of super area across the top seven cities in early 2025, up from 31% in 2019. Individual projects vary, so rely on the RERA carpet area for the unit you are buying.

### Are internal walls counted in carpet area?

Yes. The RERA definition includes the area under internal partition walls and excludes only the external walls, service shafts, exclusive balconies or verandahs and exclusive open terraces.

### How do I convert carpet area from square metres to square feet?

Multiply by 10.764. For example, 60 sq m is about 646 sq ft. To go the other way, divide square feet by 10.764.
