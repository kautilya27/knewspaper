# Notes: positive-thinking (plan #56)

## Sources for key facts
- Mayo Clinic, "Positive thinking: Stop negative self-talk to reduce stress" (competitor; intent and attributed claims only): definition, self-talk patterns, reasons unclear, coping and healthier-lifestyle explanations. https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/stress-management/in-depth/positive-thinking/art-20043950
- Rozanski et al., JAMA Netw Open 2019 (15 studies, 229,391 people, mean follow-up 13.8 years; RR 0.65 for CV events, 0.86 for all-cause mortality; I² 87.4% / 73.2%; benefit of interventions still to be evaluated): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31560385/
- Lee et al., PNAS 2019 (69,744 women and 1,429 men; 14.9% / 10.9% longer lifespan; 1.5 / 1.7 times the odds of reaching 85; reverse causality noted in full text): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6744861
- Malouff & Schutte, J Positive Psychology (online 2016): 29 RCTs, >3,000 people, a small effect, Best Possible Self did best, effect fades. Source is the BPS Research Digest summary: https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/its-possible-learn-be-more-optimistic
- Oettingen & Reininger 2016, Social and Personality Psychology Compass. Covers Oettingen & Wadden 1991 (weight loss at 3 months and 1 year), Oettingen & Mayer 2002 and Kappes et al. 2012 (effort and academic success), Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016 (depressive symptoms 1–7 months later, 4 studies), and the WOOP steps: https://www.psy.uni-hamburg.de/arbeitsbereiche/paedagogische-psychologie-und-motivation/personen/oettingen-gabriele/dokumente/oettingen-reininger-2016.pdf
- Fantasy "drains energy" explanation: WEF/Quartz summary of Oettingen's work. https://www.weforum.org/stories/leadership/positive-thinking-leads-to-success-doesnt-it/
- Wang, Wang & Gai 2021, Frontiers in Psychology (MCII meta-analysis: 21 studies, 15,907 participants, g = 0.336, possible publication bias): https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full
- Ford, Lam, John & Mauss, JPSP (vol 115; online 2017, print 2018): 3 studies, >1,300 adults, 6-month diary follow-up. Source is the Greater Good summary: https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/feeling_bad_about_feeling_bad_can_make_you_feel_worse
- NHS Every Mind Matters, "Catch it, check it, change it": https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/reframing-unhelpful-thoughts
- Tele-MANAS: 14416, free and 24x7, 20 languages (PIB Oct 2024 document): https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc20241013415701.pdf. The 1800-891-4416 number is listed by IIT Kanpur: https://iitk.ac.in/counsel/TeleManas.php

## Information gain vs top results (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, Healthline, WebMD, Psychology Today)
- The top results list benefits without rating the evidence. Ours gives a study-by-study table with sizes, effect figures and limits (observational design, reverse causation, effects that fade).
- None of the top results covers the research on positive fantasies (Oettingen) or WOOP/mental contrasting with the meta-analysis figure. That research is the main correction to "just think positive".
- Cleveland Clinic mentions toxic positivity without evidence. Ours links it to the Ford acceptance research and the phrases Indian families commonly use.
- Ours says plainly that positive thinking is not a treatment, gives a when-to-see-a-doctor list and Indian helplines, and uses Indian examples (NEET revision WOOP, comparisons from relatives).
- Overlap with our own articles: we only link to the self-confidence article's affirmations finding and to the WHO skills in stress-management rather than repeating them.
