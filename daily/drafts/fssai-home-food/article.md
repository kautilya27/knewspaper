**Quick answer:** If you bake, cook or pack food at home and sell it, you need FSSAI registration. Since 1 April 2026, basic registration covers businesses with an annual turnover of up to ₹1.5 crore (it used to be ₹12 lakh). The government fee is ₹100 a year, you apply on the FoSCoS portal using Form A, and a registration issued on or after 1 April 2026 no longer has to be renewed.

A State licence (₹5,000 a year) is needed only above ₹1.5 crore, or in a category with no registration option. The trap for tiffin services is "Caterer": pick it and FoSCoS asks for a State licence even at ₹2 lakh a year. Many guides still quote the old ₹12 lakh limit.

## What changed in 2026

FSSAI notified the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Amendment Regulations on 10 March 2026. A follow-up order dated 13 March 2026 changed the turnover limits from 1 April 2026. According to [FSSAI's press release](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Press%20Release_FSSAI%20Reforms_130326.pdf), the changes are:

| Category | Turnover before 1 April 2026 | Turnover from 1 April 2026 | Fee per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic registration (Form A) | Up to ₹12 lakh | Up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹100 |
| State licence (Form B) | ₹12 lakh to ₹20 crore | Above ₹1.5 crore, up to ₹50 crore | ₹5,000 for most kinds of business |
| Central licence (Form B) | Above ₹20 crore | Above ₹50 crore | ₹7,500 |

Fees are from the FoSCoS eligibility sheet revised in April 2026. FSSAI's FAQs of 27 March 2026, its 13 March press release and the amended regulations add three points:

- **No renewal.** Registrations issued from 1 April 2026 stay valid until suspended, cancelled or surrendered. You still pay the yearly fee (several years at once is allowed); missing it can get you suspended.
- **Automatic migration.** If you took a State licence earlier because you crossed ₹12 lakh, FoSCoS moves you to registration on your self-declaration. Your number stays the same, there's no migration fee, and the licence fee already paid is adjusted.
- **No pre-inspection.** FSSAI says micro and small businesses get instant registration; inspections now depend on risk.

## Which category to choose on FoSCoS

The "kind of business" you pick on FoSCoS decides whether you get registration or a licence. Here is how FSSAI's April 2026 categories map to home businesses:

| Your business | FoSCoS kind of business | Up to ₹1.5 crore turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Tiffin or dabba service delivering packed meals | Food Services → Home Based Canteens/Dabba Wallas ("distribution of packed meals, usually packed lunch") | Registration, ₹100 |
| Cooking full meals for parties and events | Food Services → Caterer ("preparation… of food for consumption of a group") | State licence, ₹5,000, whatever your turnover |
| Home baker selling packed cookies, cakes or rusks | Manufacturer → General manufacturing (food category "Bakery wares") | Registration, ₹100 |
| Home kitchen cooking dishes to order for delivery apps | Food Services → Restaurants | Registration, ₹100 |
| Pickles, masalas, snacks in jars or pouches | Manufacturer → General manufacturing (pick the right food category) | Registration, ₹100 |

- **Pick what you actually do, and list every food category.** FSSAI's FAQs say making a product not endorsed on your licence counts as doing business without one. Add new items (say, chocolates) through a modification on FoSCoS.
- **Some products always need a Central licence.** Health supplements, nutraceuticals and "proprietary food" (no set FSSAI standard) need one at any turnover, per the same sheet.
- **Group members are exempt.** You don't need your own registration if you cook as a registered member of a cooperative, Mahila Udyog or self-help group that holds FSSAI registration, says FSSAI.

## Steps to register on FoSCoS

1. On foscos.fssai.gov.in, choose "Apply for Licence/Registration" and select your state and district.
2. Pick the kind of business and expected turnover; the portal shows registration or licence.
3. Sign up with mobile and email, and fill Form A with your home address as premises, food categories and turnover.
4. Upload a photo and photo ID (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID or passport), plus a hygiene self-declaration. Some states also ask for a local-body NOC.
5. Pay ₹100 per year chosen.
6. You get a 14-digit number starting with 2, on a certificate with a QR code.

The authority has 7 days to grant registration (if it orders an inspection, that happens within 30 days). Answer any query within 30 days or the application lapses. Form B for a State licence needs more: a kitchen layout plan, equipment list and, for manufacturers, a water test report.

## Kitchen basics FSSAI expects

Registration means following Schedule 4, Part I of the licensing regulations, hygiene rules for small businesses. In short:

- Keep the cooking area clean, well lit and ventilated, with surfaces that are easy to wash.
- Use drinking-quality (potable) water.
- Keep pests out with nets or screens on windows. Don't spray insecticide while cooking, and store cleaning chemicals away from food.
- Wear a clean apron and head covering, wash hands with soap, cover cuts, and keep anyone with an infectious illness away from food.
- No smoking, chewing or spitting near food; keep food covered.

Since August 2025, FSSAI also asks food businesses to display the certificate and its QR code (linked to the Food Safety Connect complaints app), including on your website and online ordering pages.

## Labels if you sell packed food

Cookie jars, pickle bottles and masala pouches are prepackaged food, so the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020 apply. Labels need, among other things:

- The name of the food and a list of ingredients
- Nutritional information (not needed for single-ingredient or minimally processed items)
- The green or brown veg/non-veg logo on the front of the pack, even on small packs
- An allergen declaration (for example, milk, nuts, gluten)
- Your name and address, the FSSAI logo and your 14-digit number
- A "best before" or "use by" date, along with net quantity

A same-day tiffin isn't prepackaged in this sense, but the hygiene rules still apply.

## Selling on Swiggy, Zomato or your own website

Listing on Swiggy or Zomato needs no extra FSSAI licence: the platform holds the e-commerce licence, and your registration is enough if the category and turnover fit. But you must have one. FSSAI's advisory of 3 December 2024 says no e-commerce food business may list a seller "without displaying their valid FSSAI License or Registration."

It also says online claims ("sugar-free") must match your physical label. For how the two apps compare, see our [Zomato vs Swiggy](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/zomato-or-swiggy) comparison.

Your **own website or app** that takes orders is different. FSSAI's licensing FAQs say a business selling through its own e-commerce platform also needs an e-commerce licence, which is a Central licence at ₹7,500 a year. They don't address orders over WhatsApp or Instagram, so ask your district Designated Officer (food safety) before building a store. If you are still deciding whether to run this as a side gig or a proper business, see our [freelancing vs entrepreneurship](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/freelancing-vs-entrepreneurship) guide.

## What happens if you skip it

The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 has two relevant provisions:

- **Section 63:** If your business needs a licence and you run it without one, the punishment can be up to six months in jail and a fine of up to ₹5 lakh. The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026 changed some FSS Act penalties from 1 October 2026 but did not change this one.
- **Section 58:** Breaking the Act or its regulations where no specific penalty is set can attract a penalty of up to ₹2 lakh.

In practice, without a number you can't get onto delivery apps. For students testing a food idea, add this ₹100 step before you start, alongside the options in our guide to [side hustles for college students](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/side-hustles-for-college-students).

FSSAI registration is separate from GST, Udyam or municipal trade licences. This article explains the rules and is not legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is FSSAI registration mandatory for a home baker earning very little?

Yes. There is no turnover below which a home food seller is exempt, unless you sell as a member of a registered cooperative or self-help group that holds its own FSSAI registration. Registration covers everything up to ₹1.5 crore a year and costs ₹100 a year.

### What is the FSSAI registration fee for a home food business in 2026?

₹100 a year for basic registration, payable for several years at once; a State licence is ₹5,000 a year for most categories. Agents charge extra, but you can apply yourself on FoSCoS.

### Do I need to renew my FSSAI registration?

Not if it was issued on or after 1 April 2026. Registrations are now valid until suspended, cancelled or surrendered. You must keep paying the yearly fee on time, or the registration can be suspended.

### Which is better for a tiffin service: "Dabba Wallas" or "Caterer" on FoSCoS?

If you deliver packed meals, the official category is "Home Based Canteens/Dabba Wallas", which gives registration for up to ₹1.5 crore turnover. "Caterer" is for food prepared for a group, such as events, and needs at least a State licence at any turnover.
