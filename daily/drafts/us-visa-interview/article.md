**Quick answer:** Most US visa interview questions for Indian applicants fall into five groups: why you are travelling, what brings you back to India, how the trip is paid for, who is sponsoring or inviting you, and (for students) your study plans. Behind every question the officer is testing one thing set by US law: whether you have shown that you qualify for the visa and will leave the US after a temporary stay.

So there is no "magic answer" to learn. Know your own facts, answer briefly and truthfully, and make sure what you say matches your DS-160. For forms, fees and appointments, see our guide on [how to apply for a US visa from India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-apply-for-us-visa). Rules below were checked against US State Department pages on 28 November 2026.

## What the officer is really deciding: Section 214(b)

According to the State Department's [visa denials page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html), section 214(b) of the Immigration and Nationality Act presumes that every visitor or student applicant intends to immigrate. You have to overcome that presumption by showing "strong ties" to your home country, such as a job, a home and family, and by showing that you fit the visa category. The officer weighs your circumstances, travel plans, finances and ties outside the US.

The State Department's Foreign Affairs Manual (9 FAM 403.10) puts the burden of proof on the applicant. It also tells officers that an applicant's demographic profile "is not grounds for a visa refusal": a young, single or first-time traveller can qualify by explaining their own situation clearly. (According to the State Department's visa-denials page, H-1B and L applicants do not have to show ties under 214(b).)

## Questions about the purpose of your trip

| Common question | What the officer is checking | How to answer well |
|---|---|---|
| Why are you going to the US? | That the purpose fits a B1/B2 visa (tourism, family visit, medical treatment, business meetings) | One clear sentence: what, where, with whom |
| How long will you stay? | That the stay is temporary and sensible for the purpose | A specific period that matches your leave, tickets or event dates |
| Have you been to the US or other countries before? | Your record of travelling and returning on time | State the trips plainly, including any earlier refusal |

A B1/B2 visa does not allow work or study, so if that is your real plan, apply for the right visa. The US Embassy in India said on 11 December 2025 that it will deny tourist visas where officers believe the main purpose is to give birth in the US so the child gets citizenship.

## Questions about your ties to India

| Common question | What the officer is checking | How to answer well |
|---|---|---|
| What do you do? Where do you work? | Whether you have a job, business or studies to return to | Your role, employer and how long you've been there |
| Who lives with you in India? | Family ties that bring you back | Who is staying behind (spouse, children, parents) |
| Why will you come back? | The heart of 214(b) | Your real reasons, in your own words |

Ties are not only property or a big salary: the State Department lists a job, a home and relationships with family and friends. A retired parent visiting a child can point to a spouse, home and life in their Indian city. Don't exaggerate a job title or promise a return date you can't keep.

## Questions about money and who is paying

Expect "Who is paying for this trip?" and "What do you earn?". The State Department says you may be asked for evidence that you can pay all costs, or that someone else will pay some or all of them.

Answer with figures you actually know, such as your salary in lakh per year or the rough trip budget in rupees. Never park borrowed money in your account just to show a balance.

## Questions about sponsors and relatives in the US

"Do you have relatives in the US?" is common, especially for parents visiting children who work or study there. The officer is checking your plans and whether you might stay on.

- Answer fully: the relative, their status (student, H-1B, Green Card holder, citizen) and what they do. Having a relative is not a problem; hiding one is.
- Don't rely on letters. The State Department says a letter of invitation or Affidavit of Support is not needed for a visitor visa and "is not one of the factors" used to decide.

## F-1 questions about your study plans

The Foreign Affairs Manual (9 FAM 402.5) says officers check that you are a genuine student with readily available funds for the first year and adequate funds for later years. It also says that because most student applicants are young, "they are not expected to have a long-range plan"; the officer looks at your present intent to leave the US after your course.

| Common question | What the officer is checking |
|---|---|
| Why this university? Why this course? | That you chose the course yourself and know what you'll study |
| Who is funding your studies? | First-year funds in hand and a credible plan for the rest |
| What will you do after you graduate? | Your present plan, not a guarantee about the next ten years |

Know your I-20: school, programme, start date and costs. If it says English proficiency is required and no English classes are arranged, the manual says the interview must be in English and you may be asked to read aloud. If the school admitted you on TOEFL or IELTS scores, the officer should not re-judge that unless fraud is suspected.

**Social media:** Since 18 June 2025 the State Department has reviewed the online presence of F, M and J applicants and asked them to set all social media profiles to "public". Later announcements added H-1B, H-4 and other categories; as of November 2026, B1/B2 visitors are not on the list.

## What to carry

The State Department lists the basics: a passport valid for six months beyond your stay, the DS-160 confirmation page, the fee receipt if needed, and a photo if the online upload failed. The full checklist is in our [US visa application guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-apply-for-us-visa). Keep supporting papers (salary slips, ITRs, bank statements; I-20 and funding proof for students) in a simple folder, and show one only when the officer asks.

## What happens on the day

Interviews are held at the US Embassy in New Delhi and the consulates in Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Kolkata, usually after biometrics at a Visa Application Centre. Check your appointment letter for items not allowed inside.

- **Everyone attends in person.** Since the interview-waiver changes of 2 September 2025 (updated again from 1 October 2025), nearly all applicants need an interview, including children. The US Embassy in India reminded parents in August 2026 that the officer must see applicants "of every age", even newborns.
- **Apply where you live.** The State Department asks applicants to book in their country of nationality or residence.
- **You usually hear the outcome at the end of the interview.** If approved, the officer usually keeps your passport. A 221(g) slip means more documents or administrative processing are needed; the State Department gives you one year to send what is asked before you must reapply. A 214(b) refusal comes with a written letter, which the Foreign Affairs Manual requires.

If you don't understand a question, ask the officer to repeat it. Unlike a [job interview](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/interview-questions-and-answers), you are not selling yourself; you only need to be accurate and consistent.

## If you are refused under 214(b)

According to the State Department, a 214(b) refusal applies to that application only. It is not a permanent ban, and there is no appeal or waiver. To try again you file a new DS-160, pay the fee again and attend a new interview.

Reapplying makes sense when something has really changed, or when you have important information you didn't present the first time: a new job, a clearer trip purpose, or full funding for your course. The same facts a week later give the officer nothing new to consider.

Never change your story to get a different answer. The State Department warns that wilful misrepresentation of a material fact "may result in the permanent refusal of a visa". A 214(b) refusal can be overcome later; a misrepresentation finding can follow you permanently.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do children have to attend the US visa interview?

Yes. The US Embassy in India said in August 2026 that the in-person interview is mandatory at every age, so a parent cannot attend on a child's behalf.

### Can I take my documents to the interview?

Yes. The State Department says officers may ask for evidence of your trip purpose, ties and ability to pay, so carry it and hand it over when asked.

### Will I be refused if I have no foreign travel history?

Not on that ground alone. The State Department says officers weigh your circumstances, travel plans, finances and ties together.

### How soon can I reapply after a 214(b) refusal?

There is no fixed waiting period. Reapply when your circumstances have changed or you have new information to present, because the State Department says that is what it looks for.
