**Quick answer:** BODMAS is the order in which you solve a maths expression that has more than one operation. It stands for **B**rackets, **O**rders (powers and roots, and in Indian exams also "of"), **D**ivision, **M**ultiplication, **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction. Division and multiplication have equal rank, and so do addition and subtraction, so each pair is worked **from left to right**.

So 18 − 6 ÷ 3 × 2 + 4 is not solved letter by letter. You divide and multiply first, left to right (6 ÷ 3 = 2, then 2 × 2 = 4), then add and subtract left to right: 18 − 4 + 4 = **18**.

## What BODMAS stands for

| Letter | Stands for | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| B | Brackets | Solve everything inside brackets first, innermost first |
| O | Orders (and "of") | Powers and roots, such as 3² or √16. In Indian simplification sums, "of" also goes here |
| D, M | Division, Multiplication | Equal rank: work left to right |
| A, S | Addition, Subtraction | Equal rank: work left to right |

Without an agreed order, one expression could give several answers. Take 30 + 5 × 4. Multiply first and you get 50; add first and you get 140. BODMAS says multiplication comes first, so the answer is 50.

## BODMAS, BIDMAS and PEMDAS: same rule, different names

You may meet other names for the same rule:

- **BODMAS:** the usual name in Indian schools and coaching material.
- **BIDMAS:** used in many UK schools. The "I" stands for Indices (powers).
- **PEMDAS:** used in the US. Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication, Division, Addition, Subtraction.

The letters differ, but the maths is the same. The order of the letters can mislead you, though. PEMDAS puts M before D and BODMAS puts D before M, yet neither means one comes first. Both are done together, left to right.

## The left-to-right rule most students miss

This is where most mistakes happen. Look at 24 ÷ 4 × 2.

- **Correct:** left to right, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, then 6 × 2 = **12**.
- **Wrong:** "D comes before M, but M is bigger, so multiply first", 4 × 2 = 8, then 24 ÷ 8 = 3.

The same goes for subtraction. 10 − 4 − 2 is 6 − 2 = **4**, not 10 − 2 = 8. If you want a different order, you must write brackets: 10 − (4 − 2) = 8.

## The meaning of "of" in Indian exams

In Indian textbooks and in SSC, bank and railway "simplification" questions, the word **"of" means multiply**. For example, ½ of 40 = 20.

It also gets a higher rank than ordinary ÷ and ×. Exam solutions work out "of" **before** division and multiplication, which is why many coaching books treat "O" as "of". Compare:

- 36 ÷ 3 **of** 4 = 36 ÷ 12 = **3**
- 36 ÷ 3 **×** 4 = 12 × 4 = **48**

The two look alike but give very different answers. Exam setters use exactly this to catch students who rush.

## Types of brackets and the order to remove them

Indian textbooks and exam books use four kinds of grouping symbol. When they are nested, you work from the inside out:

1. **Bar or vinculum:** a line drawn over numbers, such as a bar over 9 − 4. It groups them like a bracket, so you solve it first.
2. **Round brackets ( )**, also called parentheses.
3. **Curly brackets { }**, also called braces.
4. **Square brackets [ ]**, the outermost.

Some exam books call this order **VBODMAS**, with V for vinculum.

## Worked examples, step by step

### Example 1: nested brackets

Simplify 50 − [20 + {12 − (3 + 5) ÷ 2}].

1. Round brackets: 3 + 5 = 8.
2. Inside the curly brackets, divide first: 8 ÷ 2 = 4, then 12 − 4 = 8.
3. Square brackets: 20 + 8 = 28.
4. Finally: 50 − 28 = **22**.

### Example 2: powers

Simplify 2 + 3² × 4.

1. Orders: 3² = 9.
2. Multiply: 9 × 4 = 36.
3. Add: 2 + 36 = **38**.

Compare (2 + 3)², which is 5² = **25**. The bracket changes everything.

### Example 3: an exam-style sum with "of"

Simplify 100 − ½ of 40 + 8 ÷ 4 × 3.

1. "Of" first: ½ of 40 = 20.
2. Division and multiplication, left to right: 8 ÷ 4 = 2, then 2 × 3 = 6.
3. Addition and subtraction, left to right: 100 − 20 = 80, then 80 + 6 = **86**.

## Where BODMAS appears in NCERT books

The new NCERT book for Class 7, **Ganita Prakash**, covers the idea in **Chapter 2, "Arithmetic Expressions"**. Interestingly, it does not use the word BODMAS. Instead, it teaches two ideas:

- **Brackets:** find the value inside the brackets before doing anything else.
- **Terms:** split an expression into parts separated by + signs (turning each subtraction into adding a negative), work out each term, then add.

In 30 + 5 × 4, the terms are 30 and 5 × 4. You find each term (30 and 20), then add them to get 50. That is the same answer BODMAS gives, explained in a way that shows *why* multiplication is done first. The chapter also covers removing brackets, including the rule that a minus sign before a bracket changes the sign of every term inside it.

You can read the chapter free on the [NCERT website](https://www.ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/gegp102.pdf). Neat step-by-step working matters in board exams too; see [how to write answers in CBSE board exams](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-write-answers-in-cbse-board-exams).

## The viral 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2) puzzle: why people disagree

Every few months a post asks: what is 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2)? The comments split between 9 and 1. Here is the honest answer: **the expression is written ambiguously**, and both camps follow a real convention.

- **Answer 9:** Solve the bracket (3), then treat 2(3) as ordinary multiplication. Working left to right, 6 ÷ 2 = 3, then 3 × 3 = 9.
- **Answer 1:** Many people, and some calculators, treat a number written right next to a bracket, as in 2(3), as one tightly bound unit. Then it is 6 ÷ 6 = 1.

Strict BODMAS, with every multiplication sign written out, gives 9: 6 ÷ 2 × (1 + 2) = 9. But real mathematicians avoid writing it this way at all. They would write 6/2 × 3 or 6/(2 × 3), or use a fraction bar, and the question disappears. The lesson for exams: if you write the question, use clear brackets. If you are given an unclear one, follow left to right.

## Calculators, Excel and Python

Not every tool follows the same rules, so check before you trust an answer.

- **Basic calculators** (the kind in a kirana shop) often calculate as you press each key. Typing 2 + 3 × 4 = may show 20 instead of 14. Scientific calculators follow the order of operations.
- **Microsoft Excel** follows precedence and works operators of equal rank left to right. But in Excel, the **negative sign comes before powers**. So =-2^2 gives **4**, while in normal maths −2² = −4. Write =-(2^2) if you mean −4.
- **Python** works left to right for × and ÷, but its power operator (written as two asterisks) binds tighter than a minus on its left, so minus 3 to the power 2 gives **−9**, matching normal maths. Python also works chained powers right to left: 2 to the power 3 to the power 2 is 2⁹ = 512, not 8² = 64.

## Common BODMAS mistakes to avoid

- Doing multiplication before division just because M looks "stronger". Go left to right.
- Doing addition before subtraction. Again, left to right.
- Forgetting that "of" means multiply, and that in Indian exam convention it comes before ÷ and ×.
- Removing a bracket with a minus sign in front without changing the signs inside.
- Squaring a negative number wrongly: (−3)² = 9 but −3² = −9.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the full form of BODMAS?

BODMAS stands for Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction. "Orders" means powers and roots, and in Indian exams the "O" is also often read as "of".

### Is multiplication done before division in BODMAS?

No. Division and multiplication have the same rank, so you do whichever comes first from left to right. For example, 24 ÷ 4 × 2 = 12, not 3.

### Is BODMAS the same as PEMDAS?

Yes. Both describe the same order of operations with different letters. PEMDAS is common in the US, BIDMAS in the UK and BODMAS in India.

### What is the answer to 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2)?

Strict left-to-right BODMAS gives 9, but the expression is ambiguous because some people and calculators treat 2(1 + 2) as one unit and get 1. Clear brackets remove the confusion.
