**Quick answer:** A completion certificate (CC) confirms that a building was built according to the sanctioned plan. An occupancy certificate (OC) is the permission from the local authority that allows people to actually live in or use it. For a buyer, the OC matters more: without it, the flat may not legally be occupied, and you can face trouble getting water and power connections, a home loan or a clean resale.

Names, order of issue and issuing body vary by city, because both come from state and municipal building bylaws, not one national rule. Before you take possession, ask for both certificates and check them yourself. This article is general legal information, not legal advice.

## Occupancy certificate vs completion certificate at a glance

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) defines both terms loosely, as the certificate "by whatever name called" that the competent authority issues. That wording is there because states use different names. Delhi, for example, issues a single "occupancy-cum-completion certificate", and Haryana's building code calls it an "occupation certificate".

| Point | Completion certificate (CC) | Occupancy certificate (OC) |
|---|---|---|
| What it says | The project was built as per the sanctioned plan, layout plan and specifications (RERA section 2(q)) | The authority permits occupation of the building (RERA section 2(zf)) |
| Who issues it | Municipal corporation, development authority or other planning authority | Usually the same authority, after checks such as fire safety and services |
| What it covers | Compliance of the structure with approved plans | Fitness for living in or using the building |
| Why buyers need it | Shows the building isn't an unauthorised deviation | Needed for legal occupation, utility connections and, increasingly, loans |
| Can it be partial? | Depends on local rules | Yes, many cities issue a part OC for one tower or block |

## What the RERA Act says about OC and CC

Four provisions of the RERA Act matter most to a buyer:

- **Section 11(4)(b):** the promoter (builder) must obtain the completion certificate, the occupancy certificate or both, "as per local laws", and make them available to allottees individually or to their association. Getting the OC is the builder's job, not yours.
- **Section 17(1):** the builder must execute a registered conveyance deed and hand over physical possession. Where no local law sets a deadline, the conveyance must happen within three months of the OC being issued.
- **Section 19(10):** the buyer must take physical possession within two months of the OC being issued for the flat. Your two-month clock starts from the OC, not from a possession letter.
- **Section 18(1):** if the builder fails to complete the project or hand over possession by the agreed date, you can withdraw and get a refund with interest and compensation. If you stay in the project, you are paid interest for every month of delay.

The full Act is on the government's [India Code portal](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19012/1/reraact.pdf); interest rates and complaint forms come from each state's RERA rules.

## What courts have said

Three Supreme Court decisions shape how OC disputes are handled:

- **Samruddhi Co-operative Housing Society v. Mumbai Mahalaxmi Construction (11 January 2022):** the court held that a builder's failure to obtain an OC is a deficiency in service under consumer protection law, and that flat owners can claim compensation. In that case the residents had only temporary water and power connections and paid property tax and water charges above the normal rates.
- **Dharmendra Sharma v. Agra Development Authority (2024 INSC 667):** relying on its earlier ruling in *Debashis Sinha v. R.N.R. Enterprise* (2023), the court held that a buyer can refuse possession offered without the completion certificate and the fire safety clearance. It said the absence of these documents "vitiates the offer of possession".
- **Rajendra Kumar Barjatya v. U.P. Avas Evam Vikas Parishad (17 December 2024):** the court directed that electricity, water and sewerage connections be given only after the completion or occupation certificate is produced. Banks must verify the certificate before sanctioning a loan secured against a building. Builders must also undertake to hand over possession only after obtaining it.

## Partial OC: what it means

Large projects finish tower by tower, so authorities often issue a **part OC** for the ready portion. Delhi's Unified Building Bye-Laws, 2016 allow a part occupancy-cum-completion certificate for at least one block that is complete in all respects, from ground to terrace, with the required external approvals.

A part OC is valid for what it covers, and only that. Check that your tower and floor are named in it. Also check whether it covers the common facilities you were promised, such as the clubhouse, lifts or parking.

## How state rules differ: three examples

- **Delhi:** a single occupancy-cum-completion certificate is issued. The bylaws give the authority 20 days to decide for moderate-risk buildings and 30 days for high-risk ones, and if it doesn't reply in time, the certificate is deemed to have been issued once fees are paid. No one may occupy a building or any part of it until the certificate is granted.
- **Karnataka (Bengaluru):** after the Supreme Court's December 2024 order, many owners couldn't get power connections. The state government then used the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024 to exempt ground-plus-two or stilt-plus-three homes on plots of up to 1,200 sq ft from needing an OC, according to Deccan Herald. That permanent exemption applies only to these small homes within Greater Bengaluru. Separately, the state allowed one-time relief so that some larger homes without an OC could get power connections: a June 2026 order covered residential buildings on plots up to 2,400 sq ft completed by 31 May 2026, but applications closed on 6 July 2026, according to Deccan Herald.
- **Maharashtra:** builders must upload the planning authority's OC to the MahaRERA website before the project can be marked complete. After a forged-documents case in Kalyan-Dombivli, MahaRERA asked planning authorities in June 2025 to confirm whether they had really issued the OCs uploaded for 3,699 lapsed projects, according to the Free Press Journal. The developers had uploaded these OCs in reply to MahaRERA notices, and the authorities were given 10 days to respond.

## Buyer's checklist: how to verify OC and CC

1. **Find the project on your state's RERA website.** Search by RERA registration number, which must be printed on every advertisement under section 11(2). The project page lists approvals, and builders must update it quarterly.
2. **Download the OC and CC uploaded there.** Compare the project name, survey or plot number, tower, number of floors and date with your allotment letter.
3. **Cross-check with the issuing authority.** Look for the certificate on the municipal or development authority's building-permission portal, or ask its office in writing. Uploads can be forged.
4. **Check the scope of a part OC.** Confirm that your tower is covered and note what is excluded.
5. **Match the plans.** Compare the sanctioned plan, uploaded under RERA section 4(2), with what was built. Under section 14(2), the builder may make only minor changes without consent. A change to your own flat needs your consent, and other changes to the building or common areas need the written consent of two-thirds of the allottees. For floor areas, see [carpet area vs built-up area](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/carpet-area-vs-built-up-area).
6. **Ask for the fire safety certificate** and other clearances the OC refers to.
7. **Tell your lender.** Banks now verify these certificates before lending; see [documents for your home loan application](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/essential-documents-for-your-home-loan-application).

## If possession is offered without an OC

- **Don't sign away your rights.** Reply in writing, asking for the OC and noting that section 11(4)(b) requires the builder to provide it. Keep the possession letter and all emails.
- **Hold back the final payment** if your agreement links it to the OC.
- **File a complaint** with your state RERA authority under section 31, seeking either interest for the delay or a refund under section 18. Alternatively, approach a consumer commission, since the Supreme Court treats a missing OC as a deficiency in service.
- **Plan for practical problems.** These include temporary utility connections, higher charges, lender hesitation and buyers who won't purchase without an OC when you resell. Registration costs still apply when the conveyance deed is executed; see [stamp duty and registration charges](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges).

If you have already moved in, the Supreme Court has said failure to provide an OC is a continuing wrong, so a later claim is not automatically time-barred. Speak to a property lawyer before deciding which forum to approach.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which comes first, the completion certificate or the occupancy certificate?

In many cities the CC is issued first and the OC follows after further checks. Delhi issues a single combined certificate, so the order depends on local bylaws.

### Is it illegal to live in a flat without an OC?

In many places, yes. Delhi's bylaws, for example, bar anyone from occupying a building or part of it until the certificate is granted. Exceptions exist, such as Greater Bengaluru's permanent exemption for small homes on plots of up to 1,200 sq ft.

### Can I get a home loan for a flat without an OC?

It has become harder. In December 2024 the Supreme Court directed banks to verify the completion or occupation certificate before sanctioning loans secured against a building.

### How long do I have to take possession after the OC is issued?

Under section 19(10) of the RERA Act, the buyer must take physical possession within two months of the OC being issued for the flat.
