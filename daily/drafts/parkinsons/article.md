**Quick answer:** Parkinson's disease is a slowly worsening brain condition in which the nerve cells that make dopamine, in a part of the brain called the substantia nigra, are gradually lost. The three main symptoms, according to the NHS, are tremor, slowness of movement and muscle stiffness; the tremor usually shows when the limb is resting. In most people the exact cause is unknown; age, family history and long-term exposure to pesticides, solvents and air pollution raise the risk.

It usually starts after 50, but Indian studies have found patients developing it younger than in Western countries. There is no single test and no cure yet, but treatment controls symptoms well for many years, so a neurologist should check any slow, one-sided tremor or slowing down.

## How common is Parkinson's disease?

The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that over 8.5 million people worldwide had Parkinson's in 2019, and that its prevalence has doubled in the past 25 years. Deaths from it more than doubled between 2000 and 2019.

In India, the ICMR, PHFI and IHME India State-Level Disease Burden study (published in The Lancet Global Health in 2021) estimated about **7.71 lakh people** living with Parkinson's in 2019. Community surveys in different Indian cities have found very different rates, from about 7 to over 300 per 1 lakh people (the highest in a single community, Mumbai's Parsis), partly because of how and where they were done, so the true number is uncertain.

### Indians may get it younger

The US National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) says Parkinson's usually starts in the early to mid-60s. In GAP-India, a study of more than 4,000 patients at 18 Indian hospitals presented in 2022, the average age when movement symptoms began was **53.5 years**, and the researchers called the younger age of onset "a striking feature" compared with Western reports. A separate pan-India study of 668 young patients (Canadian Journal of Neurological Sciences, 2022) found a family history in 15% and parents who were blood relatives in 13%.

Doctors usually call it **young-onset Parkinson's** when symptoms begin before 40 or 50 (definitions vary). It is not rare in India, so a young age is not a reason to ignore the signs.

## Early symptoms of Parkinson's disease

Symptoms come on slowly, often over years, and usually start on one side of the body.

### The main movement symptoms

| Symptom | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Resting tremor | Shaking that usually starts in one hand or fingers and is most visible when the hand is relaxed in the lap; often a "pill-rolling" movement of thumb against finger |
| Slowness (bradykinesia) | Everyday tasks such as buttoning a shirt or bathing take longer; shorter, shuffling steps |
| Stiffness (rigidity) | Stiff, sometimes painful arms, legs or neck; reduced arm swing while walking |
| Balance problems | Stooped posture and falls, usually later in the illness |

### Signs that can appear years earlier

Several non-movement symptoms often come first and are easy to miss:

- **Loss of smell**, which the NHS says can start several years before other symptoms
- **Acting out dreams** (shouting, punching or kicking in sleep), known as REM sleep behaviour disorder
- **Constipation** that has no other clear reason
- **Small, cramped handwriting** (micrographia)
- **A soft, flat voice** and a face that shows less expression, which family members may notice first
- **Low mood or anxiety**, which NINDS notes can come before the movement symptoms

None of these on its own means Parkinson's: constipation and poor sleep have many common causes. It is the combination, plus a slow tremor or slowing down, that should prompt a visit.

### Later symptoms

As it progresses, people may have trouble swallowing, drooling, dizziness on standing, frequent urination at night, pain, sleep problems, and in some cases memory and thinking problems or dementia.

## What causes Parkinson's disease?

The symptoms come from losing the dopamine-producing cells of the substantia nigra; dopamine helps the brain control smooth movement. The NHS says symptoms usually appear only after about half of the activity of these nerve cells has been lost, which explains why the illness is well under way by the time it is noticed. Inside affected brain cells, clumps of a protein called alpha-synuclein (Lewy bodies) are found.

Why the cells die is not fully understood. Known risk factors include:

- **Age:** the risk rises with age.
- **Genes:** a parent or sibling with Parkinson's raises the risk. Changes in several genes (such as LRRK2, PRKN and GBA1) are linked to it, especially in young-onset cases.
- **Sex:** men are affected somewhat more often than women.
- **Environment:** WHO says exposure to air pollution, pesticides and solvents may increase risk, though the NHS notes the evidence is not yet conclusive. In GAP-India's early results, farm work, pesticide exposure and past head injury were more common among patients, though those were early, unadjusted findings.

Parkinson's is not contagious, and it is not caused by stress or anything the person did.

## Conditions that can look like Parkinson's

This is where a neurologist's examination matters most.

### Essential tremor

A very common, usually harmless tremor. According to Mayo Clinic, essential tremor is worst when *using* the hands (holding a cup, writing), often affects the head and voice, and does not cause slowness or stiffness. Parkinson's tremor is worst when the hands are *resting*.

### Medicine-induced parkinsonism

Some medicines block dopamine and can cause Parkinson's-like stiffness and slowness, typically on both sides of the body. Australian Prescriber lists antipsychotic medicines and flunarizine and cinnarizine (often prescribed in India for vertigo and [migraine](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/migraine-symptoms-causes) prevention), among others. The NHS says these symptoms usually improve once the medicine is stopped. **Never stop a prescribed medicine yourself**: tell the doctor who prescribed it about the new symptoms.

### Small strokes (vascular parkinsonism)

Damage from small strokes can cause walking problems that mainly affect the legs, often with little tremor.

> **Sudden symptoms are not Parkinson's.** Parkinson's develops over months and years. Sudden weakness on one side, a drooping face or slurred speech can be a stroke: call 108 or 112 immediately.

## How is it diagnosed?

There is no blood test or scan that confirms Parkinson's. The NHS says diagnosis is based on symptoms, medical history and a detailed examination, usually by a neurologist. A scan may be used to rule out other causes, and doctors sometimes judge whether the symptoms improve with Parkinson's medicine.

See a doctor if you notice a resting tremor, slowing down, stiffness, a shuffling walk or shrinking handwriting, especially together with loss of smell or dream-acting. In India, government medical colleges and AIIMS hospitals have neurology departments, and several run movement disorder clinics.

## How is it treated?

There is no cure yet, but treatment can control symptoms for many years:

- **Medicines** that replace or mimic dopamine. The most commonly used is levodopa/carbidopa, which WHO includes on its Model List of Essential Medicines. A neurologist decides which medicine and dose suit each person, and adjusts it over time.
- **Physiotherapy, exercise and speech therapy**, which can help movement, balance, voice and swallowing (WHO notes that rehabilitation, including physiotherapy, can offer relief).
- **Deep brain stimulation (DBS)**, a surgery that places electrodes in the brain, for some people whose symptoms are no longer well controlled by medicines. An ICMR health technology assessment brief puts its cost in India at roughly ₹6 to ₹26 lakh, and notes it is available only in Tier 1 cities. Ask the hospital about government schemes that may help with costs.

Living with Parkinson's often brings low mood and anxiety too; these are part of the illness and treatable (see our guides to [depression](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/depression-symptoms-causes) and [anxiety](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/anxiety-symptoms-and-causes)). Support groups, such as those run by the Parkinson's Disease and Movement Disorder Society (PDMDS) in several Indian cities, help patients and carers.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is usually the first sign of Parkinson's disease?

For many people it is a slight tremor in one hand while it is resting, or a general slowing down. Loss of smell, constipation or acting out dreams can come years earlier, but these have many other causes too.

### Can young people get Parkinson's disease?

Yes. Most people develop it after 50, but some develop it in their 30s or 40s. Indian studies have found the average age of onset to be younger than in Western countries.

### Is Parkinson's disease hereditary?

Usually not directly. Having a parent or sibling with Parkinson's raises the risk, and some gene changes cause it in a small number of families, but most people with Parkinson's have no clear family history.

### Is every hand tremor Parkinson's?

No. Essential tremor, anxiety, an overactive thyroid, caffeine and some medicines can all cause shaking. A tremor that is worst when the hand is resting, together with slowness or stiffness, is more typical of Parkinson's, and a neurologist can tell the difference.
