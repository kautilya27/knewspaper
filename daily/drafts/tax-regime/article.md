**Quick answer:** For tax year 2026-27 (the year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), the new tax regime is the default and costs nothing up to a gross salary of ₹12.75 lakh. The old regime wins only if your deductions and exemptions are large. At a ₹15 lakh salary you need about ₹5.44 lakh of them (on top of the standard deduction). From about ₹25 lakh upwards you need ₹8 lakh. Few salaried people reach those numbers without a big HRA claim and a home loan together.

Union Budget 2026, presented on 1 February 2026, did not change any income-tax slabs. The Finance Bill 2026 memorandum keeps the same rates for both regimes. What changed is the law itself. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, so the familiar section numbers (115BAC, 80C, 87A) now have new ones. The table and two worked examples below show where your break-even point sits.

## Tax slabs for tax year 2026-27: both regimes side by side

These figures come from section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (new regime) and the Finance Bill 2026 memorandum (old regime rates). They apply to a resident individual below 60.

| Taxable income | New regime (default) | Old regime |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2.5 lakh | Nil | Nil |
| ₹2.5 lakh to ₹4 lakh | Nil | 5% |
| ₹4 lakh to ₹5 lakh | 5% | 5% |
| ₹5 lakh to ₹8 lakh | 5% | 20% |
| ₹8 lakh to ₹10 lakh | 10% | 20% |
| ₹10 lakh to ₹12 lakh | 10% | 30% |
| ₹12 lakh to ₹16 lakh | 15% | 30% |
| ₹16 lakh to ₹20 lakh | 20% | 30% |
| ₹20 lakh to ₹24 lakh | 25% | 30% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% | 30% |

The other rules that matter for a salaried person:

- **Standard deduction:** ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old one, under section 19 of the new Act.
- **Rebate (old section 87A, now section 156):** In the new regime, tax is fully rebated up to ₹60,000 if taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. Add the standard deduction and that means zero tax on a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh. In the old regime, the rebate is up to ₹12,500 when taxable income is ₹5 lakh or less.
- **Marginal relief:** Section 156 also says that if your taxable income goes a little over ₹12 lakh in the new regime, your tax cannot be more than the amount above ₹12 lakh. For example, a ₹13 lakh salary gives taxable income of ₹12.25 lakh. Tax is capped at ₹25,000 (₹26,000 with cess) instead of ₹63,750 (₹66,300 with cess). This relief stops at a gross salary of about ₹13.46 lakh.
- **Cess and surcharge:** A 4% health and education cess applies to all tax. Surcharge starts only above ₹50 lakh of income, so it does not affect the salaries in this article.

## Break-even table: how much deduction makes the old regime worth it

**Assumptions:** salaried, resident, below 60, no other income, and no surcharge. "Deductions" means everything you can claim in the old regime beyond the ₹50,000 standard deduction: exempt HRA, section 123 (old 80C), section 126 (old 80D), home-loan interest, NPS, professional tax and so on. All tax figures include 4% cess and were computed with a script, not estimated.

| Gross salary | Tax in new regime | Tax in old regime with zero deductions | Deductions needed for old regime to match new |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹8 lakh | ₹0 | ₹65,000 | ₹2.50 lakh |
| ₹10 lakh | ₹0 | ₹1,06,600 | ₹4.50 lakh |
| ₹12 lakh | ₹0 | ₹1,63,800 | ₹6.50 lakh |
| ₹15 lakh | ₹97,500 | ₹2,57,400 | ₹5.44 lakh |
| ₹20 lakh | ₹1,92,400 | ₹4,13,400 | ₹7.08 lakh |
| ₹25 lakh | ₹3,19,800 | ₹5,69,400 | ₹8.00 lakh |
| ₹30 lakh | ₹4,75,800 | ₹7,25,400 | ₹8.00 lakh |

If your deductions are below the last column, the new regime costs less. If they are above it, the old regime costs less. At exactly that figure, the two are equal.

### Why the numbers look odd at ₹12 lakh and ₹15 lakh

Up to ₹12.75 lakh the new regime charges nothing. The old regime can only match zero tax if your taxable income comes down to ₹5 lakh, where its own rebate kicks in. So the deduction you need grows rupee for rupee with salary. That is why ₹12 lakh needs ₹6.50 lakh, more than ₹15 lakh needs.

Above ₹12.75 lakh, new-regime tax starts, so the gap narrows for a while. From a gross of ₹24.75 lakh, both regimes charge 30% on each extra rupee. The break-even then stays at ₹8 lakh however high your salary goes, until surcharge comes in at ₹50 lakh.

## Worked example 1: a renter in Bengaluru earning ₹18 lakh

Priya earns ₹18 lakh a year. Her basic salary is ₹9 lakh, her HRA is ₹4.5 lakh, and she pays ₹40,000 a month (₹4.8 lakh a year) as rent in Bengaluru.

Under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 (Rule 279), which CBDT notified in March 2026, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad joined Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata and Chennai on the 50% HRA limit. The exempt HRA is the lowest of:

1. HRA received: ₹4,50,000
2. Rent minus 10% of basic: ₹4,80,000 − ₹90,000 = ₹3,90,000
3. 50% of basic: ₹4,50,000

So ₹3.9 lakh of her HRA is exempt. Under the earlier 40% limit for Bengaluru, the cap would have been ₹3.6 lakh. She also puts ₹1.5 lakh into EPF and PPF (section 123) and pays ₹25,000 for health insurance (section 126).

| | Old regime | New regime |
|---|---|---|
| Total deductions beyond standard deduction | ₹5,65,000 | Not allowed |
| Tax including cess | ₹1,74,720 | ₹1,50,800 |

The new regime is about ₹24,000 cheaper even with a generous HRA claim. At ₹18 lakh the break-even is about ₹6.42 lakh. Adding ₹50,000 of her own NPS contribution would bring her old-regime tax to ₹1,59,120, which is still higher.

## Worked example 2: a home-loan borrower earning ₹25 lakh

Rahul earns ₹25 lakh and lives in a flat he bought with a loan. He has no HRA. His deductions:

- Home-loan interest on a self-occupied house: ₹2,00,000 (the limit under section 22 of the new Act, old section 24(b))
- Section 123 (old 80C), including principal repayment and EPF: ₹1,50,000
- Section 126 (old 80D): ₹25,000 for his family's policy plus ₹50,000 for his senior-citizen parents' policy

| | Old regime | New regime |
|---|---|---|
| Total deductions beyond standard deduction | ₹4,25,000 | Not allowed |
| Tax including cess | ₹4,36,800 | ₹3,19,800 |

The new regime saves him ₹1,17,000. To make the old regime worth it he would need ₹8 lakh of deductions. That usually means a big HRA claim on top of the home loan, which is not possible while living in your own flat in the same city. ## Old and new section numbers under the Income-tax Act, 2025

Your Form 16 and the ITR forms for tax year 2026-27 use the new numbering. The mapping below is checked against the Income Tax Department's section pages and the Finance Bill 2026 memorandum.

| What it is | 1961 Act | Income-tax Act, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| New tax regime (default slabs) | 115BAC | Section 202 |
| Rebate up to ₹12 lakh / ₹5 lakh | 87A | Section 156 |
| Standard deduction for salary | 16(ia) | Section 19 |
| PPF, EPF, ELSS, life insurance, principal repayment | 80C | Section 123 (with Schedule XV) |
| NPS contributions | 80CCD | Section 124 |
| Health insurance | 80D | Section 126 |
| Home-loan interest (house property) | 24(b) | Section 22 |
| HRA exemption | 10(13A) | Exempt-income provisions, calculated under Rule 279 of the 2026 Rules |

You can read the full text of [section 202 on the Income Tax Department's website](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-202-76).

## Telling your employer, and switching later

Your employer deducts TDS under the new regime unless you declare the old one. This has been CBDT's instruction to employers since 2023. The declaration only affects monthly TDS. It does not lock you in.

The real choice is made when you file your return. Under section 202(4), a person **without business or professional income** chooses the regime each year along with a return filed by the due date. So a salaried person can pick the old regime one year and the new one the next. A late return stays in the new regime. Our step-by-step on [how to file ITR online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-file-itr-online) shows where this option appears.

A person **with business or professional income** has much less freedom. Once they opt out of the new regime, that choice carries forward. They can return to the new regime only once, and after that they cannot leave it again unless their business income stops.

This article explains how the rules work and is not tax advice. If your case includes capital gains, rental income or a let-out property, the comparison changes, and a chartered accountant can work it out for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Budget 2026 change the income tax slabs for FY 2026-27?

No. The Finance Bill 2026 memorandum keeps both the new-regime slabs (nil up to ₹4 lakh, rising to 30% above ₹24 lakh) and the old-regime slabs unchanged. The ₹75,000 standard deduction and the ₹60,000 rebate also continue.

### Is the new tax regime the default for tax year 2026-27?

Yes. Section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies the new slabs automatically. To use the old regime you have to opt out, and salaried people can do that every year while filing the return on time.

### Can I claim HRA and home-loan interest in the new regime?

No. The new regime does not allow the HRA exemption, section 123 (old 80C), section 126 (old 80D) or interest on a self-occupied home loan. Your employer's contribution to your NPS account is one of the few deductions that still counts.

### At what salary is the new regime always better?

Up to ₹12.75 lakh gross, the new regime means zero tax, so the old regime can only equal it. Above that, it depends on your deductions. If they are below the figure in the break-even table for your salary (₹8 lakh at ₹25 lakh and above), the new regime costs less.
