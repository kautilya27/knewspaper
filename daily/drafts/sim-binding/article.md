**Quick answer:** SIM binding is a Department of Telecommunications (DoT) rule that makes messaging apps such as WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal work on a phone only while the SIM of the registered mobile number is present and active in that phone. Apps have until **31 December 2026** to comply, so most users should expect the change from early 2027.

If your WhatsApp number's SIM sits in the same phone you use WhatsApp on, very little should change for you. The people who will feel it are those who run the app on a phone without that SIM: a second phone on Wi-Fi, an iPad, or a number kept alive abroad after the SIM was removed. The six-hour forced logout of WhatsApp Web that was first announced has been dropped.

## What the rule actually says

According to a [Press Information Bureau release of 1 December 2025](https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/4599a9925468a2648d43e3ff724e7f0c.pdf), DoT issued directions on 28 November 2025 to eight app-based communication services: WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Arattai, ShareChat, Josh, JioChat and Signal. The directions were issued under the Telecom Cyber Security Rules, 2024 (as amended).

The two main requirements were:

1. **SIM binding:** the app must stay continuously linked to the SIM of the mobile number the account was registered with. If that SIM is not in the device, the app should not work.
2. **Web sessions:** the web version of the app must log the user out periodically, and the user can link again by scanning a QR code. The PIB release set this at no later than six hours.

Today, WhatsApp checks your number once, through an OTP, when you set it up. After that the account keeps working even if you take the SIM out, let it lapse, or carry the phone abroad and put in a foreign SIM. SIM binding closes that gap by asking the app to keep checking.

## The deadline and what changed since November 2025

The original directions gave apps 90 days to implement the change and 120 days to file a compliance report. That timeline was later relaxed.

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Nov 2025 | DoT issues SIM-binding directions to eight apps | PIB, 1 Dec 2025 |
| 25 Feb 2026 | Communications Minister says no plan to relax the six-hour web logout | Lokmat Times |
| 28 Mar 2026 | Original compliance-report deadline | PTI |
| 2 Apr 2026 | Minister of State tells Rajya Sabha the deadline is 31 Dec 2026 and web logout will be risk-based, with no fixed time period | PTI, Deccan Chronicle |
| Nov 2026 | DoT reportedly tells apps to check for the SIM every 24 hours | MediaNama (unnamed industry source) |
| 31 Dec 2026 | Current compliance deadline | Rajya Sabha reply |

So the six-hour figure you may still see in older explainers is out of date. In a written reply to the Rajya Sabha on 2 April 2026, as reported by PTI, Minister of State for Communications Pemmasani Chandra Sekhar said web services must be logged out based on a risk-based analysis and that no specific time period has been set. DoT has not published a standard for this, so in practice each app decides when a web or desktop session looks risky enough to end.

How often apps must check that the SIM is present was not spelt out in the original order. MediaNama reported on 16 November 2026, citing an industry source, that DoT has asked apps to check every 24 hours. DoT has not published this, and the full directions themselves are not public, so treat the 24-hour figure as reported rather than confirmed.

## Why the government wants it

DoT's case, as set out in the PIB release, is that accounts keep working after the SIM is removed, deactivated or taken out of the country. Fraudsters use this to run scams on Indian numbers from abroad, including "digital arrest" calls and calls impersonating government officers. Long-running web sessions let someone control an account remotely without holding the phone.

The release puts cyber-fraud losses in 2024 at more than ₹22,800 crore. It also points out that banking and UPI apps already use device binding and automatic logouts, so the idea is not new for Indian users.

The concern about web sessions is shared by the Home Ministry. On 18 November 2026, the National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit of the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) issued an advisory warning that WhatsApp Web sessions can be misused to read and send messages as the account holder, and asked users to be careful on office, shared and public computers, according to MediaNama.

## What critics have said

The rule has been contested. Broadband India Forum, whose members include Meta and Google, wrote to DoT in February 2026 citing a senior counsel's opinion that the directions go beyond the parent law, according to PTI. In December 2025, the Internet and Mobile Association of India warned that periodic logouts would disrupt everyday use of WhatsApp Web. An open letter to DoT dated 5 January 2026, signed by the Internet Society's Delhi chapter among others, asked for public consultation and a deferral.

Other questions raised by policy experts include why the order was issued without consultation, whether DoT or MeitY should regulate apps, and whether scammers who already use SIMs bought with fake documents will simply keep doing so. A February 2026 survey by CUTS International found that about 86% of users let family members use their phone or SIM for messaging. The government's position, in the minister's February statement, is that security takes priority.

## What changes for you: situation by situation

These are the practical effects as far as the published rules and official statements go. Where DoT has not said anything, the table says so.

| Your situation | What to expect |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp on the phone that holds its SIM | Little change; possibly a one-time permission or verification prompt |
| SIM in phone, travelling abroad on roaming | Not affected, according to the PIB release |
| NRI or student abroad who removed the Indian SIM | Account may stop working unless the Indian SIM is in the phone and active (how eSIMs will be treated has not been confirmed) |
| Dual-SIM phone | Should work if the registered number's SIM is one of the two; behaviour if that SIM is inactive is not specified |
| iPad, tablet or second phone without the SIM | Likely to be restricted; DoT has not issued separate guidance |
| WhatsApp Web or desktop on a laptop | Can still be used, but may be logged out when the app judges the session risky; re-link by scanning a QR code |
| Prepaid SIM with recharge expired | Not specified; an inactive SIM may fail the check |
| Small business sharing one number across staff | Likely to be disrupted; no specific exemption announced |

## What to do before 31 December

- **Keep the SIM of your WhatsApp number in the phone you use it on**, and keep it recharged. If you moved WhatsApp to a phone that doesn't hold that SIM, consider moving the SIM or switching the account to a number you do carry.
- **If you live abroad**, decide whether to keep your Indian number active in a dual-SIM phone (whether an eSIM will satisfy the check has not been confirmed) or move your WhatsApp to your local number using the app's change-number option. Check your bank's alerts are on a number you can still receive.
- **Log out of WhatsApp Web on shared or office computers** when you finish, as the I4C advisory suggests. In WhatsApp, open Linked devices to see every active session and remove any you don't recognise.
- **Check the SIMs issued in your name.** SIM binding only works if the SIM really belongs to the user, so it is worth [checking how many SIMs are on your Aadhaar](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/sims-on-my-aadhaar) and reporting ones you don't use.
- **If your phone is lost or stolen**, block it quickly; with SIM binding, whoever holds the SIM holds the account. Here's [how to block a lost phone on CEIR](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/block-lost-phone-ceir).
- **Update your apps** in December so that any new verification step arrives before the deadline rather than at a bad moment.

Fake "your WhatsApp will be blocked, click here to verify" messages are likely as the deadline nears. A genuine SIM-binding check will never ask you to share an OTP or UPI PIN with someone over a call, or to click a link or make a payment. ## Frequently asked questions

### Will WhatsApp stop working if I remove the SIM?
Once the app has implemented SIM binding, it is meant to stop working when the SIM of the registered number is not in the phone. Putting the SIM back in should restore access.

### Is the six-hour WhatsApp Web logout still happening?
No. The Minister of State told the Rajya Sabha on 2 April 2026 that web sessions will be logged out on a risk-based analysis and that no fixed time period has been set.

### What is the deadline for SIM binding?
Apps must comply by 31 December 2026, according to the government's reply in the Rajya Sabha. The original November 2025 directions had given 90 days.

### Can I still use WhatsApp on an iPad or laptop?
Laptop use through WhatsApp Web or the desktop app continues as a linked device, though you may need to re-scan the QR code more often. For a standalone iPad or tablet without the SIM, DoT has not issued separate guidance, and access may be restricted.
