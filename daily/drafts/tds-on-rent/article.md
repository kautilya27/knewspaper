**Quick answer:** If you are an individual or HUF tenant paying more than ₹50,000 rent for any month, you must deduct TDS at 2% of the year's rent and deposit it online. Since 1 April 2026 this falls under section 393(1) (Table S. No. 2(i)) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the old Form 26QC has been replaced by **Form 141**. The landlord's certificate is now **Form 132** instead of Form 16C.

You deduct the tax only once: from the rent for the last month of the tax year (March) or the last month of your tenancy, whichever comes first. You don't need a TAN; your PAN is enough, according to the Income Tax Department's FAQs on TDS on rent.

## What changed on 1 April 2026

The new Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. The rule itself is almost the same, but the section, forms and certificate all have new numbers. If you read an older guide that talks about section 194-IB and Form 26QC, this table shows what each item is called now.

| Item | Under the 1961 Act (till 31 March 2026) | Under the 2025 Act (from 1 April 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Section | 194-IB | 393(1), Table S. No. 2(i) |
| Threshold | Rent above ₹50,000 for a month or part of a month | Same |
| Rate | 5% till 30 Sept 2024; 2% from 1 Oct 2024 | 2% |
| Landlord without PAN | 20%, capped at last month's rent | 20%, capped at last month's rent |
| Payment-cum-return form | Form 26QC | Form 141 (Schedule A) |
| Certificate to landlord | Form 16C | Form 132 |
| Late-filing fee | Section 234E: ₹200 a day | Section 427: ₹200 a day |
| Interest on late deposit | 1.5% a month (section 201) | 1.5% a month (section 398(3)) |

The table draws on the Income Tax Department's rent TDS FAQs and its older section 194-IB page, Business Standard (for the cut to 2% from 1 October 2024) and Taxmann (for the new fee and interest sections). For every old-to-new section, see our [section mapping guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/new-income-tax-act-section-mapping).

Form 141 is a single form that merged four older ones: 26QB (property purchase), 26QC (rent), 26QD (payments to contractors and professionals) and 26QE (crypto). Rent goes in **Schedule A**, according to the e-filing portal's Form 141 FAQs.

## Who has to deduct TDS on rent

You have to deduct if all of these are true:

- You are an **individual or HUF** paying rent for land or a building, including a flat or house you live in.
- The rent is **more than ₹50,000 for a month or part of a month**. At ₹50,000 exactly, there's no TDS.
- The landlord is **resident in India**. Rent paid to an NRI landlord falls under a different provision, which this article doesn't cover.

Salaried tenants are included. Claiming HRA doesn't exempt you, and you have to deduct TDS even if you have no business at all. (Our [HRA exemption guide](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/hra-exemption-calculation) explains the separate question of how much of that rent cuts your tax.)

### Tenants who are businesses or audited individuals

Companies, firms and individuals or HUFs whose accounts must be audited deduct under a different entry: Table S. No. 2(ii) of section 393(1), which was section 194-I. According to the Income Tax Department's FAQs, the threshold there is also ₹50,000 a month or part of a month. The rate is 10% for land, buildings, furniture or fittings and 2% for plant and machinery. These payers need a TAN, deposit tax within 7 days from the end of the month and file the quarterly Form 140 (formerly 26Q). The ₹50,000-a-month limit dates from 1 April 2025. Before that, the 194-I threshold was ₹2.4 lakh a year, according to ClearTax.

The rest of this article is about the individual or HUF tenant.

## Worked example: an 11-month lease that crosses March

Most residential agreements run for 11 months. Here is how TDS works when the lease runs from 1 June 2026 to 30 April 2027 at ₹55,000 a month, so it spans two tax years.

**Step 1, tax year 2026-27 (June 2026 to March 2027, 10 months)**

- Total rent: 10 × ₹55,000 = ₹5,50,000
- TDS at 2%: ₹11,000, deducted from the March 2027 rent
- Paid to the landlord for March: ₹55,000 − ₹11,000 = ₹44,000
- Deposit with Form 141 by 30 April 2027 (30 days from the end of March)
- Form 132 to the landlord by 15 May 2027 (15 days after the Form 141 due date)

**Step 2, tax year 2027-28 (April 2027 only, the last month of the tenancy)**

- Total rent: ₹55,000
- TDS at 2%: ₹1,100, deducted from the April 2027 rent
- Paid to the landlord for April: ₹53,900
- Deposit with a second Form 141 within 30 days from the end of April 2027

Over the full lease, the TDS comes to ₹11,000 + ₹1,100 = ₹12,100, which is 2% of ₹6,05,000 (11 months' rent).

**If the landlord has no PAN:** the rate becomes 20%, but TDS can't exceed one month's rent. For step 1, 20% of ₹5,50,000 is ₹1,10,000, so the cap applies and TDS is ₹55,000. In other words, you would pay no rent to the landlord for March and deposit the whole amount as tax.

**If the deposit is late:** interest is 1.5% for every month or part of a month from the date of deduction to the date of deposit. On ₹11,000 that is ₹165 for each month or part month. If you never deducted at all, the interest is 1% a month or part of a month until you do, and you can be treated as an "assessee in default".

**If the form is filed late:** the late fee is ₹200 a day, but it can't exceed the TDS amount. Filed 30 days late, the fee on our step 1 deduction is ₹6,000. Filed 60 days late, ₹200 × 60 = ₹12,000, so the cap applies and the fee is ₹11,000. Taxmann notes that a separate penalty of ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh can also apply under section 461 (formerly 271H) for not filing the statement.

## How to file Form 141 for rent on the income tax portal

These steps follow the [Form 141 user manual on the e-filing portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/form-141-challan-cum-statement-deduction-tax-us-3931). You must log in with your PAN, and an individual's PAN must be linked with Aadhaar.

1. Log in at incometax.gov.in and go to **e-File > e-Pay Tax**. Choose **Income Tax Act 2025**, click **New Payment**, then **Proceed** on the Form 141 tile.
2. Pick the deductee type. Choose "Corporate" only if the landlord's PAN has C as its fourth character; most landlords are "Non-Corporate".
3. Check your pre-filled details. If something is wrong, fix your profile first.
4. Enter the **tax year** and **month of deduction**, then select **Schedule A** (rent paid by an individual or HUF).
5. Fill in the property type (land, building, or both) and the full address of the rented property.
6. Add the landlords and their shares, which must add up to 100%. If you share the flat, enter your share and your co-tenants' shares; the portal works out the tax on your share only.
7. Enter the **period of tenancy** in this tax year (up to 12 months), the reason for deduction (end of tax year or end of tenancy), the total rent for the year and the last month's rent.
8. In the deductee-wise rows, enter the payment date, the amount, the rate (2) and the date of deduction. The tax is worked out for you.
9. Add any interest or late fee due, check the total and choose a payment mode. Review the preview and click **Pay Now** to go to your bank's page.
10. After payment, e-verify the form with an EVC or a DSC. You get an SMS and email, and the challan appears under **Payment History**.

Mistakes in a filed Form 141 can't be edited on the e-filing portal. You have to register as a "Taxpayer" on TRACES and correct it there, and the TDS certificate is downloaded from TRACES as well, according to the portal's FAQs. The portal asks for the address and tenancy period, so keep your lease handy; our guide on [getting a rental agreement online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-get-a-rental-agreement-online-in-india) explains how to make one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is TDS on rent still under section 194-IB?

Not for rent deducted from 1 April 2026. It is now section 393(1), Table S. No. 2(i) of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The threshold (₹50,000 a month) and the rate (2%) are the same as under 194-IB.

### Is Form 26QC still used?

No. From 1 April 2026, rent TDS by individuals and HUFs is paid and reported in Schedule A of Form 141, which replaced Forms 26QB, 26QC, 26QD and 26QE.

### Do I deduct 2% every month?

No. The rule asks for one deduction, made from the rent for March or for the last month of your tenancy if you move out earlier, and calculated on all the rent for that period.

### What if my rent is exactly ₹50,000 a month?

There's no TDS. The rule applies only when the rent is more than ₹50,000 for a month or part of a month.
