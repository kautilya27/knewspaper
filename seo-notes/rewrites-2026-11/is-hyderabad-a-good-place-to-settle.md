Hyderabad, the "City of Pearls", has grown into one of India's biggest tech and pharma hubs while keeping its old-city charm, food and festivals. If you are thinking of moving there for a job, or settling down for good with family, here is an honest look at what life in Hyderabad is like in 2026.

**Quick answer:** yes, Hyderabad is a good place to settle for most people, especially IT and pharma professionals and families. Rents and housing prices are lower than in Bangalore or Mumbai, the job market is strong, schools and hospitals are good, and traffic is less severe than in Bangalore. The main downsides are very hot summers, monsoon waterlogging in some areas, peak-hour jams in the IT corridor and a metro network that has not grown since its first phase.

Hyderabad is one of the more affordable big cities in India. According to locality ranges published by Square Yards (updated May 2026), a 1 BHK costs about:

Landlords in Hyderabad also tend to ask for smaller security deposits than in Bangalore. If you plan to buy, ANAROCK's Q1 2026 data puts the average quoted price of new homes in Hyderabad at about ₹7,990 per sq ft, below Bangalore's. One cost that is higher: petrol. On 1 November 2026 it cost ₹115.69 a litre in Hyderabad, more than in Bengaluru or Delhi, according to daily fuel price reports.

## 2. Jobs: IT, GCCs, pharma and more

Hyderabad's economy is led by IT and IT-enabled services around HITEC City, Madhapur, Gachibowli and the Financial District, where many global tech companies have large offices. The city has also become a favourite base for global capability centres (GCCs).

Life sciences are the other big pillar. Genome Valley is one of India's best-known biotech and vaccine clusters, and the state government is developing a Green Pharma City south of the city. T-Hub supports start-ups, and aerospace, defence manufacturing and education add more options.

For businesses, Telangana's **TG-iPASS** (Telangana Industrial Project Approval and Self-Certification System, earlier known as TS-iPASS) offers a single online window for state approvals needed to set up industries.

## 3. Education

Hyderabad has good schooling options across budgets, from well-known private schools to CBSE, ICSE and state-board schools in most areas. For higher education, the city and its surroundings have the Indian School of Business (ISB), IIIT Hyderabad, the University of Hyderabad, Osmania University, IIT Hyderabad (at Kandi, Sangareddy district) and AIIMS Bibinagar on the outskirts. Fees vary widely, so compare schools in the area you plan to live in.

## 4. Healthcare

The city has a large number of multi-speciality private hospitals, as well as major government hospitals such as Osmania General Hospital, Gandhi Hospital and NIMS. Families with a white ration card in Telangana can get free treatment for listed procedures under the state's **Rajiv Aarogyasri** scheme, whose cover was raised to ₹10 lakh per family per year in December 2023. Private health insurance is still a good idea for most families.

## 5. Infrastructure and getting around

- **Metro:** the Hyderabad Metro runs about **69 km across three lines** (Red, Blue and Green). The Telangana government took full ownership of the Phase-I network from L&T in April 2026. Phase II, proposed as about 76 km in Phase II-A and about 86 km in Phase II-B, was still awaiting central approval in 2026, so don't choose a home on the promise of a future metro line.
- **Roads:** the 158-km **Outer Ring Road** circles the city and links the IT corridor with the suburbs and the airport. A larger Regional Ring Road is planned further out.
- **Airport:** Rajiv Gandhi International Airport at Shamshabad has domestic and international flights.
- **Buses and MMTS:** TGSRTC city buses and the MMTS suburban trains cover areas the metro does not.

Traffic is lighter than in Bangalore. In the TomTom Traffic Index 2025, Hyderabad ranked 47th in the world for congestion, against Bengaluru's 2nd. But the IT corridor still jams badly at peak hours, so living close to work or a metro station makes a big difference.

## 6. Culture, food and lifestyle

Hyderabad's history is everywhere, from Charminar and Golconda Fort to the Qutb Shahi tombs and Chowmahalla Palace. Food is a big part of life: Hyderabadi biryani, haleem during Ramzan, Irani chai and Osmania biscuits. Festivals like Bonalu and Bathukamma, along with Diwali, Eid and Christmas, give the city a warm, mixed culture. Telugu, Urdu, Hindi and English are all widely spoken, so newcomers usually find it easy to settle in.

For leisure there are malls, multiplexes, lakes such as Hussain Sagar, and green spaces like KBR National Park.

## 7. Safety and environment

Hyderabad is generally seen as one of the safer big Indian cities to live in, and its police run dedicated **SHE Teams** to tackle harassment of women. As anywhere, safety varies by area and time of day, so check the neighbourhood, lighting and commute before you choose a home.

On the environment side, vehicle pollution has risen with the city's growth, and air quality worsens in winter, though it is usually better than in north Indian cities.

## 8. Challenges to consider

- **Heat:** summers are long and hot, with temperatures often above 40°C in April and May.
- **Monsoon waterlogging:** heavy rain can flood low-lying roads and colonies, so ask neighbours about past flooding before renting or buying.
- **Peak-hour traffic** in the IT corridor and around the Outer Ring Road exits.
- **Water supply** can be uneven in some newer suburbs that depend on tankers; check before you move.
- **Distances:** the city is spread out, and newer suburbs can be far from the old city and central areas.

## Verdict: should you settle in Hyderabad?

For most working professionals and families, **yes**. Hyderabad offers a strong job market, lower rents and deposits than Bangalore, good schools and hospitals, and a rich culture, with less traffic stress than India's most congested cities. If you can handle the summer heat and choose your neighbourhood carefully, it is one of the best-balanced big cities in India to build a life in. ## Frequently asked questions

### How does Hyderabad's cost of living compare to Bangalore or Mumbai?

Hyderabad is generally cheaper than both, mainly because of housing. In mid-2026 a 1 BHK in Hyderabad's IT areas was listed at about ₹15,000–24,000 a month, below comparable Bangalore hubs, and Mumbai is far costlier for housing. Groceries and utilities cost roughly the same, while petrol is pricier in Hyderabad.

### Is Hyderabad safe for families and working professionals?

Hyderabad is generally considered one of the safer big cities in India, and its police run SHE Teams focused on women's safety. Safety still varies by neighbourhood, so visit an area at different times of day, check street lighting and your commute, and talk to residents before choosing a home.

### What are the best residential areas in Hyderabad for IT professionals?

Gachibowli, Madhapur, Kondapur and the Financial District are popular because they are close to the main tech parks, but they are the most expensive. For lower rents with reasonable commutes, many people choose Miyapur or Kukatpally on the Metro Red Line.

### What industries drive Hyderabad's economy?

IT and IT-enabled services, including many global capability centres, are the biggest employers. Pharmaceuticals, biotech and vaccines (centred on Genome Valley), aerospace and defence manufacturing, start-ups and education are other major sectors.
