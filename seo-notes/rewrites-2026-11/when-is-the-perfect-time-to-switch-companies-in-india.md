There is no single perfect month to switch companies, but there is a perfect *situation*: you have stopped growing where you are, you have a written offer that clearly moves your career forward, and the timing does not cost you money you have already earned, such as a bonus, gratuity or vested stock. If those three line up, it is a good time to move.

**Quick answer:** in India, the best time to switch is usually **after you have one or two years of solid achievements in your role**, **after your annual bonus or appraisal payout has been credited**, and **when you hold a written offer**. Plan around your **notice period** (often one to three months), and avoid resigning just before a big payout, a stock vesting date or five years of service, when gratuity becomes payable.

## Signs it is time to switch companies

- **You have stopped learning.** Your work has become repetitive, there are no new responsibilities on the horizon, and your manager cannot show you a path to the next level.
- **Your pay has fallen behind the market.** You find that people with your skills and experience are being offered noticeably more elsewhere, and internal raises have not closed the gap.
- **Your skills are not being used.** You have learnt something valuable (a new technology, certification or domain) that your current role does not need.
- **The culture is hurting you.** Constant overwork, poor management, harassment or a toxic team affect your health and performance. If you face harassment at work, you also have rights under the POSH Act; our guide on [what POSH is and why it matters](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/what-is-posh-and-why-it-is-important) explains them.
- **The company is in trouble.** Repeated layoffs, delayed salaries or losing major clients are signals to start looking quietly.
- **Your goals have changed.** You want a different domain, a role closer to home, remote work or a better work-life balance.

One bad week is not a reason to leave. A pattern that lasts for months, and that you have already raised with your manager, usually is.

## Good times to switch

| Situation | Why it is good timing |
|---|---|
| After your bonus or appraisal payout is credited | You do not leave money on the table |
| After one to two years with clear results | You have achievements to talk about in interviews |
| After a promotion or a big project win | You negotiate from a stronger position with a better title |
| When your skills are in high demand | More interview calls and more room to negotiate |
| After an important vesting date for ESOPs or RSUs | You keep stock you have earned |
| When you have a written offer in hand | You avoid an income gap |

**About hiring seasons:** many Indian companies run appraisals around the start of the financial year in April, and hiring often picks up once budgets for the new year are approved. Practices vary a lot by industry and company, so rather than waiting for a "season", start looking when your situation is right and give yourself two to four months for interviews, offers and notice.

## Times to wait or think twice

- **Just before a bonus, retention bonus or vesting date.** Check the exact payout or vesting date in your policy; you may need to be on the rolls on that date to receive it.
- **Close to five years of service.** Under Indian gratuity rules, permanent employees generally become entitled to gratuity after five years of continuous service with the same employer. If you are a few months short, it may be worth waiting.
- **If you received a joining bonus or relocation support** that must be repaid if you leave within a set period.
- **During a training bond or a service agreement** with a repayment clause.
- **After very short stints.** Several moves of under a year each can raise questions in interviews. One early move for a clear reason is easy to explain.
- **Without a written offer,** unless your health or safety is at stake. If you do leave first, keep savings for several months of expenses and be ready to explain the gap; our guide on [how to explain a career gap](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-explain-your-career-gap) helps.

## Notice periods: what to expect

Your notice period is set by your appointment letter or employment contract. In India it is commonly anywhere from one to three months, with longer periods more common in IT services and senior roles. Before you resign:

1. **Read your contract** for the notice period, whether unused leave can be adjusted against it, and whether a **notice buyout** (paying salary in place of the remaining notice) is allowed.
2. **Tell your new employer the real notice period** at the start. Many will wait; some will offer to pay for a buyout.
3. **Ask for an early release** politely. Employers often agree once a proper handover is done.
4. **Do not abscond.** Leaving without serving notice or settling the buyout can mean no relieving letter, a negative background check and a recovery claim from the employer.

## How to think about the salary hike

Hikes when switching vary widely with your role, industry, skills, experience and how much demand there is for your profile, so treat any "standard" percentage you hear with caution. Instead:

- Research current pay for similar roles through recruiters, job listings and people you trust in the industry.
- Compare the **full package**: fixed pay, variable pay and how often it is actually paid out, bonuses, ESOPs, insurance, leave, remote-work policy and commute.
- Check **in-hand salary** after tax and deductions, not just the CTC figure.
- Negotiate on the value you bring, with examples of results, and get the final numbers in writing.

## Your job-switch checklist

**Before you resign**

- Written offer letter with role, CTC breakdown, joining date and location.
- Confirmed bonus, vesting and gratuity dates in your current company.
- Copies of your payslips, appraisal letters and tax documents for your records.
- Your resume updated with recent achievements; our list of [resume action verbs](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/resume-action-verbs) can help.

**While serving notice**

- Resign in writing (email is usually enough), politely and without complaints.
- Hand over your work with documentation so you leave on good terms.
- Return company assets and collect your **relieving letter** and **experience letter**.

**After you leave**

- Follow up on your **full and final settlement**. Under the Code on Wages, now in force, wages due to an employee who resigns must be paid within two working days of the last working day; other dues may follow different timelines.
- **Transfer your EPF** to the new employer using your UAN instead of withdrawing it, so your savings keep growing and your service history stays continuous.
- Give your new employer details of the salary and tax deducted by your previous employer during the year, so your tax is calculated correctly.

## Bottom line

Switch when you have stopped growing, you have a better offer in writing and the timing does not cost you money you have earned. Serve your notice properly, leave on good terms, and the move is likely to help your career rather than raise questions later.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I stay in a company before switching jobs?

Many employers like to see at least a year or two in a role, as it shows commitment and gives you real achievements to talk about. Several very short stints in a row can raise questions in interviews, though a single early move for a clear reason, such as a better role or a difficult workplace, is usually easy to explain.

### How much salary hike can I expect when switching jobs in India?

There is no fixed figure; the hike depends on your industry, role, skills, experience and how much demand there is for your profile. Research market pay for similar roles, compare the full package rather than just the fixed salary, and negotiate based on what you bring.

### Should I resign before getting a new job offer?

It is usually safer to have a written offer in hand before resigning, so you are not left without income during a long search. If you need to leave sooner for health or workplace reasons, make sure you have enough savings to cover several months of expenses.

### What is a notice period and can it be shortened?

A notice period is the time you must keep working after resigning, as set out in your appointment letter or employment contract. Some employers agree to release you early, or let you or your new employer pay in place of the remaining notice, so check your contract and discuss it openly with HR.
