**Quick answer:** an **SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)** lets you take a fixed amount out of a mutual fund every month, quarter or year, while the rest of your money stays invested. Each withdrawal is a sale of units, so **only the profit in it is taxed** as capital gains. For tax year 2026-27, equity fund gains are taxed at **20% if held up to 12 months** and **12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year if held longer**; debt fund units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your **income-tax slab rate**.

Most people invest in mutual funds to let money grow. An SWP is for the next stage: when you want a regular income from that money without redeeming all of it at once. Below is how it works, who it suits, and the current tax rules, including what the new Income-tax Act, 2025 changes (and doesn't).

## What is SWP in mutual funds?

SWP stands for Systematic Withdrawal Plan. You tell the fund house to redeem a fixed amount (or, with some funds, only the gains) at a fixed interval and send it to your bank account.

Think of it as the reverse of a SIP:

- In a **SIP**, you invest a fixed amount regularly.
- In an **SWP**, you withdraw a fixed amount regularly.

**Example:** you have ₹10 lakh in a hybrid fund and set an SWP of ₹10,000 a month. Each month the fund sells enough units to pay you ₹10,000. The units left keep earning (or losing) with the market.

If you are new to how funds themselves work, start with our simple guide to [what mutual funds are](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/what-are-mutual-funds).

## How does an SWP work?

1. **Invest a lump sum** in a mutual fund, or use money you have built up through SIPs.
2. **Choose the amount, frequency and date** of withdrawals (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly), on the fund house's website or app, a registrar's platform or through your distributor.
3. **Units are redeemed automatically** at that day's NAV and the money is credited to your bank account.
4. **The balance stays invested.** If the fund earns more than you withdraw, your corpus can keep growing; if it earns less, the corpus shrinks.

Because the amount is fixed but the NAV changes, the fund sells **more units when markets fall** and fewer when they rise. That is the main risk to manage.

## Key benefits of an SWP

### 1. Regular, predictable cash flow

You get a fixed amount on a fixed date, which suits monthly household expenses, rent or EMIs.

### 2. Tax only on the gain

With a fixed deposit, all the interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. With an SWP, each withdrawal is part capital and part gain, and **only the gain portion is taxed**. In the early years of an SWP most of each withdrawal is your own money coming back, so the tax can be low.

### 3. The rest of your money stays invested

You don't have to sell everything at once, so the remaining units can keep compounding.

### 4. Discipline in falling markets

A small, regular withdrawal is less damaging than selling a large chunk in panic during a market fall.

### 5. Flexible

You can usually start, pause, stop or change an SWP whenever you want, with a few working days' notice.

## SWP vs SIP vs lump sum

| Feature | SWP | SIP | Lump sum |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it does | Regular withdrawals | Regular investments | One-time investment |
| Cash flow | Money comes to you | Money goes into the fund | One payment in |
| Best for | Retirees, people needing steady income | Salaried investors building wealth | Investors with a large surplus |
| When tax applies | On the gain in each withdrawal | When you redeem | When you redeem |

## Tax rules for SWP in 2026

Each SWP instalment is a redemption, and units are treated as sold on a **first in, first out** basis. So the holding period is counted for the oldest units first, and only the gain on the units sold is taxable.

### Equity mutual funds (more than 65% in Indian equity)

| Holding period | Tax on the gain |
|---|---|
| Up to 12 months (short-term) | 20% |
| More than 12 months (long-term) | 12.5% on total long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a year |

These rates apply to redemptions on or after 23 July 2024, when the Union Budget raised short-term tax from 15% and long-term tax from 10% and lifted the tax-free limit from ₹1 lakh to ₹1.25 lakh. **Budget 2026 did not change them.** Securities transaction tax of 0.001% is also deducted on redemption of equity fund units. Surcharge and 4% cess apply on top of the tax.

### Debt mutual funds

| When you bought the units | How the gain is taxed |
|---|---|
| On or after 1 April 2023 | Always at your slab rate, however long you hold them |
| Before 1 April 2023, held more than 24 months | 12.5%, without indexation |
| Before 1 April 2023, held 24 months or less | At your slab rate |

**Indexation is no longer available** on mutual fund redemptions made on or after 23 July 2024, so ignore older articles that promise it.

### Hybrid, gold and international funds

From April 2025, the slab-rate rule above applies to funds that invest **more than 65% in debt and money market instruments** (such as conservative hybrid funds). Funds in between, for example a hybrid fund with 35% to 65% in equity, as well as most gold and international funds, are generally taxed at **12.5% after 24 months** and at slab rate before that. Listed ETFs can have shorter holding periods. Your fund house's yearly **tax reckoner** spells out the treatment for each scheme type.

### What the Income-tax Act, 2025 changes

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from **1 April 2026**. It uses a single **"tax year"** in place of "previous year" and "assessment year", and section numbers have changed. The capital gains rates above carry on for tax year 2026-27. Your return for income earned in 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27) is still filed under the old Act's forms; the official FAQs on the [Income Tax Department's e-filing portal](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/objective-and-scope-new-act-faq) explain the transition.

You report SWP gains in your income tax return; our step-by-step guide on [how to file ITR online](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-file-itr-online) shows where. Fund houses don't deduct TDS on capital gains for resident investors, so you pay any tax due yourself (NRIs have tax deducted at source).

### A simple tax example

Say you invested ₹10 lakh in an equity fund at a NAV of ₹100 (10,000 units). Two years later the NAV is ₹120 and your SWP withdraws ₹10,000.

- Units sold: about 83.3 (₹10,000 ÷ ₹120)
- Cost of those units: about ₹8,333
- Long-term gain: about ₹1,667

Only that ₹1,667 counts as a gain, and it falls within the ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption unless your other equity gains use it up.

## Things to check before you start

- **Exit load:** many funds charge an exit load if units are redeemed within a set period after purchase. Check the scheme document and start the SWP after that period if you can.
- **Withdrawal rate:** if you withdraw more than the fund earns over time, the money will run out. A smaller withdrawal from a steadier fund lasts longer than a large one from a volatile fund.
- **Fund type:** for income you'll need in the next few years, many investors prefer hybrid or debt-oriented funds over pure equity funds, which can fall sharply.
- **Returns are not guaranteed:** unlike an FD, your capital can go down.

## Who should use an SWP?

- **Retirees** who want monthly income from a retirement corpus.
- **Freelancers and business owners** who want to smooth out irregular earnings.
- **Goal-based investors** who need a fixed sum each year, for example to pay college fees.

It is less suitable if you cannot accept any fall in your capital, or if you are still in the wealth-building stage, when a SIP is the better tool. For more on choosing between direct shares and funds, see [stocks vs mutual funds](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/stocks-vs-mutual-funds).

## The bottom line

An SWP turns a mutual fund corpus into a regular income while keeping most of the money invested, and it is usually more tax-efficient than FD interest because only the gain in each withdrawal is taxed. Pick a fund that matches how soon you need the money, keep the withdrawal rate sensible, and check the current tax treatment for your fund type before you start. If your situation is complex, a SEBI-registered investment adviser or a chartered accountant can help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SWP better than a fixed deposit for monthly income?

It depends on what you need. An SWP can be more tax-efficient because only the gain in each withdrawal is taxed, while all FD interest is taxed at your slab rate. But mutual fund returns are not guaranteed, so your balance can fall in a bad market, unlike an FD's fixed interest.

### Can I stop or change my SWP at any time?

Yes. Fund houses let you pause, stop or change the amount and frequency of an SWP through their website, app or your distributor. Changes usually need a few working days' notice before the next withdrawal date.

### How is SWP taxed in 2026?

Only the gain in each withdrawal is taxed. For equity funds, gains on units held up to 12 months are taxed at 20%, and gains on units held longer are taxed at 12.5% on the amount above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Gains on debt fund units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate.

### How much should I withdraw through SWP so my money lasts?

Keep your yearly withdrawals below what the fund can reasonably earn over the long term, so the balance is not eaten up. Withdrawing a small percentage of your corpus each year from a hybrid or debt-oriented fund gives steadier income than a large withdrawal from a volatile fund.
