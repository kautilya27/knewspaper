Which one suits you depends on your qualification (Class 12 or graduate), the kind of work you want and how many years you can give to preparation.

Government jobs remain popular for job security, pay under the Central Pay Commission and the respect they carry. If you are starting now for the 2027 recruitment cycle, the list below explains what each exam leads to, its current selection stages and who can apply, based on the latest official notices. ## 1. UPSC Civil Services Exam (IAS, IPS, IFS)

The Civil Services Exam is the route to the IAS, IPS, IFS and more than 20 other central services. It has three stages:

- **Prelims:** two objective papers, General Studies and CSAT. CSAT is qualifying, so only General Studies decides who moves on.
- **Mains:** nine descriptive papers, including an essay, four General Studies papers and two papers on an optional subject, plus two qualifying language papers.
- **Interview (personality test).**

The general-category age limit is 21 to 32, with relaxations for reserved categories. Notices and the yearly exam calendar are on the [UPSC website](https://upsc.gov.in).

## 2. SSC CGL (Combined Graduate Level)

SSC CGL fills posts such as Assistant Section Officer, Income Tax Inspector, Assistant Audit Officer and Junior Statistical Officer. It now has **two tiers, not four**:

- **Tier 1:** 100 objective questions on reasoning, general awareness, quantitative aptitude and English. It is qualifying only.
- **Tier 2:** Paper 1 is compulsory for all posts: maths, reasoning, English and general awareness, plus a qualifying computer knowledge module and a data entry speed test. Paper 2 (Statistics) is for Junior Statistical Officer posts, and a Finance and Economics paper applies to Assistant Audit and Accounts Officer posts.

The old descriptive tier and separate skill-test tier have gone. Final merit is based on Tier 2. Check the current notice on the [Staff Selection Commission's website](https://ssc.gov.in).

## 3. IBPS PO (Probationary Officer)

IBPS runs a common recruitment process for Probationary Officers in public sector banks (SBI holds its own PO exam). The stages are **prelims, mains and interview**. Prelims test English, quantitative aptitude and reasoning; mains add general, economy and banking awareness, data analysis and a descriptive English test. The age limit is usually 20 to 30.

## 4. RBI Grade B Officer

RBI Grade B (General) is one of the best-paid entry-level government jobs. It has three stages:

- **Phase I:** objective test on general awareness, English, quantitative aptitude and reasoning.
- **Phase II:** Economic and Social Issues, English writing skills, and Finance and Management.
- **Interview.**

Economics and current financial news matter far more here than in other banking exams.

## 5. RRB NTPC (Railways)

The Railway Recruitment Boards fill Non-Technical Popular Category posts such as station master, goods train manager, commercial clerk and accounts clerk. There are separate notifications for **graduate-level and undergraduate (Class 12) posts**. The process is a first computer-based test (CBT 1), a second test (CBT 2), and then a typing test or aptitude test depending on the post, followed by document verification and a medical. Both CBTs cover general awareness, mathematics and reasoning, with negative marking.

## 6. State PSC exams

Each state's Public Service Commission recruits for state civil services, such as deputy collector and deputy superintendent of police. Examples are UPPSC, MPPSC, BPSC, RPSC and TNPSC. Most follow a UPSC-like pattern of prelims, mains and interview, but syllabus, language papers and age limits differ by state, so read your state's notification.

## 7. Defence exams (NDA, CDS, AFCAT, Agniveer)

- **NDA:** conducted by UPSC twice a year for students who have passed or are in Class 12. A written exam is followed by the Services Selection Board (SSB) interview and a medical.
- **CDS:** UPSC's exam for graduates who want officer entry into the Army, Navy or Air Force.
- **AFCAT:** the Indian Air Force's test for graduates for flying and ground duty officer posts.
- **Agniveer:** soldiers, sailors and airmen are now recruited under the **Agnipath scheme**. The Navy's old SSR and AA sailor entries have been replaced by **Agniveer (SSR) and Agniveer (MR)**, chosen through an online entrance test, a physical fitness test and a medical. The Army and Air Force (Agniveervayu) run their own Agniveer exams.

## 8. Judicial Services exams

Judicial Services exams (often called PCS-J) recruit **Civil Judges (Junior Division)** and are run by state PSCs or High Courts. They usually have a prelims, a mains of descriptive law papers and an interview.

The eligibility rules changed recently. In May 2025 the Supreme Court made three years of practice as an advocate compulsory. In **August 2026 it reduced this to one year** for recruitment notices issued from **1 April 2027**, with a year of training and court clerkships after selection. For notices issued before then, fresh law graduates can still apply, but selected candidates go through the same training. Check the exact terms in your state's notice.

**A note on CLAT:** CLAT is not a government job exam. It is the admission test for law degrees at National Law Universities. It is the usual first step if you want to become a lawyer or judge, and some public sector companies use CLAT PG scores to hire legal officers.

## 9. Insurance exams (LIC AAO, NIACL AO, GIC)

Public sector insurers recruit officers through their own exams, such as LIC's Assistant Administrative Officer and New India Assurance's Administrative Officer. Most have a prelims, a mains and an interview, covering reasoning, quantitative aptitude, English, general awareness and insurance knowledge. Recruitment is not held every year, so watch each company's careers page.

## 10. Teacher eligibility tests (CTET and state TETs)

CTET, conducted by CBSE, and state TETs are **eligibility tests, not recruitment exams**. Passing makes you eligible to apply for government teaching posts. Paper I is for Classes 1 to 5 and Paper II for Classes 6 to 8. A TET certificate is now valid for life. You then apply through separate recruitment exams, for example those of Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan, Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti or your state.

## How to choose the right government exam

1. **Check your eligibility first:** qualification, age and, for some posts, physical standards.
2. **Match the job to your interests:** administration, banking, railways, defence, law or teaching.
3. **Look at overlap.** SSC, banking, railway and insurance exams share reasoning, maths and English, so one base of preparation can cover several exams. UPSC and state PSC preparation also overlap heavily.
4. **Pick one main exam and a backup,** then plan around the official exam calendar.
5. **Practise with timed mock tests.** Our comparison of the [best mock test platforms in India](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/best-mock-test-platform-india) can help you choose one.

Engineering graduates should also look at PSU jobs through GATE; see [why the GATE exam is important](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/why-gate-exam-is-important). And since preparation takes months, a routine that beats delay matters as much as books: our guide on [how to stop procrastinating](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/how-to-stop-procrastinating) has practical steps.

## Final thoughts

There is no single best government exam. Graduates have the widest choice, from UPSC to banking; Class 12 students can aim for NDA, Agniveer or undergraduate railway posts. Choose one that fits your eligibility and interests, prepare consistently, and rely on official notices rather than social media for dates and patterns.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is the toughest government exam in India?

The UPSC Civil Services Exam is widely regarded as the toughest government exam in India. It has three stages (prelims, mains and an interview), leads to services such as the IAS, IPS and IFS, and needs long, dedicated preparation in general studies, current affairs and an optional subject.

### Which government exams can I take after graduation?

Graduates can apply for exams such as the UPSC Civil Services Exam, SSC CGL, IBPS PO, RBI Grade B, state PSC exams, CDS, AFCAT and graduate-level RRB NTPC posts, provided they meet the age and other eligibility rules. Always check the latest official notification, as eligibility can differ by post.

### Which government exams can I prepare for after 12th?

After Class 12, popular options include the NDA exam for officer entry into the armed forces, Agniveer recruitment in the Army, Navy and Air Force, and the undergraduate-level posts in RRB NTPC for the railways. Eligibility, age limits and stream requirements vary, so read the official notification for each exam before you apply.

### How do I choose the right government exam to prepare for?

Start with your interests and strengths, such as administration, banking, railways, defence, law or teaching, and check whether you meet the education and age criteria. Then compare the syllabus and selection stages, pick one main exam and follow a consistent study plan while keeping up with current affairs.
