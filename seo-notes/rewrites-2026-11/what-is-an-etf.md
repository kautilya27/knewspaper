**Quick answer:** An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a fund that holds a basket of investments, such as the 50 shares of the Nifty 50, gold or government bonds, and is **listed on the stock exchange**. In India you buy and sell ETF units on the **NSE or BSE** through a stockbroker, at live prices, just like a share, and the units sit in your **demat account**. Most Indian ETFs simply copy an index, so they are cheap to run and easy to understand.

For a first-time investor, an ETF is a low-cost way to own a whole market in one purchase. You need a demat and trading account, and you should check the ETF's cost, trading volume and tracking error before buying. This guide covers how ETFs work, the main types in India, the real costs, and how gains are taxed, as of 5 November 2026.

## What is an ETF in simple terms?

Think of an ETF as a ready-made basket. Instead of buying 50 different shares to own the Nifty 50, you buy units of a Nifty 50 ETF, and the fund owns all 50 shares in the same proportions as the index. When the index rises or falls, the value of your units moves with it, minus small costs.

ETFs are offered by SEBI-registered mutual fund houses (AMCs) and are regulated by **SEBI** under the same rules as mutual funds. The difference is how you buy them: a regular mutual fund is bought from the fund house at the day's NAV, while an ETF is bought from other investors on the exchange during market hours (9:15 am to 3:30 pm on trading days).

ETFs are popular worldwide; the first big US ETF launched in 1993. India's first ETF, Nifty BeES, was launched at the end of 2001. Indian ETF assets crossed **₹10 lakh crore** in late 2025, according to an industry report by Zerodha Fund House, and ETF folios grew from about 41 lakh in 2020 to over 3 crore by November 2025.

## How ETFs work in the stock market

- **Units are created and redeemed in bulk.** The fund house works with market makers and large investors who deliver the underlying shares (or cash) in exchange for big blocks of new ETF units, or hand back units to get the shares. This keeps the number of units in line with demand.
- **You trade with other investors.** When you buy one unit on the exchange, you buy it from another investor or a market maker, not from the fund house. Investors putting in more than ₹25 crore can deal with the fund house directly.
- **Price vs value.** The ETF's market price usually stays close to its **iNAV** (indicative NAV, the live value of what it holds). In thinly traded ETFs, the price can drift above (premium) or below (discount) that value, so check the iNAV on the exchange or AMC website before you place an order.

## Types of ETFs in India

| Type | What it holds | Examples of what they track |
|---|---|---|
| Broad-market equity | Shares of a major index | Nifty 50, Sensex, Nifty Next 50, Nifty Midcap 150 |
| Sector and thematic | Shares of one sector or theme | Nifty Bank, Nifty IT, PSU banks, consumption |
| Smart beta / factor | Shares picked by a rule | Low volatility, momentum, quality, value indices |
| Gold and silver | Physical gold or silver held by the fund | Domestic gold and silver prices |
| Debt | Government or PSU bonds, or overnight money | Bharat Bond ETFs, gilt ETFs, liquid ETFs |
| International | Shares listed abroad | Nasdaq 100, S&P 500, Hang Seng |
| Government disinvestment | Shares of public sector companies | CPSE ETF, Bharat 22 ETF |

A few points for Indian investors:

- **Nifty 50 and Sensex ETFs** are the most traded and the simplest starting point.
- **Gold and silver ETFs** are a convenient way to hold precious metals without storage or making charges.
- **International ETFs** let you invest abroad in rupees, but SEBI limits how much Indian funds can invest overseas. At times fund houses have stopped fresh money into these funds, and their units have traded at a noticeable premium to iNAV. Check the premium before buying.
- **Liquid ETFs** are mainly used by traders to park cash in their demat accounts.

## Benefits of ETFs for first-time investors

- **Instant diversification:** one unit gives you a share of every company in the index.
- **Low cost:** most index ETFs have low expense ratios. Under SEBI's mutual fund regulations in force from 1 April 2026, the base expense cap for index funds and ETFs is 0.9% a year, and large Nifty 50 ETFs usually charge a small fraction of that.
- **Small starting amount:** you can buy a single unit, which for many ETFs costs from under ₹100 to a few hundred rupees.
- **Transparency:** an index ETF owns what the index owns, so you always know what you hold.
- **Trading flexibility:** you can use limit orders and buy at a known price during the day.

## Risks and drawbacks

- **Market risk:** an equity ETF falls when the market falls. There is no guarantee.
- **Liquidity risk:** many smaller ETFs trade very little. You may have to accept a poor price or wait to sell.
- **Tracking error:** returns can lag the index because of costs and cash held by the fund.
- **No built-in SIP:** you must place orders yourself, though some brokers let you set up recurring purchases.
- **Extra trading charges:** brokerage (zero at many brokers for delivery), STT, exchange charges, stamp duty and a DP charge when you sell.

## How to start investing in ETFs in India

1. **Open a demat and trading account** with a SEBI-registered broker and complete KYC with your PAN and Aadhaar. Our comparison of [Zerodha, Groww and Upstox](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/best-stock-exchange-app-upstox-or-grow-or-zerodha) explains the charges of three popular brokers.
2. **Add money** to your trading account from your bank.
3. **Choose your first ETF.** A broad Nifty 50 or Sensex ETF from a large fund house is a sensible core holding.
4. **Check four things:** the expense ratio, the average daily trading volume, the tracking error and the gap between price and iNAV.
5. **Place a limit order** close to the iNAV during market hours rather than a market order, especially for less-traded ETFs.
6. **Invest regularly.** Buy a fixed amount every month, or use your broker's recurring order feature.

If you prefer automatic SIPs without a demat account, an **index fund** that tracks the same index does almost the same job. Our guide to [mutual funds vs ETFs](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/mutual-funds-vs-etfs) compares the two.

## What ETFs really cost

The **expense ratio** is deducted from the fund's value every year. On a ₹1,00,000 investment, an expense ratio of 0.05% costs about ₹50 a year, while 1% costs about ₹1,000 a year.

Small differences add up. If ₹1,00,000 grows at 10% a year for 20 years, it becomes about ₹6.73 lakh. If costs cut the return to 9% a year, it becomes about ₹5.60 lakh, roughly **₹1.1 lakh less**. These are illustrative returns, not predictions.

Also count the trading costs and the price gap between buying and selling. For small, frequent purchases, these can matter more than the expense ratio.

## How ETFs are taxed in India

In India, ETFs have no special tax advantage over mutual funds; the tax depends on what the ETF holds. The rules since the July 2024 Budget are:

| ETF type | Short-term | Long-term |
|---|---|---|
| Equity ETFs (at least 65% in Indian shares) | Sold within 12 months: 20% | After 12 months: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year (across all your equity investments) |
| Gold, silver and international ETFs | Sold within 12 months: your slab rate | After 12 months: 12.5% |
| Debt ETFs bought on or after 1 April 2023 | Your slab rate, however long you hold | Not applicable |

Cess (and surcharge, if it applies) is added. Any dividend paid out is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Tax rules change with budgets, so check the Income Tax Department's website or a tax adviser before selling large amounts.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best ETFs for beginners in India?

A broad-market ETF that tracks the Nifty 50 or Sensex, from a large fund house, with a low expense ratio and high daily trading volume, is a sensible first choice. Add gold or debt ETFs later if you want to spread risk.

### Why are ETFs suitable for first-time investors?

They give instant diversification for the price of one unit, usually cost little to hold, and are easy to understand because most simply copy an index. You do need a demat account and should place your own orders.

### What is the minimum amount to invest in ETFs?

There is no fixed minimum; you need enough to buy one unit at the current market price, which is often under ₹100 to a few hundred rupees. Indian exchanges do not allow fractional ETF units.

### What costs should I be aware of when investing in ETFs?

The expense ratio, brokerage and statutory charges on each trade, a DP charge when you sell, and the bid-ask spread or premium to iNAV in less-traded ETFs. Compare these before you choose between similar ETFs.
