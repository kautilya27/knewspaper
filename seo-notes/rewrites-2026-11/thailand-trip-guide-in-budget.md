Thailand, the "Land of Smiles", is one of the easiest and most affordable foreign trips for Indian travellers: short flights, visa-free entry, cheap street food and a good transport network. This guide covers what you need to plan a budget trip in late 2026, from the latest entry rules to realistic daily costs in baht and rupees.

**Quick answer:** Indians can visit Thailand **visa-free for up to 30 days** for tourism (the rule since 15 September 2026), but must fill in the free online **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** before arrival. A backpacker can manage on roughly **฿1,000–1,800 a day (about ₹2,800–5,100)**, excluding international flights, while a comfortable mid-range trip costs roughly **฿3,000–6,000 a day (about ₹8,500–17,000)**.

## Visa rules for Indians (as of November 2026)

Older guides, including an earlier version of this one, said Indians need a visa on arrival. That is no longer true. Thailand's rules changed twice in the last few years:

- **Visa-free entry for 30 days.** New rules published in Thailand's Royal Gazette on 31 August 2026 replaced the earlier 60-day visa exemption from **15 September 2026**. India is on the list of 60 countries and territories whose citizens can enter for tourism and stay up to 30 days without a visa, according to the [Tourism Authority of Thailand's newsroom](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/09/thailand-introduces-new-30-day-and-15-day-visa-exemption-rules-from-15-september/). There is no visa fee.
- **No visa on arrival for Indians.** Under the new rules, visa on arrival is limited to a few countries, and India is not one of them. Since Indians are visa-exempt, you go straight to the immigration counter.
- **Land borders.** If you enter overland (for example from Malaysia or Laos), the 30-day exemption can generally be used no more than twice per calendar year.
- **Staying longer or for other purposes.** If you want to stay more than 30 days or are travelling for work, business or study, apply for the right visa in advance on the official [Thai e-Visa portal](https://www.thaievisa.go.th/). Extensions of a visa-free stay are decided by Thai immigration, so check with them before you plan on one.

### The TDAC arrival card

Since 1 May 2025, every foreign visitor must complete the **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** online before arriving. It replaced the old paper TM6 form. Fill it in on the official site, [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th), within **three days before your arrival**. It is **free**; ignore websites that charge a fee to "process" it.

### What to carry at immigration

- A passport valid for at least six months.
- A return or onward ticket and your hotel bookings.
- Enough money for your stay (cash, forex card or bank cards). Officers can ask for proof.

Thailand has also discussed a ฿300 tourism fee for foreign visitors. As of 5 November 2026 we could not confirm that it is being collected; check the TAT newsroom before you fly.

## Currency: baht to rupees

Thailand's currency is the **baht (THB, ฿)**. On 5 November 2026 the mid-market rate was about **฿1 = ₹2.83**, or roughly **₹100 = ฿35**. (The old version of this article had the rate the wrong way round.) Rates change daily, so check before you exchange.

Money tips for Indian travellers:

- **A forex card** usually gives better rates than airport exchange counters and is safer than carrying lots of cash.
- **Exchange counters in the city** usually beat airport rates. Compare a couple before you change large amounts.
- **Thai ATMs charge a fee** for foreign cards on each withdrawal, so withdraw larger amounts less often.
- Carry some baht in cash for street food, markets, songthaews and tuk-tuks, which often don't take cards.

## How much does a Thailand trip cost? Daily budgets

These ranges are per person per day, excluding international flights, based on 2026 Thailand travel cost guides. Rupee figures use ฿1 = ₹2.83 and are rounded.

| Travel style | Per day (baht) | Per day (rupees, approx.) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backpacker | ฿1,000–1,800 | ₹2,800–5,100 | Hostel dorm, street food, public transport, a few paid sights |
| Mid-range | ฿3,000–6,000 | ₹8,500–17,000 | 3-star hotel with a pool, restaurant meals, day tours, Grab rides |
| Luxury | ฿10,000+ | ₹28,000+ | Resorts, fine dining, private tours and transfers |

Typical prices reported for 2026:

- **Hostel dorm bed:** about ฿200–400 a night in most cities, more on the islands.
- **Basic guesthouse room:** about ฿300–500; a 3-star hotel with a pool about ฿1,000–2,000.
- **Street food dish** (pad thai, fried rice, noodle soup): about ฿40–60; a full street-food meal with a drink under ฿100.
- **Bangkok BTS/MRT metro ride:** roughly ฿16–59 depending on distance.
- **Overnight sleeper train:** roughly ฿800–1,400.

The islands (Phuket, Koh Samui, Koh Phi Phi) cost noticeably more than Bangkok, Chiang Mai or smaller towns. A budget traveller should allow at least ฿2,500 a day there.

**Example:** a 7-day backpacker trip at about ฿1,500 a day comes to about ฿10,500 (roughly ₹30,000) on the ground, plus your return flight.

## Best time to visit Thailand

- **Cool and dry season (November to February):** the most pleasant weather and the peak season. Prices and crowds are highest, especially around Christmas and New Year, so book early.
- **Hot season (March to May):** very hot, but good for beaches and often cheaper. Songkran, the Thai New Year water festival, is in mid-April.
- **Rainy season (June to October):** short heavy showers, fewer tourists and lower hotel prices. The Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi) can be rough for boat trips in these months.

The Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao) follow a different pattern, with their wettest weather usually from about October to December.

## Flights and getting around

There are direct flights to Bangkok from several Indian cities, including Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad, on both full-service and low-cost airlines. Bangkok has two airports: **Suvarnabhumi (BKK)**, the main international airport, and **Don Mueang (DMK)**, used by many low-cost airlines. Check which one your flight uses. Low fares often exclude checked baggage, so compare the total price.

Getting around Thailand:

- **Domestic flights:** the fastest way to cover long distances or reach the islands; cheapest when booked a couple of weeks ahead.
- **Trains:** cheap and scenic. The overnight sleeper from Bangkok to Chiang Mai saves a night's hotel.
- **Buses:** an extensive network of government and private buses, including overnight services.
- **Metro (BTS and MRT):** the easiest way around central Bangkok, avoiding traffic.
- **Grab and Bolt:** app-based taxis with the fare shown upfront; useful in cities.
- **Taxis and tuk-tuks:** insist on the meter in taxis; agree a tuk-tuk fare before you get in.
- **Songthaews:** shared pickup trucks on fixed routes, very cheap and common in Chiang Mai and smaller towns.
- **Ferries:** connect the mainland to the islands; prices vary by route and operator.

## Must-see places for a first trip

- **Bangkok:** the Grand Palace, Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha), Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn), Chinatown's street food and the night markets.
- **Ayutthaya:** the former capital of Siam, an easy day trip from Bangkok. Its temple ruins form the Ayutthaya Historical Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Chiang Mai:** a slower, cheaper city in the north, with old temples such as Wat Chiang Man, Wat Phra That Doi Suthep on the hill above the city, cooking classes and trekking.
- **Phuket:** Thailand's largest island. Patong is busy and lively; Kata and Karon are calmer. A base for diving, snorkelling and boat trips.
- **Krabi:** limestone cliffs, Railay Beach, Ao Nang and kayaking through mangroves. Usually cheaper than Phuket.

**Beyond the tourist trail:** Kanchanaburi (the Death Railway and waterfalls), Khao Sok National Park's jungle and lake, and quieter islands such as Koh Lanta and Koh Yao Noi.

## Experiences not to miss

- **Thai food:** pad thai, tom yum, green curry, som tam (papaya salad) and mango sticky rice. Vegetarians can look for "jay" food or ask for *mangsawirat* (vegetarian); Bangkok also has many Indian restaurants.
- **Thai massage:** traditional massage combining acupressure and stretching, widely available and affordable.
- **Muay Thai:** watch a live bout at a stadium or try a beginners' class.
- **Ethical elephant experiences:** choose sanctuaries that do not offer riding or performances.
- **Full Moon Party:** the monthly beach party at Haad Rin on Koh Phangan, if that is your scene; plan dates and book stays early.
- **Night markets and shopping:** souvenirs, clothes and snacks at the night markets; bargain politely.

## Essential tips and staying safe

- **Learn a few words:** "Sawasdee khrap/kha" (hello) and "Khop khun khrap/kha" (thank you). Men say khrap, women say kha.
- **Dress modestly at temples:** cover shoulders and knees, and remove your shoes before entering.
- **Respect the monarchy:** criticising the Thai royal family is a serious crime in Thailand.
- **Watch for scams:** be wary of strangers who say a temple is closed and offer a cheap tour, or gem-shop detours.
- **Food and water:** drink bottled or filtered water; choose busy food stalls where food is cooked fresh.
- **Sun and mosquitoes:** use sunscreen and a hat, and use mosquito repellent, as dengue occurs in Thailand, especially in the rainy season.
- **Travel insurance:** worth buying for medical emergencies, which can be expensive in private hospitals, and for cancellations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Indians need a visa for Thailand?

Not for short holidays. Since 15 September 2026, Indian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for tourism and stay up to 30 days. You must still fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card online within three days before arrival. For longer stays or other purposes, apply for an e-Visa in advance.

### How much does a Thailand trip cost from India?

On the ground, a budget traveller can manage on about ฿1,000–1,800 a day (roughly ₹2,800–5,100), and a mid-range traveller about ฿3,000–6,000 (roughly ₹8,500–17,000), plus return flights. A week of budget travel costs roughly ₹30,000 before flights, more on the islands.

### What is the best time to visit Thailand?

November to February is the best time for most of Thailand, with cooler, drier weather, though it is the busiest and most expensive season. March to May is hot but cheaper, and June to October is rainy with fewer tourists and lower prices.

### Is Indian rupee accepted in Thailand?

Shops and restaurants in Thailand use baht. Some money changers in tourist areas may exchange rupees, but rates are often poor, so most Indian travellers use a forex card, bank cards or baht bought before the trip.
