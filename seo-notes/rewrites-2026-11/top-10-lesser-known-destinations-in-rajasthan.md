When people think of Rajasthan, they picture Jaipur, Udaipur, Jodhpur and Jaisalmer. But the state has many quieter places with just as much history, colour and landscape, and far fewer crowds. If you want to see Rajasthan beyond the usual circuit, these ten lesser-known destinations are worth adding to your trip.

**Quick answer:** the best offbeat places in Rajasthan include the bird village of **Menar** near Udaipur, the hill fort of **Kumbhalgarh**, the stepwell town of **Bundi**, the fort town of **Kuchaman**, the marble temple at **Ranakpur**, desert **Barmer**, lake-side **Dungarpur**, the painted havelis of **Shekhawati**, green **Jhalawar** with Gagron Fort, and **Chauth Ka Barwara** near Ranthambore. October to March is the best time for most of them.

## 1. Menar (Udaipur district): Rajasthan's bird village

[[IMAGE 0]]

*Image source: rajasthantourplanner [dot] com*

Menar is a village about 45 km east of Udaipur, just off the highway to Chittorgarh. Its villagers have protected their ponds, Brahma Talab and Dhand Talab, for generations, and the wetland was declared a **Ramsar site** (a wetland of international importance) in June 2025. More than 170 bird species have been recorded here, and winter brings migratory waterbirds such as cranes, ducks and, in some years, flamingos.

Menar is also known for its **Zamra Beej** celebration just after Holi, when villagers fire muskets and cannons to mark a victory over a Mughal force about 400 years ago.

- **Highlights:** the two main lakes, birdwatching from the banks, the old village temples.
- **Tips:** visit from October to March for migratory birds. Udaipur has the nearest airport. Keep your distance from birds, avoid loud music and drones, and take any litter back with you.

## 2. Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand district): the great wall of Mewar

[[IMAGE 1]]

*Image source: tripxl [dot] com*

Kumbhalgarh Fort was built in the 15th century under Rana Kumbha of Mewar and is one of six **Hill Forts of Rajasthan** listed together as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013. Its wall runs for about **36 km** over the Aravalli hills, one of the longest continuous walls in the world. Inside are many temples, including the Neelkanth Mahadev temple, and the Badal Mahal palace at the top.

The fort sits inside the Kumbhalgarh Wildlife Sanctuary, a forested stretch of the Aravallis home to leopards and many birds.

- **Highlights:** walking the ramparts, the view from Badal Mahal, the temples, the evening light-and-sound show.
- **Tips:** the fort is about 84 km from Udaipur. Go between October and March, wear good shoes for the steep paths and carry water. Kumbhalgarh and Ranakpur are only about 30–35 km apart, so many people combine them.

## 3. Bundi (Bundi district): the city of stepwells

[[IMAGE 2]]

*Image source: moneycontrol [dot] com*

Bundi, in the Hadoti region of south-east Rajasthan, is a small, relaxed town with more than 50 stepwells, blue-washed lanes below a hillside palace, and some of the finest murals in the state. **Taragarh Fort**, built by Bundi's Hada Rajput rulers, looms over the town, and the palace below it has rooms covered in miniature-style paintings. The best-known stepwell, **Raniji ki Baori**, was built in 1699 by Rani Nathawati and is decorated with carvings of Vishnu's avatars.

- **Highlights:** Taragarh Fort, the palace murals, Raniji ki Baori, Sukh Mahal on Jait Sagar lake (where Rudyard Kipling stayed), and Nawal Sagar lake with its half-submerged temple.
- **Tips:** visit from October to February. Bundi is about 35 km from Kota, the main rail hub for the area, and about 206 km from Jaipur. Kota does not have regular passenger flights at present (a new airport is under construction), so most visitors fly into Jaipur and continue by road or train.

## 4. Kuchaman (Didwana-Kuchaman district): a fort above the town

[[IMAGE 3]]

*Image source: rajasthantourplanner [dot] com*

Kuchaman City, earlier in Nagaur district and now in the Didwana-Kuchaman district, is dominated by **Kuchaman Fort**, which sits on a cliff roughly 300 m (about 1,000 ft) above the town. The fort was expanded by the local Thakurs in the 18th century and is now a heritage hotel, with courtyards and palace rooms. The old town below has havelis, temples and busy bazaars, and sees far fewer tourists than Jaipur.

- **Highlights:** Kuchaman Fort and its views, the old town's havelis and markets.
- **Tips:** Kuchaman is about 120 km from Jaipur and about 100 km from Ajmer by road. Kuchaman City railway station is around 13 km from the town. Go between October and February, carry water and wear a hat.

## 5. Ranakpur (Pali district): the marble temple in the forest

[[IMAGE 4]]

*Image source: rajasthantourplanner [dot] com*

Ranakpur, in a wooded valley of the Aravallis, is home to the **Chaturmukha Jain temple** dedicated to Adinath, built in the 15th century from pale marble. It is famous for its **1,444 carved pillars**, no two exactly alike, and its maze of domed halls. It is one of the most important Jain pilgrimage sites in India and a peaceful stop between Udaipur and Jodhpur.

- **Highlights:** the main temple's pillars and ceilings, the smaller temples in the complex, the forested setting.
- **Tips:** Ranakpur is about 90–100 km from Udaipur, and about 33 km from Kumbhalgarh. It is an active place of worship, so dress modestly, remove your shoes and leave leather items outside. Tourist visiting hours are restricted to part of the day, so check current timings before you go.

## 6. Barmer (Barmer district): crafts and music of the Thar

[[IMAGE 5]]

*Image source: timesofindia.indiatimes [dot] com*

Barmer, in western Rajasthan near the Pakistan border, shows a side of the Thar Desert that few tourists see. The district is known for **ajrakh and hand block printing, wood carving, pottery and embroidery**, and for its folk musicians. Barmer Garh, the town's fort, was built on a hillock in 1552, with the Jogmaya Devi and Nagnechi Mata temples above it. About 35 km away are the **Kiradu temples**, a group of five medieval temples known for their carvings, of which the Someshwara temple is the finest. Tilwara village hosts the annual **Mallinath cattle fair** in honour of Rawal Mallinath.

- **Highlights:** the Kiradu temples, Barmer Garh and its hilltop temples, craft villages, the Mallinath fair.
- **Tips:** visit from November to February; summers are extremely hot. Barmer has a railway station, but no regular passenger flights; an airport is still being planned, so the nearest airport with regular flights is Jodhpur. Expect basic facilities outside the town.

## 7. Dungarpur (Dungarpur district): lakes and palaces of Vagad

[[IMAGE 6]]

*Image source: incredibleindia [dot] com*

Dungarpur, in the Aravalli hills of southern Rajasthan, was founded in the 13th century and named after a Bhil chieftain, Dungaria; the Bhil community is still central to the region's culture. The town centres on **Gaib Sagar lake**, with the Shrinathji temple complex and Badal Mahal on its banks and migratory birds in winter. The seven-storey **Juna Mahal**, dating from the 13th century, has walls covered in murals, miniature paintings and mirror work, while the 19th-century **Udai Bilas Palace** on the lake is now a heritage hotel.

- **Highlights:** Juna Mahal, Gaib Sagar lake and Badal Mahal, Udai Bilas Palace, the Shrinathji and Vijay Rajrajeshwar temples.
- **Experiences:** in February, the **Baneshwar Fair** on Magh Purnima draws large crowds, mostly from Bhil communities, to the confluence of the Mahi, Som and Jakham rivers.
- **Tips:** go between October and February. Udaipur, to the north, has the nearest airport and main railway station.

## 8. Shekhawati (Mandawa, Nawalgarh, Fatehpur): the open-air art gallery

[[IMAGE 7]]

*Image source: edition.cnn [dot] com*

Shekhawati, in northern Rajasthan (mainly Jhunjhunu, Sikar and Churu districts), is often called an open-air art gallery. Wealthy merchant families built grand **havelis** here in the 18th to early 20th centuries and covered them in frescoes showing gods, folk tales, and even trains and cars from the colonial era. Many families later moved away, so some havelis are well restored while others are fading.

- **Highlights:** in Mandawa, the Chokhani Double Haveli, Murmuria Haveli, Hanuman Prasad Goenka Haveli and Jhunjhunwala Haveli, plus Mandawa's fort, now a hotel; in Fatehpur, the restored Haveli Nadine Le Prince; and Nawalgarh's painted streets.
- **Experiences:** walk the lanes with a local guide who can explain the paintings, and stay a night in a haveli hotel.
- **Tips:** visit from October to March. Shekhawati is a few hours' drive north of Jaipur; Jhunjhunu is the nearest railway station to Mandawa. Some havelis charge a small entry fee or are private homes, so ask before entering.

## 9. Jhalawar (Jhalawar district): Rajasthan's green corner

[[IMAGE 8]]

*Image source: indiantempletour [dot] com*

Jhalawar, in the south-eastern Hadoti region, is greener and wetter than most of Rajasthan. Its star sight is **Gagron Fort**, a "water fort" surrounded on three sides by rivers and one of the six UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan. In nearby Jhalrapatan, the **Sun Temple** dates from around the 11th–12th century, and the town also has old Jain temples. Jhalawar's own **Garh Palace** was built in the 1840s.

- **Highlights:** Gagron Fort, Jhalrapatan's Sun Temple and Jain temples, Garh Palace and the town museum.
- **Experiences:** drives through the countryside, especially green after the monsoon, and quiet riverside views from Gagron.
- **Tips:** the best time is from late monsoon to winter (roughly August to February). Jhalawar is a little over 80 km south of Kota on NH52, and Jhalawar City railway station, about 2 km from town, has trains to Kota. The nearest airports with regular flights are farther away (Jaipur, Indore or Bhopal), so plan to travel by train or road.

## 10. Chauth Ka Barwara (Sawai Madhopur district): temple and fort near Ranthambore

[[IMAGE 9]]

*Image source: ebnw [dot] net*

Chauth Ka Barwara is a village in **Sawai Madhopur district**, about an hour's drive from Ranthambore National Park and about 170 km (around 2.5 hours) from Jaipur airport. It is known for two landmarks. The **Chauth Mata temple** sits on a hill about 1,000 ft above the village and draws large numbers of married women around Karva Chauth. Below it, the **14th-century Barwara fort** has been restored as a luxury hotel, Six Senses Fort Barwara.

- **Highlights:** the climb to Chauth Mata temple and the view from the top, the fort's architecture seen from the village, the rural landscape.
- **Tips:** go between October and March, and combine it with a Ranthambore safari. Remove your shoes and respect temple customs, and expect crowds around Karva Chauth.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to visit offbeat places in Rajasthan?

October to March is the best time for most of Rajasthan, when days are pleasant and dry and migratory birds arrive at wetlands such as Menar. Summers are very hot, especially in desert areas like Barmer, while greener regions such as Jhalawar also look lovely just after the monsoon.

### Which hidden gems in Rajasthan can be visited from Udaipur?

From Udaipur you can reach several lesser-known places by road, including the bird village of Menar, the hill fort of Kumbhalgarh, the marble Jain temple at Ranakpur and the lake town of Dungarpur. Kumbhalgarh and Ranakpur are only about 30–35 km apart, so they are often combined in one trip.

### Is Kumbhalgarh Fort a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Yes. Kumbhalgarh Fort is one of six Hill Forts of Rajasthan listed together as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013. Built in the 15th century under Rana Kumbha in the Aravalli hills, it is known for its 36-km wall, temples and views over the surrounding hills.

### What is the Shekhawati region of Rajasthan famous for?

Shekhawati in northern Rajasthan is famous for its painted havelis, built by wealthy merchants mainly in the 18th and 19th centuries and covered in colourful frescoes of myths, folk tales and scenes from the colonial era. Towns such as Mandawa, Nawalgarh and Fatehpur are so full of murals that the region is called an open-air art gallery.
