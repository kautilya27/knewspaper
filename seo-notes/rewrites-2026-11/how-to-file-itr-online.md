**Quick answer:** you file your Income Tax Return (ITR) online on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal: log in with your PAN, choose **AY 2026-27** (for income earned from April 2025 to March 2026), pick the right form, check the pre-filled data, pay any tax due, submit and **e-verify within 30 days**. The normal deadlines for AY 2026-27 have passed (31 July 2026 for ITR-1 and ITR-2, 31 August 2026 for non-audit ITR-3 and ITR-4), but you can still file a **belated return until 31 December 2026** with a late fee.

Filing is simpler than it sounds, because most of your salary, TDS, interest and investment data is already pre-filled. This guide covers which form to use, the step-by-step process, the 2026 deadlines, how the new tax regime affects your deductions, and what changes from next year under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

## What is an ITR?

An Income Tax Return is the yearly form in which you report your total income, the tax already paid (TDS, advance tax, self-assessment tax), any deductions you claim and any refund due. Filing is compulsory if your income is above the basic exemption limit, and in some other cases set by law (for example, very high electricity bills, foreign travel spending or large bank deposits).

## Why file even if you don't have to

- **Get a refund** of TDS deducted from salary, interest or rent.
- **Carry forward losses** from shares, mutual funds or business to set off in later years. This needs a return filed by the due date.
- **Proof of income** for home loans, credit cards and visa applications.
- **Avoid the late fee** under Section 234F and interest on unpaid tax.

## Which ITR form should you use for AY 2026-27?

| Form | Who uses it | Main conditions |
|---|---|---|
| ITR-1 (Sahaj) | Resident salaried people and pensioners | Total income up to ₹50 lakh; salary, up to two house properties, interest and other sources; long-term gains on listed shares or equity funds up to ₹1.25 lakh |
| ITR-2 | Individuals and HUFs without business income | Income above ₹50 lakh, larger capital gains, foreign assets or income, company directors |
| ITR-3 | Individuals and HUFs with business or professional income | Includes freelancers who keep books, and F&O or intraday traders |
| ITR-4 (Sugam) | Residents using presumptive taxation | Total income up to ₹50 lakh; business turnover up to ₹2 crore (₹3 crore if cash receipts are 5% or less) or professional receipts up to ₹50 lakh (₹75 lakh if cash receipts are 5% or less) |
| ITR-5, 6, 7 | Firms, LLPs, companies, trusts | Entity returns |

**What changed this year:** for AY 2026-27, ITR-1 and ITR-4 can be used with income from **up to two house properties** (earlier only one), so many people with a second flat no longer need ITR-2. Both forms also allow long-term capital gains under Section 112A up to ₹1.25 lakh, as long as you have no losses brought forward.

If you sold mutual fund units during the year, our guide to [how SWP withdrawals and mutual fund gains are taxed](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/what-is-swp) explains the current rates.

## Old vs new tax regime: check this before you file

The **new tax regime is the default**. If you don't choose otherwise, your return is computed under it. For FY 2025-26:

- The new regime gives **zero tax on income up to ₹12 lakh** after the Section 87A rebate (₹12.75 lakh for salaried people, thanks to the ₹75,000 standard deduction). This rebate does not cover income taxed at special rates, such as capital gains on shares.
- Most deductions are **not allowed** in the new regime: Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, life insurance), 80D (health insurance), HRA, LTA and interest on a home loan for a self-occupied house. A few remain, such as the standard deduction and your employer's contribution to NPS.
- The **old regime** still allows these deductions, with a ₹50,000 standard deduction. Salaried people can pick it in the ITR itself, but only in a return filed **by the due date**. A belated return is processed under the new regime. People with business income have to opt out using Form 10-IEA, and can switch back only in limited cases.

Compare both on the portal's tax calculator before you submit. The Budget changes behind these numbers are summarised in our [Union Budget explainer](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/union-budget-2024), which also covers what Budgets 2025 and 2026 changed.

## Step-by-step: how to file ITR online

### Step 1: Gather your documents

- PAN and Aadhaar (they must be linked, or your PAN becomes inoperative and refunds stop)
- Form 16 from your employer, and Form 16A for other TDS
- Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS, downloadable from the portal
- Bank account details (pre-validated on the portal for refunds)
- Capital gains statements from your broker or mutual fund registrar
- Proofs for any deductions, if you choose the old regime

### Step 2: Log in to the e-filing portal

Go to the official portal at [https://www.incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in). New users register with their PAN; existing users log in with PAN (or Aadhaar) as the user ID, plus password and OTP.

### Step 3: Start a new return

Click **e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return**. Select **Assessment Year 2026-27** and **Online** as the filing mode, then **Start New Filing**.

### Step 4: Choose your status and form

Select **Individual** (or HUF), then either let the portal guide you to the right form by answering a few questions, or choose it yourself using the table above.

### Step 5: Check the pre-filled data

The portal pre-fills your salary, TDS, interest, dividends and many capital gains from your AIS and Form 26AS. Compare everything with your own records and Form 16, add anything missing (such as savings account interest or rent received), and correct mistakes. Income missing from your return but shown in your AIS can trigger a notice.

### Step 6: Claim deductions and choose the regime

Enter eligible deductions if you are using the old regime, and confirm your regime choice. The portal then computes your tax.

### Step 7: Pay any tax due

If tax is payable, pay it through **e-Pay Tax** on the portal (net banking, UPI, debit card or payment gateway) before submitting. The challan details are added to your return.

### Step 8: Preview, submit and e-verify

Preview the return, validate it and submit. Then **e-verify within 30 days** using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, a bank or demat account EVC, or a digital signature certificate. Without verification your return is treated as not filed, and no refund is processed. Refunds usually arrive in your pre-validated bank account after the return is processed.

## ITR filing deadlines for AY 2026-27

| Who | Due date |
|---|---|
| ITR-1 and ITR-2 (non-audit) | 31 July 2026 (passed) |
| ITR-3 and ITR-4, non-audit business and professional income | 31 August 2026 (passed) |
| Tax audit cases | 21 November 2026 (extended by CBDT from 31 October) |
| Transfer pricing cases | 30 November 2026 |
| Belated return (any taxpayer) | 31 December 2026 |
| Revised return | 31 March 2027 (a fee applies after 31 December 2026) |
| Updated return (ITR-U) | Up to 48 months from the end of the assessment year, with extra tax |

**Late fee for a belated return:** ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if your total income is up to ₹5 lakh, plus interest of 1% a month on unpaid tax. No late fee applies if your income is below the basic exemption limit. You also lose the right to carry forward most losses and, as noted above, the option to choose the old regime.

## What changes from next year: the Income-tax Act, 2025

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act from **1 April 2026**. Two things matter for filers:

- **This year's return (AY 2026-27)** is still filed under the old Act and its forms, because it covers income earned before 1 April 2026.
- **Next year**, income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 belongs to **"tax year 2026-27"**. The new Act drops the separate "previous year" and "assessment year" labels, and section numbers change (for example, the late fee and belated return rules now sit in different sections). New ITR forms and due dates for that year will be notified by CBDT, so check the portal in 2027 rather than relying on this year's dates.

The department's own FAQs on the new Act are on the portal's help pages.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Choosing the wrong form, for example ITR-1 when you have short-term capital gains.
- Ignoring income in your AIS, such as savings interest or dividends.
- Claiming 80C or 80D deductions without switching to the old regime.
- Not pre-validating your bank account, so the refund fails.
- Forgetting to e-verify within 30 days.
- Leaving it to the last days, when the portal is slow.

## Quick takeaways

- ITR for income earned in FY 2025-26 is filed for AY 2026-27.
- File online at [incometax.gov.in](http://incometax.gov.in) and e-verify within 30 days.
- The new regime is the default; most deductions apply only in the old regime.
- Missed the deadline? A belated return is allowed until 31 December 2026, with a fee.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to file ITR if my income is below the taxable limit?

It is generally not compulsory if your income is below the basic exemption limit and no other legal condition requires you to file. Filing anyway is still useful, because it lets you claim a refund of any TDS deducted, carry forward losses and show proof of income for loans, credit cards and visas.

### What happens if I miss the ITR filing deadline?

For AY 2026-27 you can still file a belated return until 31 December 2026. A late fee of ₹5,000 applies, capped at ₹1,000 if your total income is ₹5 lakh or less, along with interest on any unpaid tax. You also lose the right to carry forward most losses and cannot choose the old tax regime.

### Which ITR form should a salaried person file?

Most resident salaried people with total income up to ₹50 lakh, from salary, up to two house properties and other sources such as interest, can use ITR-1 (Sahaj). If you have income above that, short-term capital gains or larger long-term gains, or foreign assets, you generally need ITR-2. The portal can also suggest the right form.

### How do I e-verify my ITR, and what if I don't?

You can e-verify your return using an Aadhaar OTP, net banking, an electronic verification code through your bank or demat account, or a digital signature certificate, within 30 days of filing. A return that is not verified in time is treated as not filed and will not be processed, so any refund will also be held up.
