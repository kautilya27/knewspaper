Gurgaon (officially Gurugram) is one of India's biggest corporate hubs, home to Cyber City, Golf Course Road and hundreds of company offices. It is also one of the costlier places to live in the country, mainly because of housing.

**Quick answer:** yes, Gurgaon is expensive by Indian standards, but how expensive depends on where you live. In 2026, a 1 BHK cost about **₹25,000 a month in central Gurgaon** and **₹14,000–18,000 in outer areas** (NoBroker's cost-of-living guide, updated February 2026). A standard 2 BHK in newer belts like New Gurugram or Dwarka Expressway rented for about **₹22,000–37,000** (ANAROCK, Q1 2026), and new flats there were quoted at around **₹13,000–14,000 per sq ft**. Prime areas such as Golf Course Road cost far more.

## Housing: the biggest expense

Whether you rent or buy, housing will take the largest share of your budget in Gurgaon.

### Rent

|---|---|---|---|
| Room in a shared flat | Citywide | ₹7,000–20,000 | NoBroker guide, updated Feb 2026 |
| 1 BHK | Outer areas | ₹14,000–18,000 | NoBroker guide, updated Feb 2026 |

Flats on Golf Course Road, in the DLF phases and in luxury societies near Cyber City can cost far more than these ranges. Furnished flats, newer societies and homes close to the Rapid Metro also command higher rents. For a sector-by-sector breakdown, see our guide to [the cost of rent in Gurugram](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/what-is-the-cost-of-rent-in-gurugram).

Remember the **upfront cost**: landlords usually ask for a refundable security deposit of a few months' rent, plus the first month's rent, and you may pay brokerage too. Some fintech services, such as [CirclePe](https://www.circlepe.club/?utm_source=knewspaper&utm_medium=blog6jan&utm_campaign=knewspaper+6+jan+blog), offer zero-deposit rentals by paying the landlord upfront and charging the tenant a fee on top of monthly rent; if you consider one, compare the total cost and read the terms carefully.

### Buying

Property prices in Gurgaon have risen sharply in recent years. ANAROCK's Q1 2026 report quoted average base prices of about **₹14,000 per sq ft on Dwarka Expressway** and **₹13,300 per sq ft in New Gurugram**, well above the NCR average of about ₹9,620 per sq ft. At those rates, a 1,000 sq ft flat in these newer belts costs well over ₹1 crore before stamp duty, registration and interiors, and prime areas cost much more.

## Monthly costs beyond rent

NoBroker's February 2026 guide gives these rough monthly figures:

| Expense | Single person / bachelor | Couple | Family |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utilities and maintenance | About ₹4,000 | ₹5,000+ | ₹6,000+ |
| Transport | About ₹3,000+ | ₹4,000+ | ₹6,000+ |
| Groceries | About ₹8,000 | ₹8,000–10,000 | ₹10,000–12,000 |

Treat these as starting points. Your real spend depends on how much you eat out, how far you commute and how hard you run the air conditioner.

**Electricity:** bills climb in May to September because of air conditioning, and many societies charge separately for power backup. Ask the landlord or society for recent bills before you sign.

**Transport:** the Delhi Metro's Yellow Line reaches Gurgaon, and the Rapid Metro loops through Cyber City and Golf Course Road. Fares depend on distance. Many newer sectors along Dwarka Expressway, Sohna Road and New Gurugram are some distance from a metro station, so people living there often rely on app cabs, autos or their own vehicle, which adds fuel and parking costs.

**Food and eating out:** groceries cost about the same as in other big cities. Eating out ranges from cheap local dhabas to some of NCR's priciest restaurants and bars, so this is where lifestyle makes the biggest difference.

**Education:** Gurgaon has everything from government schools to expensive international schools. Fees vary enormously by school, so check each school's official fee structure for the current year.

**Healthcare:** the city has several large private hospitals, and private consultations and treatment can be costly. Good health insurance is worth having.

## Where can you live more cheaply?

Outer and newer areas are cheaper than the prime belts around Cyber City and Golf Course Road. Options people often consider include:

- **New Gurugram** (Sectors 76–95 area) and **Dwarka Expressway** sectors, with newer societies at lower rents than prime areas.
- **Sohna Road** and nearby sectors, with a wide range of societies and builder floors.
- **Older sectors and builder floors** in areas such as Palam Vihar and the older city sectors, which can be cheaper than big societies.

The trade-off is usually a longer commute and patchier public transport, so check travel time to your office at peak hour. Our list of [affordable localities in Gurgaon](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/affordable-localities-in-gurgaon) goes into more detail.

## Tips for saving money in Gurgaon

- **Live near your office or a metro station.** Cutting a daily cab ride can save thousands a month.
- **Share a flat.** A room in a shared flat costs a fraction of a solo 1 BHK.
- **Consider builder floors or older societies.** They often cost less than new gated projects with big amenity charges.
- **Negotiate.** Landlords may lower the rent or deposit for a longer lease or a quick move-in.
- **Cook at home** most days and save eating out for weekends.
- **Check all charges in writing**: maintenance, power backup, parking, brokerage and lock-in period.

## Verdict: is Gurgaon expensive?

**Yes, Gurgaon is one of the more expensive places to live in India**, mostly because of rent and property prices. But costs vary hugely between prime belts and outer sectors, and high salaries in the city's corporate offices offset much of the difference for many people. With a well-chosen location and a sensible budget, living in Gurgaon is manageable. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the biggest expense of living in Gurgaon?

Housing is by far the biggest expense, whether you pay rent or a home loan EMI. In 2026 a 1 BHK cost about ₹14,000–18,000 a month in outer areas and about ₹25,000 centrally, according to NoBroker's guide. After that come transport, electricity bills that rise in summer, eating out and, for families, school fees.

### Which are the more affordable areas to live in Gurgaon?

Newer and outer areas such as New Gurugram, the Dwarka Expressway sectors and Sohna Road usually cost less than prime locations like Golf Course Road and the area around DLF Cyber City. The trade-off can be a longer commute, so check travel time to your office before choosing.

### Do I need a car to live in Gurgaon?

Not necessarily. The Delhi Metro and the Rapid Metro cover parts of Gurgaon, and autos and app cabs are easy to find. But many newer sectors are far from a metro station and last-mile options can be patchy, so people living there often rely on cabs or their own vehicle.

### Is Gurgaon the same as Gurugram?

Yes. The Haryana government officially renamed the city Gurugram in 2016, but both names are still widely used, especially Gurgaon in everyday speech. Addresses, government records and property documents now generally use Gurugram.
