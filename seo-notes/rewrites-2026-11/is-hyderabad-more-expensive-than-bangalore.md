Hyderabad and Bangalore (Bengaluru) are India's two big southern tech hubs, and many people choosing a job offer end up comparing them. So which city is easier on your wallet in 2026?

**Quick answer:** no, Hyderabad is generally **cheaper than Bangalore**, mainly because of rent and the upfront deposit. In mid-2026, a 1 BHK in Hyderabad's IT areas was listed at about **₹15,000–24,000 a month**, against **₹22,000–38,000** in Bangalore hubs like Koramangala and Whitefield (Square Yards locality data, May 2026). Bangalore landlords also tend to ask for much bigger deposits. Petrol, however, costs more in Hyderabad, and Bangalore still has a deeper job market for many tech roles.

## 1. Housing: Hyderabad's biggest advantage

Rent is where the gap is widest. In the IT corridors, a 1 BHK in Hyderabad typically costs several thousand rupees a month less than a comparable flat in Bangalore's tech hubs, going by Square Yards' May 2026 locality ranges. For a standard 1,000 sq ft 2 BHK in newer projects, ANAROCK's Q1 2026 figures are much closer: Gachibowli (₹32,000–43,000) and Whitefield (₹30,000–45,000) are almost level, so the gap shrinks at the premium end.

The budget end is close too. Outer areas like LB Nagar and Miyapur in Hyderabad and Kengeri or Electronic City in Bangalore all have 1 BHK listings starting below ₹15,000.

The real difference is the **security deposit**. Bangalore landlords have long asked for six to ten months' rent upfront, while deposits in Hyderabad are typically two to three months' rent, as Deccan Herald has reported. On a ₹25,000 flat, that can mean paying ₹1.5–2.5 lakh upfront in Bangalore against ₹50,000–75,000 in Hyderabad. If you are moving on a fresher's salary, that alone can decide the matter.

## 2. Buying a home

If you plan to buy, Hyderabad is also cheaper on average. ANAROCK's Q1 2026 reports put the average quoted price of new homes at about ₹7,990 per sq ft in Hyderabad and about ₹9,310 per sq ft in Bangalore. Prices in prime areas of both cities are far above these averages.

## 3. Transport and commuting

**Fuel:** petrol is more expensive in Hyderabad. On 1 November 2026, it cost ₹115.69 a litre in Hyderabad and ₹110.82 in Bengaluru, according to daily fuel price reports. If you drive a lot, this narrows Hyderabad's advantage a little.

**Traffic:** this is Bangalore's biggest hidden cost. In the TomTom Traffic Index 2025, Bengaluru was the second most congested city in the world, with an average congestion level of 74.4% and an average of about 36 minutes to travel 10 km. Hyderabad ranked 47th, with a congestion level of 55.5%. Hyderabad's roads still jam at peak hours in the IT corridor, but long commutes are a bigger daily problem in Bangalore.

**Public transport:** both cities have growing metro networks and city bus services. Before you choose an area, check whether a metro station or a direct bus route connects it to your office.

## 4. Food, groceries and utilities

Day-to-day costs such as groceries, eating out, electricity, internet and cooking gas are broadly similar in the two cities. Both have cheap local eateries and expensive cafés and pubs. Your spending here depends far more on your lifestyle than on the city. Cooking at home, buying from local markets and avoiding daily food delivery saves more than switching cities.

## 5. Jobs and salaries

Bangalore is still India's largest tech job market, with more start-ups, product companies and senior roles. Hyderabad has grown fast as a base for global capability centres (GCCs), IT services and pharma. Pay for the same role varies more by company than by city, so compare actual offers rather than city averages. If your offers are close, Hyderabad's lower rent and deposit usually mean you save more each month.

## 6. Lifestyle

Bangalore has a bigger start-up and pub culture and a milder climate for most of the year. Hyderabad has a strong food culture, a long history and a slightly slower pace, with hot summers. Neither is "better"; it depends on what you value.

## Verdict: which city is cheaper?

- **Hyderabad is cheaper overall**, mainly because rent and especially the security deposit are lower, and commutes are usually shorter.
- **Bangalore costs more** but offers the largest tech job market in India, which can mean better long-term career options in some fields.
- **Petrol is costlier in Hyderabad**, and premium 2 BHK rents in the two cities' IT hubs are now fairly close.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Hyderabad cheaper than Bangalore overall?

Yes, for most people. Rent in Hyderabad's IT areas is generally lower than in Bangalore's tech hubs, and security deposits are much smaller, usually two to three months' rent against six to ten in Bangalore. Groceries and utilities cost about the same, while petrol is slightly more expensive in Hyderabad.

### Is the security deposit lower in Hyderabad than in Bangalore?

Usually, yes. Hyderabad landlords typically ask for two to three months' rent as a deposit, while Bangalore landlords have traditionally asked for six to ten months' rent. The amount is negotiable in both cities, so agree on it and the refund terms in writing before you pay.

### Which city has worse traffic, Hyderabad or Bangalore?

Bangalore. The TomTom Traffic Index 2025 ranked Bengaluru the second most congested city in the world, with a congestion level of 74.4%, while Hyderabad ranked 47th at 55.5%. Peak-hour traffic in Hyderabad's IT corridor can still be heavy, so living near your office or a metro station helps in both cities.

### Are salaries higher in Bangalore than in Hyderabad?

Bangalore has the larger tech job market, with more start-ups and product companies, and some roles pay more there. But pay for the same job depends more on the company than the city. Because rent and deposits are lower in Hyderabad, a similar salary often leaves you with more savings there.
