Fast fashion is bad for you and the environment because it is built on making huge volumes of cheap, trendy clothes very quickly, and on people throwing them away almost as quickly. That model wastes money and resources, relies on low-paid factory work, and leaves mountains of textile waste and microplastic pollution behind.

**Quick answer:** fast fashion hurts **you** (poor-quality clothes that wear out fast, possible skin irritation from cheap dyes and finishes, and a constant urge to buy more), **garment workers** (low pay and unsafe conditions in many supply chains) and the **planet**. The UN says the textile sector produces **2–8% of global greenhouse gas emissions** and the world generates about **92 million tonnes of textile waste a year**. Buying less, choosing better-made pieces, rewearing, repairing and buying second-hand all help.

## What is fast fashion?

Fast fashion means clothes designed to copy the latest trends, made in bulk at low cost and sold cheaply, with new styles arriving every few weeks. Think of a ₹500 top that looks great in the photos but loses its shape after a few washes. Because it is cheap and goes out of style fast, you buy another, and the cycle repeats.

The problem isn't one shirt. It's the speed and scale. A UN key-messages note for the 2025 International Day of Zero Waste estimates that some garments are thrown away after just **seven to 10 uses**.

## Why fast fashion is bad for you

### 1. It costs you more in the long run

A cheap piece that falls apart after a few wears is not a bargain. If a ₹500 shirt lasts five outings and a ₹1,500 shirt lasts fifty, the "expensive" one costs far less per wear. Fast fashion's low prices hide this, so you keep spending on replacements.

### 2. Poor quality and possible skin irritation

Cheap fabrics, dyes and finishes can irritate sensitive skin, and some people react to certain dyes or chemical treatments. Simple steps help: **wash new clothes before wearing them**, choose natural, breathable fabrics like cotton or linen for everyday wear in Indian heat, and check labels. If a rash or itching doesn't settle or keeps coming back, see a doctor or dermatologist.

### 3. The pressure to keep up

Endless new "drops", influencer hauls and sale notifications make it feel as if your wardrobe is always out of date. That constant comparison can leave you feeling dissatisfied, and impulse buying can strain your budget.

## Why fast fashion is bad for workers

### 4. Low wages and unsafe factories

Many fast-fashion supply chains depend on factories where pay is low, hours are long and safety standards are weak. The deadliest reminder was the **2013 Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh**, where a building housing garment factories fell and killed more than 1,100 people. Pressure to produce more, faster and cheaper keeps that risk alive.

## Why fast fashion is bad for the environment

These figures come from the UN's 2025 International Day of Zero Waste key messages (UNEP and UN-Habitat):

| Problem | What the UN says |
|---|---|
| Climate | The textile sector produces **2–8% of global greenhouse gas emissions** each year |
| Waste | About **92 million tonnes** of textile waste are generated worldwide every year |
| Recycling | Only **8%** of textile fibres in 2023 came from recycled sources, and **less than 1%** from textile-to-textile recycling |
| Water | The sector uses about **215 trillion litres** of water a year |
| Microplastics | Textiles contribute **9%** of the microplastic pollution flowing into the ocean |
| Synthetic fibres | Synthetics made up about **67%** of fibre production in 2023 |

### 5. Mountains of textile waste

Most discarded clothes are not turned back into new clothes. They are dumped, burned, or shipped abroad as second-hand clothing, some of which ends up as waste there. When fast fashion encourages people to buy more and wear less, the pile only grows.

### 6. Microplastics from synthetic fabrics

Polyester, nylon and acrylic are plastics. Every time they are washed, they shed tiny fibres that pass through drains into rivers and seas. Because synthetics now make up most of the fibre produced worldwide, cheap polyester-heavy fashion adds directly to this pollution.

### 7. Water, chemicals and dyes

Growing fibres, dyeing and finishing fabric use large amounts of water and chemicals. Where factory waste water isn't treated properly, dyes and chemicals can end up in local rivers, affecting the communities that live nearby.

### 8. Carbon emissions

From making synthetic fibres from fossil fuels to running factories and shipping clothes around the world, fashion has a significant carbon footprint. The UN notes that **doubling the number of times a garment is worn would cut its greenhouse gas emissions by 44%**, which shows how much simply wearing clothes longer matters.

## What can you do? Simple ways to cut fast fashion

You don't have to stop shopping. Small changes add up:

### Buy less, choose better

Before you buy, ask: will I wear this at least 30 times? Pick well-made basics in timeless cuts and check the stitching, fabric weight and finishing. Our guide to [what makes a good polo T-shirt](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/polo-t-shirt-for-men-key-features-to-look-for) shows what quality details to look for.

### Shop second-hand

Thrift stores, flea markets, resale apps and family hand-me-downs give clothes a second life and usually cost less than new.

### Choose brands that are open about how they make clothes

Look for brands that say where their clothes are made, what they are made of and how workers are treated, and that back up claims with recognised certifications. Be wary of vague "eco" labels with no details.

### Repair and restyle

Sew on the button, fix the seam, take jeans to the local tailor. Restyling also stretches a wardrobe; see our ideas for [styling oversized T-shirts](https://www.knewspaper.in/post/5-ways-to-style-oversized-t-shirts-for-men) in different ways.

### Wash smarter

Wash clothes only when they need it, use cold water, run full loads and air-dry instead of using a dryer. A microfibre-catching laundry bag can reduce shedding from synthetic clothes.

### Swap with friends

A clothes swap is free, fun and keeps wearable clothes out of the bin.

### Donate or recycle responsibly

Give clothes in good condition to people who need them or to collection drives, and send worn-out cotton to textile collection points where available rather than throwing it in the bin.

## Time to break up with fast fashion

Fast fashion is convenient, but its low price hides real costs for your wallet, for workers and for the planet. You don't need a perfect, ethical wardrobe overnight. Buying fewer, better clothes and wearing them for longer is the single biggest change most people can make.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is fast fashion?

Fast fashion is cheap, trendy clothing produced quickly and in large volumes so that shoppers can keep buying the latest styles. The clothes are often low in quality and wear out after a few washes, which creates a cycle of buying, throwing away and buying again.

### Why is fast fashion bad for the environment?

Because clothes are made cheaply and discarded quickly, huge amounts of textile waste are created; the UN estimates about 92 million tonnes a year. Synthetic fabrics also shed microplastic fibres when washed, and the textile sector accounts for an estimated 2–8% of global greenhouse gas emissions.

### Can cheap clothes cause skin problems?

They can for some people. Low-cost fabrics, dyes and finishes may irritate sensitive skin or trigger allergic reactions. Washing new clothes before you wear them can help, and if you get a rash that does not settle or keeps coming back, see a doctor or dermatologist.

### How can I avoid fast fashion on a budget?

Buy fewer but better-made pieces in timeless styles, shop at thrift and second-hand stores, swap clothes with friends and repair or restyle what you already own. Washing clothes less often and in cold water also helps them last longer, which saves money over time.
